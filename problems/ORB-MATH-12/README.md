# ORB-MATH-12: The Bost conjecture: is the $\ell^1$-assembly map an isomorphism for every countable discrete group?

The Bost conjecture predicts that for every countable discrete group $G$ the assembly map $\mu^G\colon K^G_*(\underline{E}G)\to K^{\mathrm{top}}_*(\ell^1(G))$, from the equivariant K-homology of the classifying space for proper $G$-actions to the topological K-theory of the Banach algebra $\ell^1(G)$, is an isomorphism. Berrick, Chatterji and Mislin proved that the Bost conjecture implies the classical Bass conjecture on Hattori–Stallings ranks of finitely generated projective $\mathbb{Z}G$-modules, and consequently that all amenable groups satisfy the Bass conjecture. The Bost conjecture itself remains open for general countable discrete groups: it is known for a-T-menable (Haagerup) groups, word-hyperbolic groups, lattices in reductive Lie groups, and related geometric classes, and no counterexample is known.

## Background

For a discrete group $G$ and a commutative ring $R$, the group ring $RG$ consists of finite formal $R$-linear combinations of group elements, with multiplication induced by the group law. A finitely generated projective $RG$-module is a direct summand of a finite free $RG$-module. The Hattori–Stallings rank of such a module is its trace, valued in the quotient of $RG$ by the subspace spanned by commutators $gh-hg$, which identifies with the free module on the conjugacy classes of $G$ (over a field of characteristic $0$). The classical Bass conjecture asserts that for a finitely generated projective $\mathbb{Z}G$-module the Hattori–Stallings rank is supported on the conjugacy class of the identity element; equivalently, the trace vanishes on all non-identity conjugacy classes. It is a finiteness statement about the integral group ring that controls, for example, which idempotents can exist in matrix algebras over $\mathbb{C}G$.

On the analytic side, $\ell^1(G)$ is the Banach algebra of absolutely summable complex-valued functions on $G$ with convolution product; it embeds continuously into the reduced group $C^*$-algebra $C^*_r(G)$, the completion of the complex group ring $\mathbb{C}G$ for its action by left convolution on the Hilbert space $\ell^2(G)$. Topological K-theory $K^{\mathrm{top}}_*(\ell^1(G))$ is defined as for Banach algebras generally: $K_0$ is the Grothendieck group of finitely generated projective $\ell^1(G)$-modules, and the higher groups are built from homotopy classes of invertible matrices over $\ell^1(G)$ and over its suspensions. The Baum–Connes conjecture with trivial coefficients predicts that an assembly map from the equivariant K-homology $K^G_*(\underline{E}G)$ — where $\underline{E}G$ denotes the classifying space for proper $G$-actions, a proper $G$-CW-complex universal among such spaces — to $K_*(C^*_r(G))$ is an isomorphism; the Bost conjecture is the variant of this statement with the Banach algebra $\ell^1(G)$ in place of the reduced $C^*$-algebra. The Baum–Connes assembly map factors through the Bost assembly map: it is the composite of $\mu^G$ with the map on K-theory induced by the inclusion $\ell^1(G)\hookrightarrow C^*_r(G)$ (Lafforgue; Gómez Aparicio–Julg–Valette). Consequently, the Bost conjecture implies the Baum–Connes conjecture exactly for those groups for which this inclusion induces an isomorphism in K-theory. That comparison holds whenever $\ell^1(G)$ is closed under holomorphic functional calculus in $C^*_r(G)$, by the classical density theorem for such dense subalgebras (Swan, Karoubi, Bost), but it is itself open in general: $\ell^1(G)$ fails to be stable under holomorphic calculus already when $G$ is a non-compact semisimple Lie group, and the Bost conjecture is known for lattices in reductive Lie groups, such as $\mathrm{SL}_3(\mathbb{Z})$, for which the Baum–Connes conjecture remains open.

The $\ell^1$-formulation is more flexible than the $C^*$-formulation for homological arguments. Berrick, Chatterji and Mislin (Math. Ann. 329, 2004) used this flexibility to prove that the Bost conjecture implies the classical Bass conjecture; combined with Lafforgue's bivariant K-theory for Banach algebras (Invent. Math. 149, 2002), which establishes the conjecture for a-T-menable groups — groups admitting a proper affine isometric action on Hilbert space, also called groups with the Haagerup property, a class that contains all amenable groups (groups admitting a left-invariant finitely additive probability measure on all subsets) — this yields the Bass conjecture for all amenable groups. Lafforgue's methods also cover word-hyperbolic groups (finitely generated groups whose Cayley graph is negatively curved in Gromov's sense) and lattices in reductive Lie groups; the Novikov conjecture for word-hyperbolic groups — the assertion that the higher signatures of closed oriented manifolds are invariant under orientation-preserving homotopy equivalences — had been proved earlier by Connes and Moscovici via cyclic cohomology (Topology 29, 1990), and Higson and Kasparov (Invent. Math. 144, 2001) proved the corresponding $C^*$-assembly isomorphism for a-T-menable groups. Later work has extended the known classes further — to open subgroups and groups acting on trees, and to proper Banach algebra coefficients (Paravicini, J. K-Theory 4, 2009 and J. Noncommut. Geom. 7, 2013) — but always within geometrically or structurally restricted families. The Bost conjecture for arbitrary countable discrete groups — the hard cases being groups fitting none of the known geometric patterns, for instance many groups with Kazhdan's property (T), a representation-theoretic rigidity property that rules out proper affine isometric actions on Hilbert space, that do not arise as lattices in Lie groups — remains open, and no counterexample is known (Gómez Aparicio–Julg–Valette, 2019).

## Problem Statement

Is the Bost assembly map
$$\mu^G\colon K^G_*(\underline{E}G)\longrightarrow K^{\mathrm{top}}_*(\ell^1(G))$$
an isomorphism for every countable discrete group $G$? Here $\underline{E}G$ is the classifying space for proper $G$-actions, $K^G_*(\underline{E}G)$ its equivariant K-homology, $\ell^1(G)$ the convolution Banach algebra of absolutely summable functions on $G$, and $K^{\mathrm{top}}_*(\ell^1(G))$ the topological K-theory of that Banach algebra. The task is to resolve this question: either prove that $\mu^G$ is an isomorphism for every countable discrete group $G$, or exhibit a countable discrete group $G$ for which $\mu^G$ fails to be an isomorphism. By the theorem of Berrick–Chatterji–Mislin, an affirmative answer would in particular settle the classical Bass conjecture (vanishing of the Hattori–Stallings rank off the identity conjugacy class) for all finitely generated projective $\mathbb{Z}G$-modules, for every group $G$.

The verification contract below evaluates answers to this statement. It does not narrow or redefine the research question.

Known solving difficulties:

- The known techniques — Lafforgue's bivariant Banach KK-theory (a bivariant K-theory for Banach algebras built for assembly-map arguments), Higson–Kasparov Dirac/dual-Dirac methods (Kasparov's technique for proving assembly maps invertible via an equivariant homotopy), and controlled algebra (comparing modules over group rings via metric control) — all require the group to act suitably on a non-positively curved or Hilbert-type space; extending them to arbitrary groups, e.g. groups with Kazhdan's property (T) lacking such actions, needs genuinely new machinery.
- The conjecture sits at the interface of analytic K-theory of Banach algebras, equivariant homotopy theory, and algebraic K-theory of group rings; no single existing framework computes both sides of the assembly map for a general group.
- A proof must handle all countable discrete groups uniformly, including groups with exotic geometry (e.g. Gromov monster groups, i.e. groups whose Cayley graphs contain large expander graphs) where even the Baum–Connes conjecture with coefficients is known to fail and standard descent arguments break down.
- A disproof requires computing, for one group, both $K^G_*(\underline{E}G)$ and $K^{\mathrm{top}}_*(\ell^1(G))$ exactly enough to detect a mismatch — itself a hard problem in analytic K-theory, since $\ell^1(G)$ is a singular, non-$C^*$, non-Noetherian algebra.
- Partial routes (e.g. proving only rational surjectivity in degree $0$, which already implies the $\ell^1$ Bass conjecture by Theorem 1.4 of the source paper) may be more accessible but do not settle the full isomorphism.

## Current Progress

The primary sources are arXiv:1004.1941 and DOI 10.1007/s00208-004-0521-6. The paper by Berrick, Chatterji and Mislin (Math. Ann. 329, 2004) proves that the Bost conjecture on the $\ell^1$-assembly map implies the classical Bass conjecture, and since Lafforgue's Banach K-theory establishes Bost for a-T-menable (Haagerup) groups — a class containing all amenable groups — all amenable groups satisfy the Bass conjecture (Theorems 1.2 and 1.3 of the paper). The paper also proves (Theorem 1.4) that rational surjectivity in degree $0$ of the Bost assembly map already implies the $\ell^1$ Bass conjecture for the group, so the general isomorphism (or even rational surjectivity) question is exactly the logical bottleneck the paper leaves open.

Known partial results: the Bost conjecture is established for a-T-menable/Haagerup groups (hence for all amenable groups) and for word-hyperbolic groups via Lafforgue's bivariant Banach K-theory (Invent. Math. 149, 2002, DOI 10.1007/s002220200213), and for semisimple Lie groups and their lattices — including $\mathrm{SL}_3(\mathbb{Z})$ — by the same circle of methods. The $C^*$-assembly counterpart for a-T-menable groups is the Higson–Kasparov theorem (Invent. Math. 144, 2001, DOI 10.1007/s002220000118), and the Novikov conjecture for word-hyperbolic groups was proved earlier by Connes–Moscovici via cyclic cohomology (Topology 29, 1990, DOI 10.1016/0040-9383(90)90003-3). Permanence properties and coefficient extensions of the Bost conjecture (open subgroups, groups acting on trees, proper Banach algebras) were studied by Paravicini (J. K-Theory 4, 2009, DOI 10.1017/is009010006jkt074; J. Noncommut. Geom. 7, 2013, DOI 10.4171/jncg/113). All known positive results remain within geometrically or structurally restricted families of groups.

Wang–Xie–Yu (Proc. Sympos. Pure Math. 105, 2023, DOI 10.1090/pspum/105/25) state the Bost conjecture as open in general and record that it is proved for all lattices in reductive Lie groups, while the Baum–Connes conjecture for $\mathrm{SL}_3(\mathbb{Z})$ is still open. The extended survey of Gómez Aparicio–Julg–Valette (2019, DOI 10.1007/978-3-030-29597-4_3) records that no counterexample to the Bost conjecture is known. No source located by any route claims a general proof or a counterexample; recent activity consists of class-by-class verifications and $\ell^1$-index-theoretic applications rather than any general resolution.

This affects only the fine detail of the newest verified classes of groups, not the open/closed verdict: a general resolution of the Bost conjecture would be a major, highly visible result, and no source located by any route claims one. The surviving open core is precisely the Bost conjecture for countable discrete groups outside the known geometric classes — and, as a corollary of the source paper's theorem, the classical Bass conjecture for such groups.

## Scientific Significance

Affected-field significance: `high`.

A resolution would directly change core knowledge in noncommutative geometry and algebraic K-theory. A proof of the Bost conjecture for all countable discrete groups would, by the audited theorem of Berrick–Chatterji–Mislin, settle the full classical Bass conjecture for group rings — one of the central open problems of algebraic K-theory — together with the $\ell^1$ Bass conjecture and, for torsion-free groups, the idempotent conjecture for $\ell^1(G)$ (the assertion that $\ell^1(G)$ contains no idempotents other than $0$ and $1$). Since the Baum–Connes assembly map with trivial coefficients factors through the Bost assembly map via the inclusion $\ell^1(G)\hookrightarrow C^*_r(G)$, such a proof would also settle the Baum–Connes conjecture for every group for which that inclusion induces an isomorphism in K-theory; the comparison is itself an open problem in general, so a proof of Bost would reduce Baum–Connes to this comparison rather than settle it outright. A counterexample would be equally transformative: no counterexample to the Bost conjecture is currently known, and one would provide the first obstruction to $\ell^1$-assembly methods and delimit the reach of Banach-algebraic index theory. The impact is direct rather than indirect.
