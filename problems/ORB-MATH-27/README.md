# ORB-MATH-27: Existence of global energy-admissible weak solutions of the incompressible Euler equations for arbitrary initial data in the energy space

For the incompressible Euler equations on the periodic box $Q=[0,2\pi]^d$, $d\ge 2$, global distributional weak solutions are known to exist for every divergence-free initial datum $v_0\in H(Q)$ (the energy space of solenoidal zero-mean $L^2$ vector fields), but the known constructions force the kinetic energy to jump upward at $t=0$. A weak solution is called admissible if its total kinetic energy $E(t)$ never exceeds the initial energy $E(0)$ for all $t\ge 0$. Admissible global weak solutions are currently constructed only for an $L^2$-dense subset of initial data, and convex integration cannot plainly be extended to all data, since for smooth data any admissible solution must coincide with the classical solution by weak-strong uniqueness. This audit, based on the source paper (Wiedemann, 2011), a full forward-citation sweep, and the recent literature through 2026, concludes that the problem remains open in every dimension: does every $v_0\in H(Q)$ admit a global admissible weak solution, and if not, which data do? Recent partial results (generic 2D well-posedness with energy balance, prescribed-energy-profile constructions, and topological smallness results for decaying solutions) sharpen the landscape on both sides without settling the core question.

## Background

The incompressible Euler equations $\partial_t v+\operatorname{div}(v\otimes v)+\nabla p=0$, $\operatorname{div}v=0$ model an ideal (inviscid, incompressible) fluid. On the periodic box $Q=[0,2\pi]^d$, $d\ge 2$, the natural finite-energy phase space is $H(Q)$: the space of $Q$-periodic, square-integrable, divergence-free vector fields with zero mean (the zero-mean condition is harmless because of Galilean invariance). A weak solution with initial datum $v_0\in H(Q)$ is a map $v\in L^\infty((0,\infty);H(Q))$ satisfying the equations in the sense of distributions against smooth periodic divergence-free test fields and attaining $v(\cdot,0)=v_0$. Its kinetic energy is $E(t)=\frac12\int_Q|v(x,t)|^2\,dx$, which for smooth solutions is conserved. For the viscous Navier–Stokes equations, global weak solutions satisfying the energy inequality $E(t)\le E(0)$ have been known since the 1930s, but the inviscid case offers no parabolic smoothing and hence no comparable compactness argument. Following the terminology of De Lellis and Székelyhidi (2010), a weak solution of Euler is called admissible if $E(t)\le E(0)$ for all $t\ge 0$; admissibility is the minimal physical energy constraint, and it implies weak-strong uniqueness: any admissible solution whose initial datum admits a (Lipschitz) classical solution must coincide with that classical solution for as long as the latter exists. Modern existence theory for non-smooth data rests on convex integration, a flexible-construction technique in which one first builds a smooth subsolution — a triple solving the linear parts of the equations whose associated generalized energy strictly undershoots a prescribed energy profile — and then corrects it to infinitely many exact weak solutions; the solutions produced this way are often called wild solutions. De Lellis and Székelyhidi (2010) showed that admissibility criteria (energy conservation or dissipation) do not restore uniqueness of such wild solutions, and Buckmaster, De Lellis, Székelyhidi, and Vicol (2019) proved Onsager's conjecture for admissible weak solutions on the three-dimensional torus (Onsager's conjecture asserts that Hölder-continuous weak solutions with exponent above $1/3$ conserve energy while below $1/3$ dissipative solutions exist). Wiedemann (2011) proved that every $v_0\in H(Q)$ admits infinitely many global weak solutions, but with a discontinuous energy increase at $t=0$, so no energy inequality holds; and Székelyhidi and Wiedemann (2012) proved, via generation of admissible measure-valued solutions (solution concepts relaxed to Young measures carrying oscillation and concentration effects) by exact weak solutions, that an $L^2$-dense set of initial data each admits infinitely many admissible weak solutions. Whether the dense set can be enlarged to all of $H(Q)$ is stated in both papers to be a delicate open issue: the initial-time energy jump is intrinsic to the subsolution construction for rough data, while for smooth data weak-strong uniqueness forces any admissible solution to be the classical one, so any all-data construction must be rigid exactly where data are smooth and flexible where they are rough.

## Problem Statement

Let $Q=[0,2\pi]^d$ with $d\ge2$, and let $H(Q)$ be the space of $Q$-periodic, square-integrable, divergence-free vector fields with zero mean. Consider the incompressible Euler equations $\partial_t v+\operatorname{div}(v\otimes v)+\nabla p=0$, $\operatorname{div}v=0$ on $Q\times[0,\infty)$, with weak solutions understood distributionally against smooth $Q$-periodic divergence-free test fields and kinetic energy $E(t)=\frac12\int_Q|v(x,t)|^2\,dx$. The problem, stated as open in the source paper and still open as of 2026, has one coherent objective — an admissible-existence theory over the whole energy space — with two facets: (i) does every initial datum $v_0\in H(Q)$ admit a global weak solution $v\in L^\infty((0,\infty);H(Q))$ with $v(\cdot,0)=v_0$ and $E(t)\le E(0)$ for all $t\ge0$ (an admissible solution)? (ii) if not, characterize the class of initial data in $H(Q)$ for which admissible global weak solutions exist. Any resolution must be consistent with weak-strong uniqueness (admissible solutions with data admitting a local classical solution coincide with that classical solution on its lifespan, so non-unique admissible solutions can occur only for highly irregular data) and must handle the initial time: the inequality is required from $t=0$ onward, which excludes the upward energy jumps at $t=0$ inherent in the known all-data weak-existence constructions. The question is open in every dimension $d\ge2$ on the periodic box, and analogously on smooth bounded domains with the corresponding energy space.

The verification contract below evaluates answers to this statement. It does not narrow or redefine the research question.

Known solving difficulties:

- Initial-time energy barrier: subsolution-based convex integration for arbitrary rough data forces the generalized energy of the subsolution at $t=0$ to exceed $\frac12|v_0|^2$ pointwise, producing an energy jump at the initial time; admissibility requires suppressing this jump without destroying the strict subsolution inequality that drives the construction.
- Rigidity–flexibility tension: weak-strong uniqueness forces any admissible solution with smooth data to be the classical one, so an all-data construction must degenerate to uniqueness on smooth data while remaining flexible on rough data, whereas convex-integration schemes naturally produce non-uniqueness uniformly.
- Absence of compactness: the energy bound alone provides no compactness for the quadratic nonlinearity (no parabolic smoothing, no a priori smoothness), so the compactness arguments that give energy-admissible weak solutions for Navier–Stokes do not transfer to Euler.
- Vanishing-viscosity selection is obstructed: viscosity limits of Leray-type solutions satisfy only an almost-everywhere-in-time energy inequality, may oscillate (shear-flow and boundary-layer phenomena), and no known selection principle yields the all-times inequality with strong convergence.
- A negative answer would require a nonexistence argument excluding every finite-energy weak solution for a concrete datum; available techniques (energy-conservation criteria from regularity, weak-strong uniqueness) reach only subclasses of solutions and regularity regimes.
- Dimension-dependent structure: in 2D, vorticity transport and Yudovich theory give well-posedness for integrable vorticity classes, so the frontier is measure-type or distributional vorticity where energy behavior is hardest to control; in $d\ge3$ even generic-data admissible existence is open, and smooth data beyond possible singularity formation reintroduce the question.

## Current Progress

Wiedemann (2011) establishes the partial results described below. The published paper proves that every $v_0\in H(Q)$, $Q=[0,2\pi]^d$, $d\ge2$, has infinitely many global weak solutions with bounded energy, and states verbatim that if the energy is required to be bounded at all times by the initial energy, existence 'is not known for arbitrary initial data (but only for an $L^2$-dense subset)'; it also records the obstruction that producing infinitely many weak solutions for smooth data would contradict local well-posedness and weak-strong uniqueness.

Székelyhidi–Wiedemann (2012, ARMA) proved Corollary 3: there is an $L^2$-dense set of solenoidal data for each of which there exist infinitely many admissible weak solutions, obtained by generating admissible measure-valued solutions (which exist for all $L^2$ data) with sequences of exact weak solutions. Their introduction states that improving this dense set 'seems to be a very delicate issue' and that any wild datum must be highly irregular for consistency with weak-strong uniqueness — this is exactly the surviving open core.

Subsequent convex-integration work enlarged flexibility but not admissible all-data existence: Daneri (2014) produced infinitely many Hölder data with dissipative Hölder solutions of preassigned energy; Buckmaster–De Lellis–Székelyhidi–Vicol (2019) resolved Onsager's conjecture for admissible weak solutions on $\mathbb{T}^3$ with prescribed smooth energy profiles (for data arising in the construction, not arbitrary prescribed data); Brenier (2018) reformulated the initial value problem as a concave maximization whose relaxed solutions are subsolution-type objects, again not admissible weak solutions for all data. The Buckmaster–Vicol (2020) survey of convex-integration constructions records no all-data admissible existence result.

Regularity increments toward $L^2$ stopped short of admissibility: Khor–Miao (2025) constructed infinitely many distributional $H^\beta$ solutions with time-continuous energy for any datum with a little Sobolev regularity, contrasting explicitly with Wiedemann's discontinuous-energy all-$L^2$ result; Khor–Miao–Ye (2022) reached arbitrary divergence-free $C^{\bar\beta}$ data, $\bar\beta<1/3$, with infinitely many non-conservative $C^\beta$ solutions, noting that their solutions 'must increase the energy' and that admissible solutions with smooth data are classical by weak-strong uniqueness. Admissible existence for non-smooth Hölder data — a fortiori for general $L^2$ data — remains untouched by these constructions.

Guerra–Koch–Lindberg (2023, Adv. Math.) proved via nonlinear open-mapping and Baire-category arguments on $\mathbb{R}^n$ that for a residual set of solenoidal $L^2$ data the Euler Cauchy problem admits no solution whose kinetic energy is $p$-integrable in time over $[0,\infty)$; in particular generic data admit no energy-decaying solution. This is a decay obstruction, not a decision of the bare admissibility inequality $E(t)\le E(0)$, since energy-conserving solutions are not time-$p$-integrable on the infinite horizon, and it does not contradict generic 2D well-posedness results.

In two dimensions the frontier is sharper: for vorticity in $L^p$ classes, Yudovich-type theory yields well-posedness, and Mengual (2024) showed non-uniqueness of admissible solutions with vorticity in $L^1\cap L^p$, $2<p<\infty$ (sharpness of weak-strong uniqueness and of Yudovich uniqueness for bounded vorticity), while Buck–Modena (2023) constructed energy-dissipating 2D solutions with vorticity in Hardy spaces $\mathcal{H}^p$, $2/3<p<1$. Galeati (2026) proved that a residual (Baire-generic) set of divergence-free $L^2$ data on $\mathbb{R}^2$ or $\mathbb{T}^2$ admits a unique global solution satisfying the energy balance, obtained as the vanishing-viscosity limit — hence admissible solutions exist generically in 2D. The exceptional meagre class (vortex-sheet-type data) and all of $d\ge3$ remain unresolved.

Most recent direct status statement: Schindler–Wiedemann (arXiv:2601.14813, January 2026), in a study of the inviscid Leray-$\alpha$ regularization on bounded domains, write verbatim, their (13) being the energy inequality: 'For the Euler equations ($\alpha=0$), the existence of admissible weak solutions (i.e. such that satisfy an energy inequality like (13)) for any initial data in $H$ is still open.'

Adjacent compressible analogue: Boutros–Markfelder (2026) proved that wild initial data (data with infinitely many global admissible solutions satisfying the local energy inequality) are dense in $L^r$ for the isentropic compressible Euler system, noting that the analogous incompressible statement with the local energy inequality remains open — evidence that the admissible-existence frontier is active but unsettled even at the density level.

No work was found that constructs, for all divergence-free $L^2$ periodic data in any dimension $d\ge2$, a global weak solution with $E(t)\le E(0)$ for all $t\ge0$, nor a datum for which none exists. The problem is open as stated.

## Scientific Significance

Affected-field significance: `high`.

Direct impact on the core of mathematical fluid dynamics and PDE analysis: this is the existence side of global well-posedness for the foundational model of ideal flow in the energy space. A positive answer would complete the analogue for Euler of the classical Navier–Stokes weak-existence theory under the physical energy constraint, changing what is known about the Euler initial value problem at the level of first principles and forcing a major extension of convex-integration or selection methods; a negative answer would exhibit finite-energy data incompatible with the energy principle at the weak-solution level, showing a fundamental obstruction no current method has formalized. Either outcome directly constrains admissibility-based selection principles (energy inequality, vanishing viscosity, anomalous dissipation) that underpin the mathematical theory of turbulence, with indirect consequences for turbulence physics.
