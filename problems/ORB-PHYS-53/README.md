# ORB-PHYS-53: Is the quantum capacity of a general quantum channel uncomputable?

The asymptotic quantum capacity $Q(\Phi)$ — the optimal rate at which qubits can be transmitted reliably through many uses of a noisy quantum channel $\Phi$ — is defined by a regularization (a limit over arbitrarily many channel uses) with no known single-letter formula. A 2026 preprint by Bhattacharyya, Mehta, and Zhao proves that approximating $Q(\Phi)$ is QMA-hard when the channel is specified by a quantum circuit, and that the one-shot zero-error classical capacity assisted by maximally entangled states is uncomputable (for classical-to-quantum channels with projective decoding); however, the computability of $Q(\Phi)$ itself for general channels remains unresolved. This problem asks for a resolution of that question: either a proof that computing or deciding $Q(\Phi)$ from a computable description of $\Phi$ is undecidable (e.g., RE-hard), or a proof of computability (an algorithm, possibly with a convergence guarantee, that determines $Q(\Phi)$ to any specified precision).

## Background

A quantum channel is the most general noise process in quantum mechanics, mathematically a completely positive trace-preserving (CPTP) linear map $\Phi$ on density matrices; a quantum circuit description specifies $\Phi$ succinctly by a gate list, while a Kraus-operator or formula description gives it explicitly. The quantum capacity $Q(\Phi)$ is the asymptotic number of qubits per channel use that can be sent with vanishing error. Unlike Shannon's classical capacity, which is given by a single maximization of a mutual information, $Q(\Phi)$ is only known to equal the regularized coherent information: $Q(\Phi)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\,\max_{\rho}\, I_c(\rho,\Phi^{\otimes n})$, where the coherent information $I_c$ is an entropy difference optimized over input states and the limit exists by superadditivity (Lloyd; Shor; Devetak 2005). Two structural phenomena make this regularization genuinely infinite: superactivation, where two channels each with zero quantum capacity combine to give positive capacity (Smith–Yard 2008), and the result of Cubitt, Elkouss, Matthews, Ozols, Pérez-García, and Strelchuk (2015) that for every $n$ there exist channels whose capacity is undetectable from fewer than $n$ uses, so no computable bound on the regularization length exists. These results are widely read as strong evidence that $Q(\Phi)$ may be uncomputable — that no Turing machine can compute it from any computable description of the channel — but no proof either way is known. (Uncomputability is already known for one asymptotic capacity notion: Elkouss and Pérez-García (2018) proved that the capacity of classical channels with memory cannot be computed even to within constant precision, but their construction relies essentially on memory, and the memoryless quantum case has remained open.) QMA is the quantum analogue of NP (quantum Merlin–Arthur verification), and RE is the class of recursively enumerable languages (those recognized by a Turing machine that halts on yes-instances); RE-hardness of a decision problem implies undecidability. The relevant undecidability machinery comes from nonlocal games — cooperative games in which separated players answering questions may share entanglement: Slofstra (2019) proved that deciding whether such a game admits a perfect quantum strategy is undecidable, and Ji, Natarajan, Vidick, Wright, and Yuen (MIP$^*$=RE, 2021) strengthened this to RE-completeness. Building on this, Bhattacharyya, Mehta, and Zhao (arXiv:2601.22471, 2026; v3 of March 2026 is the current version) initiated the study of the hardness of computing quantum channel capacities. They proved two results. First (their Theorem 1.1, formalized as Corollary 3.13): given a channel $\Phi_\mathcal{C}$ presented by a quantum circuit, it is QMA-hard to decide whether $Q(\Phi_\mathcal{C})\ge 3/4$ or $Q(\Phi_\mathcal{C})\le 1/4$, promised one holds. Second (their Theorem 4.1, restated as Theorem 1.3, with Corollary 4.2, restated as Theorem 1.2): for the one-shot zero-error classical capacity assisted by a maximally entangled state with projective decoding — denoted $C^{(1)}_{0,\mathrm{PME}}(\Phi)$ in the paper, and defined for classical-to-quantum channels (channels with a classical input alphabet and quantum outputs) — there is a computable mapping from graphs $G$ to channels $\Phi_G$ such that $C^{(1)}_{0,\mathrm{PME}}(\Phi_G)=\log(\alpha_q(G))$, where $\alpha_q(G)$ is the quantum independence number, the largest $t$ for which the independent-set nonlocal game on $G$ admits a perfect quantum strategy (Mančinska, Roberson, Varvitsiotis 2016); consequently, deciding $C^{(1)}_{0,\mathrm{PME}}(\Phi)\ge\log t$ is RE-hard, i.e., uncomputable. Zero-error capacity, introduced classically by Shannon (1956) and extended to quantum channels via noncommutative confusability graphs by Duan, Severini, and Winter (2013), measures perfect transmission; its assisted variant allows sender and receiver to share entanglement beforehand. Thus a one-shot, maximally-entanglement-assisted, zero-error capacity is provably uncomputable, while for the central capacity $Q(\Phi)$ only finite-complexity hardness (QMA) is known — a gap analogous to the historical progression for nonlocal games from hardness results toward MIP$^*$=RE. Notably, since uncomputability is encoding-independent in the zero-error case, the authors' open question also concerns whether hardness of $Q$ is robust across natural computable encodings (circuit versus explicit Kraus-operator descriptions). A related open question they highlight — already raised for classical channels by Mančinska and Roberson (2016) — is the computability of the zero-error classical capacity when arbitrary (not necessarily maximal) preshared entangled states are allowed; their reduction inherently uses maximal entanglement, and Stahlke (2016) showed that there exist quantum channels whose one-shot entanglement-assisted zero-error capacity is achievable only with non-maximally entangled assistance.

## Problem Statement

Determine the computability status of the asymptotic quantum capacity of general quantum channels. Precisely: given a quantum channel $\Phi$ specified by any fixed natural computable description (e.g., a quantum circuit implementing $\Phi$, or an explicit computable list of Kraus operators), determine whether there exists a Turing machine that, on input such a description and a precision $\varepsilon>0$, outputs a value within $\varepsilon$ of $Q(\Phi)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\max_{\rho} I_c(\rho,\Phi^{\otimes n})$ — and settle the question by proving one of the two branches: (a) an undecidability theorem, e.g., that a natural decision problem for $Q$ (such as deciding $Q(\Phi)\ge a$ versus $Q(\Phi)\le b$ for constants $a>b\ge 0$, under a stated encoding of channels) is RE-hard or otherwise undecidable, with the encoding-dependence of the statement analyzed (which encodings the hardness holds for, mirroring the encoding-independence known for $C^{(1)}_{0,\mathrm{PME}}$); or (b) a computability theorem, giving an algorithm together with a proven termination/accuracy guarantee valid for all computably described channels. This is the principal open problem left by Bhattacharyya, Mehta, and Zhao (arXiv:2601.22471), whose QMA-hardness result for circuit-specified channels is the strongest known lower bound (their paper shows the gapped decision problem lies in RE and conjectures it is RE-complete); a complete answer either strengthens that hardness to uncomputability or overturns the prevailing expectation. A directly related open problem from the same work, which a solver may additionally address as part of the same objective of mapping the computability boundary of quantum channel capacities, is the computability of the zero-error classical capacity assisted by arbitrary (not necessarily maximally) entangled preshared states.

The verification contract below evaluates answers to this statement. It does not narrow or redefine the research question.

Known solving difficulties:

- The capacity is an asymptotic regularization with no known single-letter formula; both superactivation (Smith–Yard 2008) and unbounded detection depth (Cubitt et al. 2015) block any reduction of $Q(\Phi)$ to a finite-blocklength computation, so standard approximation algorithms cannot be proven convergent by existing techniques.
- An undecidability proof appears to require embedding a halting-problem-type object (e.g., via MIP*=RE, operator-algebraic constructions à la Slofstra, or spectral-gap-style gadgets) into the regularized coherent information, whose variational structure over $n$-fold tensor products is poorly matched to finite-dimensional reductions; the source paper's authors themselves describe the objective as 'extremely challenging'.
- The answer may depend on the channel encoding (succinct circuit descriptions versus explicit Kraus-operator lists), so a complete solution must control or delineate this dependence rather than prove a statement for one encoding only.
- The prove-or-refute structure is asymmetric: a computability proof must rule out the adversarial channel families constructed in the superadditivity literature, while an undecidability proof must handle the fact that $Q$ is a limit of computable quantities (each finite-$n$ coherent-information maximum is semidefinite-representable), so uncomputability can only emerge from the regularization — the subtlest possible source.
- Prior partial results (QMA-hardness; RE-hardness of the one-shot assisted zero-error capacity) required new technical machinery (symmetric direct sums of channels with circuit constructions; synchronous-game/quantum-independence-number correspondences), suggesting substantial further invention is needed for the asymptotic, unassisted setting.

## Current Progress

The source work (Bhattacharyya, Mehta, Zhao, arXiv:2601.22471, submitted 30 January 2026; current version v3, 30 March 2026, which corrects an error in Section 4 of v2) establishes: (i) QMA-hardness of deciding whether the quantum capacity of a circuit-specified channel is $\ge 3/4$ or $\le 1/4$ (Theorem 1.1, formalized as Corollary 3.13), and (ii) uncomputability (RE-hardness) of deciding whether the one-shot maximally-entanglement-assisted zero-error classical capacity $C^{(1)}_{0,\mathrm{PME}}(\Phi)$ of a classical-to-quantum channel exceeds $\log t$ (Theorem 4.1, restated as Theorem 1.3, and Corollary 4.2, restated as Theorem 1.2, via the identity $C^{(1)}_{0,\mathrm{PME}}(\Phi_G)=\log(\alpha_q(G))$ and MIP$^*$=RE). The paper also proves that the gapped decision problem 'is $Q(\Phi)\ge 3/4$ or $Q(\Phi)\le 1/4$' (GapQcap) lies in RE and conjectures it is RE-complete (Conjecture 1.5). Its Section 1.4 'Discussion and outlook' explicitly states the long-term objective 'to understand whether the quantum capacity is uncomputable for general channels' and that the present results 'fall short' of it.

Prior literature provides strong evidence toward non-computability of $Q(\Phi)$ but no theorem for memoryless channels. Cubitt, Elkouss, Matthews, Ozols, Pérez-García, and Strelchuk (Nature Communications 2015; DOI: 10.1038/ncomms7739) proved that an unbounded number of channel uses may be required even to detect whether the quantum capacity is nonzero, so the regularized coherent-information formula admits no computable convergence bound; Smith and Yard (Science 2008; DOI: 10.1126/science.1162242) proved superactivation of quantum capacity, ruling out finite-blocklength reductions. Beigi and Shor (arXiv:0709.2090, 2007) had earlier proved QMA-completeness of the quantum clique problem (existence of $k$ mutually nonconfusable inputs), a zero-error distinguishability question. The nearest prior uncomputability result for an asymptotic capacity concerns classical channels with memory — Elkouss and Pérez-García (Nature Communications 2018; DOI: 10.1038/s41467-018-03428-0) showed that the capacity of information-stable finite-state-machine channels cannot be computed to within constant precision — but no undecidability result for the capacity of memoryless quantum channels existed before 2026.

The uncomputability machinery now available reaches only one-shot, zero-error, maximally-entanglement-assisted quantities: Slofstra (2019; DOI: 10.1017/fmp.2018.3) proved the set of quantum correlations is not closed and that deciding the existence of a perfect quantum strategy is undecidable; Ji, Natarajan, Vidick, Wright, and Yuen (MIP$^*$=RE, 2021; DOI: 10.1145/3485628) proved deciding the quantum value of nonlocal games is RE-complete; Mančinska, Roberson, and Varvitsiotis (CJTCS 2016; DOI: 10.4086/cjtcs.2016.005) showed independent-set games are complete for deciding perfect strategies with projective measurements on maximally entangled states. The 2026 source paper is the first to transfer this to a channel capacity, and its authors note the reduction does not extend to arbitrary (non-maximal) preshared entanglement, where Stahlke (IEEE TIT 2016; DOI: 10.1109/TIT.2015.2496377) exhibited channels whose one-shot entanglement-assisted zero-error capacity is achievable only with non-maximally entangled assistance — leaving the computability of the generally-assisted zero-error classical capacity as a second named open problem (a question already raised for classical channels by Mančinska and Roberson, 'Quantum homomorphisms', J. Combin. Theory Ser. B 118:228–267, 2016).

No resolution of the general-channel question was identified at the August 2026 curation cutoff. The source, the Cubitt et al. (2015) unbounded-uses result, and the Elkouss–Pérez-García (2018) memory-channel result address distinct settings.

Adjacent-scope check: the Physics Reports review of undecidability in physics by Perales-Eceiza, Cubitt, Gu, Pérez-García, and Wolf (2025; DOI: 10.1016/j.physrep.2025.06.004) surveys undecidable problems (spectral gap, quantum value of games, etc.) and likewise reports no computability result for quantum capacity. Secondary open questions recorded by the source (robustness of hardness across channel encodings — circuit versus Kraus-operator descriptions; existence of any efficiently computable single-letter formula for $Q$; fine-grained bounds on Shannon capacity of graphs beyond 5-cycles à la Lovász) also remain untouched in the sources discussed here.

## Scientific Significance

Affected-field significance: `high`.

Solving this problem would directly settle a foundational question of quantum Shannon theory: whether the quantum capacity — the field's central quantity, quantifying the fundamental limit of quantum noiseless communication — is computable at all. A proof of uncomputability would establish a permanent, structural separation between classical channel capacity (computable by Shannon's single-letter formula) and quantum capacity, showing that no algorithm, however inefficient, can determine the transmission capabilities of an arbitrary quantum channel; it would also delineate exactly which assisted or zero-error capacity notions remain computable (the maximally-entanglement-assisted one-shot zero-error capacity being already known uncomputable). A computability proof would be equally transformative, since it would have to overcome the known no-computable-convergence phenomena (unbounded detection depth, superactivation) and would likely yield a fundamentally new regularization structure for coherent information. Either way the result would directly change what the field knows and can compute about channel capacities, and would calibrate the reach of undecidability techniques (MIP*=RE, noncommutative graph theory) into asymptotic information theory.
