# ORB-PHYS-74: Rigorous justification of the replica coordinate Bethe ansatz for the log-gamma polymer: Bethe-string norms, completeness, and analytic continuation in replica number

The replica coordinate Bethe ansatz is a physics technique for computing exact distribution functions of directed polymer models in the KPZ universality class: the integer moments of the partition function are mapped to an n-boson quantum problem solved by the coordinate Bethe ansatz, the result is analytically continued to complex replica number, and a formal saddle-point analysis of the sum over Bethe 'string' eigenstates yields a Fredholm determinant for the generating function of the free energy. For the log-gamma polymer this program produced the fixed-endpoint solution of Thiery and Le Doussal and, for the point-to-half-line geometry, the GUE-to-GOE free-energy crossover derived by Grange — but every step rests on unproven ingredients: the conjectured norm of Bethe string states (a Gaudin–Korepin-type formula in a non-standard weighted scalar product), the completeness of the string eigenstates in the thermodynamic limit, a Mellin–Barnes analytic continuation in the replica number necessitated by the divergence of moments of order n greater than gamma, and uncontrolled string-expansion and saddle-point asymptotics. The problem is to prove, or refute and correct, these ingredients and to show rigorously that the Fredholm determinant indeed captures the full large-time generating function. The same observables are already known rigorously by unrelated Whittaker-function methods, which validate the conjectured answers but not the Bethe-ansatz derivation itself.

## Background

A directed polymer on the square lattice is a nearest-neighbour up-right path that accumulates a product of independent random Boltzmann weights attached to the visited sites; its partition function $Z$ (the sum of the path weights) is a random variable whose logarithm, the free energy, is the central observable. In one space dimension such models lie in the Kardar–Parisi–Zhang (KPZ) universality class of random growth: at large time $t$ the free energy fluctuates on the scale $t^{1/3}$, with a universal limiting one-point distribution given by a Tracy–Widom law — the GUE flavor (fluctuations of the largest eigenvalue of large random Hermitian matrices) for point-to-point ('droplet') geometries, and the GOE flavor (real symmetric matrices) for point-to-line ('flat') geometries.

The log-gamma polymer, introduced by Seppäläinen, is the canonical exactly solvable lattice polymer: the weights are inverse-gamma distributed, $P_\gamma(\omega)d\omega=\Gamma(\gamma)^{-1}\omega^{-1-\gamma}e^{-1/\omega}d\omega$ with parameter $\gamma>0$, and its fluctuation exponents and Tracy–Widom limits are rigorously known.

The replica Bethe ansatz is a physics technique for computing the full probability law of $Z$. The $n$-th integer moment $\overline{Z^n}$ (the overbar denotes averaging over the random weights) is interpreted as the wave function of an $n$-boson quantum problem: for the log-gamma polymer, the transfer matrix acting on the moments is a lattice analogue of the Lieb–Liniger Hamiltonian (the one-dimensional Bose gas with delta-function interactions) with effective coupling $\bar c=4/(\gamma-1)$. The coordinate Bethe ansatz diagonalizes such operators: eigenfunctions are superpositions of plane waves whose quasi-momenta ('rapidities') satisfy algebraic Bethe equations. This problem is of attractive type, and the contributing eigenstates organize into strings — complexes of $m$ rapidities lying regularly spaced along a segment of the complex plane, interpreted as bound-state clusters. Two structural inputs are then required: the norm of each Bethe state with respect to the relevant (here non-standard, weight-dependent) scalar product, in order to decompose the initial condition and perform the sum over eigenstates; and the completeness of the string eigenstates. For the Lieb–Liniger model with real rapidities the norm is given by the Gaudin–Korepin determinant formula (Korepin 1982), and for string states of the attractive Bose gas by the heuristic formula of Calabrese and Caux (2007). Thiery and Le Doussal (2014) conjectured the generalization of these formulas to the log-gamma polymer replica transfer matrix — a norm formula for arbitrary Bethe states (their Eq. (38)) and its thermodynamic-limit form for string states (their Eq. (52), reproduced as Eq. (27) in the source paper of this candidate) — assuming the string system to be complete.

A second obstruction is specific to the log-gamma polymer: because the inverse-gamma weight has a fat tail, the integer moments $\overline{Z^n}$ diverge for $n>\gamma$, so the law of $Z$ cannot be reconstructed from its integer moments. The replica Bethe ansatz instead analytically continues the moment formula to complex replica number $m$ through a Mellin–Barnes integral on a contour $C=-a+i\mathbb R$ with $a>0$ — a prescription whose validity is assumed, not proven. Finally, the sum over string configurations is resummed into a Fredholm determinant (the determinant of the identity plus an explicit integral operator, the standard representation of such limit laws), and a saddle-point analysis at large time extracts the scaling limits. For the point-to-half-line geometry, where the partition function $Z_w(x,t)=\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}_-}e^{wy}\sum_{\phi:(y,0)\to(x,t)}\prod_{(x',t')\in\phi}\omega_{x',t'}$ sums the weights of all up-right paths of $t$ steps ending at $(x,t)$ over starting points $y$ on the negative half-line, each weighted by $e^{wy}$ with slope $w$, this program yields a crossover between the GUE and GOE Tracy–Widom laws in the rescaled slope $\tilde w$ (Grange 2017), matching the droplet-to-flat crossover of the KPZ equation derived by the same techniques (Le Doussal 2014).

The same observables have been obtained rigorously by entirely different methods based on the geometric Robinson–Schensted–Knuth correspondence (a birational lifting of a classical combinatorial bijection) and Whittaker functions (special functions of representation-theoretic origin): Borodin–Corwin–Remenik (2013) proved the Fredholm determinant identity for the point-to-point log-gamma free energy at all times; Bisi–Zygouras (2019) computed Laplace transforms for point-to-line and point-to-half-line geometries; Barraquand–Corwin–Dimitrov (2021) proved GUE Tracy–Widom fluctuations for general parameters and slopes. These rigorous results validate the conjectured answers, but they justify no step of the replica Bethe ansatz derivation itself: the string norms, the completeness of the string eigenstates, the analytic continuation in replica number, and the string-expansion/saddle-point asymptotics all remain unproven.

## Problem Statement

The problem is to give a rigorous justification of the replica coordinate Bethe ansatz derivation of the generating function of the log-gamma polymer free energy — as performed for the fixed-endpoint geometry by Thiery and Le Doussal (2014) and extended to the point-to-half-line geometry with boundary slope $w$ by Grange (2017) — by proving, or refuting and correcting, each of its conjectural ingredients. These four items are the load-bearing steps of one and the same derivation; the overall objective is a single complete rigorous derivation of the large-time generating function by the replica coordinate Bethe ansatz route, items 1-3 being prerequisites of item 4.

1. (String norms.) Prove, or disprove and correct, the conjectured norm of the Bethe string states of the log-gamma polymer replica transfer matrix in its weighted scalar product. In the thermodynamic limit of a periodic system of $L$ sites, for a system of $n_s$ strings with lengths $m_j$ and real centers $k_j$, the string rapidities are $t_{j,a}=ik_j/2+\bar c(m_j+1-2a)/4$, $a=1,\dots,m_j$, with $\bar c=4/(\gamma-1)$, and the conjectured norm (Eq. (27) of the source; Eq. (52) of Thiery-Le Doussal 2014) is

$$
\|\mu\|^{2}=\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n_s}m_j\Big)!\,L^{n_s}\prod_{1\le i<j\le n_s}\frac{4(k_i-k_j)^2+\bar c^{2}(m_i+m_j)^2}{4(k_i-k_j)^2+\bar c^{2}(m_i-m_j)^2}\prod_{j=1}^{n_s}\frac{m_j}{\bar c^{m_j-1}}\Big(\sum_{a=1}^{m_j}\frac{1}{1-t_{j,a}^2}\Big)\prod_{b=1}^{m_j}(1-t_{j,b}^2)
$$

together with its finite-size version (Eq. (38) of Thiery-Le Doussal 2014). Any proof must recover the previously checked special cases (single-string norms) and reduce to the Gaudin-Korepin determinant in the continuum Lieb-Liniger limit.

2. (Completeness.) Prove that the Bethe string eigenstates used in the expansion form a complete basis of the relevant Hilbert space in the thermodynamic limit $L\to\infty$, so that the sum over eigenstates performed in the derivation is legitimate.

3. (Analytic continuation.) Justify the Mellin-Barnes prescription in the replica number: starting from the exact Bethe-ansatz formulas for the integer moments $\overline{Z_w(x,t)^n}$, which exist only for $n<\gamma$, prove that the continuation to complex replica order $m$ on the contour $C=-a+i\mathbb R$ and the resulting contour integral indeed reconstruct the true Laplace transform of the partition function.

4. (Asymptotics.) With the above ingredients established, rigorously control the resummation over all string configurations and the saddle-point approximation, and prove that the resulting Fredholm determinant captures the full large-time generating function of the free energy — including the GUE-to-GOE crossover in the rescaled slope — and establish its relation (equivalence of the large-contour and small-contour formulas) to the Fredholm determinants obtained from rigorous Whittaker-function methods.

The candidate formulation also suggests, as a secondary direction, extending the method to further discrete directed polymer models (e.g., the beta and strict-weak lattice polymers) and to finite-time corrections as further tests of universality; this is a natural continuation of the same program rather than an additional requirement for completing the problem.

The verification contract below evaluates answers to this statement. It does not narrow or redefine the research question.

Known solving difficulties:

- Proving norm identities for Bethe string states of the polymer replica transfer matrix in its non-standard, weight-dependent scalar product: the Gaudin-Korepin/Slavnov determinant technology applies to the usual scalar product and real rapidities and must be generalized to complex-rapidity string states of a lattice transfer matrix whose Bethe roots are only known transcendently.
- Establishing spectral completeness of the string eigenstates in the thermodynamic limit, where the standard completeness arguments for the coordinate Bethe ansatz (real rapidities on a finite interval) do not apply; the suggested route is a Plancherel-type isomorphism analogous to the q-Boson spectral theory of Borodin-Corwin-Petrov-Sasamoto, adapted to the log-gamma (Whittaker-type) setting.
- Justifying the Mellin-Barnes analytic continuation in replica number: the moment problem is ill-posed (moments of order $n>\gamma$ diverge), so one must prove that the continued Bethe-ansatz moment formulas reconstruct the true Laplace transform — presumably by identifying them with the rigorous Whittaker-based transform or by direct contour analysis.
- Controlling the resummation over infinitely many string configurations and the saddle-point asymptotics uniformly in the crossover parameters, to prove the Fredholm determinant formula and its GUE and GOE scaling limits with error bounds.
- Relating the large-contour Fredholm determinants produced by the Bethe ansatz to the small-contour rigorous ones — a contour-collapse argument with infinitely many residues, which may require a q-deformed regularization of the log-gamma polymer.
- The components are interdependent: if a conjecture (e.g., the norm formula) turns out to be false in part, the formalism must first be corrected before the remaining steps can be completed.

## Current Progress

The conjectural ingredients are stated in P. Grange, 'Crossover in the log-gamma polymer from the replica coordinate Bethe Ansatz' (arXiv:1707.03521, 2017; no journal version found): the analytic-continuation prescription to complex replica number (integer moments diverge for $n>\gamma$ because of the fat tail of the inverse-gamma weight), the norm of Bethe string states conjectured in the fixed-endpoint solution (Eq. (27) there), the assumed completeness of the Bethe eigenstates, and the formal character of the string expansion and saddle-point approximation. Attribution was verified in the primary sources: Eq. (27) of Grange is exactly the thermodynamic-limit string norm conjectured by Thiery-Le Doussal (J. Stat. Mech. 2014), whose general finite-size conjecture is Eq. (38). Extensions to beta and strict-weak lattice polymers and finite-time corrections are related directions rather than questions posed explicitly in the source.

The norm conjecture originates in T. Thiery and P. Le Doussal, 'Log-gamma directed polymer with fixed endpoints via the replica Bethe Ansatz' (J. Stat. Mech. 2014 P10018, arXiv:1406.5963), who state that they will 'proceed assuming this formula to be correct' (Eq. (38); thermodynamic string form Eq. (52), presented as a generalization of the Calabrese-Caux formula for the attractive Bose gas, itself heuristic). They note explicitly that a rigorous proof of the norm result and of the completeness of the eigenstates 'should be possible' using Plancherel-type isomorphism techniques of the kind developed for the q-Boson particle system by Borodin-Corwin-Petrov-Sasamoto (arXiv:1308.3475), and that a full proof of the equivalence of their large-contour Fredholm determinant with the rigorous small-contour one may require a q-deformed regularization of the log-gamma model. None of this was carried out.

Thiery-Le Doussal, 'On integrable directed polymer models on the square lattice' (J. Phys. A 48, 465001, 2015, arXiv:1506.05006) extended the same transfer-matrix Bethe ansatz toolbox and norm conjecture to the inverse-beta polymer, again obtaining the Laplace transform of the partition function only as a conjecture via the Mellin-Barnes prescription; the ingredients remained open.

Thiery-Le Doussal, 'Exact solution for a random walk in a time-dependent 1D random environment: the point-to-point Beta polymer' (J. Phys. A 50, 045001, arXiv:1605.07538) applied the replica Bethe ansatz to the beta polymer. That case turned out to be 'repulsive': the Bethe roots are real, no string states or string norms are needed, and the moment formula was shown to be equivalent to nested-contour integral formulas of the type used in rigorous work (Barraquand-Corwin, arXiv:1503.04117). This partial rigorization shows precisely that the unresolved difficulty is specific to string-forming (attractive-type) models such as the log-gamma polymer.

Rigorous counterparts exist by unrelated methods: Borodin-Corwin-Remenik (Comm. Math. Phys. 324, 2013, arXiv:1206.4573) proved the Fredholm determinant identity for the point-to-point log-gamma free energy at all times; Bisi-Zygouras (Trans. Amer. Math. Soc. 371, 2019, arXiv:1703.07337) computed Laplace transforms for point-to-line and point-to-half-line geometries — the latter being the geometry of the source paper — via the geometric RSK correspondence and orthogonal Whittaker functions; Barraquand-Corwin-Dimitrov (Probab. Theory Rel. Fields 181, 2021, arXiv:2012.12316) proved GUE Tracy-Widom fluctuations for general parameters and slopes. These works prove the conjectured answers (or their close analogues) but contain no proof of the string norms, of the completeness of the string eigenstates, or of the analytic-continuation prescription.

The physics literature still treats all these ingredients as conjectures: De Nardis-Krajenbrink-Le Doussal-Thiery, 'Delta-Bose gas on a half-line and the KPZ equation' (J. Stat. Mech. 2020, arXiv:1911.06133) states 'we have no proof that this set of states is complete … This will be our working hypothesis' and again conjectures Gaudin-type norms for string states; Krajenbrink-Le Doussal (SciPost Phys. 8, 035, 2020, arXiv:1905.05718) and Barraquand-Le Doussal (arXiv:2504.18292, 2025) continue to solve KPZ-type problems with the replica Bethe ansatz as a formal tool.

Grange's companion paper, 'Log-gamma directed polymer with one free end via coordinate Bethe Ansatz' (J. Stat. Mech. 2017; arXiv:1701.08606), relies on the same norm conjecture. No rigorous treatment of the missing ingredients was identified in the literature considered at curation. The source is an arXiv preprint rather than a peer-reviewed publication.

Related extensions. The beta polymer has already been solved by the replica Bethe ansatz (Thiery-Le Doussal, J. Phys. A 50, 045001, arXiv:1605.07538, in the string-free repulsive case, with equivalence to rigorous nested-contour formulas); rigorous results for the strict-weak lattice polymer exist by other methods (Corwin-Seppäläinen-Shen, J. Stat. Phys. 160, 2015, arXiv:1409.1794), though no replica-Bethe-ansatz treatment of it was found; all-time (finite-time) generating functions for the log-gamma polymer are rigorous (Borodin-Corwin-Remenik, arXiv:1206.4573; Bisi-Zygouras, arXiv:1703.07337). The surviving open core — rigorous justification of the Bethe-string formalism (norms, completeness, analytic continuation, asymptotic control) — is therefore retained in its original generality, with no restriction added.

## Scientific Significance

Affected-field significance: `medium`.

The problem sits in mathematical physics / integrable probability, at the interface of the Bethe ansatz and KPZ-universality models. Solving it would have direct methodological impact: it would convert the replica coordinate Bethe ansatz — used for two decades of formal physics work on the KPZ equation, directed polymers, and half-space or boundary problems — into a provable derivation route, and would rigorously validate or invalidate the analytic-continuation prescription used throughout that literature. It would also establish new theorems of independent interest in integrable systems: norm identities and completeness for Bethe string states in a non-standard weighted scalar product, going beyond the Gaudin-Korepin and Calabrese-Caux heuristics. The impact on core knowledge of the log-gamma polymer itself is indirect, since the target one-point distributions are already rigorously known through Whittaker-function methods; what changes directly is the reliability of the Bethe-ansatz route and its availability for observables where it is currently the only tool.
