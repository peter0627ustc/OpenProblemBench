# ORB-PHYS-57: Exact critical manifold of the five-probability inhomogeneous bow-tie lattice: full-domain proof and rigorous foundation of the negative-probability balancing construction

Ziff, Scullard, Wierman and Sedlock (2012) derived the algebraic critical manifold $B(p,r,s,t,u)=0$ for bond percolation on the bow-tie lattice with five distinct bond probabilities, by decomposing the lattice into right- and left-pointing triangular cells, tuning each cell to its self-duality condition Prob(all)=Prob(none), and recombining the two central bonds. The derivation is rigorous only when both triangles can be tuned with genuine probabilities ($p+r\le 1$ and $s+t\le 1$); the extension to the remaining admissible domain — where exactly one triangle is supercritical and an intermediate 'bond probability' is negative, reinterpreted heuristically as a sink balancing supercriticality against subcriticality — is conjectural. Because the bow-tie lattice admits no isoradial embedding, the star-triangle transport method that rigorously settled the checkerboard special case (Wu's 1979 percolation formula) does not apply. The problem is to prove or refute the full-domain validity of $B=0$, or to give the negative-parameter balancing a rigorous probabilistic or combinatorial foundation together with a criterion for which non-identical triangular arrangements admit the per-hyperedge duality argument; a certified counterexample at any admissible parameter point settles the conjecture in the negative. A systematic sweep of the citing and adjacent literature (2012–2026) finds no proof, no refutation, and no competing foundation; the problem is open.

## Background

In bond percolation on an infinite connected graph, each edge (bond) is independently declared open with probability $p$ and closed otherwise; the percolation threshold $p_c$ separates a regime of only finite open clusters from one with an infinite cluster. In the inhomogeneous version, edges fall into classes with distinct probabilities $(p_1,\dots,p_n)$, and one asks for the critical manifold $f(p_1,\dots,p_n)=0$: fixing $n-1$ of the probabilities, the manifold gives the critical value of the remaining one. Only a small family of two-dimensional lattices admits exact critical manifolds, all obtained through planar duality. For a planar lattice $L$, the dual lattice $L^*$ has one vertex per face of $L$ and one dual edge crossing each edge of $L$; the classical Sykes–Essam matching argument relates the thresholds of dual pairs, and self-duality ($L\cong L^*$) can pin the threshold exactly, as for the square lattice where $p_c=1/2$.

The sharpest duality tool is the star-triangle (here: triangle-triangle) transformation: a triangular cell of bonds with edge probabilities $(a,b,c)$ can be replaced by its dual configuration with complemented probabilities, and the connectivity among the cell's three boundary vertices is preserved exactly when Prob(all)=Prob(none) — for a simple triangle, Prob(all)=$ab+ac+bc-2abc$ (all three boundary vertices mutually connected) and Prob(none)=$(1-a)(1-b)(1-c)$ (no two connected), so the condition reduces to

$$
abc-a-b-c+1=0.
$$

Scullard and Ziff (2008) conjectured general critical surfaces, including those for the checkerboard and bow-tie lattices, from duality and multilinearity consistency arguments, and the hypergraph formulation developed by Scullard, Ziff and collaborators expresses this as: if a lattice can be written as a self-dual 3-uniform hypergraph — a periodic arrangement of three-boundary-vertex cells ('hyperedges', each an arbitrary network of bonds with three distinguished boundary vertices) whose dual arrangement is isomorphic to itself — then imposing the per-cell condition above yields the exact critical manifold. Wierman and Ziff proved rigorous theorems of this type for self-dual planar hypergraphs, and Bollobás and Riordan proved the analogous result for self-dual polygon configurations. A second rigorous route is isoradiality: Grimmett and Manolescu proved that on graphs embedded so that the vertices of every face lie on a circle of common radius (an isoradial embedding), with bond probabilities tied to the angles subtended by the bonds through a local formula, the system is critical.

The bow-tie lattice is built from pairs of triangles joined through a shared central vertex; Wierman found its homogeneous bond threshold in 1984 via the star-triangle transform. Ziff, Scullard, Wierman and Sedlock (2012) treated the fully inhomogeneous version with five bond probabilities. Splitting the central connection into two parallel bonds, the right-pointing triangle carries probabilities $(p,r,u_1)$ and the left-pointing one $(s,t,u_2)$. Setting each triangle to its duality condition gives $u_1=(1-p-r)/(1-pr)$ and $u_2=(1-s-t)/(1-st)$; recombining the two parallel bonds, $u=u_1+u_2-u_1u_2$, yields the manifold

$$
B(p,r,s,t,u)=1-u-pr-ps-rs-pt-rt-st+prs+prt+pst+rst+pru+stu-prstu=0,
$$

which at $u=0$ reduces to the checkerboard polynomial $C(p,r,s,t)=1-pr-ps-rs-pt-rt-st+prs+prt+rst+pst$ of Wu's 1979 conjecture for the square lattice whose four edge probabilities repeat in a checkerboard pattern around each square.

Whenever $p+r\le 1$ and $s+t\le 1$, both $u_1$ and $u_2$ are genuine probabilities in $[0,1]$, each triangle is individually critical, and the self-dual-hypergraph theorem makes the derivation rigorous. The gap opens when, say, $p+r>1$: then $u_1<0$ — not a probability at all, but a parameter in $(-\infty,0]$ measuring that triangle's supercriticality. The authors reinterpret a negative $u_1$ as a 'sink' attached to the other central bond and conjecture that criticality is achieved by balancing supercriticality on one side against subcriticality on the other, extending $B=0$ to all regimes in which not both $p+r>1$ and $s+t>1$ (if both exceed 1 the system is supercritical for every $u$). Precisely this negative-parameter regime is needed for the checkerboard limit beyond the trivial line $p+r=s+t=1$, and there the conjecture is independently confirmed: the source paper shows the checkerboard lattice isoradially embedded, so that Wu's percolation formula follows from Grimmett and Manolescu's theorem (the $q$-state Potts extension, by contrast, remains unproven — Enting showed its conjectured $S_4$ symmetry fails for the three-state checkerboard Potts model). But the bow-tie lattice itself, in the authors' assessment, admits no isoradial embedding, so no analogous proof exists for $B=0$ at $u>0$; the same conjecture underlies the exact thresholds claimed for various asymmetric bow-tie lattices and, in Potts form, for the five-coupling frontiers of Scullard and Jacobsen — none of which are proven.

## Problem Statement

Settle, with full mathematical rigor, the criticality conjecture produced by the negative-probability balancing construction of Ziff–Scullard–Wierman–Sedlock for the five-probability inhomogeneous bow-tie lattice (the split-bond bow-tie lattice: right-pointing triangles with edge probabilities $(p,r,u_1)$, left-pointing triangles with $(s,t,u_2)$, and the recombined central bond $u=u_1+u_2-u_1u_2$; equivalently, the bow-tie lattice whose five bond-probability classes are $p,r,s,t,u$):

(1) Full-domain manifold. Prove, or disprove, that for all $p,r,s,t,u\in[0,1]$ with not both $p+r>1$ and $s+t>1$, bond percolation on this lattice is critical exactly on

$$
B(p,r,s,t,u)=1-u-pr-ps-rs-pt-rt-st+prs+prt+pst+rst+pru+stu-prstu=0.
$$

The established theory covers only the region $p+r\le 1$ and $s+t\le 1$; the open regime is the one in which exactly one triangle is supercritical and the derivation passes through a negative intermediate parameter $u_1$ or $u_2$, and it also carries the exact thresholds claimed for the asymmetric bow-tie lattices generated by the same mechanism.

(2) Foundation of the balancing mechanism. Provide the negative-parameter ('sink') balancing step with a rigorous probabilistic or combinatorial interpretation — a proof that the algebraic balancing of a supercritical and a subcritical triangle preserves or locates criticality even though the intermediate quantity lies in $(-\infty,0]$ and is not a probability — such that the construction provably generates exact critical manifolds for the wider family of inhomogeneous lattices to which it has been applied.

(3) Admissible arrangements. As the method-level criterion underpinning (1)–(2), characterize which periodic arrangements of non-identical 3-hyperedge generators with prescribed probability assignments admit the per-hyperedge Prob(all)=Prob(none) argument (arrangements whose triangle-triangle transformation reproduces the true dual process), and which do not — the checkerboard arrangement being admissible, the 'scrambled' arrangements of the source paper's Figure 2 not, and the kagome-lattice arrangement (the lattice of corner-sharing triangles) out of reach.

These components serve one objective: placing the exact criticality of the inhomogeneous bow-tie family — the canonical non-isoradial test case of the balancing method — on rigorous footing. Any route is admissible (for example a new geometric embedding, a coupling or stochastic-domination argument, or a rigorous real-space renormalization). A complete proof of (1) by an argument that bypasses the mechanism resolves the central conjecture, in which case (2) and (3) are required only insofar as the answer claims generality of the method; a certified refutation of (1) at any admissible parameter point resolves the conjecture in the negative.

The verification contract below evaluates answers to this statement. It does not narrow or redefine the research question.

Known solving difficulties:

- The balancing step leaves the space of product probability measures: the intermediate parameter takes values in $(-\infty,0]$, so no direct coupling, stochastic-ordering, or FKG-type argument applies to the transformed system.
- The bow-tie lattice admits no isoradial embedding, blocking the star-triangle transport technique that proved the checkerboard special case via Grimmett and Manolescu's theorem.
- With non-identical hyperedge generators, the triangle-triangle transformation generally scrambles the dual bond probabilities, so global self-duality theorems for uniform arrangements do not apply; a proof must track the probability assignment, not only the graph.
- Establishing criticality (not merely a self-dual surface) requires showing sub- and supercritical behavior strictly off the manifold — sharpness-type or crossing estimates tied to the construction.
- The classification component requires connecting the combinatorics and symmetries of periodic hyperedge arrangements with admissible probability assignments, a mixed combinatorial–probabilistic question with no existing template.
- A refutation would need certified bounds measurably far from the manifold at a specific admissible point; current rigorous bounding techniques (substitution and stochastic-ordering methods) have limited precision.

## Current Progress

Ziff, Scullard, Wierman, and Sedlock (J. Phys. A 45, 494005 (2012); arXiv:1210.6609v2) introduce the criticality and sink parameters. The rigorous region is $p+r\le1$ and $s+t\le1$, established through the self-dual hyperlattice theorem of Bollobás–Riordan. The full-domain extension of $B=0$ is conjectural, and the impossibility of an isoradial embedding of the bow-tie lattice is stated informally. The checkerboard special case ($u=0$, Wu's percolation formula) is not open — the source paper itself proves it via the isoradial theorem of Grimmett and Manolescu — so the surviving open core is the genuinely non-isoradial bow-tie regime ($u>0$ with exactly one triangle supercritical), together with the foundation of the balancing mechanism and the arrangement criterion.

Direct follow-up in the same circle: Scullard and Jacobsen (J. Phys. A 46, 125006 (2013), DOI 10.1088/1751-8113/46/12/125006; arXiv:1210.7845) extended the triangle-decomposition construction to the five-coupling $q$-state Potts model on inhomogeneous and asymmetric bow-tie lattices, reproducing the percolation manifold at $q=1$ as a special case; they state explicitly that the derivation 'is not completely rigorous, even from a physicist's perspective', because it rests on the same negative-parameter step (mapped in the Potts setting to antiferromagnetic couplings). The conjectural status is unchanged, and the Potts frontiers inherit it.

Alternative non-rigorous derivations: Ohzeki (Phys. Rev. E 87, 012137 (2013), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevE.87.012137; arXiv:1211.1521) rederived the inhomogeneous square-lattice (checkerboard) thresholds using duality combined with real-space renormalization imported from spin-glass theory, presenting it as more straightforward than the source paper's route; it does not treat the bow-tie lattice and does not rigorize the negative-probability step.

Rigorous work adjacent to, but not covering, the core: the isoradial program of Grimmett and Manolescu (Ann. Probab. 41 (4), 2990–3025 (2013), DOI 10.1214/11-AOP729; Probab. Theory Relat. Fields 159, 273–327 (2014), DOI 10.1007/s00440-013-0507-y) proves criticality for canonical inhomogeneous percolation on isoradial embeddings — which is what rigorously settles the checkerboard case — but the bow-tie lattice lies outside this class. Damavandi and Ziff (J. Phys. A 48, 405004 (2015), DOI 10.1088/1751-8113/48/40/405004, with a 2016 erratum, DOI 10.1088/1751-8113/49/18/189501) found explicit probability assignments solving the Bollobás–Riordan self-duality conditions for hypergraphs with four-vertex hyperedges, yielding new rigorous exact thresholds (and, per work they cite, rigorous bounds for a four-boundary-vertex generator); this extends the rigorous solvable class by a different route and leaves the negative-parameter balancing mechanism untouched.

Review status: the survey of Araújo, Grassberger, Kahng, Schrenk and Ziff (Eur. Phys. J. Spec. Top. 223, 2307 (2014), DOI 10.1140/epjst/e2014-02266-y) records that exact percolation thresholds are known for self-dual 3-uniform hypergraph lattices and for isoradial graphs, treats the source paper as the proof of Wu's 1979 checkerboard conjecture, and lists exact thresholds for lattices outside these two classes (e.g., kagome bond, honeycomb site percolation) as an open challenge; the bow-tie full-domain conjecture and the negative-probability foundation fall within that still-open challenge.

Numerical program: the critical-polynomial transfer-matrix method (Jacobsen and Scullard–Jacobsen–Ziff, culminating in J. Phys. A 54, 055006 (2021), DOI 10.1088/1751-8121/abcddb, on the kagome hypergraph) pins thresholds of kagome-family lattices to 17–18 digits and maps a whole kagome-hypergraph critical surface, but produces no closed forms and no proofs; the bow-tie and checkerboard manifolds remain exact-by-construction only through the duality arguments above. Within the source paper itself, the checkerboard point $p=73/90$, $s=t=r$ (where the intermediate parameter $u_1=-5/16$ is negative) agrees with simulation to $r_c=0.40000004(10)$ against the predicted $0.4$, and the bow-tie point $u=r=s=p$, $t=1/2$ agrees at $p_c=0.3819654(5)$ against $0.38196601\dots$ (the latter in the rigorous regime).

Alternative frameworks: Coupette and Schilling (Phys. Rev. E 105, 044108 (2022), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevE.105.044108) propose a branching-process ('effectively treelike') criterion that reproduces several known exact thresholds, including bond percolation on lattices with triangular hypergraphs; it is not rigorous for planar lattices and does not address the negative-parameter regime, the bow-tie manifold, or the arrangement criterion.

No post-2012 work was found that proves or refutes the full-domain bow-tie manifold, that gives the sink-parameter balancing a rigorous foundation, or that classifies admissible non-identical hyperedge arrangements; no counterexample to $B=0$ is known.

## Scientific Significance

Affected-field significance: `medium`.

Direct impact on the exact-solvability niche of two-dimensional percolation and statistical mechanics: currently, exact critical manifolds can be proved only for self-dual hypergraph lattices whose (identical) generators are individually critical, and for isoradially embeddable lattices — and the inhomogeneous bow-tie family lies outside both classes. A proof would certify a new mechanism (negative-parameter duality balancing) for generating exact thresholds, settle the exact thresholds claimed for the asymmetric bow-tie lattices, and provide a template for rigorizing the Potts critical frontiers built on the same construction; a refutation would prune a family of claimed exact results and refocus the exact-threshold program. The impact is direct but confined to percolation duality and exact solvability rather than the field's core questions (critical exponents, conformal invariance), hence medium.
