# ORB-MATH-14: Unconditional uniform boundedness of preperiodic points for unicritical polynomials $z^d+c$ over number fields

Nicole R. Looper (Inventiones mathematicae 225 (2021) 1–44) proved that for every degree $d\ge2$ and number field $K$ there is a constant $B(d,K)$ bounding the number of $K$-rational preperiodic points of every unicritical polynomial $f(z)=z^d+c$ with $c\in K$ — but the proof assumes the classical $abc$-conjecture for $d\ge5$ and the stronger, still-open $abcd$-conjecture for $2\le d\le4$. This problem asks for an unconditional proof of that uniform bound over number fields (equivalently, removal of these Diophantine hypotheses, whose needed cases are a $(1+\epsilon)$ radical-versus-height inequality for tuples on the hyperplane $Z_1+\cdots+Z_n=0$), or a refutation exhibiting a fixed $(d,K)$ with unboundedly many preperiodic points as $c$ varies. Even the flagship case $(d,K)=(2,\mathbb{Q})$ is open: as of 2026 even the weaker periodic-points form of the Morton–Silverman uniform boundedness conjecture is, in Rajagopal–Zhang's words, 'not known unconditionally for any tuple $(N,d,D)$, even when $(N,d,D)=(1,2,1)$'.

## Background

Arithmetic dynamics studies the behavior of points under repeated application of a rational map. A point is called preperiodic if its forward orbit is eventually periodic, i.e. $f^{m+n}(P)=f^m(P)$ for some $m\ge0$, $n\ge1$; for maps over number fields these are exactly the points of canonical height zero, the dynamical analogue of torsion points on abelian varieties. The Uniform Boundedness Conjecture of Morton and Silverman (1994) predicts that for all integers $D,N\ge1$ and $d\ge2$ there is a constant $B(D,N,d)$ such that every morphism $\varphi:\mathbb{P}^N\to\mathbb{P}^N$ of degree $d$ defined over a number field $K$ of degree $D$ has at most $B(D,N,d)$ preperiodic points in $\mathbb{P}^N(K)$. It is a vast dynamical generalization of the torsion-boundedness theorems of Mazur and Merel for elliptic curves, and it remains open in essentially every instance. Its simplest nontrivial test family consists of the unicritical polynomials — polynomials with a single finite critical point — which, up to conjugacy, are exactly the maps $f_{d,c}(z)=z^d+c$ with $c\in K$. Here the conjecture reduces to: for each $d\ge2$ and number field $K$ there is $B(d,K)$ with $\#\mathrm{PrePer}(z^d+c,K)\le B(d,K)$ for all $c\in K$. Poonen (1998) showed for $K=\mathbb{Q}$, $d=2$ that if $f_{2,c}$ has no rational periodic point of period 4 or higher, then it has at most 9 rational preperiodic points, and gave a refined conjectural classification; since periods 4 and 5 were excluded unconditionally by Morton and by Flynn–Poonen–Schaefer, respectively, the hypothesis is equivalent to excluding periods at least 6. Benedetto (2007) proved general bounds of order $O(s\log s)$ — concretely $\#\mathrm{PrePer}(f,K)\le C(d)\,s\log s$ — where $s$ is the number of places of bad reduction of $f$ (non-archimedean primes at which $f$ does not reduce to a polynomial map of the same degree; for $z^d+c$, the primes dividing a denominator of $c$). The central obstacle is that $s$ is unbounded as $c$ varies, so all such bounds fail to uniformize over the family. Looper's theorem (Theorem 1.2 of the cited paper) overcomes this obstacle conditionally: for $K$ a number field (or a one-variable function field of characteristic zero) and $f(z)=z^d+c$ non-isotrivial (isotriviality — being conjugate over $\overline{K}$ to a map defined over the constant subfield — being a condition that can arise only over function fields), such a $B(d,K)$ exists, assuming the $abc$-conjecture for $K$ when $d\ge5$ and the $abcd$-conjecture when $2\le d\le4$. The $abc$-conjecture over a number field asserts, roughly, that for coprime nonzero $a,b,c\in K$ with $a+b=c$, the logarithmic height of the triple is at most $(1+\epsilon)$ times its radical (the height contribution of the primes dividing $abc$) plus a constant. The $abcd$-conjecture (Conjecture 2.1 of the cited paper; the $n=3$ case is $abc$) asserts: for $n\ge3$, $\epsilon>0$, there is a proper Zariski-closed subset $\mathcal{Z}$ of the hyperplane $\mathcal{H}:Z_1+\cdots+Z_n=0$ in $\mathbb{P}^{n-1}$ and a constant $C$ such that every point $P=(z_1,\dots,z_n)\in\mathcal{H}(K)\setminus\mathcal{Z}$ with all $z_i\ne0$ satisfies $h(P)<(1+\epsilon)\,\mathrm{rad}(P)+C$, where $h$ is the standard logarithmic Weil height and $\mathrm{rad}(P)$ its radical part. It is a special case of Vojta's conjecture with truncated counting function (a higher-dimensional generalization of $abc$ for divisors with normal crossings), and it is open over number fields; the remark following Conjecture 2.1 notes that for the purposes of proving Theorem 1.2 the coefficient $1+\epsilon$ 'cannot simply be replaced by a larger constant', so weaker known radical–height bounds do not substitute for the dynamical application. The proof strategy produces special algebraic configurations (hexagons for $d=2$, quadrilaterals for $d=3,4$, triangles for $d\ge5$) whose vertices are $K$-rational preperiodic points and whose coordinate differences would violate the assumed inequality; local ($p$-adic) equidistribution of preperiodic points on the $v$-adic Julia sets (the nonarchimedean counterpart of the classical Julia set of complex dynamics) is transposed into global adelic control. For function fields, Looper's own introduction notes the theorem is superseded by the unconditional work of Doyle–Poonen, so the unresolved content of Theorem 1.2 is over number fields: whether the uniform bound exists with no unproved Diophantine hypothesis at all.

## Problem Statement

Prove, unconditionally — that is, without assuming the $abc$-conjecture, the $abcd$-conjecture (Conjecture 2.1 of the cited paper), Vojta's conjecture, or any other unproved Diophantine hypothesis — that for every number field $K$ and every integer $d\ge2$ there exists a constant $B(d,K)$ such that for every $c\in K$ the unicritical polynomial $f(z)=z^d+c$ has at most $B(d,K)$ preperiodic points in $K$ (a preperiodic point is a point of $K$ whose forward orbit under iteration of $f$ is eventually periodic). Equivalently: remove from Looper's Theorem 1.2 the $abc$-assumption used for $d\ge5$ and the $abcd$-assumption used for $2\le d\le4$ over number fields, whether by proving the required number-field cases of the $abcd$-conjecture (a $(1+\epsilon)$ radical-versus-height inequality for tuples on $Z_1+\cdots+Z_n=0$ with only a proper Zariski-closed exceptional subset) or by an entirely different argument. The flagship open special case is $(d,K)=(2,\mathbb{Q})$: prove there exists $B$ with $\#\mathrm{PrePer}(z^2+c,\mathbb{Q})\le B$ for all $c\in\mathbb{Q}$. A refutation is equally admissible as an answer: exhibit a fixed degree $d\ge2$ and number field $K$ for which $\#\mathrm{PrePer}(z^d+c,K)$ is unbounded as $c$ ranges over $K$.

The verification contract below evaluates answers to this statement. It does not narrow or redefine the research question.

Known solving difficulties:

- The known route requires the number-field cases of the $abcd$-conjecture — a special case of Vojta's conjecture with truncated counting function, itself a major open problem in Diophantine geometry that lies strictly between classical $abc$ and full Vojta; bypassing it demands a fundamentally new mechanism for the height-versus-radical control of tuples of preperiodic-point differences.
- The $(1+\epsilon)$ coefficient in the radical–height inequality is essential and provably cannot be weakened to any larger constant, so all currently known unconditional bounds toward 4-term $abc$ (subexponential or with weaker constants) are structurally insufficient.
- The principal obstacle identified in the source: there is no uniform bound on the number of places of bad reduction of $z^d+c$ as $c$ varies, so all prior unconditional bounds that decay with the number of bad-reduction places (e.g. Benedetto's bound $\#\mathrm{PrePer}(f,K)\le C(d)\,s\log s$) cannot uniformize; any new argument must control the adelic geometry across unboundedly many bad places.
- The geometric ingredient — forcing special algebraic configurations (hexagons, quadrilaterals, triangles) of $K$-rational preperiodic points whose side-length differences contradict the height–radical inequality, using local equidistribution of preperiodic points on $v$-adic Julia sets transposed into global adelic control — is deep and tightly adapted to the conditional input, and would need to be rebuilt or replaced.
- A refutation, if attempted, requires constructing unicritical maps with genuinely unboundedly many rational preperiodic points over a fixed number field, contradicting the Morton–Silverman philosophy and all accumulated evidence (Poonen's classification program and cycle-exclusion results such as Flynn–Poonen–Schaefer's proof that rational 5-cycles never occur for quadratic polynomials over $\mathbb{Q}$), so both branches of the problem demand large new ideas rather than incremental refinements.

## Current Progress

Looper (Inventiones mathematicae 225 (2021), 1–44, DOI 10.1007/s00222-020-01029-7; arXiv:1901.04385) proves a uniform bound $\#\mathrm{PrePer}(f,K)\le B(d,K)$ for $f(z)=z^d+c$, assuming the $abc$-conjecture for $K$ when $d\ge5$ over number fields and the $abcd$-conjecture when $2\le d\le4$ (Theorem 1.2 and Conjecture 2.1). Looper's introduction states the function-field case of Theorem 1.2 is superseded by Doyle–Poonen's unconditional theorem, so the surviving conditional open core concerns number fields.

The $abcd$-conjecture remains open over number fields. Robin Zhang's 2024 survey (Publications mathématiques de Besançon 2024, pp. 119–134, DOI 10.5802/pmb.58; arXiv:2206.09725) situates it strictly between the classical $abc$-conjecture and Vojta's conjecture with truncated counting function, presents it as unproved, and derives further conditional dynamical consequences from it. The best unconditional progress toward it is Pasten–Sepúlveda-Manzo (Bulletin of the Brazilian Mathematical Society 56 (2025), no. 4, DOI 10.1007/s00574-025-00476-1; arXiv:2406.05083), who prove unconditional subexponential bounds toward the 4-term $abc$ conjecture under a hypothesis on the size of the variables; these cannot substitute for the dynamical application because Looper's remark following Conjecture 2.1 notes that the $1+\epsilon$ coefficient 'cannot simply be replaced by a larger constant'.

Later dynamical work retains the hypotheses rather than removing them. Doyle–Hindes (arXiv:2408.14657, 2024) classify the portraits of $K$-rational preperiodic points of $z^d+c$ for all sufficiently large $d$, assuming $abc$ over number fields (their function-field cases being unconditional); Panraksa (International Journal of Number Theory 18 (2022), DOI 10.1142/S1793042122500580; arXiv:2105.03715) proves, assuming $abc$, that $f_{d,c}$ has no rational periodic point of exact period greater than 1 for sufficiently large $d$ and $c\ne-1$; Looper's companion Trans. AMS 373 (2020) paper on dynamical Zsigmondy sets is likewise $abc$-conditional.

Unconditional uniform boundedness is known in neighboring settings but not for the full unicritical family over a number field. Doyle–Poonen (Compositio Mathematica 156 (2020), DOI 10.1112/S0010437X20007022) prove it unconditionally for non-isotrivial $z^d+c$ over one-variable function fields and, over number fields, reduce preperiodic uniform boundedness to periodic uniform boundedness; Doyle–Faber (Mathematische Annalen 388 (2024), DOI 10.1007/s00208-022-02536-z) extend the function-field principle to new families in positive characteristic; Pasten (Proceedings of the AMS 154 (2026), DOI 10.1090/proc/17498; arXiv:2408.13262) constructs explicit unconditional non-isotrivial one-parameter families $z^d+\psi(c)$ with the uniform boundedness property, but his introduction explicitly states that the standard unicritical family $\{z^2+c:c\in\mathbb{Q}\}$ remains open and 'very difficult'; Rajagopal–Zhang (Monatshefte für Mathematik 2026, DOI 10.1007/s00605-026-02196-0; arXiv:2510.26119) prove the unconditional bound $\#\mathrm{Per}_K(\varphi)\le d^{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}$ for polynomials with inseparable good reduction at a prime dividing $d$, which covers only the subclass of $z^d+c$ with $c$ integral at some prime dividing $d$ (periodic points; equivalent to the preperiodic statement for unicritical maps by the Doyle–Poonen reduction). Rajagopal–Zhang state in their introduction that the uniform boundedness conjecture (their Conjecture 2, the periodic-points form, which is implied by the preperiodic form) 'is not known unconditionally for any tuple $(N,d,D)$, even when $(N,d,D)=(1,2,1)$' — a direct, recent confirmation that the core problem is open.

Removing the $abc$/$abcd$ hypotheses for the full unicritical family over number fields remains open in the literature considered at curation. Indexing of very recent 2026 preprints may lag on any single service, but the cross-service check surfaced nothing indicating closure. Looper's secondary open directions — making the constants $B(d,K)$ effective, and generalizing the polygon constructions to other polynomial families — also remain open in the cited literature, but they are separable follow-ups rather than components of the problem stated below.

## Scientific Significance

Affected-field significance: `high`.

An unconditional proof would deliver the first full unconditional instance of the Morton–Silverman Uniform Boundedness Conjecture for the standard unicritical family of maps over number fields — a statement whose weaker periodic-points form is currently 'not known unconditionally for any tuple $(N,d,D)$, even $(1,2,1)$' (Rajagopal–Zhang, 2026) — directly changing the core knowledge of arithmetic dynamics after thirty years. Because the only known route passes through the $abcd$-conjecture, a proof would also require or entail unconditional progress on a special case of Vojta's conjecture with truncated counting function, directly advancing Diophantine geometry; conversely, a refutation would overturn the standard expectation that abc-type input is sufficient and reshape the field's strategy for uniform boundedness. The impact on core methods is direct, not incremental.
