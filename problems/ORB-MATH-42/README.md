# ORB-MATH-42: Tutte's 3-Flow Conjecture

Tutte's 3-flow conjecture (posed in 1972) asserts that every 4-edge-connected finite graph admits a nowhere-zero 3-flow, an orientation of the edges together with flow values in $\{\pm1,\pm2\}$ that conserve flow at every vertex; equivalently, every 4-edge-connected graph has a modulo-3 orientation, an orientation in which each vertex has indegree congruent to outdegree modulo 3. This is the surviving open core of the closing open-problems section of Thomassen's 2012 paper 'The weak 3-flow conjecture and the weak circular flow conjecture', which proved the weak version at 8-edge-connectivity. Of the other open problems listed there, the Goddyn–Seymour $(2+\epsilon)$-flow conjecture was proved by Thomassen (2014), the Bárat–Thomassen tree-decomposition conjecture was proved by Bensmail, Harutyunyan, Le, Merker, and Thomassé (2017), and Jaeger's circular flow conjecture was refuted for odd moduli $k\ge 7$ by Han, Li, Wu, and Zhang (2018), leaving the 3-flow conjecture (the $k=3$ case) and the adjacent $k=5$ case open. The best known general result is that 6-edge-connectivity suffices (Lovász, Thomassen, Wu, Zhang, 2013); the conjecture asserts that 4 suffices, and it remains open.

## Background

An integer flow on a graph is built in two steps: first orient every edge, then assign to each edge a nonzero integer so that, at every vertex, the total value on edges directed into the vertex equals the total value on edges directed out. If all values can be chosen from $\{\pm1,\dots,\pm(k-1)\}$, the result is a nowhere-zero $k$-flow. Flow conservation forces the value 0 on any bridge (an edge whose deletion disconnects the graph), so only bridgeless graphs admit nowhere-zero flows. The edge-connectivity $\lambda(G)$ is the minimum number of edges whose deletion disconnects $G$, and it is the natural connectivity parameter for flow problems. In this language Tutte's celebrated 5-flow conjecture says every bridgeless graph has a nowhere-zero 5-flow, and his 3-flow conjecture (1972) says that $\lambda(G)\ge 4$ already guarantees a nowhere-zero 3-flow. A standard reformulation: a graph has a nowhere-zero 3-flow if and only if it has a modulo-3 orientation, an orientation in which indegree is congruent to outdegree modulo 3 at every vertex (each edge of value $\pm1$ or $\pm2$ reduces mod 3 to a directed edge counted with sign). By planar duality, the planar case of the 3-flow conjecture is equivalent to Grötzsch's classical theorem that every triangle-free planar graph is 3-colorable. Jaeger's 1984 circular flow conjecture generalized the conjecture: for every odd $k$, every $(2k-2)$-edge-connected graph should admit a modulo-$k$ orientation; writing $k=2p+1$ this says every $4p$-edge-connected graph has a modulo-$(2p+1)$-orientation, equivalently a nowhere-zero circular $(2+1/p)$-flow, and the case $p=1$ is exactly Tutte's 3-flow conjecture. Thomassen (2012) proved the weak versions: every 8-edge-connected multigraph has a modulo-3 orientation (with arbitrary prescribed outdegrees mod 3), and every $(2k^2+k)$-edge-connected multigraph has prescribed outdegrees modulo $k$; he also proved from this that highly edge-connected graphs decompose into stars. Lovász, Thomassen, Wu, and Zhang (2013) improved the thresholds to 6-edge-connected for nowhere-zero 3-flows and $(3k-3)$-edge-connected for modulo-$k$ orientations, introducing entropy-based orientation arguments; no better general bound has been found since. A stronger conjecture of Jaeger, Linial, Payan, and Tarsi (1992) predicts that every 5-edge-connected graph is $\mathbb{Z}_3$-connected, meaning every admissible prescription of the outdegree-minus-indegree residue mod 3 can be realized; this implies the 3-flow conjecture, and its best known case is again 6-edge-connected. Kochol's reductions (recorded in Hasanvand 2016 and in Li–Li–Liu 2025) show the conjecture suffices to verify for 5-edge-connected graphs and, equivalently, for 5-regular 4-edge-connected graphs (orientations with every indegree equal to 1 or 4). The full-strength conjecture at 4-edge-connectivity is the target: closing the gap between the known threshold 6 and the conjectured threshold 4.

## Problem Statement

Does every finite 4-edge-connected graph (multiple edges allowed, no loops) admit a nowhere-zero 3-flow? Equivalently, does every finite graph of edge-connectivity at least 4 admit a modulo-3 orientation, that is, an orientation in which every vertex has indegree congruent to outdegree modulo 3? This is Tutte's 3-flow conjecture in its standard full-strength form, the $k=3$ case of Jaeger's circular flow conjecture and the unweakened form of the 'weak 3-flow conjecture' proved at 8-edge-connectivity in the source paper. The conjecture makes no restriction to planar, bounded-genus, bounded-independence, regular, or random graphs: a positive answer must establish the 3-flow for all 4-edge-connected graphs at once (known reductions permit restricting attention to 5-edge-connected, or to 5-regular 4-edge-connected, graphs); a negative answer consists of a single explicit 4-edge-connected graph with no nowhere-zero 3-flow. The gap to close is between the conjectured threshold 4 and the best established threshold 6.

The verification contract below evaluates answers to this statement. It does not narrow or redefine the research question.

Known solving difficulties:

- All general methods that currently prove existence of modulo-3 orientations (Mader-type edge lifting with induction on prescribed outdegree residues, entropy/potential-function arguments of Lovász–Thomassen–Wu–Zhang, and contractible configurations) certify the conclusion only from 6-edge-connectivity upward; pushing down to 4 requires arguments that survive in sparse 4-edge-connected graphs, where lifting operations destroy the structure a minimal-counterexample induction needs.
- Deciding whether a given graph has a nowhere-zero 3-flow is computationally hard in general, so no simple complete invariant is expected; a proof likely needs a developed structural theory of contraction-critical minimal counterexamples, analogous to the snark theory built for Tutte's 4- and 5-flow conjectures, which took decades.
- Kochol's reductions confine the problem to 5-edge-connected graphs and to 5-regular 4-edge-connected graphs (orientations with all indegrees 1 or 4) but provide no bound on the order of a minimal counterexample, so exhaustive or computer search for a refutation is unbounded and has found nothing.
- Topological dual arguments settle the planar case via Grötzsch's theorem and have been pushed to the projective plane and the torus, but no known surface-type or coloring-type technique reaches arbitrary 4-edge-connected graphs.
- The stronger conjecture that every 5-edge-connected graph is $\mathbb{Z}_3$-connected (which would imply the 3-flow conjecture) is itself open beyond 6-edge-connectivity, indicating that current group-connectivity machinery is insufficient for the harder connectivity level.
- If the conjecture is false, a refutation requires an explicit 4-edge-connected graph with no modulo-3 orientation; the counterexample machinery of Han–Li–Wu–Zhang works only for moduli $2p+1\ge 7$ and does not extend to modulus 3, and no 4-edge-connected obstruction is known.

## Current Progress

Thomassen's 2012 paper lists these questions in its concluding open-problems section. The five bundled items are all genuinely present in the source: Tutte's 3-flow conjecture at 4-edge-connectivity; Jaeger's circular flow conjecture; the Goddyn–Seymour $(2+\epsilon)$-flow conjecture; the Bárat–Thomassen tree-decomposition conjecture; and the question of the optimal constant for star decompositions. The Goddyn–Seymour conjecture, as formulated in Thomassen's 2014 follow-up, asks for a connectivity threshold $f(\epsilon)$ such that every sufficiently edge-connected graph admits a flow with all values in $[1,1+\epsilon]$. The ratio bounds the largest to smallest flow value, with exact conservation, rather than the outdegree-to-indegree ratio.

The source paper itself proved the weak versions: every 8-edge-connected multigraph admits a modulo-3 orientation with arbitrary prescribed outdegree residues, more generally every $(2k^2+k)$-edge-connected multigraph admits prescribed outdegrees modulo $k$, and consequently every $(2k^2+k)$-edge-connected simple graph with size divisible by $k$ decomposes into $k$-stars. These resolved the weak 3-flow conjecture of Jaeger (1988) and the star case of the Bárat–Thomassen conjecture as they stood in 2012.

Lovász, Thomassen, Wu, and Zhang (2013) improved the general thresholds: every 6-edge-connected graph has a nowhere-zero 3-flow (indeed is $\mathbb{Z}_3$-connected), and every $(3k-3)$-edge-connected graph has a modulo-$k$ orientation, equivalently every $6p$-edge-connected graph has a nowhere-zero circular $(2+1/p)$-flow. The unrestricted sufficient bound remains 6-edge-connectivity, leaving the conjectured threshold 4 for 3-flows bracketed by $4\le\lambda\le6$.

Thomassen (2014) resolved the Goddyn–Seymour $(2+\epsilon)$-flow conjecture, one of the source's open items: every $6k$-edge-connected graph has a flow whose flow values are exactly $1$ and $1+1/k$ (as real numbers), so $f(\epsilon)=6\lceil 1/\epsilon\rceil$ suffices. The same paper also proved that every $(3k-1)$-edge-connected graph admits a flow with values in any prescribed set of group elements satisfying an odd-sum condition, and it records the modulo-$k$ threshold as $2k-2\le f(\{1\},\mathbb{Z}_k)\le 3k-3$.

Han, Li, Wu, and Zhang (2018) refuted Jaeger's circular flow conjecture for large moduli, another of the source's open items: they constructed infinite families of $4p$-edge-connected graphs with no modulo-$(2p+1)$-orientation for every $p\ge 3$, and even $(4p+1)$-edge-connected counterexamples for $p\ge 5$. In the source's parameterization the conjecture fails for every odd $k\ge 7$ and remains open only for $k=3$ and $k=5$; the $k=3$ case is precisely Tutte's 3-flow conjecture considered here, while the $k=5$ case (every 8-edge-connected graph has a modulo-5 orientation, which would imply Tutte's 5-flow conjecture) is a distinct surviving open problem. Han et al. propose a repaired conjecture that some $\epsilon(p)<1/2$ makes every $\lceil(4+\epsilon)p\rceil$-edge-connected graph modulo-$(2p+1)$-orientable; the exact minimal threshold as a function of $p$ is open.

Bensmail, Harutyunyan, Le, Merker, and Thomassé (2017) proved the full Bárat–Thomassen tree-decomposition conjecture: for every tree $T$ on $m$ edges there exists $k_T$ such that every $k_T$-edge-connected graph with number of edges divisible by $m$ admits an edge-decomposition into copies of $T$. This resolves the tree-decomposition item of the source; the subsidiary question left there, whether Thomassen's constant $2k^2+k$ for decompositions into $k$-stars is optimal, appears to remain open, but it is a sharp-constant question rather than a named major conjecture, and no source discussed here determines the exact minimum.

On the surviving core, the 3-flow conjecture, the literature after 2012 contains only class-restricted confirmations and equivalences, none of which settles the general 4-edge-connected case: the planar case is Grötzsch's 1959 three-colorability theorem and the projective-planar case was verified by Steinberg and Younger in 1989 (both as recorded in Li, Ma, Miao, Shi, Wang, Zhang 2022, which then verified the toroidal case); Li, Luo, and Wang (2018) proved the conjecture for graphs of independence number at most 4 (order at least 21); Han, Lai, and Li (2018) proved that graph classes of bounded independence number contain only finitely many contraction obstructions to modulo-$(2p+1)$-orientations; Zhang and Zhou (2022) proved it for Cayley graphs of valency at least 4 on supersolvable groups with noncyclic Sylow 2-subgroups, extended by Ahanjideh and Kovács (2026) to solvable groups of twice square-free order; Prałat and Wormald (2020, as recorded in Li–Li–Liu 2025) proved that random 5-regular graphs asymptotically almost surely have modulo-3 orientations, and Li, Li, and Liu (2025) extended this to random $(4p+1)$-regular graphs for $p\le 4$; de Jong and Richter (2023) proved the stronger prescription-form (Jaeger–Linial–Payan–Tarsi strong 3-flow conjecture) for projective planar graphs; Kochol's reductions (2001, as recorded in Hasanvand 2016 and Li–Li–Liu 2025) reduce the conjecture to 5-edge-connected graphs and to 5-regular 4-edge-connected graphs with all indegrees 1 or 4.

The recent treatments discussed here (Li–Li–Liu, Discrete Applied Mathematics, December 2025, stating that the $p=1$ case 'remains open'; Ahanjideh–Kovács, arXiv March 2026; and a 2026 Discrete Mathematics paper on $\mathbb{Z}_3$-connectivity of signed graphs that quotes the 5-edge-connected group-connectivity conjecture as open) all treat the conjecture as unresolved, and no resolution or refutation claim was found in any source.

## Scientific Significance

Affected-field significance: `high`.

A resolution would directly change core knowledge of integer flow theory, one of the central chapters of structural graph theory: it would settle one of the three classical Tutte flow conjectures, fix the exact edge-connectivity threshold for nowhere-zero 3-flows (currently only known to lie between 4 and 6), and complete the surviving $k=3$ case of Jaeger's circular-flow program after its refutation for odd $k\ge 7$ in 2018. The impact is direct rather than indirect: every 4-edge-connected graph would acquire a modulo-3 orientation and, by the prescribed-outdegree strengthenings already proved at higher connectivity, the boundary of what orientation and decomposition theorems high edge-connectivity implies (including star decompositions and dual 3-coloring-type statements for planar and near-planar graphs) would move from threshold 6 to threshold 4. The proof techniques required — a structural theory of minimal counterexamples to modulo-3 orientation, extending the entropy-based and contractible-configuration machinery of the 2012-2013 breakthroughs — would themselves become new core methods of the field.
