Gomes (2021) proved that in electromagnetism coupled to a charged scalar on a simply-connected universe divided into two regions, when charged matter vanishes on the shared interface but not in the bulks, the pair of regional physical states fails to determine a unique global physical state: a residual variety parametrized by U(1) survives, which he identifies with the direct empirical significance (DES) of regional gauge transformations — a theorem-level version of 't Hooft's beam-splitter. For non-Abelian structure groups only an infinitesimal, perturbative characterization is available (the variety's parametrization recovers the Lie algebra of the charge group about homogeneous backgrounds), and the paper states it is not clear how to obtain the finite version. This problem asks for the finite, non-perturbative non-Abelian analogue: a characterization of the set of physically distinct gluings of two regional states — whether it is parametrized by the charge group G, a subgroup or quotient, or a state-dependent structure, with inequivalence certified by gauge-invariant quantities — together with the completion of the DES classification, namely the status of boundary stabilizers that do not extend into the bulk and the cases where charged matter does not vanish at the interface. The literature audit found this core open: the author's own companion preprints reiterate the perturbative-only articulation (naming the Gribov obstruction), and adjacent edge-mode and asymptotic-symmetry work from 2019–2026 does not address the finite parametrization.

## Background

In Newtonian mechanics the empirical significance of a symmetry is familiar from Galileo's ship: a boost changes no event inside the cabin but is registered relationally, in the ship's velocity relative to the shore. Whether local gauge transformations — usually regarded as pure descriptive redundancy of a gauge theory — can carry an analogous relational empirical significance has been debated in philosophy of physics since the 2000s. Brading and Brown (2004) argued that regional gauge transformations must match across the boundary between a subsystem and its environment and hence are never observable; Greaves and Wallace (2014) defended the opposite: a symmetry has direct empirical significance (DES) when a transformation acts as a symmetry of a subsystem while the state of the total system changes in an empirically distinguishable way. The cleanest illustration is 't Hooft's beam-splitter: two arms of an interferometer carrying electron fields can be given independent constant phase shifts, transformations that leave each arm's internal physics unchanged but are visible in the interference pattern.

Gomes (2021) made this precise for Yang–Mills theory. Take a simply-connected universe $\Sigma$ decomposed into two bounded regions $\Sigma_+$ and $\Sigma_-$ meeting on an interface $S=\partial\Sigma_+=\partial\Sigma_-$. Write $[\varphi]$ for the physical content of a field configuration, i.e. its gauge-equivalence class restricted to the region in question, and $[\varphi_+]\cup_S^{(i)}[\varphi_-]$ for a physically legitimate composition (gluing) of the two regional physical contents. Global supervenience on subsystems (GSS) holds when the pair $([\varphi_+],[\varphi_-])$ determines a unique global physical content; its failure ($\neg$GSS) means there is a residual variety of physically distinct universes built from the same regional states, indexed by a set $I$ of gluings. Gomes identifies $\neg$GSS with DES of regional gauge transformations: the variety is encoded in relations between subsystems, not in their intrinsic states — a form of holism.

His main result, Theorem 1, concerns electromagnetism (structure group $U(1)$) coupled to a charged Klein–Gordon scalar: if the charged matter vanishes on the interface $S$ but not in the bulk of the regions, the glued physical state is underdetermined and the residual variety is parametrized by $U(1)$, realized as regional rigid (constant) phase shifts acting on the matter but not on the gauge potential — the beam-splitter situation derived as a theorem. For a non-Abelian structure group $G$ the situation differs qualitatively. The analogue runs through stabilizers: regional gauge transformations $\xi$ with $D\xi=0$ ($D$ the gauge-covariant derivative), which leave the gauge potential invariant but can still transform the matter. In the Abelian case every configuration has the same stabilizers, namely the constant transformations; for a non-Abelian group, generic configurations have trivial stabilizers, and non-trivial stabilizers exist only on special homogeneous strata of field space (e.g. the vacuum $A=0$, whose stabilizers are the constant maps into $G$). About such backgrounds Gomes recovers only the infinitesimal version: the variety is parametrized by elements of the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ (his Eq. A.12), and he states that 'for non-Abelian groups, it is not clear how to obtain the finite version', i.e. a parametrization by the charge group itself — the group of constant gauge transformations carrying the regional charges, isomorphic to the structure group $G$ (his Eq. A.13, $I\simeq G$). The integration tools that work in the Abelian case — Coulomb-type gauge fixings and dressings (gauge-covariant constructions that attach a charged field to a reference structure via a Wilson line — the path-ordered exponential of the gauge potential along a curve — so as to build gauge-invariant variables) — do not globalize: as the same author records in a companion preprint, the obstruction is tied to the Gribov problem, i.e. the absence of any global gauge-fixing section for non-Abelian theories (Gribov ambiguities). Related results in the edge-modes program — the study of the extra boundary degrees of freedom that appear when a gauge theory is restricted to a finite region (Gomes, Hopfmüller and Riello 2019; Gomes and Riello 2021) — split regional degrees of freedom into radiative and Coulombic parts and prove, via a field-space connection (a connection on the space of field configurations, used to compare fields across the region boundary), that regional radiative data determine the glued state uniquely whenever stabilizers are absent, and give a connection-dependent notion of non-Abelian gluing; but they do not deliver a canonical, group-parametrized residual variety, which is exactly what the identification of DES requires. The theorem-level non-Abelian analogue of Theorem 1, and with it the completion of the classification of when regional gauge transformations have DES, is what remains open.

## Problem Statement

For Yang–Mills theory with compact non-Abelian structure group $G$, coupled to charged matter, on a simply-connected manifold $\Sigma=\Sigma_+\cup\Sigma_-$ with regions sharing the interface $S=\partial\Sigma_+=\partial\Sigma_-$ (under the source's idealizations: Euclidean signature, closed $\Sigma$, configurations admitting stabilizers such as homogeneous backgrounds): obtain the finite, non-perturbative analogue of the Abelian Theorem 1 — a characterization of the residual gluing variety

$$[\varphi(i)] = [\varphi_+] \cup_S^{(i)} [\varphi_-], \qquad i \in I, \qquad [\varphi(i)] \neq [\varphi(i')] \ \text{iff}\ i \neq i'.$$

Specifically: (1) determine the index set $I$ for the non-Abelian theory — whether it is parametrized by the charge group $G$ itself (the finite counterpart of the $U(1)$ theorem and of the infinitesimal Eq. A.12), by a subgroup or quotient of $G$, or by some state-dependent structure — and prove the inequivalence (or collapse) of the resulting glued states using gauge-invariant quantities, such as Wilson-line correlators crossing $S$ (a Wilson line is the path-ordered exponential of the gauge potential along a curve crossing $S$, transporting gauge charge between the regions) or charge correlators; (2) settle, within the same classification, the status of boundary stabilizers, i.e. transformations fixing the fields on $S$ that do not extend to stabilizers of the bulk: do they label physically distinct gluings, and can they be associated with DES or must they be given another interpretation; and (3) settle the boundary-matter cases, classifying which conditions on the charged matter and field data at $S$ (vanishing, compatible non-vanishing, or partial) leave residual variety. The expected completion is a theorem-level non-Abelian counterpart of Theorem 1 together with the resulting classification of the sources of regional physical variety; extensions to Lorentzian signature, non-simply-connected topology, and asymptotic regimes are further directions of the source and are not part of this problem's completion boundary.

The verification contract below evaluates answers to this statement. It does not narrow or redefine the research question.

Known solving difficulties:

- The stabilizer structure of non-Abelian configuration space is stratified: generic configurations have trivial stabilizers, so any global statement must handle orbit-type stratification and Gribov copies, with the variety living only on special homogeneous strata.
- The gauge-fixing route that integrates the infinitesimal result in the Abelian case is blocked by the Gribov problem (no global gauge-fixing section), so the finite parametrization needs alternative structure — field-space connections, dressings, or invariant observables — whose choice-dependence must be controlled or shown innocuous.
- Non-Abelian nonlinearity makes the variety state-dependent: the label set $I$ may fail to be a fixed group, and proving group structure or its failure requires explicit gauge-invariant witnesses (e.g. Wilson-line and charge correlators crossing the interface) that separate the glued states.
- Boundary-matter compatibility couples matter and flux data through interface matching conditions, requiring PDE-level analysis (Green-function and Dirichlet-to-Neumann-type operators) to decide in which sectors variety survives.
- The interpretive component — deciding whether inextendible boundary stabilizers can be associated with DES or must be reinterpreted (e.g. as edge modes without empirical significance) — requires both the mathematical classification and a conceptual criterion connecting residual variety to regional rigid symmetries, in dialogue with the Greaves–Wallace and Brading–Brown positions.

## Current Progress

- Status: `ready`

Source fidelity was verified against the open-access publisher PDF of Gomes (2021) (DOI: 10.1007/s13194-021-00397-y, https://doi.org/10.1007/s13194-021-00397-y): Theorem 1 (residual U(1) gluing variety for electromagnetism coupled to a charged Klein–Gordon scalar, with matter vanishing on the interface $S$ but not in the bulk), the infinitesimal non-Abelian characterization of Eq. A.12 (recovering the Lie algebra of the charge group about the vacuum $A=0$), and the statement 'for non-Abelian groups, it is not clear how to obtain the finite version' (Appendix A.3.3, targeting Eq. A.13 with $I\simeq G$) all appear as the candidate summarizes them; the candidate is correctly attributed to the sole author Henrique Gomes, and no conflation of adjacent results was found. The passage on inextendible boundary stabilizers ('cannot be associated with DES') and the boundary-matter discussion (compatible non-vanishing boundary matter fixes the relative phase and leaves no variety) were also confirmed.

Gomes's companion physics preprint (arXiv:2110.07208, 'Noether charges: the link between empirical significance of symmetries and non-separability') restates the same limitation from the technical side: in the non-Abelian case the reconstruction recovers only the physical content of a perturbation of the potential, while full reconstruction is available only in the Abelian case; the chain 'conserved regional charges ⇔ non-separability ⇔ DES' is established only where stabilizers exist. It does not obtain the finite non-Abelian parametrization.

A second companion preprint by Henrique Gomes (arXiv:2110.14563, https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.14563, 'The role of representational conventions in assessing the empirical significance of symmetries') develops a qualitative DES classification — Abelian variety requires charged matter in the bulk but not on the interface; non-Abelian variety requires shared regional or boundary stabilizers, hence highly homogeneous fields (e.g. the $A\approx 0$ sector with constant SU(N) stabilizers, and boundary conditions such as $[\tau^I,E_+]|_S=0$); inextendible boundary stabilizers give non-uniqueness of the composition (edge-mode-like) — but its Appendix A.5 explicitly states that in the non-Abelian case the analogue can only be articulated perturbatively, 'for reasons mainly to do with the Gribov problem' (no global gauge-fixing section). The finite, non-perturbative statement therefore remains open even in the author's own follow-up work, which also identifies the main structural obstruction. Both companion preprints appear to be unpublished.

The pre-existing edge-modes program supplies partial, choice-dependent answers without closing the question. Gomes, Hopfmüller and Riello (Nucl. Phys. B 941, 2019, DOI: 10.1016/j.nuclphysb.2019.02.020, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nuclphysb.2019.02.020) treat non-Abelian boundary charges and dressings via a field-space connection (the Singer–DeWitt connection), through which gluing is defined by parallel transport; the resulting finite gluing depends on the choice of connection and path and does not provide the canonical parametrization of the residual variety by the charge group $G$ that Eq. A.13 asks for. Gomes and Riello (SciPost Phys. 10, 130, 2021, DOI: 10.21468/SciPostPhys.10.6.130, https://doi.org/10.21468/SciPostPhys.10.6.130) prove quasilocal reconstruction theorems: regional radiative data determine the glued state uniquely exactly when stabilizers are absent — delimiting where the variety can live (the stabilizer strata) without parametrizing it there.

Recent 2024–2026 literature stays adjacent rather than resolving the core. Borsboom and Posthuma (SciPost Phys. 20, 185, 2026, DOI: 10.21468/SciPostPhys.20.6.185, https://doi.org/10.21468/SciPostPhys.20.6.185) rigorously derive the physical global gauge group $\mathcal{G}^I/\mathcal{G}^\infty_0$ of Yang–Mills theory from spatial asymptotic boundary conditions, unifying characterizations of when the global gauge group carries physical significance, and Borsboom (arXiv:2606.31265, https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31265, 2026) shows for the instantaneous formulation that the physical boundary symmetry group of electromagnetism is a copy of the global gauge group, discussing conditions under which the same holds non-Abelianly; both agree with Gomes's framework in special cases but address asymptotic/external boundaries, not the regional DES gluing variety of Theorem 1's setting. Gomes, Langenscheidt and Oriti (arXiv:2412.00993, 2024) review finite-region physics and list open issues without touching the finite non-Abelian parametrization; Gomes's 'Ghosts that Connect' (arXiv:2607.27297, 2026) relates ghosts and BRST symmetry to a principal connection on field space, a neighboring conceptual development.

Coverage and residual uncertainty: a Semantic Scholar citation sweep of the Holism paper (DOI: 10.1007/s13194-021-00397-y, https://doi.org/10.1007/s13194-021-00397-y; 17 citing works, predominantly philosophy of physics — e.g. Rivat 2023, Wolf and Read 2023, Landsman 2022, de Haro and Butterfield 2025, Carrozza and Höhn 2021–2022) found none claiming to resolve the finite non-Abelian gluing, the inextendible-stabilizer question, or the boundary-matter classification; arXiv full-text search for 'direct empirical significance' and 'empirical significance of gauge' returns only Gomes's own papers. I could not inspect Wallace's 'Isolated Systems and Their Symmetries, Part II' (2022, no accessible preprint found) or Gomes's 2025 Cambridge Elements book 'Gauge Theory and the Geometrisation of Physics' (paywalled); given that the 2024–2026 works above still frame the question as open, resolution there is unlikely but not verified. Two further items acknowledged in the source — extension to Lorentzian signature, non-simply-connected topology and asymptotic regimes, and the detailed comparison between a gauge-free global parametrization of gluings and the sophistication/projection method — are broader future directions; they are recorded here but deliberately kept outside the audited problem's completion boundary, which is the finite non-Abelian analogue of Theorem 1 plus the associated classification of regional variety. On the evidence inspected, that core is open.

## Scientific Significance

Affected-field significance: `medium`.

Solving this problem would settle whether the direct empirical significance of gauge symmetry — the thesis that local gauge transformations acting on a subsystem can have observable relational effects, as in 't Hooft's beam-splitter — extends from electromagnetism to realistic non-Abelian theories such as QCD, directly changing the core knowledge of the DES debate in philosophy of physics (Greaves–Wallace's positive view versus Brading–Brown's denial) and of the interpretation of global/rigid gauge symmetry and regional stabilizers. It would also give the edge-modes program a canonical (rather than connection-choice-dependent) account of the non-Abelian gluing ambiguity, an indirect but substantive gain for the mathematical treatment of gauge theories on bounded regions; the impact is conceptual and foundational rather than a change in calculational capability.
