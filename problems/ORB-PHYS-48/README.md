# ORB-PHYS-48: Coexistence of infinite $+$ and $-$ clusters in the Ising model on the square matching lattice $\mathbb{Z}_*^2$ for all subcritical temperatures

Jiang and Lang (arXiv:2503.21147, to appear in the Annals of Probability) proved that in the Ising model on $\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{Z}$ and on the three-dimensional triangular lattice, where $\mathbb{T}$ is the two-dimensional triangular lattice, a unique infinite $+$ cluster and a unique infinite $-$ cluster coexist at every subcritical inverse temperature $\beta\in[0,\beta_c)$ provided the external field is small enough, $|h|<h_c(\beta)$. They posed as an explicitly open conjecture the same coexistence statement for the two-dimensional square matching lattice $\mathbb{Z}_*^2$, the square lattice with both diagonals of every unit square added, with ferromagnetic Ising interactions on all bonds. On $\mathbb{Z}_*^2$ coexistence is known only for small $\beta$ (an easy consequence of Higuchi's results for the model without diagonal interactions); the conjecture asks for the full range $\beta\in[0,\beta_c(\mathbb{Z}_*^2))$ with a nontrivial field window. This audit verifies the candidate's fidelity to the source and finds, as of August 2026, no proof or refutation in the literature: the conjecture is open.

## Background

The Ising model is the classical lattice model of ferromagnetism. To each vertex $x$ of a graph one attaches a spin $\sigma_x\in\{-1,+1\}$; at inverse temperature $\beta\ge 0$ and external field $h$, the Gibbs measure of a finite subgraph weights a configuration $\sigma$ proportionally to $\exp(\beta\sum_{xy}\sigma_x\sigma_y+h\sum_x\sigma_x)$, the first sum running over the edges of the graph; note that the external field is not rescaled by $\beta$ (this is Equation (1) of the source). On the square lattice $\mathbb{Z}^2$ (nearest-neighbor edges only) the infinite-volume model has a critical inverse temperature $\beta_c(\mathbb{Z}^2)\in(0,\infty)$: for $\beta>\beta_c(\mathbb{Z}^2)$ and $h=0$ the system is magnetized, while for $\beta<\beta_c(\mathbb{Z}^2)$ it is disordered.

A geometric question, independent of magnetization, is percolation of spin clusters: in an infinite-volume spin configuration, the $+$ clusters and $-$ clusters are the maximal connected sets of equal sign (connectivity following the graph's edges), and one asks whether an infinite cluster of a given sign exists. For translation-invariant measures with finite energy — every spin takes each value with probability bounded away from $0$ on every finite set, which the Ising model satisfies — an infinite cluster, when it exists, is unique (Burton and Keane, 1989).

In two dimensions at zero field the percolation picture is sharp, and it involves a second graph. The matching lattice (or star lattice) $\mathbb{Z}_*^2$ of $\mathbb{Z}^2$ is obtained by adding, to the nearest-neighbor edges, both diagonals of every unit square, so that same-sign spins two diagonal steps apart are declared adjacent; clusters read with this connectivity are called star clusters. Coniglio, Nappi, Peruggi and Russo (1976) predicted, and Higuchi (1982, 1993) proved, that for the Ising model on $\mathbb{Z}^2$ at $h=0$ and every $\beta\in[0,\beta_c(\mathbb{Z}^2))$ no infinite $+$ or $-$ cluster exists with nearest-neighbor connectivity, yet an infinite $+$ star cluster and an infinite $-$ star cluster coexist. (The 1982 paper establishes the Bernoulli-site-percolation case: at occupation density $1/2$ on $\mathbb{Z}^2$, the star clusters of both signs are infinite; in particular the site-percolation threshold of $\mathbb{Z}_*^2$, i.e. the occupation density above which an infinite occupied cluster first appears, lies strictly below $1/2$.) Crucially, in these results the diagonal bonds of $\mathbb{Z}_*^2$ serve for connectivity only; they carry no interaction. On the triangular lattice $\mathbb{T}$ — which, unlike $\mathbb{Z}^2$, is its own matching graph and has site-percolation threshold exactly $1/2$ — the behavior is the opposite: Bálint, Camia and Meester (2010) proved that for $\beta<\beta_c(\mathbb{T})$ the model sits exactly at the percolation threshold of its natural two-parameter generalization (a percolation transition at spin density $1/2$), and consequently at $h=0$ there is no infinite cluster of either sign.

In three dimensions both signs can percolate with genuine interactions on every bond. Campanino and Russo (1985) proved that on the cubic lattice $\mathbb{Z}^3$ an infinite $+$ cluster and an infinite $-$ cluster coexist when $\beta$ is small and $|h|$ is small; Aizenman, Bricmont and Lebowitz (1987) showed that in sufficiently high dimension the minority spins percolate even inside the opposite phase at temperatures slightly below critical. Jiang and Lang (2025) pushed the three-dimensional coexistence all the way to criticality for two lattices: for the Ising model on $\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{Z}$ (the Cartesian product of the triangular lattice with the line) and on the three-dimensional triangular lattice, for every $\beta\in[0,\beta_c)$ there exists $h_c(\beta)>0$ such that a unique infinite $+$ cluster and a unique infinite $-$ cluster coexist whenever $|h|<h_c(\beta)$. Their proof introduces an auxiliary percolation parameter $p$ (the probability that a bond is available for a connection event) and establishes a differential inequality — a uniform-in-volume bound on the ratio of the $h$- and $p$-partial derivatives of the probability of increasing events (events closed under flipping $-$ spins to $+$) — from which coexistence follows.

Jiang and Lang call Higuchi's two-dimensional coexistence result "somewhat artificial", since its diagonal bonds carry no interaction, and raise the natural question for the fully interacting model: the Ising model on $\mathbb{Z}_*^2$ with ferromagnetic interactions on all nearest-neighbor and diagonal bonds. They record that coexistence is easy to derive from Higuchi's results when $\beta$ is small, conjecture that it persists for all $\beta\in[0,\beta_c(\mathbb{Z}_*^2))$ with the same small-field window, and note that their method "should be applicable" because $\mathbb{Z}_*^2$ is the triangular lattice together with one additional family of diagonal edges. No proof is given, and none has appeared since.

## Problem Statement

The problem is the conjecture raised in Remark 4 of Jiang and Lang (arXiv:2503.21147). Consider the ferromagnetic Ising model on the square matching lattice $\mathbb{Z}_*^2$ — the graph with vertex set $\mathbb{Z}^2$ whose edges join every nearest-neighbor pair as well as both diagonal pairs of each unit square — with a uniform ferromagnetic coupling on every edge, at inverse temperature $\beta\ge 0$ and external field $h$, with Gibbs weights $\exp(\beta\sum_{xy}\sigma_x\sigma_y+h\sum_x\sigma_x)$ in finite volume (the field term is not multiplied by $\beta$, following Equation (1) of the source) and the corresponding infinite-volume Gibbs state; let $\beta_c(\mathbb{Z}_*^2)$ denote the critical inverse temperature of this model at $h=0$. Prove, or refute, the following statement: for every $\beta\in[0,\beta_c(\mathbb{Z}_*^2))$ there exists $h_c(\beta)>0$ such that whenever $|h|<h_c(\beta)$, the infinite-volume state contains almost surely a unique infinite cluster of $+$ spins and a unique infinite cluster of $-$ spins. The already-known small-$\beta$ regime, which follows from Higuchi's results, is part of the claim; the open content is the full range of subcritical inverse temperatures up to $\beta_c(\mathbb{Z}_*^2)$, including the zero-field slice $h=0$ for intermediate $\beta$.

The verification contract below evaluates answers to this statement. It does not narrow or redefine the research question.

Known solving difficulties:

- Adapting the source's method requires introducing an auxiliary percolation parameter $p$ so that $\mathbb{Z}_*^2$ is treated as the triangular lattice plus one family of slope $-1$ diagonal edges, and then proving the central differential inequality — a uniform-in-volume bound on the ratio of the $h$- and $p$-partial derivatives of the probability of increasing events — in this geometry; this constituted the technical core of the source's eighteen-page proof of the easier $\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{Z}$ case.
- The missing criticality input: the three-dimensional proof consumes a known slab-criticality theorem, whereas on $\mathbb{Z}_*^2$ the analogous input — that at $h=0$ and every $\beta<\beta_c(\mathbb{Z}_*^2)$ the same-sign connections are at least critical — does not follow from Higuchi's theorems (which cover only the model without diagonal interactions) and is essentially part of what must be proved.
- Non-planarity: the two diagonal families of $\mathbb{Z}_*^2$ cross, so the planar-duality and matching-lattice (Sykes–Essam-type) arguments underlying two-dimensional percolation theory do not apply directly; any alternative attack must replace planar duality, e.g. by renormalization or Russo–Seymour–Welsh (RSW) box-crossing estimates valid in this geometry.
- A refutation would require new comparison or non-percolation theorems for positively associated dependent percolation on a non-planar two-dimensional lattice, exhibiting a subcritical $\beta$ and field values at which one sign provably has no infinite cluster.

## Current Progress

The question is posed in Remark 4 of Jiang and Lang, 'Percolation of both signs in a triangular-type 3D Ising model above $T_c$' (arXiv:2503.21147v2). The paper proves coexistence of a unique infinite $+$ cluster and a unique infinite $-$ cluster on $\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{Z}$ and the 3D triangular lattice for every $\beta\in[0,\beta_c)$ with $|h|<h_c(\beta)$, and states as an explicitly unproved conjecture the same statement for the square matching lattice $\mathbb{Z}_*^2$ with interactions on all bonds, for all $\beta\in[0,\beta_c(\mathbb{Z}_*^2))$. The coexistence statement includes uniqueness of each sign's infinite cluster. The source records the small-$\beta$ subcase as an easy consequence of Higuchi's results. Reviewer convention check against the source PDF: Equation (1) of Jiang and Lang gives the field term as $\sum_x h\sigma_x$ (not scaled by $\beta$), and the source phrase 'both nearest-neighbor and diagonal interactions' is read throughout this record as a uniform ferromagnetic coupling on every edge of $\mathbb{Z}_*^2$.

Literature lineage: Coniglio–Nappi–Peruggi–Russo (1976) anticipated, and Higuchi (1982 for Bernoulli site percolation, 1993 for the Ising model) rigorously proved, the two-dimensional coexistence of infinite $+$ and $-$ star clusters at $h=0$ for all $\beta\in[0,\beta_c(\mathbb{Z}^2))$ — but with the diagonal bonds of $\mathbb{Z}_*^2$ carrying no interactions, which Jiang and Lang explicitly flag as artificial. Bálint–Camia–Meester (2010) established the complementary triangular-lattice fact (no infinite clusters at $h=0$ below criticality), which delimits where 2D coexistence is geometrically possible. In 3D, Campanino–Russo (1985) proved small-$\beta$ coexistence on $\mathbb{Z}^3$ and Aizenman–Bricmont–Lebowitz (1987) high-dimensional minority-spin percolation; Jiang–Lang (2025) reached all subcritical temperatures on $\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{Z}$ and the 3D triangular lattice. None of these addresses the fully interacting matching lattice $\mathbb{Z}_*^2$.

No proof or refutation of the $\mathbb{Z}_*^2$ conjecture was identified at the August 2026 curation cutoff. The arXiv record has only v1 (2025-03-27) and v2 (2025-12-16), and v2 still states the conjecture as open, with the strategy sketch that $\mathbb{Z}_*^2$ can be viewed as $\mathbb{T}$ plus slope $-1$ diagonal edges each selected with an auxiliary probability $p$. No follow-up by either author addresses it: their subsequent joint arXiv paper, 'The free state for the Potts model on Cayley trees is either extremal or glassy' (arXiv:2607.05138, July 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05138), concerns extremality of Gibbs states on trees and does not touch the conjecture, and Jianping Jiang's other subsequent arXiv entries are on other topics.

Related results that do not resolve the question include general no-infinite-cluster criteria for two-dimensional dependent percolation (e.g. Rangel Baldasso, 'No infinite clusters at criticality for two-dimensional dependent percolation under abstract hypotheses', arXiv:2607.13308, 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13308) concern planar, monotone, critical settings and do not touch the non-planar matching lattice with interactions; Kertész-line work for random-cluster models on $\mathbb{Z}^d$ concerns the majority-sign percolation line, not two-sign coexistence on $\mathbb{Z}_*^2$. The paper's own Remark 1 also notes that even in the proven $\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{Z}$ case the boundary behavior at $|h|=h_c(\beta)$ remains unproved, confirming the coexistence window itself is at the frontier.

No conflicting or partial-resolution evidence was found. The open core is the full statement: coexistence, with uniqueness of each sign's infinite cluster, for every $\beta\in[0,\beta_c(\mathbb{Z}_*^2))$ with some field window $|h|<h_c(\beta)$, of which only the small-$\beta$ regime (and the $\beta=0$ Bernoulli point) is known.

## Scientific Significance

Affected-field significance: `medium`.

A proof would extend two-sign cluster coexistence — previously established only in three dimensions (on $\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{Z}$ and the 3D triangular lattice, by Jiang and Lang) and in Higuchi's two-dimensional model whose diagonal bonds carry no interactions — to the fully interacting two-dimensional matching lattice, completing the two-dimensional geometric picture of the Ising model above its critical temperature and demonstrating that the auxiliary-parameter differential-inequality method transfers to two dimensions. The impact is direct but confined to rigorous statistical mechanics and percolation theory: it would settle the sign-percolation phase diagram of the eight-neighbor Ising model on $\mathbb{Z}_*^2$ in the temperature–field plane and clarify the reach of the new method; a refutation would be equally informative, revealing an unexpected correlation-driven suppression of percolation on a non-planar two-dimensional lattice.
