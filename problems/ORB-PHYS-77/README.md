# ORB-PHYS-77: Completing the approximability classification of the parameterized Tutte polynomial at rational points with $(x-1)(y-1)\notin[0,1]$

Roth, Schmitt and Wellnitz (ICALP 2021) introduced the parameterized Tutte polynomial $T^k_G(x,y)$, the restriction of the classical Tutte polynomial's defining sum to edge subsets of cardinality exactly $k$, and completely classified its exact evaluation complexity over rational coordinates: polynomial time on the hyperbola $(x-1)(y-1)=1$ and at $(1,1)$, fixed-parameter tractable but #P-hard on the line $x=1$, and #W[1]-hard at every other rational point. For randomized approximation they proved that an FPTRAS exists at all rational points with $0\le(x-1)(y-1)\le1$ (an FPRAS if additionally $x\neq1$ or $y=1$), via log-concavity of the graphic matroid's basis-generating polynomial, and explicitly left the approximability of every rational point outside this region open. A literature audit covering all known citing works, the adjacent exact-counting programs (minor-closed properties, induced-subgraph counting, sub-hypergraph counting, modular counting) and the approximation-side literature (counting-to-decision meta-theorems, database-query counting, treewidth-parameterized and density-restricted Tutte evaluation) finds neither an FPTRAS/FPRAS construction nor an inapproximability result for any point with $(x-1)(y-1)\notin[0,1]$ — points at which $T^k_G$ encodes, for instance, the number of $k$-edge subsets spanning an even number of connected components, the number of pairs consisting of a $k$-edge subset and a proper $c$-coloring ($c\ge2$), and counts associated with acyclic orientations of $k$-edge subsets. The audited problem is to complete the approximability classification of the rational parameterized Tutte plane: for each rational point with $(x-1)(y-1)<0$ or $(x-1)(y-1)>1$, either construct an FPTRAS or FPRAS, or prove that none exists under a standard hypothesis of parameterized counting complexity.

## Background

The Tutte polynomial is a two-variable graph invariant
$$
T_G(x,y)=\sum_{A\subseteq E(G)}(x-1)^{k(A)-k(E(G))}\cdot(y-1)^{k(A)+|A|-|V(G)|},
$$
where $k(S)$ denotes the number of connected components of the spanning subgraph $(V(G),S)$. It simultaneously encodes many classical invariants — the chromatic polynomial, the flow polynomial, the number of spanning trees — and coincides, up to a change of variables, with the partition function of the $q$-state Potts model of statistical mechanics for $q=(x-1)(y-1)$, the central object in the study of exactly solved lattice models. Jaeger, Vertigan and Welsh (1990) completely classified the computational complexity of exactly evaluating $T_G$ at every fixed pair of complex coordinates: the evaluation is polynomial-time computable only on the hyperbola $(x-1)(y-1)=1$ and at four exceptional points, and #P-hard everywhere else, where #P is the counting analogue of NP.

This record concerns a parameterized analogue introduced by Roth, Schmitt and Wellnitz (ICALP 2021). In parameterized complexity, a problem is fixed-parameter tractable (FPT) with respect to a parameter $k$ if it is solvable in time $f(k)\cdot|G|^{O(1)}$ for a computable function $f$; the counting analogue of NP-hardness is #W[1]-hardness, and conditional running-time lower bounds are usually stated under the Exponential Time Hypothesis (ETH), which asserts that 3-SAT requires $2^{\Omega(n)}$ time. Writing $\binom{E(G)}{k}$ for the family of edge subsets of size exactly $k$, the parameterized Tutte polynomial is
$$
T^k_G(x,y)=\sum_{A\in\binom{E(G)}{k}}(x-1)^{k(A)-k(E(G))}\cdot(y-1)^{k(A)+k-|V(G)|},
$$
with input $(G,k)$ and parameter $k$; it is the classical sum restricted to $k$-edge subsets, i.e., a weighted count of small subgraph patterns. Many classical interpretations survive the restriction: $T^k_G(2,1)$ equals the number of $k$-edge forests of $G$; $T^k_G(1-c,0)$ for integer $c$ determines the number of pairs $(A,\sigma)$ where $A$ is a $k$-edge subset and $\sigma$ is a proper $c$-coloring of $(V(G),A)$; $T^k_G(2,0)$ determines the number of pairs $(A,\eta)$ with $\eta$ an acyclic orientation of $(V(G),A)$, and the number of $k$-edge subsets whose induced graph has even Betti number (the rank of its cycle space); $T^k_G(0,2)$ determines the number of $k$-edge subsets spanning an even number of connected components.

Roth, Schmitt and Wellnitz completely classified exact computation over the rational points of the Tutte plane: computing $T^k_G(x,y)$ is polynomial-time computable if $(x,y)=(1,1)$ or $(x-1)(y-1)=1$, fixed-parameter tractable but #P-hard on the line $x=1$ ($y\neq1$), and #W[1]-hard at every other rational point. The hardness proofs express $T^k_G(x,y)$ as a linear combination of graph homomorphism counts (counts of edge-preserving vertex maps between graphs) and apply the complexity-monotonicity framework of Curticapean, Dell and Marx (STOC 2017), with coefficients controlled through the symmetry groups of toroidal grids (grid graphs with wrapped-around boundaries, i.e., Cartesian products of cycles) and Cayley graph expanders of 2-groups (sparse, highly connected graphs constructed from group multiplication tables).

For randomized approximation, the relevant notion is a fixed-parameter tractable randomized approximation scheme (FPTRAS), going back to Arvind and Raman (2002): a randomized algorithm that, on input $(G,k,\varepsilon,\delta)$, outputs in time $f(k)\cdot\mathrm{poly}(|G|,\varepsilon^{-1},\log(1/\delta))$ a value within a factor $(1\pm\varepsilon)$ of the true answer with probability at least $1-\delta$; if $f$ is a polynomial, the scheme is an FPRAS, the parameterized analogue of the classical fully polynomial randomized approximation scheme. Since $T^k_G(x,y)$ can be negative, the source paper uses the convention that the algorithm outputs a pair $(z,s)$ approximating the absolute value and returning the correct sign. The main positive result of the source paper states that $T^k_G(x,y)$ admits an FPTRAS whenever $0\le(x-1)(y-1)\le1$, and even an FPRAS if additionally $x\neq1$ or $y=1$; the proof reduces to the multivariate basis-generating polynomial of the graphic matroid (the matroid whose independent sets are the forests of $G$, so that its bases are the spanning trees), which is log-concave precisely in this region — its logarithm is a concave function on the positive orthant, the property that enables sampling — and applies the FPRAS of Anari, Liu, Oveis Gharan and Vinzant (STOC 2019) for counting matroid bases via high-dimensional random walks. The source paper emphasizes that while the classification of exact computation is complete, 'the complexity of approximation remains open outside of the coloured area' — that is, at every rational point with $(x-1)(y-1)<0$ or $(x-1)(y-1)>1$.

This gap is not an artifact of an overlooked classical result. Classical FPRASs for the unrestricted Tutte polynomial are known only for special graph classes — e.g., dense graphs (Alon, Frieze and Welsh 1995; extended to subdense graphs by Hauptmann and Tiling 2022) — and such results say nothing about the cardinality-restricted sum defining $T^k_G$, in which exponentially many weighted terms of vastly different magnitudes (and, for $(x-1)(y-1)<0$, mixed signs) must be combined; conversely, the #W[1]-hardness of exact computation does not by itself rule out multiplicative approximation, since no general transfer from exact hardness to parameterized approximation hardness is known.

## Problem Statement

For every pair of rational numbers $(x,y)$ with $(x-1)(y-1)\notin[0,1]$ — equivalently, with $(x-1)(y-1)<0$ or $(x-1)(y-1)>1$ — determine the parameterized approximation complexity of computing
$$
T^k_G(x,y)=\sum_{A\in\binom{E(G)}{k}}(x-1)^{k(A)-k(E(G))}\cdot(y-1)^{k(A)+k-|V(G)|}
$$
on input $(G,k)$ with parameter $k$, where $k(S)$ denotes the number of connected components of the spanning subgraph $(V(G),S)$ and $\binom{E(G)}{k}$ denotes the family of $k$-element subsets of the edge set of $G$. Approximation is understood in the sign-plus-magnitude sense of Roth, Schmitt and Wellnitz, since $T^k_G(x,y)$ can be negative: the algorithm outputs a pair $(z,s)$ with $z$ within a factor $(1\pm\varepsilon)$ of $|T^k_G(x,y)|$ and $s$ the correct sign, with probability at least $1-\delta$, in time $f(k)\cdot\mathrm{poly}(|G|,\varepsilon^{-1},\log(1/\delta))$; such a scheme is an FPTRAS, and an FPRAS if $f$ is a polynomial. For each such point, either construct an FPTRAS (and, where possible, an FPRAS), or prove that no such scheme exists under a standard hypothesis of parameterized counting complexity (for example, $\mathsf{W[1]}\neq\mathsf{FPT}$ under randomized parameterized reductions, or the randomized Exponential Time Hypothesis). The completion boundary is a dichotomy for the rational parameterized Tutte plane matching the completeness of the known exact classification: an explicit partition of $\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{Q}^2:(x-1)(y-1)\notin[0,1]\}$ into points that admit an FPTRAS or FPRAS (with the algorithms and their correctness proofs) and points at which approximation is provably hard. Answers may proceed point-wise or region-wise; natural subregions include the all-positive-weight sector $\{x>1, y>1, (x-1)(y-1)>1\}$ (e.g., the point $(2,3)$), the mixed-sign sector with $(x-1)(y-1)<0$ (e.g., $(0,2)$ and $(2,0)$, which determine parity-type counts of $k$-edge subsets), and the points $(1-c,0)$ for integers $c\ge2$ (coloring-type counts of $k$-edge subsets). The problem is source-faithful to the open region explicitly depicted as unresolved in Figure 1(b) of the source paper.

The verification contract below evaluates answers to this statement. It does not narrow or redefine the research question.

Known solving difficulties:

- The positive region $0\le(x-1)(y-1)\le1$ is tractable because the relevant multivariate basis-generating polynomial of the graphic matroid is log-concave exactly there, enabling the high-dimensional-walk FPRAS of Anari et al.; outside the region the log-concavity breaks, and no comparable sampling technology is known for the cardinality-restricted weighted sums.
- For $(x-1)(y-1)<0$ the summands have mixed signs, so $T^k_G(x,y)$ is a difference of exponentially many terms of varying magnitude; multiplicative approximation of the absolute value and correct sign recovery require controlling cancellations, which near-uniform sampling and self-reducibility arguments do not provide.
- Hardness of approximation in the parameterized regime has no general transfer from exact #W[1]-hardness: a reduction must encode an exactly-hard quantity into a value that a hypothetical FPTRAS pins down multiplicatively (e.g., via isolated witnesses or modular encodings); the only known separation of decision-FPT from no-FPTRAS (the source paper's construction of a property that is decidable in FPT time but admits no FPTRAS for counting unless $\mathsf{W[1]}=\mathsf{FPT}$) uses an artificially contrived property, indicating the difficulty of proving such results for natural points.
- A complete dichotomy must couple the algebraic case analysis over rational points (the source paper's nonvanishing criteria for coefficients of toroidal-grid homomorphism counts modulo primes) with approximation-theoretic arguments, likely requiring quantitative lower bounds on coefficient norms to preclude cancellations.
- Points with $(x-1)(y-1)>1$ and $x,y>1$ have all-positive summands, so the obstacle there is biasing a sampler toward high-weight $k$-edge subsets — a random-cluster-like measure (the edge-subset probability measure whose normalizing constant is the Tutte/Potts partition function) with a cardinality constraint, for which no fixed-parameter mixing or sampling results are known.

## Current Progress

The source is arXiv:2011.03433v2, published in LIPIcs ICALP 2021. Main Theorem 7 gives an FPTRAS for $0\le(x-1)(y-1)\le1$, and an FPRAS if additionally $x\ne1$ or $y=1$. The hyperbola $(x-1)(y-1)=1$ is on the boundary of this region, and the line $x=1$ lies inside it. The open approximation region is therefore $\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{Q}^2:(x-1)(y-1)<0\ \text{or}\ (x-1)(y-1)>1\}$. For the lower bounds, toroidal grids give only square-root treewidth growth; the paper uses Peyerimhoff–Vdovina Cayley expanders to obtain almost-tight ETH bounds. Peyerimhoff, Roth, Schmitt, Stix and Vdovina later remove the restriction to 2-groups using expanders from $p$-groups.

The exact-counting side of the source paper's program was completed and refined by its companion line: Peyerimhoff, Roth, Schmitt, Stix and Vdovina (MFCS 2021; journal version in SIAM J. Discrete Math. 2023, with Wellnitz) close the fine-grained gap left for minor-closed properties whose forbidden minors have no vertex of degree at least 3, and additionally treat modular counting of paths, cycles, forests and matroid bases. Their work does not touch the parameterized Tutte polynomial or any approximation question. The source paper's Table 1 leaves both approximate counting and decision complexity unknown for #EdgeSub(Φ) and EdgeSub(Φ) at Hamiltonicity, Eulerianity and claw-freeness, and the survey of Roth (Computer Science Review 2026) confirms these remain open; they are natural neighboring questions, kept distinct from the present problem because they concern unweighted subgraph counting rather than Tutte-plane points.

The induced-subgraph analogue #IndSub(Φ) flourished on the exact side: Roth–Schmitt–Wellnitz (FOCS 2020) settled monotone properties; Curticapean and Neuen (SODA 2025) strengthened the lower bounds via Fourier analysis; and Curticapean, Döring and Neuen (ESA 2025) refuted the conjecture that #IndSub(Φ) is #W[1]-hard for all non-meager properties by giving a polynomial-time algorithm for counting scorpion graphs. None of these works addresses approximation, and the analogous bounded-matching-number conjecture for #EdgeSub(Φ) stated in the source paper likewise remains open. Further exact-side classifications in adjacent settings — Goldberg and Roth (Algorithmica 2023) on subgraph counting modulo 2, Bressan, Brinkmann, Dell, Roth and Wellnitz (SODA 2026) on sub-hypergraph counting, and Mannens and Nederlof (ESA 2023) on the classical unrestricted Tutte polynomial parameterized by treewidth and cutwidth — confirm that the community's active frontier is exact counting; the approximation questions of the source paper are untouched.

The approximation side has progressed only in adjacent settings: Dell, Lapinskas and Meeks (SIAM J. Comput. 2022) show how to turn colorful decision oracles into approximate counting and sampling algorithms — a route the source paper already identifies as blocked for the open Table 1 properties, since fixed-parameter tractability of the edge-colorful decision version is itself unknown for Hamiltonicity, Eulerianity and claw-freeness; Focke, Goldberg, Roth and Živný (PODS 2022) establish a complete FPTRAS dichotomy for approximating answers to conjunctive queries with disequalities and negations (tractable iff bounded treewidth, under the randomized Exponential Time Hypothesis); Hauptmann and Tiling (2022) extend the dense-graph FPRAS of Alon–Frieze–Welsh to subdense and superdense graphs; Eden, Levi, Ron and Rubinfeld (STOC 2025) give sublinear-time approximate counting of motifs including Hamiltonian motifs, in the edge-query model rather than the FPT setting. None of these constructs an FPTRAS or FPRAS for, or proves inapproximability of, $T^k_G(x,y)$ at any rational point with $(x-1)(y-1)\notin[0,1]$.

No resolution of the open Tutte-plane region was identified at curation. The open questions in the source paper's Table 1 (FPTRAS and decision complexity for Hamiltonicity, Eulerianity and claw-freeness), the extension of both dichotomies to non-rational coordinates, and the bounded-matching-number conjecture for #EdgeSub(Φ) remain open as far as traced; they are neighboring questions recorded here rather than folded into the present problem, which is the Tutte-plane approximability classification.

## Scientific Significance

Affected-field significance: `medium`.

Solving the problem would directly extend the core tractability knowledge of parameterized counting complexity from exact to approximate evaluation of the parameterized Tutte polynomial: it would fix, for every rational point of the Tutte plane, whether the associated weighted count of $k$-edge subgraphs is approximable in fixed-parameter tractable time, completing the approximation counterpart of Roth–Schmitt–Wellnitz's exact dichotomy and of the classical Jaeger–Vertigan–Welsh classification of the Tutte plane. It would also directly settle the approximability of concrete counting problems expressible at the open points — the number of $k$-edge subsets spanning an even number of components, and the number of pairs of a $k$-edge subset with a proper $c$-coloring or an acyclic orientation — and would calibrate how far log-concavity-based sampling (Anari et al.) is from optimal for cardinality-restricted matroid sums. The impact is direct for parameterized and holant counting complexity and their interface with the statistical physics of exactly solved models (the Tutte plane is the Potts partition-function plane), but it does not by itself change methods or capabilities in the broader discipline.
