# ORB-PHYS-77: Completing the approximability classification of the parameterized Tutte polynomial at rational points with $(x-1)(y-1)\notin[0,1]$


Roth, Schmitt and Wellnitz (ICALP 2021) introduced the parameterized Tutte polynomial $T^k_G(x,y)$, the restriction of the classical Tutte polynomial's defining sum to edge subsets of cardinality exactly $k$, and completely classified its exact evaluation complexity over rational coordinates: polynomial time on the hyperbola $(x-1)(y-1)=1$ and at $(1,1)$, fixed-parameter tractable but #P-hard on the line $x=1$, and #W[1]-hard at every other rational point. For randomized approximation they proved that an FPTRAS exists at all rational points with $0\le(x-1)(y-1)\le1$ (an FPRAS if additionally $x\neq1$ or $y=1$), via log-concavity of the graphic matroid's basis-generating polynomial, and explicitly left the approximability of every rational point outside this region open. The problem is to complete the approximability classification of the rational parameterized Tutte plane: for each rational point with $(x-1)(y-1)<0$ or $(x-1)(y-1)>1$, either construct an FPTRAS or FPRAS, or prove that none exists under a standard hypothesis of parameterized counting complexity.

## Background

The Tutte polynomial is a two-variable graph invariant
$$
T_G(x,y)=\sum_{A\subseteq E(G)}(x-1)^{k(A)-k(E(G))}\cdot(y-1)^{k(A)+|A|-|V(G)|},
$$
where $k(S)$ denotes the number of connected components of the spanning subgraph $(V(G),S)$. It simultaneously encodes many classical invariants — the chromatic polynomial, the flow polynomial, the number of spanning trees — and coincides, up to a change of variables, with the partition function of the $q$-state Potts model of statistical mechanics for $q=(x-1)(y-1)$, the central object in the study of exactly solved lattice models. Jaeger, Vertigan and Welsh (1990) completely classified the computational complexity of exactly evaluating $T_G$ at every fixed pair of complex coordinates; over rational coordinates, the evaluation is polynomial-time computable only on the hyperbola $(x-1)(y-1)=1$ and at the four exceptional points $(1,1)$, $(-1,-1)$, $(0,-1)$, $(-1,0)$, and #P-hard at every other rational point, where #P is the counting analogue of NP.

This record concerns a parameterized analogue introduced by Roth, Schmitt and Wellnitz (ICALP 2021). In parameterized complexity, a problem is fixed-parameter tractable (FPT) with respect to a parameter $k$ if it is solvable in time $f(k)\cdot|G|^{O(1)}$ for a computable function $f$; the counting analogue of NP-hardness is #W[1]-hardness, and conditional running-time lower bounds are usually stated under the Exponential Time Hypothesis (ETH), which asserts that 3-SAT requires $2^{\Omega(n)}$ time. Writing $\binom{E(G)}{k}$ for the family of edge subsets of size exactly $k$, the parameterized Tutte polynomial is
$$
T^k_G(x,y)=\sum_{A\in\binom{E(G)}{k}}(x-1)^{k(A)-k(E(G))}\cdot(y-1)^{k(A)+k-|V(G)|},
$$
with input $(G,k)$ and parameter $k$; it is the classical sum restricted to $k$-edge subsets, i.e., a weighted count of small subgraph patterns. Many classical interpretations survive the restriction: $T^k_G(2,1)$ equals the number of $k$-edge forests of $G$; $T^k_G(1-c,0)$ for integer $c$ determines the number of pairs $(A,\sigma)$ where $A$ is a $k$-edge subset and $\sigma$ is a proper $c$-coloring of $(V(G),A)$; $T^k_G(2,0)$ determines the number of pairs $(A,\eta)$ with $\eta$ an acyclic orientation of $(V(G),A)$, and the number of $k$-edge subsets whose induced graph has even Betti number (the rank of its cycle space); $T^k_G(0,2)$ determines the number of $k$-edge subsets spanning an even number of connected components.

Roth, Schmitt and Wellnitz completely classified exact computation over the rational points of the Tutte plane: computing $T^k_G(x,y)$ is polynomial-time computable if $(x,y)=(1,1)$ or $(x-1)(y-1)=1$, fixed-parameter tractable but #P-hard on the line $x=1$ ($y\neq1$), and #W[1]-hard at every other rational point. The hardness proofs express $T^k_G(x,y)$ as a linear combination of graph homomorphism counts (counts of edge-preserving vertex maps between graphs) and apply the complexity-monotonicity framework of Curticapean, Dell and Marx (STOC 2017), with coefficients controlled through the symmetry groups of toroidal grids (grid graphs with wrapped-around boundaries, i.e., Cartesian products of cycles) and Cayley graph expanders of 2-groups (sparse, highly connected graphs constructed from group multiplication tables).

For randomized approximation, the relevant notion is a fixed-parameter tractable randomized approximation scheme (FPTRAS), going back to Arvind and Raman (2002): a randomized algorithm that, on input $(G,k,\varepsilon,\delta)$, outputs in time $f(k)\cdot\mathrm{poly}(|G|,\varepsilon^{-1},\log(1/\delta))$ a value within a factor $(1\pm\varepsilon)$ of the true answer with probability at least $1-\delta$; if $f$ is a polynomial, the scheme is an FPRAS, the parameterized analogue of the classical fully polynomial randomized approximation scheme. Since $T^k_G(x,y)$ can be negative, the source paper uses the convention that the algorithm outputs a pair $(z,s)$ approximating the absolute value and returning the correct sign. The main positive result of the source paper states that $T^k_G(x,y)$ admits an FPTRAS whenever $0\le(x-1)(y-1)\le1$, and even an FPRAS if additionally $x\neq1$ or $y=1$; the proof reduces to the multivariate basis-generating polynomial of the graphic matroid (the matroid whose independent sets are the forests of $G$, so that its bases are the spanning trees), which is log-concave precisely in this region — its logarithm is a concave function on the positive orthant, the property that enables sampling — and applies the FPRAS of Anari, Liu, Oveis Gharan and Vinzant (STOC 2019) for counting matroid bases via high-dimensional random walks. The source paper emphasizes that while the classification of exact computation is complete, 'the complexity of approximation remains open outside of the coloured area' — that is, at every rational point with $(x-1)(y-1)<0$ or $(x-1)(y-1)>1$.

This gap is not an artifact of an overlooked classical result. Classical FPRASs for the unrestricted Tutte polynomial are known only for special graph classes — e.g., dense graphs (Alon, Frieze and Welsh 1995; extended to subdense graphs by Hauptmann and Tiling 2022) — and such results say nothing about the cardinality-restricted sum defining $T^k_G$, in which exponentially many weighted terms of vastly different magnitudes (and, for $(x-1)(y-1)<0$, mixed signs) must be combined; conversely, the #W[1]-hardness of exact computation does not by itself rule out multiplicative approximation, since no general transfer from exact hardness to parameterized approximation hardness is known.

## Problem Statement

For every pair of rational numbers $(x,y)$ with $(x-1)(y-1)\notin[0,1]$ — equivalently, with $(x-1)(y-1)<0$ or $(x-1)(y-1)>1$ — determine the parameterized approximation complexity of computing
$$
T^k_G(x,y)=\sum_{A\in\binom{E(G)}{k}}(x-1)^{k(A)-k(E(G))}\cdot(y-1)^{k(A)+k-|V(G)|}
$$
on input $(G,k)$ with parameter $k$, where $k(S)$ denotes the number of connected components of the spanning subgraph $(V(G),S)$ and $\binom{E(G)}{k}$ denotes the family of $k$-element subsets of the edge set of $G$. Approximation is understood in the sign-plus-magnitude sense of Roth, Schmitt and Wellnitz, since $T^k_G(x,y)$ can be negative: the algorithm outputs a pair $(z,s)$ with $z$ within a factor $(1\pm\varepsilon)$ of $|T^k_G(x,y)|$ and $s$ the correct sign, with probability at least $1-\delta$, in time $f(k)\cdot\mathrm{poly}(|G|,\varepsilon^{-1},\log(1/\delta))$; such a scheme is an FPTRAS, and an FPRAS if $f$ is a polynomial. For each such point, either construct an FPTRAS (and, where possible, an FPRAS), or prove that no such scheme exists under a standard hypothesis of parameterized counting complexity (for example, $\mathsf{W[1]}\neq\mathsf{FPT}$ under randomized parameterized reductions, or the randomized Exponential Time Hypothesis). The completion boundary is a dichotomy for the rational parameterized Tutte plane matching the completeness of the known exact classification: an explicit partition of $\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{Q}^2:(x-1)(y-1)\notin[0,1]\}$ into points that admit an FPTRAS or FPRAS (with the algorithms and their correctness proofs) and points at which approximation is provably hard. The classification may be presented point-wise or region-wise, but completing any single point or listed subregion is a partial result that does not by itself satisfy the completion boundary; the following subregions are natural targets within the required partition: the all-positive-weight sector $\{x>1, y>1, (x-1)(y-1)>1\}$ (e.g., the point $(2,3)$), the mixed-sign sector with $(x-1)(y-1)<0$ (e.g., $(0,2)$ and $(2,0)$, which determine parity-type counts of $k$-edge subsets), and the points $(1-c,0)$ for integers $c\ge2$ (coloring-type counts of $k$-edge subsets).
