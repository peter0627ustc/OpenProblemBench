# ORB-PHYS-76: Deletion–contraction recurrences for local flows and tensions on non-orientable surfaces

For a graph cellularly embedded in an orientable surface, the packaged surface Tutte polynomial of Moffatt and Thompson admits deletion–contraction relations for every edge type, a Universality Property, and, as consequences, the first recursive computation of the numbers of nowhere-identity local flows and local tensions taking values in any finite group. The extension of this whole framework to non-orientable surfaces was stated as open in that work. Thompson's 2024 PhD thesis subsequently resolved the polynomial-level part: an Euler-genus version of the packaged surface Tutte polynomial is defined for non-orientable packaged ribbon graphs and satisfies deletion–contraction relations, duality, and universality without orientability hypotheses. What remains open is the analytic core: the signed-genus grading of the Goodall–Litjens–Regts–Vena surface Tutte polynomial — the invariant that enumerates local flows and tensions on non-orientable surfaces — is incompatible with recursive relations, and no deletion–contraction recurrences for these counts are known at the level of the embedded graphs, nor for any non-abelian group (for abelian groups the counts depend only on the underlying signed graph, for which signed-graph-level recurrences already exist). The problem is to find recursive deletion–contraction-type relations, with initial conditions, that compute the numbers of nowhere-identity local flows and local tensions of graphs cellularly embedded in non-orientable surfaces, for every finite group.

## Background

The Tutte polynomial of a graph can be defined recursively: for an edge $e$ that is neither a bridge nor a loop, $T(G)=T(G\setminus e)+T(G/e)$, with separate rules for bridges and loops and base values on edgeless graphs. It is universal for graph parameters satisfying such deletion–contraction relations, and two of its classical evaluations count nowhere-zero group flows and proper vertex colourings (tensions) — the property for which Tutte originally introduced it as the dichromate.

When a graph is cellularly embedded in a surface (each face homeomorphic to an open disc), the natural analogues of flows and colourings are local flows and local tensions. A convenient combinatorial encoding of a cellular embedding is a ribbon graph: a surface with boundary assembled from vertex discs and edge discs glued along arcs, orientable or non-orientable according to the underlying surface. Directing the edge-ends (half-edges), a local $\Gamma$-flow, for a finite (possibly non-abelian) group $\Gamma$, is an assignment of group elements to the directed half-edges — the two half-edges of an edge receiving mutually inverse values — such that the product around each vertex, taken in the cyclic order induced by the embedding, is the identity; a local $\Gamma$-tension is the dual condition, with the product around each boundary component (face) equal to the identity. The cyclic order makes both notions well-defined even for non-abelian groups, and a flow or tension is nowhere-identity (also called nowhere-zero) when no edge receives the identity element.

Goodall, Krajewski, Regts and Vena constructed a surface Tutte polynomial for maps — graphs cellularly embedded in orientable surfaces — whose evaluations give, for every finite group $\Gamma$, the number $q_\Gamma(G)$ of nowhere-identity local $\Gamma$-flows and the number $p_\Gamma(G)$ of nowhere-identity local $\Gamma$-tensions; Goodall, Litjens, Regts and Vena extended it to non-orientable surfaces. This polynomial is defined by a state sum in which each connected component of a spanning subgraph contributes a monomial graded by its genus; in the non-orientable version the grading is by signed genus, equal to $+g$ on orientable components of genus $g$ and $-g$ on non-orientable ones. Unlike the classical Tutte polynomial, the surface Tutte polynomial admits no deletion–contraction relations for all edge types, and hence no full recursive definition or universality property; Goodall et al. explicitly asked what form such a recurrence could take. For abelian groups $\Gamma$ these counts depend only on the underlying signed graph of the embedding — the graph with each edge marked twisted or untwisted — and deletion–contraction recurrences at the signed-graph level are known; for non-abelian groups, whose counts genuinely involve the cyclic order at vertices, the signed-graph framework does not apply.

Moffatt and Thompson resolved the orientable case by enlarging the domain. A packaged ribbon graph is a ribbon graph together with a partition of its vertex set and a partition of its set of boundary components, each block carrying a non-negative integer weight; these objects describe graphs embedded in pseudo-surfaces (surfaces with finitely many pinch points) with vertex and face weights, building on the pseudo-surface and coloured-ribbon-graph theory of Huggett and Moffatt. For orientable packaged ribbon graphs they defined the packaged surface Tutte polynomial and proved: deletion–contraction relations valid for every edge type; a Universality Property, meaning every invariant of packaged ribbon graphs satisfying the deletion–contraction relations and the edgeless initial conditions is a specialization of it; specialization identities recovering the surface Tutte polynomial and the canonical Tutte polynomials of graphs in pseudo-surfaces (hence the Las Vergnas, Bollobás–Riordan and Krushkal polynomials); and, as consequences, the first deletion–contraction recurrences for $q_\Gamma$ and $p_\Gamma$ on orientable surfaces. Their development carries the standing convention that every ribbon graph is orientable, and they record that the extension of the polynomial, its deletion–contraction relations, universality, and the resulting recurrences for local flows and tensions to non-orientable surfaces is left open, with the non-orientable analysis to appear in the second author's PhD thesis.

That thesis (Thompson, Royal Holloway, 2024) settled the polynomial-level part: regrading the packaged surface Tutte polynomial by Euler genus $\gamma=2k-v+e-b$ (which, unlike orientable genus, is defined for non-orientable ribbon graphs) produces a polynomial of arbitrary, possibly non-orientable, packaged ribbon graphs that satisfies deletion–contraction relations for all edge types, duality, and a Universality Property — results on which Thompson's later published quasi-tree expansion relies. The specialization back to the surface Tutte polynomial is proved only for orientable ribbon graphs, however, and the flow-and-tension section of the thesis explicitly assumes orientability, with the remark that although flows and tensions in non-orientable ribbon graphs are understood in the literature, it is non-trivial to obtain recursive relations for them, so the non-orientable case is excluded entirely. The recorded obstruction is that the signed-genus grading that carries the non-orientable flow and tension counts is not compatible with recursive relations. What survives is precisely the non-orientable recurrence problem for the counts themselves, for every finite group; the abelian case admits recurrences through the signed-graph quotient, but these neither extend to non-abelian groups nor operate on the embedded graphs with their genus grading.

## Problem Statement

Find recursive relations of deletion–contraction type, valid for every edge type of a graph cellularly embedded in a non-orientable surface (including non-orientable, i.e. twisted, loops) and with explicit initial conditions on edgeless graphs, that compute the number $q_\Gamma(G)$ of nowhere-identity local $\Gamma$-flows and the number $p_\Gamma(G)$ of nowhere-identity local $\Gamma$-tensions, for every finite group $\Gamma$ — the non-orientable counterpart of the recurrences Moffatt and Thompson established for orientable surfaces. The quantities computed must agree with the existing enumerations of Goodall–Litjens–Regts–Vena. This is the surviving component of the open problem stated in that source work — extending the packaged surface Tutte polynomial, its deletion–contraction relations, universality, and the resulting recurrences for local flows and tensions to non-orientable surfaces — the first three components having since been resolved in Thompson's thesis in Euler-genus form. The scope is every finite group, non-abelian groups included: for abelian groups the counts factor through the underlying signed graph of the embedding, for which deletion–contraction recurrences are already known, so an answer restricted to abelian groups, or operating only on the signed-graph quotient rather than on embedded graphs, does not meet the target. A natural route, not the only admissible one, is to construct a packaging-type extension of the surface Tutte polynomial that retains the orientability information carried by the signed genus while admitting deletion–contraction relations for all edge types, together with the specialization identities recovering the local flow and tension counts; an answer that instead proves the signed-genus polynomial itself admits no full recurrence must still exhibit a recursive scheme of some form that computes $q_\Gamma$ and $p_\Gamma$ on non-orientable surfaces.

The verification contract below evaluates answers to this statement. It does not narrow or redefine the research question.

Known solving difficulties:

- The signed-genus grading that carries the non-orientable flow and tension counts is recorded as incompatible with deletion–contraction, so the orientable construction cannot be transposed directly; a new framework must retain orientability information while remaining recursive.
- Edge types proliferate in the non-orientable setting: twisted (non-orientable) loops change the topology of contractions in ways ordinary loops do not, and the packaging data — partitions of vertices and boundary components with weights — must be extended or restructured to track orientation behaviour.
- The counting connection passes through representation theory of finite groups (formulas involving the dimensions of irreducible representations), which must be reconciled with a topological grading that distinguishes orientable from non-orientable components.
- It is unclear a priori whether any invariant that specializes to the signed-genus surface Tutte polynomial admits a full deletion–contraction recurrence; the solver may need to identify an appropriate weakening or reformulation before the recurrences can even be stated.
- The abelian case is already computable through the underlying signed graph, where deletion–contraction recurrences exist; the difficulty is therefore concentrated in the non-abelian case — where Frobenius–Schur indicators distinguishing orientable from non-orientable components enter the counting formulas — and in any construction that operates on the embedded graphs with their genus grading.

## Current Progress

- Status: `ready`

Source fidelity verified against the primary source: Moffatt and Thompson (Eur. J. Comb. 118 (2024) 103933; arXiv:2212.12371) develop the packaged surface Tutte polynomial for orientable packaged ribbon graphs with full deletion–contraction relations (Thm. 3.3), a Universality Property (Thm. 3.6), specializations to the surface Tutte polynomial and the canonical Tutte polynomials of graphs in pseudo-surfaces (Thm. 4.3), and deletion–contraction recurrences for the numbers of nowhere-identity local flows and tensions (Thms. 5.5–5.6). All results carry the standing convention that every ribbon graph is orientable; the acknowledgements state the second author's PhD thesis will include additional analysis of the non-orientable case. The candidate formulation (LKM open-question node gcn_38619f0587eb4ee0) is faithful: the source contains no literal “remains open” sentence, its statement being a consolidation of the orientable-only convention plus that acknowledgement, but the four components it lists accurately mirror the paper's main result groups, and attribution is correct.

The pre-existing non-orientable ingredient: Goodall, Litjens, Regts and Vena (Eur. J. Comb. 86 (2020) 103095; arXiv:1804.01496) extended the surface Tutte polynomial to non-orientable maps using a signed genus (negative on non-orientable components) and proved its evaluations give the numbers of nowhere-identity local flows and local tensions for every finite group. Their open-problems section explicitly asks what form a deletion–contraction recurrence for this polynomial takes and how many edge types it involves; no such recurrence is given there, nor in the earlier orientable paper (Combin. Probab. Comput. 27 (2018) 913–945).

Partial resolution by the announced thesis, inspected in full: Thompson's PhD thesis (Royal Holloway, 2024) regrades the packaged surface Tutte polynomial by Euler genus, giving a polynomial defined for arbitrary — including non-orientable — packaged ribbon graphs (Def. 4.2.1, with a worked non-orientable example), deletion–contraction relations for every edge type including non-orientable loops (Thm. 4.2.4), duality (Thm. 4.2.6), and a Universality Property (Thm. 4.2.7), all stated without orientability hypotheses. This resolves the first three of the four components of the source problem for non-orientable surfaces, and the resolution is publicly relied upon: Thompson's quasi-tree expansion (Adv. Appl. Math. 175 (2026) 103045) states its deletion–contraction theorem with attribution to both the EJC paper (orientable case) and the thesis (non-orientable case), and uses the thesis's Euler-genus definition.

The surviving core is explicitly left open by the same thesis: Section 4.3, which derives the recurrences for local flows and tensions, assumes all ribbon graphs are orientable and remarks that there are results in the literature concerning flows and tensions in non-orientable ribbon graphs, but it is non-trivial to obtain recursive relations for them, so the non-orientable case is excluded entirely. The specialization of the Euler-genus packaged polynomial to the surface Tutte polynomial is proved only for orientable ribbon graphs (Thm. 4.2.8), and Thompson's 2026 paper records the obstruction: the signed genus of the non-orientable surface Tutte polynomial is not compatible with recursive relations. No recursive relations for the non-orientable flow and tension counts are known at the level of maps (ribbon graphs) or for any non-abelian group; the abelian case is addressed only through the signed-graph quotient, as recorded in the next item.

Adjacent partial result, located and inspected at review (the “forthcoming paper” the research audit could not find): the paper announced in Goodall–Litjens–Regts–Vena 2020 appeared as Goodall–Litjens–Regts–Vena, “Tutte's dichromate for signed graphs” (Discrete Appl. Math. 289 (2021) 153–184; arXiv:1903.07548), building on DeVos–Rollová–Šámal, “A Note on Counting Flows in Signed Graphs” (Electron. J. Comb. 26(2) (2019) P2.38). These establish a five-edge-type deletion–contraction recurrence for the trivariate Tutte polynomial of signed graphs and explicit deletion–contraction recurrences for the nowhere-zero flow and tension counts of signed graphs — for abelian groups only. By Remark 4.12 of the 2020 non-orientable paper, local G-flows of a signed graph correspond to local G-flows of a map with that underlying signed graph, and for abelian G the map-level counts are evaluations of a signed-graph invariant; so for abelian groups the non-orientable counts are recursively computable through the signed-graph quotient. This does not resolve the stated problem: the dichromate paper explicitly restricts to abelian groups (noting the technical difficulties that general finite groups bring), the signed-graph notion of local flow is developed only for abelian groups, and no recurrences are known on the embedded graphs themselves with their genus grading, or for any non-abelian group. The open core — deletion–contraction recurrences computing $q_\Gamma$ and $p_\Gamma$ on non-orientable surfaces for every finite group $\Gamma$ — stands.

Coverage and sweep: forward citations of the source paper (the irreducibility paper of Ellis-Monaghan–Goodall–Moffatt–Noble–Vena, Moffatt's introductory survey “Constructing a Tutte polynomial for graphs embedded in surfaces”, arXiv:2502.15318, and Thompson's quasi-tree paper) and of Goodall–Litjens–Regts–Vena (including Garijo–Goodall–Vena on map homomorphisms) were checked; none addresses recursive relations for local flows or tensions on non-orientable surfaces; this was re-verified at review time, the only additional forward citation of the quasi-tree paper being a 2026 hypergraph Tutte polynomial paper (off-core). The adjacent existence-theory thread on nowhere-zero local tensions on non-orientable surfaces (Bouchet's conjecture via bidirected graphs) concerns bounds rather than enumeration recurrences. The subfield literature on this line is compact (essentially the Royal Holloway group and the Goodall–Regts–Vena collaboration) and both outputs, including the previously unlocated dichromate paper, were checked.

## Scientific Significance

Affected-field significance: `medium`.

Solving the problem would directly complete the deletion–contraction theory of topological Tutte polynomials: it would give the first recursive computation of local flow and tension counts on non-orientable surfaces valid for every finite group and operating on the embedded graphs themselves — for abelian groups the counts are already recursively computable through the underlying signed graph, but for non-abelian groups they are currently obtainable only from state sums or inclusion–exclusion formulas — and the first framework connecting the signed-genus surface Tutte polynomial of Goodall–Litjens–Regts–Vena to full deletion–contraction relations and a universality property, extending the packaged-surface-Tutte programme (Eur. J. Comb. 2024, Adv. Appl. Math. 2026) to its non-orientable completion. The impact is direct but confined to topological graph theory and adjacent areas (delta-matroids, map enumeration, medial-graph and knot-graph methods); it would not by itself change the core knowledge or methods of the wider discipline.
