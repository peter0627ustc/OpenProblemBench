# ORB-PHYS-82: Integrality of singular values of the generalized lambda function $\Lambda_k=W_{[k,2,1]}$ in the excluded regime $\delta=\gcd(k,N)>1$ with $3\mid\delta$ or $N/\delta$ a prime power

The generalized lambda function $\Lambda_k=W_{[k,2,1]}$ is the ratio of differences of Weierstrass $\wp$-values $\big(\wp(k/N;\tau)-\wp(1/N;\tau)\big)/\big(\wp(2/N;\tau)-\wp(1/N;\tau)\big)$, a modular function for the congruence subgroup $\Gamma_1(N)$ introduced in Lang's framework. Ishii (2011 preprint, published as Ramanujan J. 2014) proved that $\Lambda_k$ together with the modular invariant $j$ generates the modular function field of $\Gamma_1(N)$, so that CM values of $\Lambda_k$ generate ray class fields, and proved that, writing $\delta=\gcd(k,N)$, whenever $\delta=1$, or $\delta>1$ with $3\nmid\delta$ and $N/\delta$ not a prime power, every $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$-transform of $\Lambda_k$ has $q$-expansion over $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_N]$ and every CM value $\Lambda_k(\alpha)$ is an algebraic integer. We audit the complementary regime, $\delta>1$ with $3\mid\delta$ or $N/\delta$ a prime power, where the status of both the $q$-expansion integrality and the algebraic integrality of the CM values is unresolved. A systematic search of the 2011-2026 literature (arXiv, zbMATH, Crossref, and forward/backward citation graphs) found no resolution: the only works engaging this family are the author's own follow-ups, which treat the analogous principal-congruence function (where prime-power levels provably produce non-integral values), and Shin-Yoon (2024), which addresses a different field-generation question. The problem is recorded as open.

## Background

A central theme of explicit class field theory for imaginary quadratic fields is to produce, in the spirit of Hilbert's twelfth problem, concrete modular functions whose special values generate the abelian extensions of such a field $K$. If $\alpha$ lies in the complex upper half plane $\mathbb{H}$ and $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)=K$ is imaginary quadratic, $\alpha$ is called an imaginary quadratic point or CM point; the classical singular moduli $j(\alpha)$ generate the Hilbert class field (the maximal unramified abelian extension) of $K$. Refinements with level structure generate ray class fields, the abelian extensions classified by class field theory with a prescribed modulus, and are sought as class invariants: generators with small height that are algebraic integers, so that their minimal polynomials over $\mathbb{Z}$ (class polynomials) have integer coefficients.

Fix an integer $N\ge 6$ and let
$$\Gamma_1(N)=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})\ :\ a\equiv 1,\ c\equiv 0 \pmod{N}\right\},$$
a congruence subgroup of $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$; denote by $\mathcal{A}_1(N)$ its field of modular functions (meromorphic functions on $\mathbb{H}$ invariant under $\Gamma_1(N)$ with compatible behavior at the cusps). Write $L_\tau=\mathbb{Z}+\mathbb{Z}\tau$ for the lattice generated by $1$ and $\tau$, and $\wp(z;L)$ for the Weierstrass $\wp$-function of a lattice $L$. In his book Elliptic Functions, Lang introduced the generalized lambda functions: for a triple $a=[a_1,a_2,a_3]$ of distinct integers with $0<a_i\le N/2$,
$$W_a(\tau)=\frac{\wp(a_1/N;L_\tau)-\wp(a_3/N;L_\tau)}{\wp(a_2/N;L_\tau)-\wp(a_3/N;L_\tau)},$$
a family generalizing the classical elliptic modular lambda function (the squared elliptic modulus parametrizing elliptic curves with a basis of $2$-torsion, itself the corresponding ratio of $\wp$-differences at the half-periods). Lang proposed investigating the special values of these functions at imaginary quadratic points, in particular whether they generate ray class fields. The function studied in the audited source is $\Lambda_k:=W_{[k,2,1]}$ (note that the related display $\big(\wp(k\tau/N;\tau)-\wp(\tau/N;\tau)\big)/\big(\wp(2\tau/N;\tau)-\wp(\tau/N;\tau)\big)$, which appears in Corollary 3.3 of the preprint and Corollary 4.6 of the published version, equals $\Lambda_k\circ S$ for $S=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$ by the transformation of $\wp$ under the homothety $L_{-1/\tau}=(-1/\tau)L_\tau$, not $\Lambda_k$ itself); the surrounding theory of modular units is relevant here: a modular unit is a modular function with no zeros or poles on $\mathbb{H}$, and for congruence subgroups such units are generated by Siegel functions, explicit $q$-products built from the Dedekind eta function.

Put $\zeta=e^{2\pi i/N}$ and $q=e^{2\pi i\tau/N}$. The standard route from $q$-expansion arithmetic to integrality of special values is the modular equation argument: if $f$ is a modular function for $\Gamma_1(N)$ with no poles on $\mathbb{H}$ and every transform $f\circ A$ ($A\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$) has Laurent expansion $f\circ A\in\mathbb{Z}[\zeta]((q))$ (a formal Laurent series in $q$ with coefficients in the ring of cyclotomic integers $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta]$), then the product $\Phi(X,j)=\prod_{A}(X-f\circ A)$ over coset representatives of $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})/\Gamma_1(N)\{\pm I\}$ is a monic polynomial with coefficients in $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta][j]$; Galois invariance gives $\Phi\in\mathbb{Z}[j][X]$, so $f$ is integral over $\mathbb{Z}[j]$, and since $j(\alpha)$ is an algebraic integer at every CM point $\alpha$ (a classical fact of complex multiplication), $f(\alpha)$ is an algebraic integer.

The lineage of results is as follows. Ishida and Ishii built the function-field machinery of $\mathcal{A}_1(N)$; Hong and Koo obtained ray class field generators from singular values of $\Gamma_1(N)$-modular functions; Ishii and Kobayashi proved that $W_a$ is integral over $\mathbb{Z}[j]$ (with algebraic-integer CM values) under a strong coprimality condition on the triple, essentially $a_1a_2a_3(a_1\pm a_3)(a_2\pm a_3)$ prime to $N$. The audited source, Ishii's preprint arXiv:1110.4429 and its published version of record (Ramanujan J. 33 (2014) 121-130), proves that $\Lambda_{k,\ell}=W_{[k,\ell,1]}$ generates $\mathcal{A}_1(N)$ over $\mathbb{C}(j)$ for all $1<\ell\ne k<N/2$, and that consequently $\Lambda_k(\alpha)$ together with $\zeta$ generates the ray class field of $K=\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$ modulo $N$ over its Hilbert class field whenever $\mathbb{Z}[\alpha]$ is the maximal order (via the Gee-Stevenhagen form of Shimura reciprocity, which computes the Galois action on such special values). For the integrality statements, with $\delta=\gcd(k,N)$, the hypothesis is: (i) $\delta=1$, or (ii) $\delta>1$, $(\delta,3)=1$ and $N/\delta$ is not a power of a prime. Under it, $\Lambda_k\circ A\in\mathbb{Z}[\zeta]((q))$ for every $A\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$, $\Lambda_k$ is integral over $\mathbb{Z}[j]$, and all CM values of all transforms are algebraic integers. Where the hypothesis bites: the leading coefficient of $\Lambda_k\circ A$ is a product of ratios of the shape $(1-\zeta^a)/(1-\zeta^b)$, whose integrality is governed by the cyclotomic-unit lemma that $1-\zeta^k$ is a unit of $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta]$ exactly when $N/\gcd(k,N)$ is not a prime power (used in the branch $c\equiv 0\pmod N$ of the transform computation), while the branch ${2c}={c}$, possible only when $3\mid N$, introduces a denominator $1-\zeta^{3d}$ and thereby the condition $(\delta,3)=1$.

Evidence from a sibling setting points to genuine arithmetic sensitivity of exactly the excluded regimes: for the principal-congruence analogue $\Lambda(\tau;Q_1,Q_2)$ (the same ratio of $\wp$-differences at points $Q_1,Q_2,Q_1+Q_2$ with $\{Q_1,Q_2\}$ a basis of $(\mathbb{Z}/N)^2$), Ishii proved that $C_N\Lambda$ is integral over $\mathbb{Z}[j]$ with $C_N=1$ precisely when $N$ is not a prime power ($C_N=p^2$ for $p=2,3$, $C_N=p$ for $p>3$ otherwise), sharpened this to $(1-\zeta)^3\Lambda(\alpha)$ and $(1-\zeta)^3/\Lambda(\alpha)$ being algebraic integers at all CM points $\alpha$, proved $\Lambda(\alpha)$ is a unit when $N$ is not a prime power, and exhibited explicit CM values that are not algebraic integers when $N$ is a prime power (norm $3^{-3}$ at $N=3$, norm $1/2$ at $N=4$). Those results, however, are proved for a different group and a different function and do not transfer to $\Lambda_k=W_{[k,2,1]}$.

## Problem Statement

Let $N\ge 6$, let $k$ be an integer with $2<k<N/2$, put $\delta=\gcd(k,N)$, and let
$$\Lambda_k(\tau)=W_{[k,2,1]}(\tau)=\frac{\wp(k/N;L_\tau)-\wp(1/N;L_\tau)}{\wp(2/N;L_\tau)-\wp(1/N;L_\tau)},$$
where $L_\tau=\mathbb{Z}+\mathbb{Z}\tau$ is the lattice generated by $1$ and $\tau$, $\wp(z;L)$ denotes the Weierstrass $\wp$-function of a lattice $L$, $\zeta=e^{2\pi i/N}$, $q=e^{2\pi i\tau/N}$, $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta]((q))$ is the ring of formal Laurent series in $q$ with coefficients in the cyclotomic integer ring $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta]$, $j$ denotes the classical elliptic modular invariant, and an imaginary quadratic point is an $\alpha$ in the complex upper half plane $\mathbb{H}$ such that $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$ is an imaginary quadratic field. It is known that if $\delta=1$, or if $\delta>1$ with $(\delta,3)=1$ and $N/\delta$ not a power of a prime, then $\Lambda_k\circ A\in\mathbb{Z}[\zeta]((q))$ for every $A\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$, $\Lambda_k$ is integral over $\mathbb{Z}[j]$, and $\Lambda_k(\alpha)$ is an algebraic integer for every imaginary quadratic point $\alpha$. Determine the complementary regime, namely $\delta>1$ with $3\mid\delta$ or $N/\delta$ a power of a prime:

(a) for which such pairs $(k,N)$ does $\Lambda_k\circ A\in\mathbb{Z}[\zeta]((q))$ hold for every $A\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$;

(b) for which such pairs $(k,N)$ is $\Lambda_k$ integral over $\mathbb{Z}[j]$, and in particular $\Lambda_k(\alpha)$ an algebraic integer at every imaginary quadratic point $\alpha$.

The two prongs are linked parts of one objective, completing the integrality picture of the family: (a) implies (b) through the modular equation argument, while failure of (a) at some transform does not by itself decide (b), since denominators may cancel in the Galois-invariant product $\prod_A(X-\Lambda_k\circ A)$. A complete answer either proves that (a) and (b) hold throughout the regime, or establishes exactly which pairs $(k,N)$ fail, with explicit witnesses for the failing pairs (an imaginary quadratic point $\alpha$ with $\Lambda_k(\alpha)$ not an algebraic integer, or a transform $A$ with $\Lambda_k\circ A\notin\mathbb{Z}[\zeta]((q))$) together with the corresponding statements for the remaining pairs.

The verification contract below evaluates answers to this statement. It does not narrow or redefine the research question.

Known solving difficulties:

- The obstruction is cyclotomic-unit arithmetic at primes dividing $N$: the leading coefficient of the transform $\Lambda_k\circ A$ is a product of ratios $(1-\zeta^a)/(1-\zeta^b)$ whose integrality depends on the prime-power structure of $N/\delta$ (through the unit lemma that $1-\zeta^k$ is a unit of $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta]$ exactly when $N/\gcd(k,N)$ is not a prime power) in the branch $c\equiv 0\pmod N$, and on divisibility by $3$ in the branch ${2c}={c}$ (possible only when $3\mid N$); the elementary unit arguments of the source stop working in both branches, and whether integrality of the leading coefficient actually fails, and for which $A$, must be decided case by case.
- The two prongs can diverge: non-integrality of individual transforms $\Lambda_k\circ A$ does not by itself yield a non-integral CM value, because denominators can cancel in the Galois-invariant modular equation $\Phi(X,j)=\prod_A(X-\Lambda_k\circ A)$; a positive resolution of the CM-value prong therefore likely requires a route other than termwise $q$-expansion inspection, for instance expressing $\Lambda_k$ as a product of Siegel functions with controlled exponents and applying the integrality theory of such modular units.
- A negative resolution requires explicit CM witnesses: computing $\Lambda_k(\alpha)$ inside ray class fields whose degree over the Hilbert class field grows with $N$ (the relevant coset index is of order $N^3$) and certifying non-integrality by norm or valuation computations, as the source author did for the principal-congruence analogue at $N=3$ and $N=4$; the required class-polynomial computations grow quickly with the level.
- The two excluded branches ($3\mid\delta$, and $N/\delta$ a prime power) arise from different steps of the transform computation and may have different answers, so a complete resolution is plausibly a classification over the pairs $(k,N)$ rather than a uniform statement.

## Current Progress

Origin and prior results. Lang's Elliptic Functions (Ch. 18, section 6) introduced the generalized lambda functions $W_a$ and posed investigating their special values at imaginary quadratic points and whether these generate ray class fields. Ishida and Ishii (Acta Arith. 101, 2002) obtained generators and defining equations for the modular function field of $\Gamma_1(N)$, the machinery on which the later work rests; Hong and Koo (Ramanujan J. 16, 2008) had earlier produced ray class field generators from singular values of $\Gamma_1(N)$-modular functions built from Klein forms. Ishii and Kobayashi (Ramanujan J. 24, 2011) proved that $W_a$ is integral over $\mathbb{Z}[j]$, and its CM values algebraic integers, under a strong coprimality condition on the triple, essentially $a_1a_2a_3(a_1\pm a_3)(a_2\pm a_3)$ prime to $N$.

The source and its published counterpart. The question is motivated by Ishii's preprint arXiv:1110.4429 (2011), which appears to be unpublished; its content appears in published form as arXiv:1110.6489, Ramanujan J. 33 (2014) 121-130, in a slightly more general framework ($\Lambda_{k,\ell}=W_{[k,\ell,1]}$). Both versions prove: (1) unconditionally, $\Lambda_{k,\ell}$ generates $\mathcal{A}_1(N)$ over $\mathbb{C}(j)$ for $1<\ell\ne k<N/2$, and $\Lambda_{k,2}(\alpha)$ with $\zeta$ generates the ray class field of $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$ modulo $N$ over the Hilbert class field when $\mathbb{Z}[\alpha]$ is maximal (via Gee-Stevenhagen); (2) under $\delta=\gcd(k,N)$ satisfying (i) $\delta=1$ or (ii) $\delta>1$, $(\delta,3)=1$, $N/\delta$ not a prime power, that $\Lambda_{k,2}\circ A\in\mathbb{Z}[\zeta]((q))$ for all $A\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$, that $\Lambda_{k,2}$ is integral over $\mathbb{Z}[j]$, and that all CM values of all transforms are algebraic integers (published numbering: Proposition 4.2, Theorem 4.4, Theorem 4.5, Corollary 4.6; preprint numbering: Proposition 3.1, Theorem 3.2, Corollary 3.3). Neither version addresses the complementary regime ($\delta>1$ with $3\mid\delta$ or $N/\delta$ a prime power), and neither states a conjecture about it.

The author's own follow-ups treat a sibling problem, not this one. Ishii's Tokyo J. Math. 37 (2014) paper and the preprint arXiv:1504.05272 (2015) study the principal-congruence analogue $\Lambda(\tau;Q_1,Q_2)$ (the same shape of $\wp$-ratio at a basis of $(\mathbb{Z}/N)^2$, for $\Gamma(N)$ rather than $\Gamma_1(N)$): $C_N\Lambda$ is integral over $\mathbb{Z}[j]$ with $C_N=1$ exactly when $N$ is not a prime power ($C_N=p^2$ for $p=2,3$, $C_N=p$ for $p>3$); $(1-\zeta)^3\Lambda(\alpha)$ and $(1-\zeta)^3/\Lambda(\alpha)$ are algebraic integers at all CM points; $\Lambda(\alpha)$ is a unit when $N$ is not a prime power, with norm equal to $\pm$ a power of $p$ when $N=p^e$; and integrality genuinely fails at some CM points when $N$ is a prime power (explicit values with norm $3^{-3}$ at $N=3$ and norm $1/2$ at $N=4$). These results show the prime-power exclusion reflects a real arithmetic obstruction in the principal-congruence setting and suggest Ishii's hypothesis is close to optimal, but they do not settle, and do not transfer to, the $\Gamma_1(N)$ function $\Lambda_k=W_{[k,2,1]}$ in the excluded regime.

The only later non-author work citing this circle is Shin and Yoon (JMAA 530, 2024), which addresses a different question in the same setting: whether a single generator $h$ of an intermediate modular function field has $h(z_K)$ generating the ray class field $K_{(N)}$ (their Claim A). They give counterexamples to the unrestricted claim and prove it for all but finitely many imaginary quadratic fields $K$, describing the exceptions via affine plane curves. This concerns field generation, not the integrality of $\Lambda_k$ or of its transforms in the excluded parameter regime, and it leaves that regime untouched.

No resolution of either part in the complementary parameter regime was identified at the August 2026 curation cutoff.

## Scientific Significance

Affected-field significance: `low`.

Solving the problem determines whether the generalized-lambda generators $\Lambda_k(\alpha)$ can serve as integral class invariants (monic minimal polynomials with integer coefficients) for the ray class fields of imaginary quadratic fields at the levels $N$ and indices $k$ excluded by Ishii's hypothesis, and settles whether that hypothesis is optimal for this family; the closely related principal-congruence results of the same author show the answer is likely a delicate classification. The direct impact is confined to this specific family of explicit class-field generators and to class-invariant computations at the affected levels within explicit class field theory for imaginary quadratic fields; the bearing on the broader program of generating ray class fields by special values of modular functions and modular units is indirect.
