# ORB-MATH-24: The Weinstein conjecture for closed contact manifolds of dimension at least five

The Weinstein conjecture predicts that every Reeb vector field on a closed contact manifold has at least one closed periodic orbit. It is a theorem in dimension three (Taubes, 2007) but remains open in full generality in dimensions $2n-1\ge 5$. The audited source, Acu and Moreno's 'Planarity in higher-dimensional contact manifolds', proved the conjecture for iterated-planar (IP) contact manifolds — those supported by open books whose pages admit iterated planar Lefschetz fibrations — and posed the problem of settling further broad classes of higher-dimensional contact manifolds and of extending the open-book/holomorphic-curve framework. This audit verified the source against the primary literature and traced later work (2018–2026). The conjecture is now additionally known for manifolds of finite planarity in the rational-symplectic-field-theory hierarchy of Moreno–Zhou (which recovers the IP theorem), for all non-orderable contact manifolds together with several newly discovered non-orderable families (via Albers–Fuchs–Merry, Hedicke–Shelukhin, Arlove), for quasiconformal Reeb fields (Finamore), and sufficient conditions follow from higher-dimensional Heegaard Floer homology (Colin–Honda–Tian); Moreno–Zhou moreover prove that non-iterated-planar contact structures exist in every fillable almost-contact homotopy class in dimensions at least five, so IP-type constructions cannot settle the general case. The only claimed general proof (Oh, 2024, via contact instantons) was withdrawn in September 2025. The general higher-dimensional Weinstein conjecture remains open.

## Background

A contact manifold is a closed (compact, without boundary) odd-dimensional manifold $M^{2n-1}$ together with a hyperplane field $\xi=\ker\alpha$ defined by a 1-form $\alpha$ satisfying the maximal non-integrability condition $\alpha\wedge(d\alpha)^{n-1}\neq 0$; $\alpha$ is called a contact form. Each contact form determines a unique Reeb vector field $R_\alpha$ through the equations $\iota_{R_\alpha}d\alpha=0$ and $\alpha(R_\alpha)=1$, and the Weinstein conjecture (Weinstein, 1979) asserts that on a compact contact manifold the flow of $R_\alpha$ has at least one closed periodic orbit, for every contact form defining the given contact structure. The conjecture grew out of Rabinowitz's work on periodic orbits of Hamiltonian systems: energy hypersurfaces of contact type in symplectic manifolds carry natural Reeb flows, so the conjecture predicts periodic dynamics on all such hypersurfaces.

In dimension three the conjecture is a theorem: Taubes (2007) proved it for all closed contact 3-manifolds using the Seiberg–Witten equations. In higher dimensions only partial results were classically known: Viterbo (1987) settled compact contact-type hypersurfaces of Euclidean space $\mathbb{R}^{2n}$; Floer–Hofer–Viterbo (1990) settled certain products $P\times\mathbb{C}^l$; Hofer–Viterbo (1992) settled cases where the symplectization contains enough holomorphic spheres; and Albers–Hofer (2009) settled contact structures containing a plastikstufe, a higher-dimensional analogue of an overtwisted disk (an explicit family of Legendrian disks through a common singular point whose presence forces flexible, non-rigid behavior). Relatedly, Dörner–Geiges–Zehmisch (2014) obtained contractible periodic Reeb orbits for contact structures supported by open books whose binding is of restricted contact type in a subcritical Stein manifold, and Suhr–Zehmisch (2017) established the strong Weinstein conjecture — existence of a null-homologous link of finitely many closed orbits — for concave boundaries of cobordisms carrying an essential foliation by holomorphic spheres.

A central tool in higher dimensions is the open book decomposition: a codimension-two binding $B\subset M$ together with a fibration of $M\setminus B$ over $S^1$ whose pages are the closures of the fibers. A fundamental correspondence asserts that every closed contact manifold is supported by some open book with Liouville pages (pages carrying an exact symplectic form whose primitive restricts to a contact form on the boundary, which is the binding). Abbas–Cieliebak–Hofer (2005) proved the Weinstein conjecture for planar contact 3-manifolds, i.e. those supported by open books with genus-zero pages. The audited source, Acu–Moreno ('Planarity in higher-dimensional contact manifolds', arXiv 2018, published in Int. Math. Res. Not. 2022), generalized this to higher dimensions: a page is iterated planar if it admits an iterated planar Lefschetz fibration — an exact symplectic Lefschetz fibration (a map to the disk with finitely many isolated critical points modeled on complex singularities) whose generic fiber is itself the total space of a lower-dimensional such fibration, the bottom layer having genus-zero (planar) fibers. Their Theorem C establishes that every iterated-planar (IP) contact manifold satisfies the Weinstein conjecture, via a symplectic handle attachment that produces a moduli space of holomorphic spheres, regularized using polyfold technology (an analytic framework for achieving transversality in moduli spaces of holomorphic curves). Their paper states explicitly that in higher dimensions the conjecture 'is still open' beyond the known partial results, and asks which further broad classes can be settled and whether the open-book/holomorphic-curve framework extends to them.

## Problem Statement

Let $(M,\xi)$ be a closed contact manifold of dimension $2n-1$ with $n\ge 3$, i.e. of dimension at least five. Does every contact form $\alpha$ with $\ker\alpha=\xi$ have at least one closed periodic Reeb orbit? Equivalently: prove, or refute by an explicit closed contact manifold of dimension at least five together with a contact form whose Reeb flow has no periodic orbit, the Weinstein conjecture in the dimensions in which it remains unresolved. This is the famous Weinstein conjecture restricted to the open dimension range (dimension three having been settled by Taubes). Results that settle further classes of manifolds — such as the iterated-planar, plastikstufe-containing, non-orderable, finite-planarity, or quasiconformal classes — constitute partial progress toward this problem, and the extension of open-book and holomorphic-curve methods to broader classes is the associated research program, but only a proof or refutation of the general statement in dimensions $2n-1\ge 5$ answers the question.

The verification contract below evaluates answers to this statement. It does not narrow or redefine the research question.

Known solving difficulties:

- The three-dimensional proof rests on Seiberg–Witten gauge theory tied to four-manifold topology; no comparable gauge-theoretic package exists in dimensions at least five, where higher-dimensional Heegaard Floer homology is still being developed and has so far yielded only sufficient conditions (Colin–Honda–Tian).
- Holomorphic-curve methods in symplectizations and cobordisms face transversality and compactness obstacles in general higher-dimensional settings; the virtual or polyfold regularization needed for the full rational symplectic field theory hierarchy is only partially constructed (Moreno–Zhou build and use only a partial construction).
- Open-book techniques depend on the geometry of the page: Moreno–Zhou prove that in every fillable almost-contact homotopy class in dimensions at least five there exist contact structures that are not iterated planar, so IP-style handle constructions provably cannot cover all contact structures, and genuinely new geometric input is required for the general case.
- A counterexample would require a closed contact manifold with a completely aperiodic Reeb flow; all known rigidity phenomena of contact topology appear to force at least one closed orbit, and no candidate mechanism for aperiodicity on a closed contact manifold is currently known.
- The statement is uniform over all contact forms for a fixed contact structure and over all contact structures, so any proof must control arbitrary degeneracies of the dynamics (e.g. non-generic contact forms) or introduce a new invariant that uniformly detects closed orbits without genericity assumptions.

## Current Progress

The primary source is arXiv:1810.11448v2, published in Int. Math. Res. Not. 2022, no. 6, 4222–4258. Bahar Acu and Agustin Moreno prove (Theorem C) that iterated-planar (IP) contact manifolds satisfy the Weinstein conjecture, as a higher-dimensional generalization of the planar three-dimensional theorem of Abbas–Cieliebak–Hofer, and their introduction states that in higher dimensions the conjecture 'is still open' beyond the partial results of Viterbo (1987), Floer–Hofer–Viterbo (1990), Hofer–Viterbo (1992), and Albers–Hofer (2009). Albers–Hofer's plastikstufe theorem states that the existence of a plastikstufe implies the Weinstein conjecture for every supporting contact form. No conflation with adjacent results was found; the underlying problem is the classical Weinstein conjecture in the dimensions where it remains unresolved.

Class extensions since 2018: Moreno–Zhou ('A landscape of contact manifolds via rational SFT', arXiv:2012.04182, published as Geometry & Topology 29 (2025), no. 7, 3465–3565, DOI 10.2140/gt.2025.29.3465) define a planarity functor on the exact symplectic cobordism category via rational symplectic field theory and prove (Corollary D) that finite planarity implies the Weinstein conjecture, recovering the IP theorem as a special case; they also prove (Corollary E) that in all dimensions at least five, every almost-contact homotopy class admitting an exactly fillable representative contains a contact structure that is not iterated planar, and that counterexamples to the conjecture, if any, must lie in the top complexity level of their hierarchy. This directly answers part of the source's methodological question: IP-style open-book constructions provably cannot reach all contact structures.

Orderability route: Albers–Fuchs–Merry (Compositio Mathematica 151 (2015), no. 12, 2251–2272, DOI 10.1112/S0010437X15007642) proved that a closed contact manifold whose contactomorphism identity component is non-orderable (admits a positive loop of contactomorphisms) satisfies the Weinstein conjecture, with a contractible closed orbit. Hedicke–Shelukhin (arXiv:2411.19887, 2024/2025) produced many new non-orderable families — contact boundaries of subcritical Weinstein domains (including the standard $S^1\times S^2$), open books whose page skeleton is a smooth closed loose Legendrian, and open books with displaceable skeletons — and deduce (their Corollary 4.6) that every contact form on these manifolds has a contractible closed Reeb orbit. Arlove (arXiv:2509.12856, 2025) generalized the Albers–Fuchs–Merry theorem using spectral selectors on strongly orderable contact manifolds.

Other sufficient conditions: Colin–Honda–Tian (arXiv:2006.05701, 2020) developed higher-dimensional Heegaard Floer homology and derived from the contact class a sufficient condition for the Weinstein conjecture in higher dimensions, the first input into the conjecture from the Heegaard Floer side beyond dimension three. Finamore (arXiv:2301.01738, 2023) proved the conjecture for quasiconformal Reeb fields (Reeb fields whose contact action has globally bounded eccentricity), with a lower bound on the number of closed orbits.

No resolution: the only located claimed general proof is Yong-Geun Oh's 'Contact instantons and Proofs of Weinstein's conjecture and Arnold's chord conjecture' (arXiv:2412.20731, December 2024), which claimed the conjecture in full generality via the moduli theory of contact instantons; version 2 (17 September 2025) was withdrawn by the author, and no replacement or accepted proof has appeared as of August 2026. Literature from 2024–2025 uniformly describes the higher-dimensional Weinstein conjecture as open or widely open.

Adjacent but distinct questions, which do not affect the open core: multiplicity results (lower bounds on the number of orbits under non-degeneracy or dynamical convexity assumptions), the strong Weinstein conjecture (Suhr–Zehmisch, Advances in Mathematics 305 (2017), 1250–1267, DOI 10.1016/j.aim.2016.06.030, for cobordisms with holomorphic-sphere foliations; Remark 1.1 of the source for IP manifolds), generalized, foliated, and singular ($b^m$-contact) versions of the conjecture, and closed-orbit counts for Reeb fields on Sasakian manifolds are separate problems; none of them resolves or refutes the classical conjecture in dimensions at least five.

## Scientific Significance

Affected-field significance: `high`.

The Weinstein conjecture is one of the central open problems of contact and symplectic topology, directly linking contact rigidity to periodic dynamics. A proof in dimensions at least five would establish that every closed contact manifold forces periodic motion, directly converting the field's core existence question into a theorem and validating the holomorphic-curve, Floer-theoretic, and orderability-based frameworks as complete instruments; it would also directly yield periodic-orbit statements for all contact-type energy hypersurfaces in Hamiltonian dynamics. A counterexample would be equally foundational, showing that contact rigidity does not force recurrence and reshaping the surrounding programs (symplectic field theory, Reeb dynamics, Hamiltonian periodic-orbit theory). Either outcome changes the field's core knowledge directly, not merely incrementally.
