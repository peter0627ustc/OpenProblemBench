# ORB-PHYS-71: Iterated binary fusion versus single m-fold fusion for families of modules over the enlarged periodic Temperley–Lieb algebra

Ikhlef and Morin-Duchesne (arXiv:2509.11756; published as SciPost Phys. Core 9, 053 (2026)) constructed a fusion product $\times_f$ on the category of families of modules over the enlarged periodic Temperley–Lieb algebra $\mathcal{E}PTL_N(\beta)$ — the algebra of connectivity diagrams on an annulus that governs two-dimensional loop models — and proved it is a bifunctor that is distributive, commutative and associative, with associativity established for three families through an explicit triple-fusion presentation (their Theorem 4, the $m=3$ case). They leave open, as an explicit conjecture, the generalization to any number $m \ge 4$ of input families: that the iterated two-fold fusion $(((\mathsf{M}_1 \times_f \mathsf{M}_2) \times_f \mathsf{M}_3) \times_f \cdots) \times_f \mathsf{M}_m$ is isomorphic to the single $m$-fold fusion family whose modules are $\sum \mathcal{L}(N,\widehat{N}_m)\cdot(\mathsf{M}_1(N_1)\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathsf{M}_m(N_m))$ subject to the generator-entry relations (5.2a)–(5.2c) of their Section 5. The problem is the lattice analogue of order-independence of the operator product expansion for the nonlocal connectivity operators of loop models. The full task includes the well-definedness of the $m$-fold presentation and, along the authors' suggested route, the diagrammatics of connectivity diagrams on discs with an arbitrary number of holes. A literature audit (arXiv, Semantic Scholar, INSPIRE, Crossref, through 2026-08-31) found no resolution: the published version still states the conjecture, the two citing papers address other questions, and the authors' announced follow-up on $\mathsf{W}_{k,x}$ decompositions has not appeared.

## Background

The Temperley–Lieb (TL) algebra $\mathrm{TL}_N(\beta)$ is the associative algebra generated by $e_1,\dots,e_{N-1}$ with relations $e_i^2=\beta e_i$, $e_i e_{i+1} e_i=e_i$ and $e_i e_j=e_j e_i$ for $|i-j|>1$; its basis elements can be drawn as non-crossing pairings of $N$ boundary points, where $\beta$ is the weight of a closed loop. It is the common symmetry algebra of the dense $O(n)$ loop model, the XXZ spin-$\frac{1}{2}$ chain (an anisotropic Heisenberg quantum spin chain) and the RSOS (restricted solid-on-solid) lattice models, and its representation theory controls the spectra of their transfer matrices. On a periodic lattice one needs the periodic TL algebra, whose diagrams live on an annulus and include the rotation $\Omega$ of the annulus by one site. Ikhlef and Morin-Duchesne work with the enlarged periodic Temperley–Lieb algebra $\mathcal{E}PTL_N(\beta)$, generated by $\Omega^{\pm 1}$ and $e_0,\dots,e_{N-1}$ (indices modulo $N$) with the TL relations plus $\Omega e_j \Omega^{-1}=e_{j-1}$ and $\Omega^2 e_1=e_{N-1}e_{N-2}\cdots e_1$. Its elements are connectivity diagrams on an annulus, and for each pair of sizes there is a diagram space $\mathcal{L}(N,N')$ of connectivities between $N$ nodes on the outer boundary and $N'$ nodes on the inner boundary: algebraically the words in generators $c_j$ and $c^{\dagger}_j$ that respectively remove and add pairs of adjacent boundary nodes, subject to TL-type relations, and diagrammatically the annular connectivities; Theorem 1 of the source paper proves these two definitions equivalent.

A family of modules is a sequence $\mathsf{M}=\{\mathsf{M}(N)\mid N=N_0,N_0+2,\dots\}$ of modules over $\mathcal{E}PTL_N(\beta)$ for all sizes $N$ of a fixed parity $N_0\in\{0,1\}$, together with compatible actions of the diagram spaces $\mathcal{L}(N',N)$ on the modules. The paradigmatic examples are the standard modules $\mathsf{W}_{k,x}(N)$ of Graham and Lehrer: finite-dimensional modules spanned by link states, drawn on a disc with a marked point at its center, consisting of $N/2-k$ arches pairing boundary nodes and $2k$ defects (lines connecting the marked point to the boundary), with a twist parameter $x\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}$ that weights the winding of the defects around the marked point (for $k=0$, each non-contractible loop around the marked point is replaced by the factor $x+x^{-1}$). In the dense $O(n)$ loop model, the vectors of configuration probabilities on a closed contour around an inserted operator live in such a module, and every connectivity operator is naturally associated with one of them. Families also arise from the XXZ spin chain and the RSOS models, and from quotient families such as quotients of the standard modules.

In loop models, the observables of interest are connectivity operators — nonlocal operators measuring whether given points of the lattice lie on the same loop. Their correlation functions are governed by an algebraic fusion of the associated modules that is the lattice analogue of the operator product expansion (OPE) of conformal field theory. Ikhlef and Morin-Duchesne introduced this fusion in two earlier papers: first for standard modules, using link states with two marked points, which produced the OPE structure of connectivity operators of the dense $O(n)$ model; then for irreducible and quotient modules — determining the fusion rules of $\mathsf{W}\times\mathsf{W}$ and constructing the products $\mathsf{W}\times\mathsf{R}$, $\mathsf{W}\times\mathsf{Q}$ and $\mathsf{Q}\times\mathsf{Q}$, where $\mathsf{R}_{\ell,y}$ and $\mathsf{Q}_{\ell,y}$ are the radical submodule and the quotient of a reducible standard module — the fusions involving the quotient $\mathsf{Q}$ turning out to obey very simple rules reminiscent of the fusion rules of $\mathfrak{sl}(2)$ (the Lie algebra of traceless $2\times2$ matrices). Both constructions were tied to specific module classes.

The source paper defines, for the first time, a fusion product on the category of all families of $\mathcal{E}PTL_N(\beta)$-modules. For two families $\mathsf{M}_a,\mathsf{M}_b$ it sets
$$
(\mathsf{M}_a \times_f \mathsf{M}_b)(N) = \sum_{N_a,N_b} \mathcal{L}(N,N_{ab})\cdot\big(\mathsf{M}_a(N_a)\otimes\mathsf{M}_b(N_b)\big), \qquad N_{ab}=N_a+N_b,
$$
a quotient of the direct sum by local relations that prescribe how each generator $c_j$, $c^{\dagger}_j$ and $\Omega$ enters the tensor factor on which it naturally acts. Diagrammatically, fused states are connectivity diagrams drawn on a disc with two holes, one hole carrying each input family — exactly the intermediate states that arise in lattice correlation functions. The authors prove that $\times_f$ is a bifunctor (a construction defined both on pairs of objects and on pairs of homomorphisms, respecting composition) that is distributive over direct sums, commutative, and associative, with the vacuum family $\mathsf{V}$ — whose modules $\mathsf{V}(N)$ are spanned by link states with no marked point — as neutral element. The associativity proof goes through a triple fusion product with the explicit presentation (4.12)–(4.13): for $N_{abc}=N_a+N_b+N_c$,
$$
(\mathsf{M}_a \times_f \mathsf{M}_b \times_f \mathsf{M}_c)(N) = \sum_{N_a,N_b,N_c} \mathcal{L}(N,N_{abc})\cdot\big(\mathsf{M}_a(N_a)\otimes\mathsf{M}_b(N_b)\otimes\mathsf{M}_c(N_c)\big)
$$
with seven types of generator-entry relations, and Theorem 4 establishes $(\mathsf{M}_a\times_f\mathsf{M}_b)\times_f\mathsf{M}_c \simeq \mathsf{M}_a\times_f\mathsf{M}_b\times_f\mathsf{M}_c \simeq \mathsf{M}_a\times_f(\mathsf{M}_b\times_f\mathsf{M}_c)$, where two families are isomorphic when their modules are isomorphic at every admissible size. A noteworthy subtlety recorded in the paper: the presentation induced by iterating the two-fold relations involves ten types of relations, while the direct three-fold presentation needs only seven, so the isomorphism identifies genuinely different presentations and is not a formal triviality.

The paper closes by conjecturing the analogous statement for an arbitrary number of input families — the open problem stated below — and by announcing, as the authors' own work in preparation (not part of the conjecture), applications of the fusion functor to explicit decompositions of the families $\mathsf{W}_{k,x}$ and their quotients, and to lattice connectivity correlation functions.

## Problem Statement

Prove or refute the following conjecture of Ikhlef and Morin-Duchesne (arXiv:2509.11756, Section 5). Let $m\ge 4$ and let $\mathsf{M}_1,\dots,\mathsf{M}_m$ be arbitrary families of modules over the enlarged periodic Temperley–Lieb algebra $\mathcal{E}PTL_N(\beta)$, for any $\beta\in\mathbb{C}$. For admissible sizes $N_i$ write $\widehat{N}_i=\sum_{k=1}^{i}N_k$ for the partial sums ($\widehat{N}_0=0$). Define the $m$-fold fusion family by
$$
(\mathsf{M}_1 \times_f \mathsf{M}_2 \times_f \cdots \times_f \mathsf{M}_m)(N) = \sum_{N_1,\dots,N_m} \mathcal{L}(N,\widehat{N}_m)\cdot\big(\mathsf{M}_1(N_1)\otimes\mathsf{M}_2(N_2)\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathsf{M}_m(N_m)\big),
$$
where the sum runs over all admissible $N_1,\dots,N_m$, the action of $\mathcal{L}(N',N)$ is by left multiplication, and the quotient is taken subject to the generator-entry relations, for all $u_i\in\mathsf{M}_i(N_i)$:
$$
c_{j+\widehat{N}_{i-1}}\cdot(u_1\otimes\cdots\otimes u_m) = u_1\otimes\cdots\otimes c_j u_i\otimes\cdots\otimes u_m, \qquad 1\le j\le N_i-1,\; 1\le i\le m,
$$
$$
c^{\dagger}_{j+\widehat{N}_{i-1}}\cdot(u_1\otimes\cdots\otimes u_m) = u_1\otimes\cdots\otimes c^{\dagger}_j u_i\otimes\cdots\otimes u_m, \qquad 1\le j\le N_i+1,\; 1\le i\le m,
$$
$$
\Omega\cdot(u_1\otimes\cdots\otimes u_m) = c_{\widehat{N}_{m-1}}\cdots c_{\widehat{N}_2}c_{\widehat{N}_1}\cdot\big(\Omega u_1\otimes c^{\dagger}_0 u_2\otimes c^{\dagger}_0 u_3\otimes\cdots\otimes c^{\dagger}_0 u_m\big), \qquad N_1>0.
$$
The conjecture asserts that (i) this presentation is well defined — the relations are consistent and the resulting quotients form a family of modules over the diagram spaces — and (ii) the iterated two-fold fusion
$$
\big(\big(\big((\mathsf{M}_1 \times_f \mathsf{M}_2) \times_f \mathsf{M}_3\big) \times_f \mathsf{M}_4\big) \times_f \cdots\big) \times_f \mathsf{M}_m
$$
is isomorphic to it as a family of modules, i.e. the modules are isomorphic at every admissible size $N$. (The source states the conjecture as the isomorphism (ii), taking the $m$-fold presentation as given; part (i) makes that presentation explicit and is the $m$-fold analogue of Proposition 4.1, which establishes the corresponding trifunctor for $m=3$.) The case $m=3$ is Theorem 4 of the source paper and is not part of the open problem. The authors' suggested route is to extend the diagrammatic framework to connectivity diagrams on discs with an arbitrary number of holes, including the proof that the algebraic and diagrammatic definitions of these diagram spaces are equivalent (generalizing their Theorems 1–3), but a proof by any rigorous route — diagrammatic, inductive, or categorical — is acceptable, as is a refutation by explicit counterexample.

The verification contract below evaluates answers to this statement. It does not narrow or redefine the research question.

Known solving difficulties:

- The $m=3$ proof rests on explicit bases for the three-holed diagram spaces (Appendix A of the source) and on hand-constructed families of homomorphisms (Section 4.3); generalizing to arbitrary $m$ requires new combinatorial basis arguments in which both the number of relation types and the interplay of the shifted indices $\widehat{N}_i$ grow with $m$.
- The isomorphism is not a formal consequence of associativity: the source paper itself observes that the iterated presentation involves ten types of relations while the direct three-fold presentation needs only seven, so identifying which induced relations are redundant — and proving well-definedness of the $m$-fold quotient — is genuine work that must be redone at every order.
- An inductive strategy on $m$, combining Theorem 4 with the $(m-1)$-fold result, must reconcile the relations induced by the last binary fusion step with the direct relations above; no off-the-shelf coherence theorem applies, because the fusion product is associative only up to non-canonical isomorphism and the conjecture concerns an explicit presentation, not just existence of an associator.
- No genericity or semisimplicity assumption is available: the statement is for all $\beta\in\mathbb{C}$, and the fused families can be infinite-dimensional (the source notes that fusing two XXZ families already yields infinite-dimensional modules), so finite-dimensional counting arguments cannot settle the general claim.

## Current Progress

- Status: `ready`

Source fidelity verified against the primary source. I downloaded and read the full text of arXiv:2509.11756 v3 (posted 2026-06-23) and confirmed through Crossref that this is the version published as SciPost Phys. Core 9, 053 on 2026-08-27. Section 4 (Theorem 4, together with Proposition 4.1 and relations (4.12)–(4.13)) proves the $m=3$ case: the triple fusion presentation is well defined and $(\mathsf{M}_a\times_f\mathsf{M}_b)\times_f\mathsf{M}_c \simeq \mathsf{M}_a\times_f\mathsf{M}_b\times_f\mathsf{M}_c \simeq \mathsf{M}_a\times_f(\mathsf{M}_b\times_f\mathsf{M}_c)$. Section 5 then poses, explicitly as a conjecture, the same statement for $m>2$ input families with the explicit $m$-fold presentation (5.1) and relations (5.2a)–(5.2c). The LKM record reproduces this faithfully; one benign nuance is that the paper writes 'm > 2' while the record writes 'm \ge 4' — since $m=3$ is Theorem 4, the genuinely open cases are $m\ge 4$, so the record's reading is correct. The record's additional mention of forthcoming work on $\mathsf{W}_{k,x}$ decompositions and connectivity correlators corresponds to the authors' own announced project ('work in preparation'), which is context rather than part of the conjecture.

Lineage. The conjecture completes a program: the 2022 companion paper introduced fusion of standard periodic-TL modules via link states with two marked points and used it to derive the OPE structure of connectivity operators of the dense $O(n)$ loop model; the 2024 companion paper computed the fusion rules of irreducible and quotient modules ($\mathsf{W}\times\mathsf{W}$, $\mathsf{W}\times\mathsf{R}$, $\mathsf{W}\times\mathsf{Q}$, $\mathsf{Q}\times\mathsf{Q}$), the fusions involving the quotient modules $\mathsf{Q}$ giving very simple rules reminiscent of $\mathfrak{sl}(2)$ fusion. Both were restricted to specific module classes. The 2025/2026 bifunctor paper defines the product on arbitrary families of modules, proves the bifunctor properties and the $m=3$ associativity, and poses the $m$-fold generalization as its explicit open problem — in the published, peer-reviewed version of August 2026.

Citation sweep. Semantic Scholar lists exactly two papers citing the preprint (Crossref lists zero for the published version, which appeared four days before this audit). (a) Ikhlef, arXiv:2606.31313 (2026), cites the bifunctor paper only to motivate the fusion rules of simple TL modules (his Eq. (4.37)) in a study of non-invertible symmetries and modular invariance; the $m$-fold conjecture is not addressed. (b) Jacobsen, Nivesvivat, Ribault and Roux, SciPost Phys. 20, 125 (2026), cite the fusion product as an alternative way to construct states in $\mathsf{W}_{r_3,s_3}$ from two modules and leave understanding its relation to their morphism as an open direction; the $m$-fold conjecture is not addressed. A third paper using the framework, Ikhlef–Morin-Duchesne arXiv:2602.15742 (2026) on TL modules for critical ADE models, cites the bifunctor paper for the study of families of modules but does not touch the conjecture.

Coverage and residual uncertainty. INSPIRE keyword searches ('multiply fused', multi-hole TL, 'families of modules' with TL) returned nothing relevant. The arXiv export API was unreachable from this audit environment, so citation coverage relies on Semantic Scholar, Crossref and INSPIRE author sweeps; a resolution posted only days before this audit and not yet indexed could in principle be missed, but the published version itself (2026-08-27) still states the conjecture, so the open core is nonempty and precise: for arbitrary $m\ge 4$ and arbitrary families of $\mathcal{E}PTL_N(\beta)$-modules (any $\beta\in\mathbb{C}$), the isomorphism between the iterated binary fusion and the explicit $m$-fold presentation (5.1)–(5.2) remains unproven in the literature.

## Scientific Significance

Affected-field significance: `medium`.

A resolution would complete the fusion formalism for the periodic Temperley–Lieb algebra at arbitrary order: it would establish that the $m$-fold fusion of families of modules admits the explicit presentation (5.1)–(5.2) independently of the order of the binary fusion steps, which is the lattice analogue of a coherent, order-independent operator product expansion for the nonlocal connectivity operators of two-dimensional loop models. The impact is direct within Temperley–Lieb representation theory and loop-model integrability: it supplies exactly the algebraic ingredient that the authors identify as needed for their announced program of decomposing fused standard families $\mathsf{W}_{k,x}$ and computing multi-point connectivity correlation functions, and it removes the need for case-by-case verification whenever an application requires more than three fused modules. It does not, by itself, change core methods or knowledge outside this program.
