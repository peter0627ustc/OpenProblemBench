# ORB-PHYS-65: Analytic evaluation of the square-lattice k-coloring entropy W(k) beyond the exactly solved k=3 ice point: higher-cycle algebra, non-Eulerian subgraphs for k>3, and finite-width Toeplitz transfer-matrix recursions

The number of proper k-colorings of the square lattice — equivalently, the close-packing entropy of a lattice gas of k hard-square particle species with infinite repulsion between same-species nearest neighbors, or of a generalized vertex model with $M_k=(k-1)^3-(k-1)^2+(k-1)$ allowed vertex configurations — grows as $[W(k)]^N$ on $N$ sites. $W(3)=(4/3)^{3/2}$ is exact (Lieb's square-ice constant), and for $k\geq 4$ the values are already pinned numerically by convergent large-q series ($W(4)\approx 2.33606$), but no closed-form analytic evaluation exists for any $k\geq 4$. Singh and Jain (2018) recast $W(k)$ as a vertex-model transfer-matrix problem and initiated a Nagle-type subgraph expansion in which elementary Eulerian cycles contribute $(k-2)(k-1)^n/M_k^n$ in closed form, while non-Eulerian subgraphs, whose contributions cancel identically at $k=3$ by Nagle's theorem, contribute nonzero terms $(k-2)-2(k-2)^2-(k-2)^3+2$ for $k>3$. The open problem is the analytic (closed-form or provably convergent) evaluation of $W(k)$ for general $k$: closed-form contributions of complicated cycles and their lattice multiplicities; systematic incorporation or resummation of the non-Eulerian contributions for $k>3$ into a convergent expansion; closed-form finite-width Toeplitz-block trace recursions beyond transverse size $m=4$; and controlled (rigorous) extrapolation of transfer-matrix eigenvalue estimates to $W(k)$.

## Background

The residual entropy of ice (the entropy that remains at zero temperature because many atomic arrangements are compatible with the same ground state) was estimated by Pauling (1935) by treating local bond arrangements as independent, giving $W\approx 3/2$ per molecule; Lieb (1967) computed the exact value $W(3)=(4/3)^{3/2}\approx 1.5396$ for the two-dimensional analogue, square ice, by solving the six-vertex model — the vertex model on the square lattice in which each of the four edges at a vertex carries an arrow and only the six 'ice-rule' configurations with two arrows in and two out are allowed, each with unit weight. Lenard (as recounted in Lieb's paper) observed that, up to a factor of three, counting proper 3-colorings of the square lattice (coloring the faces with colors $1,2,3$ so that adjacent faces differ) is the same problem: orienting every edge from color $i$ to color $i+1$ (mod 3) turns a proper 3-coloring into an ice-rule configuration. Baxter (1970) solved the three-coloring model with arbitrary color activities exactly, formulating it as a hard-squares model (a lattice gas whose particles each forbid occupation of the adjacent sites).

For general $k$, the number $C_N(k)$ of proper k-colorings of an $N$-site square lattice — the chromatic polynomial $P(G,k)$ of the lattice graph, i.e. the zero-temperature partition function of the k-state Potts antiferromagnet (the spin model whose interaction energy penalizes equal colors on adjacent sites) — grows as $[W(k)]^N$. Physically, $W(k)$ is the close-packing entropy of a lattice gas of $k$ particle species with infinite repulsion between same-species nearest neighbors: each species occupies an independent set (a set of sites no two of which are nearest neighbors), and close packing forces a proper coloring of every site. Singh and Jain (2018) map the k-coloring problem to a generalized vertex model: put a state $e\in\mathbb{Z}_k\setminus\{0\}$ on each edge, defined as the difference of the two adjacent face colors modulo $k$, so that (i) the two states of any edge, read from its two ends, sum to $0$ mod $k$, and (ii) the four edge states around any vertex sum to $0$ mod $k$. Exactly $M_k=(k-1)^3-(k-1)^2+(k-1)$ vertex configurations satisfy these rules ($k=3$: the six-vertex model; $k=4$: a 21-vertex model), and each admissible edge-state configuration corresponds to exactly $k$ colorings, a factor irrelevant per site in the thermodynamic limit.

Nagle (1966, 1971) developed a subgraph expansion for such entropy constants: writing each vertex weight relative to its mean-field value, the correction terms to the Pauling estimate are indexed by subgraphs of the lattice, and at $k=3$ all 'non-Eulerian' subgraphs — those containing a vertex of odd degree 1 or 3 in the subgraph — cancel identically, leaving only even-degree ('Eulerian') subgraphs whose lowest members are elementary cycles. The generalized Pauling estimate reads $W(k)\approx M_k/(k-1)^2=k-2+1/(k-1)$, with $W(k)\sim k$ at large $k$. Singh and Jain generalized the expansion to arbitrary $k$: an elementary Eulerian cycle with $n$ vertices contributes $(k-2)(k-1)^n/M_k^n$ in closed form (at $k=3$, $n=4$ this reproduces Nagle's series term $1/3^4$), so the corrected estimate $W(k)\approx(M_k/(k-1)^2)(1+(k-2)(k-1)^4/M_k^4)$ already tracks the known values for $k=4,\dots,7$ well. However, the three-edge non-Eulerian vertex contribution sums to $(k-2)-2(k-2)^2-(k-2)^3+2$, which vanishes at $k=3$ (Nagle's result) and is nonzero for every $k>3$: the cancellation mechanism that makes the ice expansion tractable fails, and no systematic treatment of these terms exists.

On the numerical side, $W(k)$ for $k\geq 4$ has long been pinned: Bakaev and Kabanovich (1994) and subsequently Jacobsen (2010) — building on the transfer-matrix and chromatic-zero (zeros of the chromatic polynomial in the complex plane) studies of Salas and Sokal (2001) — computed the large-q series of the bulk free energy of the square-lattice chromatic polynomial to order $z^{79}$ in $z=1/(k-1)$, with radius of convergence $|z|=1/2$ (the boundary $k=3$ is the edge of the square-lattice chromatic critical region $0\leq q\leq 3$). The series therefore converges for all real $k\geq 4$ and gives, e.g., $W(4)\approx 2.33606$, $W(5)\approx 3.25040$. These coefficients are exact integers obtained by the finite-lattice method (exact partition functions of finite rectangles combined with fixed weights); they are not closed-form algebraic expressions organized per subgraph class, and they yield no exact value of $W(k)$ for any $k\geq 4$. On the triangular lattice, by contrast, Baxter obtained analytical expressions for the bulk chromatic free energy (verified term by term against the series by Jacobsen 2010); the square lattice beyond the ice point remains analytically unsolved, and the $k\geq 4$ vertex models (such as the 21-vertex model at $k=4$) are not known to be integrable (exactly solvable by analytic means, as the six-vertex model is).

For the transfer-matrix program of the source paper: with open (non-periodic) boundary conditions along the rows, the transfer matrices possess a block Toeplitz structure (block matrices constant along diagonals, with blocks $A_{n,p}$ indexed modulo $k$, of dimension $(k-1)^p$ for general $k$). At $k=3$ this yields closed-form trace identities $\mathrm{Tr}[A_p^n]=2^p$ and $\mathrm{Tr}[A_p^nA_p^{T}]=(n+2)^p$, and closed-form partition functions for cylindrical lattices (periodic boundary conditions in one direction) of transverse size $m\leq 4$: $Z_{2\times p}=2(2^p+3^p)$, $Z_{3\times p}=2^{p+1}+6\cdot 4^p$, and $Z_{4\times p}$ governed by the eigenvalues $(7\pm\sqrt{33})/2$. Numerically, $[\lambda_{\max}(B_p)]^{1/p}$ — where $B_p=A_p+A_p^{T}$ is the transfer matrix of a strip of transverse size $p$ — at $k=3$ for $p\leq 10$ extrapolates to $1.53967$, versus Lieb's exact $1.5396007$.

## Problem Statement

Let $W(k)=\lim_{N\to\infty}C_N(k)^{1/N}$, where $C_N(k)$ is the number of proper k-colorings of the faces of an $N$-site square lattice, i.e. assignments of the $k$ colors to the faces so that adjacent faces differ (coloring the sites instead of the faces gives the same $W(k)$, since the square lattice is self-dual). Equivalently, $W(k)$ is the exponential growth constant of the chromatic polynomial of the square lattice, or the close-packing entropy of the k-species hard-square lattice gas / generalized vertex model with $M_k=(k-1)^3-(k-1)^2+(k-1)$ vertex configurations. $W(3)=(4/3)^{3/2}$ is exact and the numerical values of $W(k)$ for $k\geq 4$ are pinned by convergent large-q series. The problem is the analytic — closed-form or provably convergent — evaluation of $W(k)$ for general $k$ beyond the exactly solved $k=3$ case, along the concrete routes identified by Singh and Jain (2018), all serving this single objective:

(1) Cycle algebra. In the subgraph expansion of $W(k)$, in which the generalized Pauling estimate $M_k/(k-1)^2$ is corrected by subgraph terms and elementary Eulerian cycles of $n$ vertices contribute the closed form $(k-2)(k-1)^n/M_k^n$, derive the general algebraic expressions for the contributions of complicated cycles (beyond the elementary four-edge plaquette order) together with their lattice combinatorial multiplicities, for arbitrary $k$.

(2) Non-Eulerian sector. The three-edge vertex contribution sums to $(k-2)-2(k-2)^2-(k-2)^3+2$, which vanishes at $k=3$ (Nagle's theorem) but is nonzero for every $k>3$, so the cancellation that organizes the ice expansion is lost. Determine how the non-Eulerian subgraphs are to be systematically incorporated — or resummed — into a convergent expansion of $W(k)$ for $k>3$, and quantify their effect on approximations of $W(k)$ at moderate $k$.

(3) Finite-width transfer matrices. The Toeplitz-block transfer matrices yield closed-form trace recursions and partition functions only for transverse sizes $m\leq 4$ (at $k=3$); for $m>4$ the coupled recursions for traces of the strip transfer matrix $A_p$ and its transpose, such as $\mathrm{Tr}[A_p^{m_1}(A_p^{T})^{m_2}\cdots]$, resist closed-form prediction. Extend the closed-form finite-width trace recursions and dominant-eigenvalue extraction to $m>4$, and to general $k$.

(4) Rigorous extrapolation. Establish, with finite-p error control or convergence rates, that $[\lambda_{\max}(B_p)]^{1/p}\to W(k)$ as $p\to\infty$ for general $k$ (here $B_p$ is the transfer matrix of a strip of transverse size $p$), going beyond the existing $k=3$ numerical fits ($p\leq 10$, fitted limit 1.53967 vs the exact 1.5396007).

An admissible answer takes one or more of the following forms, each evaluated against the stated objective rather than as an isolated exercise: an exact closed-form expression for $W(k)$ (symbolic in $k$, or for at least one $k\geq 4$); a systematic expansion of $W(k)$ in $1/(k-1)$ (or $1/k$) with closed-form coefficients that includes the non-Eulerian sector for $k>3$ and a controlled convergence statement; or closed-form finite-width results together with a proven or certified extrapolation to the infinite lattice. Purely numerical re-estimates of $W(k)$ for $k\geq 4$ do not answer the question, since those values are already pinned by the convergent large-q series.

The verification contract below evaluates answers to this statement. It does not narrow or redefine the research question.

Known solving difficulties:

- No integrable structure is known: the $k\geq 4$ vertex models (e.g. the 21-vertex model at $k=4$) have not been shown to satisfy any Yang-Baxter relation, so the Bethe-ansatz and commuting-transfer-matrix machinery behind Lieb's and Baxter's solutions is unavailable, and an exact $W(k)$ for $k\geq 4$ would require discovering hidden solvability or a fundamentally new technique.
- Combinatorial explosion of cycle classes: closed-form contributions require summing mod-k arithmetic constraints over all embeddings of each subgraph class, and the number of cycle types and their lattice multiplicities grow rapidly with order; only the elementary Eulerian cycles admit known closed forms.
- Structural obstruction in the non-Eulerian sector: the vertex-sum cancellations that kill odd-degree subgraphs at $k=3$ fail for $k>3$, so the series must be reorganized or resummed, and no ordering of the naive term-by-term expansion is known to converge.
- Finite-width recursions for $m>4$ involve coupled multi-matrix traces whose coefficients resist closed-form prediction, while direct diagonalization of the $(k-1)^p$-dimensional Toeplitz-block matrices grows exponentially in $p$.
- Rigorous control is hard: proving the radius of convergence of the $1/k$ expansion (whose boundary is the $k=3$ critical point) and convergence rates for $[\lambda_{\max}(B_p)]^{1/p}\to W(k)$ requires error bounds beyond current rigorous technology for this model class.
- Any new analytic result must be matched against the exact finite-lattice data and the pinned numerical values to order, which is itself a nontrivial bookkeeping task across subgraph classes.

## Current Progress

- Status: `ready`

Source fidelity was verified against the primary source (arXiv:1806.06751v3, published as J. Stat. Mech. 2018 123102). The four open issues in the candidate faithfully render what the paper leaves undone: only the first is stated verbatim as an open avenue ('Calculating the general algebraic expressions for contributions due to complicated cycles is tricky and can be another avenue of investigation', Sec. 3.2); the others are accurate descriptions of scope — closed-form finite-width trace recursions are given only for transverse sizes $m=2,3,4$ at $k=3$ (Appendix A-C), with $m>4$ called 'too involved' and the coupled recursion coefficients 'difficult to predict analytically'; numerical transfer-matrix work is confined to $k=3$, $p\leq 10$ (fitted limit 1.53967 vs Lieb's exact 1.5396007); and the non-Eulerian three-edge contribution is computed, $(k-2)-2(k-2)^2-(k-2)^3+2$, and shown nonzero for $k>3$, without any resummation. The LKM record's minor embellishments ('non-planar cycles', 'explicitly acknowledged by the authors') do not change the content; no misattribution or conflation of adjacent results was found, and the paper's lowest-order algebra was independently checked against Nagle's $k=3$ series (the elementary 4-cycle term $(k-2)(k-1)^4/M_k^4$ equals Nagle's $1/3^4$ at $k=3$).

Forward citation search: the paper has essentially no follow-up. Semantic Scholar, Crossref and OpenAlex each report exactly one citation — Martinez-Raton and Velasco, Phys. Rev. E 100, 062604 (2019), a density-functional-theory study of confined hard-square mixtures that reuses the multicomponent hard-square framing but addresses none of the four open items. Neither author has returned to the problem (Sudhir R. Jain's arXiv record was checked through early 2025; no follow-up exists).

The adjacent literature already settles the purely numerical part of the third item: convergent large-q series for the square-lattice chromatic bulk free energy (Bakaev and Kabanovich 1994; the transfer-matrix and chromatic-zero studies of Salas and Sokal 2001; Jacobsen 2010 to order $z^{79}$ in $z=1/(k-1)$ with radius of convergence $|z|=1/2$) pin $W(k)$ for every $k\geq 4$; summing the published coefficients (recomputed in this audit) gives $W(4)\approx 2.33606$, $W(5)\approx 3.25040$, $W(6)\approx 4.20010$, $W(7)\approx 5.16670$. Hence new numerical eigenvalue extrapolations would not constitute new knowledge; what survives of that item is the rigorous layer — proven convergence rates and finite-p error control for $[\lambda_{\max}(B_p)]^{1/p}\to W(k)$ — and the paper's specific Toeplitz-block route.

Exact-solubility status: no exact $W(k)$ is known on the square lattice for any $k\geq 4$. The $k\geq 4$ vertex models (e.g. the 21-vertex model at $k=4$) are not known to possess an integrable (Yang-Baxter) structure; the zero-temperature k-state Potts antiferromagnet on the square lattice is disordered for $k\geq 4$ (Baxter's conjectured antiferromagnetic critical curve $v=-2+\sqrt{4-k}$ leaves the physical region for $k>3$, as discussed by Salas and Sokal 2001), and the analytical bulk free energy that Baxter obtained for the triangular-lattice chromatic polynomial has no square-lattice counterpart (Jacobsen 2010 verified Baxter's triangular expression term by term against the series, making the square lattice the conspicuously unsolved case). Searches of arXiv (2018-2026) for chromatic-polynomial/square-lattice work, 21-vertex models, Eulerian orientations of grids, k-species hard-square gases, and coloring-vertex-model connections, plus the citation graphs above, found no exact or partial resolution.

The first, second and fourth items remain untouched in the literature: no closed-form algebraic contributions of higher-order or complicated cycles for general $k$ beyond the elementary Eulerian formula $(k-2)(k-1)^n/M_k^n$; no treatment or resummation of non-Eulerian subgraphs for $k>3$ (the adjacent large-q series are produced by the finite-lattice method as exact integer coefficients, not as per-subgraph closed-form algebra, and so do not answer the structural question of how non-Eulerian graphs enter and whether they can be resummed); and no closed-form trace recursions for transverse sizes $m>4$. The paper's $m=2,3,4$ closed forms and its $p\leq 10$, $k=3$ numerics remain the state of the art of that program.

Coverage and uncertainty: retrieval relied on the arXiv API, Semantic Scholar, Crossref and OpenAlex (the last budget-limited after one record); Google Scholar and the WebSearch tool were unavailable or empty in this environment. Purely combinatorial literature on Eulerian orientations of grid cylinders or fully-packed-loop enumeration — where a closed form for $m>4$ traces could conceivably exist — was probed via arXiv full-text search with nothing found; a specialist in that corner could add coverage. The open core is nevertheless precise and nonempty: the closed-form or certified analytic evaluation of $W(k)$ for general $k$ ($k\geq 4$), including the algebra of higher cycles, the non-Eulerian sector for $k>3$, and controlled finite-width extrapolation.

## Scientific Significance

Affected-field significance: `high`.

Direct impact on the core knowledge of exactly solved models in statistical mechanics and on chromatic-polynomial asymptotics. Full completion would convert $W(k)$ for $k\geq 4$ on the square lattice — the exponential growth constant of proper k-colorings, a quantity that has resisted exact treatment since Lieb's 1967 ice solution and Baxter's 1970 three-coloring solution — from a numerically pinned constant into an analytically known one, closing the conspicuous gap with the triangular lattice (where Baxter's analytical chromatic free energy exists). It would also settle the algebraic structure of the Nagle-type subgraph expansion (which subgraph classes contribute, and why non-Eulerian graphs cancel only at $k=3$) and deliver the first provably convergent closed-form-coefficient series or certified finite-width extrapolation scheme for this entropy, changing the field's capability to bound and compute lattice coloring entropies exactly. Partial completions, such as the $m>4$ recursions alone, would have primarily local, incremental impact.
