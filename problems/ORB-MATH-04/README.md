# ORB-MATH-04: Quillen's conjecture $Q_n$: are all projective modules over the localization of a regular local ring at a regular parameter free?

Quillen's conjecture, posed by Daniel Quillen in 1976 as an avenue of attack on the Bass–Quillen conjecture, predicts that if $(R,\mathfrak m)$ is a Noetherian regular local ring of dimension $n$ and $r\in\mathfrak m\setminus\mathfrak m^2$ is a regular parameter, then every finitely generated projective module over the localization $R[1/r]$ is free. The conjecture has been confirmed when $R$ contains a field (Bhatwadekar–Rao, Rao, Popescu) and when $\dim R\le 3$ (Gabber, Swan), but it remains open for regular local rings of mixed characteristic, especially ramified ones, in dimension at least $4$. The audited candidate originates from the paper of Mathew and Keshari on unimodular rows over monoid extensions of overrings of polynomial rings (arXiv:2104.09383), which recalls this conjecture, denoted $Q_n$ there, as the standing open question behind its freeness results for monoid algebras.

## Background

A finitely generated module $P$ over a commutative ring $A$ is called projective if it is a direct summand of a free module; geometrically, projective modules correspond to algebraic vector bundles on the affine scheme $\mathrm{Spec}\,A$. The classical Serre problem asked whether every finitely generated projective module over a polynomial ring $k[X_1,\dots,X_d]$ over a field $k$ is free; this was settled affirmatively and independently in 1976 by Quillen and Suslin (the Quillen–Suslin theorem). Its natural extension, the Bass–Quillen conjecture, predicts that for a regular ring $R$ (a Noetherian ring whose localization at every prime ideal has finite projective dimension over itself, the algebraic analogue of smoothness), every finitely generated projective module over the polynomial ring $R[t_1,\dots,t_d]$ is extended from $R$, i.e. of the form $P\otimes_R R[t_1,\dots,t_d]$; when $R$ is local this forces freeness. By a reduction of Quillen now called Quillen patching, the conjecture reduces to the case of a regular local ring $(R,\mathfrak m)$ and a single variable. A Noetherian local ring $(R,\mathfrak m)$ of dimension $n$ is a regular local ring if its maximal ideal can be generated by $n$ elements; such a minimal system of generators $r_1,\dots,r_n$ is called a regular system of parameters, and a single $r\in\mathfrak m\setminus\mathfrak m^2$ that belongs to one is called a regular parameter. To extend a vector bundle on the affine line $\mathbb A^1_R$ to the projective line $\mathbb P^1_R$ (the input needed for Horrocks' theorem, a classical criterion asserting that a vector bundle on the affine plane over a local ring that is trivial on a line is free, and which then delivers Bass–Quillen), one is led via formal gluing (a Beauville–Laszlo-type technique that compares modules over $R$, over its $r$-adic completion, and over the localization of that completion) to exactly the following question, recorded by Quillen as comment (2) on page 170 of his 1976 paper: is every finitely generated projective module over the localization $R[1/r]$ free? An affirmative answer to this question, conventionally called Quillen's conjecture or $Q_n$ where $n=\dim R$, implies the Bass–Quillen conjecture. The hypothesis that $r$ be a regular parameter is essential: Bhatwadekar and Rao showed that over the ring $R=\mathbb R[x,y,z]_{(x,y,z)}$, localized at $r=x^2+y^2+z^2\in\mathfrak m^2$, the kernel of the surjection $R[1/r]^3\to R[1/r]$, $(a,b,c)\mapsto ax+by+cz$, is a projective module of rank $2$ that is not free. A regular local ring is of equicharacteristic if it contains a field and of mixed characteristic otherwise; in mixed characteristic $0$ it has characteristic $0$ but residue characteristic $p$, and it is called unramified if $R/pR$ is regular. The conjecture is known for equicharacteristic rings (Bhatwadekar–Rao for localizations of affine algebras over a field; Popescu in general, via his general Néron desingularization, a smoothing theorem for regular homomorphisms) and for rings of dimension at most $3$ (Gabber, with a simpler proof by Swan). What remains open is the mixed-characteristic case, especially for ramified rings, in dimension at least $4$. The source paper audited here (Mathew–Keshari 2021) proves freeness results for projective modules over monoid algebras $A[M]$ (the semigroup algebra of a commutative monoid $M$ with coefficients in $A$), where $M$ is cancellative ($a+c=b+c$ implies $a=b$ in $M$), torsion-free, and seminormal (a mild algebraicity condition on the monoid), and $A$ is an overring of a polynomial ring built from a regular local ring containing a field, and it explicitly recalls Quillen's conjecture $Q_n$ as the unresolved question underlying this circle of ideas.

## Problem Statement

Let $(R,\mathfrak m)$ be a Noetherian regular local ring and let $r\in\mathfrak m\setminus\mathfrak m^2$ be a regular parameter, i.e. an element of some regular system of parameters of $R$. Must every finitely generated projective module over the localization $R[1/r]$ be free? Equivalently, must every finitely generated projective $R[1/r]$-module extend to a finitely generated projective $R$-module? This is Quillen's conjecture $Q_n$ with $n=\dim R$, recorded as comment (2) on page 170 of Quillen's 1976 paper; a positive answer for all regular local rings would imply the Bass–Quillen conjecture. The conjecture is known when $R$ contains a field and when $\dim R\le 3$; the unresolved regime is regular local rings of mixed characteristic in dimension at least $4$, especially ramified ones.

The verification contract below evaluates answers to this statement. It does not narrow or redefine the research question.

Known solving difficulties:

- All principal existing techniques — Quillen patching, Horrocks-type extension theorems, Lindel's étale-neighborhood method, and Popescu's general Néron desingularization — reduce the problem to the equicharacteristic (or at best unramified) setting and give no purchase on ramified mixed-characteristic regular local rings, so genuinely new ideas in mixed-characteristic commutative algebra are required.
- The conjecture quantifies over all regular local rings and all finitely generated projective modules simultaneously; a proof must handle every rank and arbitrary (including non-excellent and non-complete) rings, while the standard reduction permits at most passing to the $r$-adic completion.
- Known dimension inductions stall at dimension $3$: the Gabber–Swan arguments rely on the $2$-dimensional punctured spectrum, and no analogous analysis of vector bundles on higher-dimensional punctured spectra of mixed-characteristic regular local rings (their $K_0$ and Picard groups) is available.
- A counterexample would require a nonfree projective over $R[1/r]$ with $r$ a regular parameter; all known constructions of nonfree projectives over such localizations need $r\in\mathfrak m^2$, and producing nontrivial $K_0$-classes on punctured spectra of ramified mixed-characteristic regular rings is itself an open-ended difficulty.

## Current Progress

Mathew–Keshari (arXiv:2104.09383, 2021) study freeness over monoid algebras. Their paper recalls Quillen's conjecture $Q_n$ in its introduction as a standing conjecture, and notes after its Theorem 1.2 that the freeness conclusions fail for rings of type $R[d,m,n]$ (non-$*$ type) with counterexamples from Keshari–Lokhande. The counterexamples rule out the naive extension to all non-$*$-type overrings. The question retained here is Quillen's conjecture $Q_n$, originating in Quillen (1976).

Quillen (1976) posed the question in his paper resolving Serre's problem: for a regular local ring $(R,\mathfrak m)$ and a regular parameter $r\in\mathfrak m\setminus\mathfrak m^2$, is every finitely generated projective $R[1/r]$-module free? He observed that a positive answer, combined with his patching theorem and Horrocks' theorem, would imply the Bass–Quillen conjecture for all regular rings; this is recorded and confirmed in Česnavičius's 2022 survey (§2.2), which states the conjecture and the implication.

Bhatwadekar–Rao (1983) settled the question for regular $k$-spots, i.e. localizations of affine algebras over a field at regular prime ideals, and showed the hypothesis $r\in\mathfrak m\setminus\mathfrak m^2$ is necessary by a counterexample with $r=x^2+y^2+z^2$. Bhatwadekar (1987) settled the dimension-$3$ unramified case and, for rings with a coefficient field, ranks at least $d-1$. Rao (1985), following a suggestion of Nori, proved the generalization to localizations $R[1/(r_1\cdots r_t)]$ at products of parameters for $k$-spots with $t\le 2$ or $\dim R\le 5$.

The equicharacteristic case was settled completely by Popescu: building on Lindel's proof of the Bass–Quillen conjecture in the geometric case (1981) and his own general Néron desingularization (1989), Popescu (2002) proved that every finitely generated projective $R[1/r]$-module is free whenever the regular local ring $R$ contains a field. This is the case that the source paper and its predecessor Keshari–Lokhande (2014) build on and extend to overrings of polynomial rings and monoid algebras.

The mixed-characteristic case is known only up to dimension $3$: Gabber (1981, in his Brauer group seminar notes) proved the conjecture for regular local rings of dimension at most $3$, and Swan (1988) gave a simpler proof; both exploit the low dimension through the geometry of the $2$-dimensional punctured spectrum. Česnavičius's 2022 survey summarizes the state of the art verbatim: 'the main case in which the Quillen conjecture remains open is when the regular local ring $R$ is of mixed characteristic and, especially, ramified.'

Recent adjacent work does not touch the open core: Popescu (2020) poses a further question whose positive answer would yield the full Bass–Quillen conjecture (so it remains open); Česnavičius (2024) proved the Nisnevich conjecture in equal characteristic and gave a new proof of the Bass–Quillen conjecture for reductive group torsors in equal characteristic, reaching mixed characteristic only under quasi-splitness hypotheses on the closed fiber; Guo–Liu (2025) established the Bass–Quillen property for torsors over valuation rings; Stavrova (2025) generalized the dimension-$2$ Bass–Quillen case to split reductive groups. These results do not resolve or refute Quillen's conjecture in mixed characteristic.

The remaining regime consists of regular local rings of mixed characteristic and dimension at least $4$, especially ramified ones; Česnavičius (2022) describes this gap.

## Scientific Significance

Affected-field significance: `high`.

The conjecture is the missing local input in the standard patching-and-Horrocks reduction of the Bass–Quillen conjecture, the flagship open problem about vector bundles over regular rings. A positive answer would directly and mechanically imply that every finitely generated projective module over a polynomial extension of any regular ring is extended, and free in the local case — core knowledge of commutative algebra and algebraic K-theory changing what is known about the Grothendieck group $K_0$ of finitely generated projective modules over regular rings and their localizations. A refutation would directly refute Bass–Quillen itself. The impact is direct rather than indirect: the freeness of projective modules over $R[1/r]$ is exactly the statement used, via Quillen patching and formal gluing, to extend vector bundles from the affine line to the projective line over regular local rings.
