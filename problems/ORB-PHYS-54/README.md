# ORB-PHYS-54: Scheme-independent quark-gluon decomposition of the nucleon mass in QCD and the physical role of the trace anomaly

Almost all visible mass resides in nucleons, and in quantum chromodynamics (QCD) it is generated dynamically rather than by the Higgs-scale quark masses. How the nucleon mass should be partitioned into quark and gluon contributions is contested: several exact sum rules - Ji's four-term rest-frame decomposition, the variants studied by Lorcé and by Metz, Pasquini and Rodini, the Hatta-Rajan-Tanaka decomposition that splits the QCD trace anomaly into quark and gluon pieces, and the two-term trace decomposition - partition the same mass differently, and their individual terms are generally renormalization-scheme and scale dependent. The literature through August 2026, surveyed via the full forward-citation webs of the two anchor reviews and the primary sources, shows the problem open and actively contested: a 2026 first-principles lattice study verified several sum rules simultaneously yet obtained materially different partitions; a 2025 NNLO analysis quantified the scale dependence of each term and added yet another variant; and an August 2026 paper re-opened the operator-renormalization dispute over whether the trace anomaly can be split into quark and gluon parts at all. The open core is whether any decomposition possesses scheme-independent physical content - in particular the status of the gluonic trace anomaly and of the proposed quantum anomalous energy - and which observables could discriminate empirically between the competing decompositions.

## Background

Nucleons (protons and neutrons) account for nearly all of the visible mass in the universe, and their masses far exceed the sum of the current masses of their valence quarks: the bulk of the mass is generated by the dynamics of quantum chromodynamics (QCD), the gauge theory of quarks and gluons. In QCD the nucleon mass $M$ is the rest-frame expectation value of the energy-momentum tensor (EMT) $T^{\mu\nu}$, $M=\langle P|\int d^3x\, T^{00}(x)|P\rangle$ at zero momentum, and the question of the origin of the mass is formalized as decomposing $M$ into forward (zero-momentum-transfer) matrix elements of renormalized quark and gluon operators. Those forward EMT matrix elements are conventionally parameterized by gravitational form factors (GFFs), whose dominant terms are fixed by the momentum fractions $\langle x\rangle_q,\langle x\rangle_g$ carried by quarks and gluons - the same second moments of the parton distribution functions measured in deep-inelastic scattering.

Several exact sum rules compete. Ji's 1995 rest-frame decomposition splits $M$ into four terms: the quark-mass contribution $\sum_q\langle m_q\bar q q\rangle$ (the sigma terms), the quark kinetic-plus-potential energy and the gluon field energy (both from the traceless parts of the quark and gluon EMTs and expressible through $\langle x\rangle_{q,g}$), and a trace-anomaly term $M_a=\langle\frac{\beta(g)}{2g}G^a_{\mu\nu}G^{a\mu\nu}\rangle$. The trace anomaly is the quantum breaking of classical scale invariance: even with massless quarks the renormalized QCD EMT has a nonzero trace $T^\mu_{\ \mu}=\sum_q m_q\bar q q+\frac{\beta(g)}{2g}G^a_{\mu\nu}G^{a\mu\nu}$, where $\beta(g)$ is the QCD beta function governing the running of the coupling. Taking the forward matrix element of the trace itself yields the two-term trace decomposition $M=\sum_q\langle m_q\bar q q\rangle+\langle\frac{\beta(g)}{2g}G^2\rangle$, which partitions the mass into sigma terms plus anomaly only. Lorcé (2018) argued that decompositions with more than two terms run into consistency problems of physical interpretation and that the quark-mass and trace-anomaly terms are purely conventional, while also accounting for internal pressure effects; Metz, Pasquini and Rodini (2020) systematized the sum rules, computed their terms through three loops, and showed that the individual terms (beyond the anomaly as a whole) depend on the renormalization scheme and scale even though every complete sum rule is an exact identity. Hatta, Rajan and Tanaka (2018) renormalized the quark and gluon EMTs in a reducible operator basis - mixing the Lorentz trace and traceless components rather than treating them as separate irreducible blocks - using the gradient-flow representation (a smearing procedure that renders composite operator products finite), and concluded that in the minimal-subtraction scheme the trace anomaly itself can be unambiguously decomposed into quark and gluon parts, a result extended to three loops by Tanaka (2019). Ji, Liu and Schäfer (2021) argued that scale-symmetry breaking generates a distinct quantum anomalous energy (QAE) contribution to the mass, related through the field-theoretic virial theorem to $M/4$ in the chiral limit; Lorcé, Metz, Pasquini and Rodini (2021) reviewed the entire landscape and concluded from translation (Poincaré) symmetry that QAE is not a genuine separate contribution to the mass sum rule. On the empirical side, the forward GFFs entering the decompositions are constrained by parton-distribution moments and lattice QCD, and anomaly-related mass radii have been extracted from near-threshold $J/\psi$ photoproduction (photoproduction of the charmonium ground state just above threshold, which probes the gluonic scalar density of the nucleon), though with model dependence. As of 2026 the disagreement is unresolved: a first-principles lattice study by Bollweg, Ding, Gao, Luo and Mukherjee (2026) simultaneously verified several sum rules for charmonia (bound states of a charm quark and its antiquark, in their case $\eta_c$ and $J/\psi$) in a common scheme yet found materially different partitions (the anomaly share is about 6% of the mass in Ji's sum rule while the gluonic component of the anomaly in the Hatta-Rajan-Tanaka sum rule is about 35%); Tanaka (2025) evaluated the decompositions at next-to-next-to-leading order and introduced a further traceless/trace-organized variant; and Yang (2026) argued that the reducible-basis renormalization underlying the anomaly split is itself ill-conceived because the anomaly is regulator-, scheme- and scale-independent as a whole and cannot be traced back to separate quark and gluon ultraviolet contributions.

## Problem Statement

Establish the physical, renormalization-scheme-independent content of the quark-gluon decomposition of the nucleon mass in QCD. Concretely: (1) does there exist a decomposition of the nucleon mass into quark and gluon contributions (with or without a separate anomaly term) whose individual terms are invariant under changes of renormalization scheme and scale, consistent with QCD symmetries (translation/Poincaré invariance and the anomalous dilatation Ward identity, i.e. the EMT trace identity stated above, which fixes the scalar sector of any decomposition in terms of the quark masses and the beta function) - or does every term beyond the exact total carry conventional, scheme-dependent content only, as Lorcé (2018) argues? (2) In particular, is the trace-anomaly contribution uniquely attributable to the gluon sector, does it admit a scheme-independent quark/gluon split as constructed by Hatta, Rajan and Tanaka, or is no sector assignment of the trace part meaningful, as Yang (2026) argues? (3) Is the proposed quantum anomalous energy a genuine additional contribution to the mass sum rule, or is it not independent, as Lorcé, Metz, Pasquini and Rodini (2021) conclude? (4) Which observables - forward gravitational form factors, near-threshold heavy-quarkonium photoproduction, lattice energy-momentum-tensor matrix elements - can discriminate empirically between the competing decompositions, and what precisely would they have to measure? A complete answer derives, within QCD operator renormalization theory, the scheme- and scale-transformation properties of every candidate term and thereby either establishes one decomposition (existing or new) as physically meaningful and measurable, or proves that no scheme-independent quark/gluon partition of the mass exists beyond the exact sum-rule identities; it must engage the existing constructions (Ji's four-term sum rule, the Lorcé and Metz-Pasquini-Rodini variants, the Hatta-Rajan-Tanaka anomaly split, and the quantum anomalous energy) explicitly rather than sidestep them.

The verification contract below evaluates answers to this statement. It does not narrow or redefine the research question.

Known solving difficulties:

- Composite-operator renormalization subtlety: the trace anomaly is scheme independent as a whole yet nominally of order alpha_s, the same order at which finite, scheme-dependent subtraction terms appear, so separating anomaly from regular operator mixing requires careful treatment of evanescent operators (operators that vanish in four space-time dimensions but survive inside dimensional regularization) and anomalous Ward identities.
- Proving non-uniqueness of a quark/gluon split of the anomaly requires ruling out all conceivable operator bases and renormalization schemes, not merely those already used (reducible bases, gradient flow, minimal subtraction).
- Physical interpretation must resolve frame dependence and the field-theoretic virial theorem for a relativistic interacting bound state, where kinetic and interaction energies are not separately gauge invariant.
- Empirical discrimination is indirect and model dependent: forward gravitational form factors are constrained by parton-distribution moments, anomaly-related mass radii rely on models of near-threshold quarkonium photoproduction, and lattice determinations require nonperturbative renormalization and matching with their own uncertainties.
- Any resolution must simultaneously engage several self-consistent competing frameworks (Ji's group, Lorcé-Metz-Pasquini-Rodini, and the Hatta-Rajan-Tanaka/Tanaka line) and reconcile or explicitly refute each construction on its own terms.

## Current Progress

One refinement: the competing sum rules are not 'mutually inconsistent' as identities - all are exact consequences of the QCD energy-momentum tensor - rather they partition the mass differently and their individual terms carry different (and generally scheme-dependent) physical interpretations. The sources include the Lorcé–Metz–Pasquini–Rodini review (arXiv:2109.11785), Metz–Pasquini–Rodini (Phys. Rev. D 102, 114042), and Yang (arXiv:2608.01002).

Ji (1995, PRL 74, 1071 and PRD 52, 271) introduced the four-term rest-frame decomposition of the nucleon mass into sigma terms, quark kinetic-plus-potential energy, gluon energy, and a trace-anomaly term, with the energy terms tied to the measurable quark and gluon momentum fractions; this remains the reference point of the debate.

Lorcé (2018, EPJC 78, 120) argued that decompositions with more than two terms create inconsistencies of physical interpretation, that the quark-mass and trace-anomaly terms are purely conventional, and that pressure effects had been overlooked; Metz, Pasquini and Rodini (2020, PRD 102, 114042) reviewed the sum rules, computed all terms through three loops, and demonstrated the renormalization-scheme and scale dependence of the individual terms. These works established that the dispute is about the scheme-independent content of the terms, not about the validity of the sum rules.

Hatta, Rajan and Tanaka (2018, JHEP 12, 008) constructed, in the minimal-subtraction scheme using a reducible operator basis and the gradient-flow formalism, finite quark and gluon parts of the trace anomaly itself, claiming the split is unambiguous; Tanaka (2019, JHEP 01, 120) extended it to three loops. This is the origin of the competing 'Hatta-Rajan-Tanaka decomposition' in which the anomaly, not just the traceless energies, is divided between quark and gluon sectors.

Ji, Liu and Schäfer (2021, NPB 971, 115537) and Ji and Liu (2021, Sci. China PMA 64, 281012) proposed the quantum anomalous energy (QAE) as a distinct contribution to the nucleon mass, related by the field-theoretic virial theorem to a quarter of the mass in the chiral limit; Ji (2021, Front. Phys. 16, 64601) defended the 1995 decomposition against recent criticisms and stressed the importance of measuring the QAE experimentally.

Lorcé, Metz, Pasquini and Rodini (2021, JHEP 11, 121, arXiv:2109.11785) reviewed the whole question - the QFT virial theorem, EMT renormalization in minimal-subtraction-type schemes, the role of Poincaré symmetry - and concluded that the quantum anomalous energy is not a genuine contribution to the mass sum rule, as a consequence of translation symmetry. The QAE dispute between this group and Ji's group remains unreconciled.

Lattice input: Yang et al. (2018, PRL 121, 212001) computed the EMT-based mass decomposition from lattice QCD; Bollweg, Ding, Gao, Luo and Mukherjee (2026, PRL, arXiv:2601.13070) performed the first simultaneous first-principles validation of multiple sum rules (Ji, Lorcé, Metz-Pasquini-Rodini, and Hatta-Rajan-Tanaka) in a common renormalization scheme and scale, for eta_c and J/psi charmonia, finding for example an anomaly share of about 6% of the mass in Ji's sum rule but a gluonic anomaly component of about 35% in the Hatta-Rajan-Tanaka sum rule. The identities all hold; the partitions differ materially, and the paper does not (and cannot, by itself) settle which partition has scheme-independent physical meaning.

Tanaka (2025, arXiv:2511.18310) evaluated the decompositions quantitatively at next-to-next-to-leading order in QCD, computed the renormalization-scale dependence of every component, and introduced yet another decomposition organized strictly by the traceless/trace separation of each gauge-invariant quark and gluon EMT part - evidence that the proliferation of decompositions and the scale-dependence question are still live.

Yang (2026, arXiv:2608.01002, 2 August 2026) argued that the reducible-basis renormalization underlying the Hatta-Rajan-Tanaka anomaly split mixes the trace anomaly with regular operators and is ill-conceived: the anomaly is regulator-, scheme- and scale-independent as a whole and cannot be uniquely assigned to quark and gluon ultraviolet contributions; the same paper criticizes the trace-based decompositions as non-additive bookkeeping. A companion line by Ji and Yang (2026, NPB 1024, 117342) questions the pressure/shear-stress interpretation of EMT form factors, a parallel interpretational dispute over the same tensor. The operator-level contradiction (Hatta-Rajan-Tanaka's 'unambiguous' split versus Yang's 'cannot be uniquely assigned') stands unresolved in the literature at the curation cutoff.

Empirical discrimination remains indirect and model dependent: near-threshold J/psi photoproduction has been used to extract anomaly-related mass radii and trace-anomaly contributions (Wang, Evslin and Chen 2020; Wang and Bu 2024, PRC 110, 025206), with large model uncertainties; no observable has yet been established that cleanly discriminates between the competing decompositions.

No published work resolves, refutes, or collapses the open core; the most recent contributions (2025-2026) actively contest it.

## Scientific Significance

Affected-field significance: `high`.

Solving this problem would directly settle a flagship question of hadron physics: the origin of the nucleon mass, which generates nearly all visible mass in the universe. It would fix which numbers quoted as fractions of the proton mass from quark masses, quark and gluon energies, and the trace anomaly are scheme-independent physical quantities rather than conventions; decide the status of the quantum anomalous energy and of the quark/gluon split of the trace anomaly; and thereby define the physical targets of lattice-QCD gravitational-form-factor computations and of Electron-Ion Collider near-threshold quarkonium programs that currently quote anomaly-related quantities. The impact on the standard interpretation of QCD mass generation is direct; the impact on confinement-adjacent phenomenology (mass radii, mechanical properties of the nucleon) is indirect.
