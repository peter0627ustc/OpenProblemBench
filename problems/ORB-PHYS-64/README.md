# ORB-PHYS-64: Griffiths–Kelly–Sherman-type correlation inequalities for the eight-vertex model with non-uniform vertex weights

Björnberg and Lees (arXiv:2201.00176; Rev. Math. Phys. 35 (2023) 2350029) proved that the uniform eight-vertex model on the square-lattice torus — equivalently, diagonal observables of Kitaev's toric code — satisfies Griffiths–Kelly–Sherman (GKS)-type positive-correlation inequalities: events forcing the vertices of a set $C$ to carry arrow configuration $\eta$ or its complete reversal are positively correlated across two sets $C_1, C_2$ (under a topological side condition). The proof represents the model as the strong-coupling limit of a ferromagnetic four-body Ising model and applies the classical GKS inequalities. Remark 3.2 of the same paper observes that for general vertex weights $(a,b,c,d)$ the generalized quantum Hamiltonians realizing the measure $\mu_{a,b,c,d}$ require sign-indefinite operators, so the non-negativity underlying the proof is lost, and explicitly suggests that the inequalities may fail for non-uniform weights. The audited open problem is to determine whether these correlation inequalities hold for eight-vertex measures with non-uniform weights: prove them in the weight regimes where they are valid, exhibit explicit finite-volume counterexamples in the regimes where they fail, and thereby characterize the subset of the weight simplex on which the inequalities hold. A literature audit (forward-citation tracking on Semantic Scholar and INSPIRE, topical searches on arXiv and zbMATH, inspection of adjacent 2024–2026 work on Ashkin–Teller antiferromagnetism, toric-code Gibbs representations, and general correlation inequalities) found no published proof or refutation; the question is open.

## Background

The eight-vertex model lives on a finite square lattice with periodic boundary conditions (a torus). One places an arrow on each edge, and an allowed configuration must satisfy the ice rule: an even number of arrows point into every vertex. There are then eight possible local vertex configurations, denoted I, ..., VIII — the six types of the six-vertex model (two arrows in, two out) plus the all-in sink (VII) and all-out source (VIII). With general vertex weights $(a,b,c,d) \ge 0$ the model is governed by the Gibbs measure $\mu_{a,b,c,d}(\omega) \propto a^{\#I+\#II} b^{\#III+\#IV} c^{\#V+\#VI} d^{\#VII+\#VIII}$, counting how many vertices of each type occur; the uniform model has $a=b=c=d$, and the six-vertex model is the subcase $d=0$. Baxter computed the free energy of the general model exactly, and its phase diagram splits into ferroelectric and antiferroelectric regimes (long-range order in aligned and in staggered vertex patterns, respectively) separated by a disordered (critical, no long-range order) regime; but exact solvability says little about monotonicity or correlation inequalities between events.

For ferromagnetic classical spin systems such inequalities are classical: the Griffiths–Kelly–Sherman (GKS) inequalities state that in an Ising model with non-negative couplings, the expectation of any product of spins is non-negative, and any two such spin monomials are positively correlated. GKS-type inequalities are the standard tool behind monotonicity in couplings and comparison arguments, and they are what Björnberg and Lees transported to the vertex-model setting.

The quantum side of the source is Kitaev's toric code: qubits on the edges, commuting star operators $Z_s$ (product of the diagonal Pauli matrices $\sigma^{(3)}$ on the four edges meeting a vertex $s$) and plaquette operators $X_p$ (product of $\sigma^{(1)}$ around a face), and the Hamiltonian $H = -\sum_p J^x_p X_p - \sum_s J^z_s Z_s$ with $J^x, J^z \ge 0$, whose Gibbs and ground states are the equilibrium states of interest. Björnberg and Lees showed that the expectation of any observable diagonal in the $\sigma^{(3)}$ product basis equals its expectation under a classical Ising model with four-spin (four-body) interaction $P_J(\sigma) \propto \exp(\sum_s J_s \prod_{e \sim s} \sigma_e)$ with $J_s \ge 0$ — a ferromagnetic model, so GKS applies — and that the uniform eight-vertex measure is recovered as all $J_s \to \infty$. Consequently (their Theorem 1.1): for $\eta \in \{I,\dots,VIII\}$, letting $A^\eta_C$ be the event that every vertex in $C$ carries configuration $\eta$ or its complete arrow reversal, and $A^{\eta,\nu}_C$ ($\nu \ne \eta$) the event that every vertex in $C$ carries $\eta$, $\nu$, or their reversals, one has $\mu(A^\eta_{C_1} \cap A^\eta_{C_2}) \ge \mu(A^\eta_{C_1})\mu(A^\eta_{C_2})$ and the analogous inequality for $A^{\eta,\nu}$, provided $C_1 \cup C_2$ is contractible on the torus or all its non-contractible loops have even length; the same holds for the toric-code observables built from the projectors $Q^\epsilon_C$ and $Q^{\epsilon,\delta}_C$.

The obstruction beyond the uniform point is explicit in the source. Ardonne, Fendley and Fradkin constructed generalized quantum Hamiltonians whose ground states realize general eight-vertex measures; Björnberg and Lees note in Remark 3.2 that the operators which must be added to the toric-code Hamiltonian to reproduce the weights $(a,b,c,d)$ do not satisfy the non-negativity properties used in the proofs, so the GKS route breaks down, and that this suggests the inequalities of Theorem 1.1 may not hold for non-uniform weights.

The question matters beyond the uniform model: the Ashkin–Teller model (a pair of interacting Ising copies whose spin products form an eight-vertex configuration) has regimes, for instance with a strongly antiferromagnetic four-spin coupling, in which no correlation inequalities are available — Dober (2025) had to circumvent exactly this absence when analyzing antiferromagnetic Ashkin–Teller regimes — and recent work on classical representations of toric-code Gibbs states (Páez-Velasco et al., preprint 2025, published 2026, via a poly-depth duality — conjugation by a polynomial-depth quantum circuit — to decoupled classical Ising chains) delivers sampling but no ferromagnetic representation of eight-vertex observables and no GKS-type inequality.

## Problem Statement

Consider the eight-vertex model on the square-lattice torus $\Lambda$ with general vertex weights $(a,b,c,d) \ge 0$ (not all equal, and including the six-vertex subcase $d=0$), i.e. the measure
$$
\mu_{a,b,c,d}(\omega) \propto a^{\#I+\#II}\, b^{\#III+\#IV}\, c^{\#V+\#VI}\, d^{\#VII+\#VIII}
$$
on arrow configurations with an even number of arrows into every vertex. For $\eta \in \{I,\dots,VIII\}$ let $A^\eta_C$ denote the event that every vertex in $C \subseteq V$ carries the local arrow configuration $\eta$ or its complete reversal, and for $\nu \ne \eta$ let $A^{\eta,\nu}_C$ denote the event that every vertex in $C$ carries $\eta$, $\nu$, or their complete reversals, exactly as in Theorem 1.1 of Björnberg–Lees. Determine whether, under the same side conditions as in that theorem (namely that $C_1 \cup C_2$ is contractible on the torus, or that every non-contractible loop in $C_1 \cup C_2$ has even length), the inequalities
$$
\mu_{a,b,c,d}(A^\eta_{C_1} \cap A^\eta_{C_2}) \ge \mu_{a,b,c,d}(A^\eta_{C_1})\,\mu_{a,b,c,d}(A^\eta_{C_2})
$$
and
$$
\mu_{a,b,c,d}(A^{\eta,\nu}_{C_1} \cap A^{\eta,\nu}_{C_2}) \ge \mu_{a,b,c,d}(A^{\eta,\nu}_{C_1})\,\mu_{a,b,c,d}(A^{\eta,\nu}_{C_2})
$$
hold for non-uniform weights. A complete answer settles the question raised in Remark 3.2 of the source by (i) proving the inequalities in the weight regimes where they are valid and (ii) exhibiting explicit finite-volume counterexamples — weights, torus, and sets $C_1, C_2$ — in the regimes where they fail, thereby characterizing the subset of the weight simplex $(a,b,c,d) \ge 0$ on which the inequalities hold; equivalently for the quantum formulation, establishing the corresponding positive-correlation statements (or counterexamples) for diagonal observables of the generalized, Ardonne–Fendley–Fradkin-type quantum Hamiltonians whose ground states realize $\mu_{a,b,c,d}$. Constructing a classical non-negative (ferromagnetic) representation for such Hamiltonians is the natural proof route blocked in the source, and a solution may either construct one or bypass it; proofs, counterexamples, and representations are alternative discharges of this single objective — deciding the reach of GKS-type positive correlation beyond the uniform eight-vertex point — not independent subproblems.

The verification contract below evaluates answers to this statement. It does not narrow or redefine the research question.

Known solving difficulties:

- The only known proof route for the uniform case — representing the model as the $J \to \infty$ limit of a ferromagnetic four-body Ising model and applying GKS — provably breaks down for general weights, because the generalized quantum Hamiltonians realizing $\mu_{a,b,c,d}$ require sign-indefinite operators, so an essentially new representation or method is needed.
- The configuration space of allowed arrow configurations has no natural lattice or partial order under the plaquette-flip dynamics, so FKG (Fortuin–Kasteleyn–Ginibre)-type lattice-condition arguments do not apply directly.
- Positive correlation plausibly fails in antiferroelectric regimes with staggered long-range order while holding near the uniform point, so the answer likely requires a regime-by-regime analysis and a sharp delimitation of the weight region where the inequalities are valid.
- The events $A^\eta_C$ and $A^{\eta,\nu}_C$ constrain all vertices of $C$ simultaneously, so even evaluating their probabilities in general regimes involves nontrivial constrained-partition-function estimates.
- Exact correlation functions of the eight-vertex model are known only for special integrable weights, limiting direct computational guidance for general $(a,b,c,d)$.
- On the quantum side, constructing a generalized toric-code Hamiltonian whose diagonal Gibbs observables admit a classical ferromagnetic representation would require new representation techniques, since the Ardonne–Fendley–Fradkin-type construction necessarily contains sign-indefinite terms.

## Current Progress

Björnberg and Lees (arXiv:2201.00176; Rev. Math. Phys. 35 (2023), 2350029) establish the inequalities described below. Theorem 1.1 proves GKS-type positive-correlation inequalities for the uniform eight-vertex measure and for diagonal observables of the toric-code Gibbs state via the ferromagnetic four-body Ising representation; Remark 3.2 verbatim states that the operators that must be added to the Hamiltonian to realize general weights $(a,b,c,d)$ (display (52)) "do not satisfy the required non-negativity. This suggests that the inequalities in Theorem 1.1 may not hold for non-uniform weights." No conflation of adjacent results was found. One formulation correction: the source poses the question only as a suggestion attached to the failure of its method; the sub-question about a generalized quantum Hamiltonian with a ferromagnetic classical representation is a proof-strategy question rather than a separately stated problem, and is treated as such in the problem statement. The first inequality in Theorem 1.1 of arXiv v1 has a typographical error: both marginals carry index $C_2$. The intended expression is the product of the two marginals.

Dober (2025, Electronic Journal of Probability 30, Paper No. 73; arXiv:2406.06266) studies antiferromagnetic regimes of the Ashkin–Teller model — a model whose spin-product variables map to an eight-vertex model with non-uniform (indeed staggered) weights — and states in its abstract that "No correlation inequalities are available in this part of the phase diagram", circumventing the gap with the OSSS inequality (an influence inequality for Boolean functions on product spaces, due to O'Donnell–Saks–Schramm–Servedio) and a graphical representation. This is direct evidence that, as of 2025, correlation inequalities remain unavailable in at least the antiferromagnetic-sign regimes adjacent to the uniform point; the paper does not cite Björnberg–Lees and does not settle the validity question for general $(a,b,c,d)$.

Adjacent results on classical representations of toric-code Gibbs states do not resolve the quantum facet: Páez-Velasco, Schilling, Scalet, Verstraete and Capel (arXiv:2508.00126; published as Physical Review Research 8, 023277 (2026), DOI 10.1103/sbxq-sqvp) prove that the 2D toric code Gibbs state is poly-depth dual to two decoupled classical Ising chains — a circuit-conjugation duality useful for Gibbs sampling that yields no ferromagnetic weight representation of eight-vertex observables and derives no GKS-type inequality, and which does not cite Björnberg–Lees. Hangleiter, Ju and Vazirani (arXiv:2510.08446, Code Swendsen-Wang dynamics) construct rapidly mixing cluster dynamics for Gibbs states of code Hamiltonians — again sampling rather than correlation inequalities.

General correlation-inequality work in 2024–2026 was reviewed and does not touch non-uniform vertex weights: Manai and Warzel (arXiv:2608.23177) prove correlation inequalities for transversal-field pair-interaction models via a Gaussian convolution estimate (a different model class, requiring strong fields); Chan and Pak (arXiv:2607.06275) prove equality conditions for the FKG and Ahlswede–Daykin inequalities with combinatorial applications; Tokushige (arXiv:2406.08944) gives a new graphical proof of Ginibre's inequality for XY models. None addresses eight-vertex measures $\mu_{a,b,c,d}$.

No published proof and no published counterexample — including for the six-vertex subcase $d=0$ — was found, so the problem is recorded as open. A heuristic observation, rather than a published counterexample: elementary low-temperature heuristics in antiferroelectric regimes, where staggered order forces neighbouring vertices into different vertex types, suggest that the $A^\eta$-inequality plausibly fails there; no such counterexample appears to be published.

The surviving open core is precise: for $\mu_{a,b,c,d}$ on the square-lattice torus with $(a,b,c,d) \ge 0$ not all equal (including $d=0$), decide whether the two Theorem-1.1 inequalities hold under the same contractibility/even-loop side conditions on $C_1 \cup C_2$; a complete answer proves them where valid, exhibits explicit finite-volume counterexamples where they fail, and characterizes the weight regimes accordingly. The uniform case is settled affirmatively by the source and is not part of the open core.

## Scientific Significance

Affected-field significance: `medium`.

A resolution would determine whether the Griffiths–Kelly–Sherman (GKS)/Fortuin–Kasteleyn–Ginibre (FKG) family of positive-correlation inequalities — a core monotonicity toolkit of classical ferromagnetic statistical mechanics — extends beyond the uniform point of the exactly solved eight-vertex model. The impact would be direct but confined to the eight-vertex/Ashkin–Teller/toric-code probability literature: valid-regime proofs would immediately supply the monotonicity and comparison tools whose absence is currently felt (for instance, Dober's 2025 analysis of antiferromagnetic Ashkin–Teller regimes had to circumvent precisely the unavailability of correlation inequalities, and finite-temperature toric-code analyses would gain a ferromagnetic-representation handle), while explicit counterexamples would establish a genuine boundary of the GKS mechanism and delimit where non-negativity-based methods cannot be replaced by sign-indefinite integrable computations. It would change how monotonicity arguments can be used in this model family rather than reshape the field's core methods or capabilities at large.
