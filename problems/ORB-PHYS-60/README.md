# ORB-PHYS-60: Constructive derivation of the monomer–topological-defect correspondence from the Grassmann-integral representation of the two-dimensional classical dimer model

The close-packed classical dimer model on a two-dimensional bipartite lattice is described at long distances by a Gaussian height field theory. A June 2026 derivation by S. Powell shows constructively how this theory — its stiffness, its boundary-tilt structure, and the identification of dimer observables with height operators — follows from an exact Grassmann-integral representation of the partition function, via a continuum Dirac fermion and path-integral bosonization, for both square and honeycomb lattices. The paper explicitly defers one piece to future work: monomers, the defects of the close-packing constraint, are known to correspond to topological defects (vortices) of the height field — monomer insertions are expressed through vertex operators dual to $e^{\pm 2\pi i h}$ — but this correspondence has never been derived constructively from the fermionic representation. The obstacle is concrete: the microscopic monomer insertion is a nonlocal Jordan–Wigner-type string, which the earlier transfer-matrix bosonization of Wilkins and Powell (2023) explicitly could not handle, forcing them to construct the vortex operator in the continuum theory and confirm its asymptotics instead. The open problem is to close this gap: bosonize the monomer insertion itself, track the spin-sector sum with its complex weights, and reproduce the exact Fisher–Stephenson monomer correlations (the $r^{-1/2}$ decay with the exact Fisher–Hartwig amplitude, and the exact vanishing for same-sublattice pairs). Forward-citation and keyword searches as of August 2026 show that no published or preprinted work does this.

## Background

In the classical dimer model, each link (edge) of a lattice carries either zero or one dimer, and in the close-packed ensemble every site is touched by exactly one dimer. On bipartite lattices — whose sites split into two sublattices A and B, with every link joining an A site to a B site; the square and honeycomb lattices are the standard examples — the equal-weight ensemble of close-packed configurations forms a Coulomb phase: a topologically ordered, critical phase in which correlations decay algebraically with distance. The long-wavelength description is obtained by rewriting the close-packing constraint as a lattice Gauss law for a flux carried by each dimer, and resolving it in two dimensions with a scalar height field $h$, defined on the faces of the lattice so that crossing a dimer changes $h$ by a fixed amount. The resulting continuum theory is a Gaussian free scalar with action proportional to $\int(\nabla h)^2$ and stiffness $\kappa$ (equal to $\pi$ for the uniform model), equivalent to a two-dimensional Coulomb gas; Kenyon (2001) proved rigorously that the height is distributed as a Gaussian free field. Microscopic dimer observables map to gradients and exponentials (vertex operators) of $h$.

On planar graphs the dimer model is exactly solvable: the partition function is a Pfaffian (the signed square root of a determinant of the Kasteleyn matrix), and can equivalently be written as an integral over anticommuting Grassmann variables, one per lattice site, which makes the free-fermion structure explicit. Bosonization — the exact equivalence, in two dimensions, between massless Dirac fermions and a compactified free boson, under which fermion bilinears become gradients or exponentials of the boson field — then offers a route from the exact solution to the height theory.

The height theory was historically justified as a heuristic coarse-graining, with its coefficients fixed by matching to exact correlations. Wilkins and Powell (2023) gave the first constructive derivation, by bosonizing the continuum limit of Lieb's transfer-matrix (free-fermion) solution; Powell (2026), the source of this problem, gave a more direct real-space version: expanding the Grassmann-integral representation in slowly varying fermion modes yields a massless Dirac fermion in two-dimensional Euclidean space, which path-integral bosonization maps exactly onto the height theory, with stiffness $\kappa=\pi$, for both square and honeycomb lattices. Sources coupling to the flux density and to the valence-bond-solid (VBS) order parameter — the standard complex, sublattice-valued symmetry-breaking order parameter of dimer models — map to the gradient and vertex operators of $h$, and the sum over fermion boundary phases (spin sectors) maps to the sum over height boundary tilts.

A monomer is a defect of the close-packing constraint: a site left uncovered. Monomers are the fundamental fractionalized excitations of the dimer Coulomb phase: each carries an effective magnetic charge whose sign is fixed by its sublattice, opposite-sublattice pairs attract through a logarithmic entropic Coulomb interaction, and Fisher and Stephenson (1963) computed the exact leading decay $G_m \sim r^{-1/2}$ of the two-monomer distribution function (the ratio of the partition functions with and without the two monomers); by sublattice charge parity, the two-monomer distribution vanishes identically when both monomers sit on the same sublattice. In the height-field description a monomer is a topological defect (vortex): a point around which the height winds, represented in the continuum theory by a magnetic vertex operator (in the source's normalization, dual to $e^{\pm 2\pi i h}$) rather than by the ordinary gradient and exponential operators that represent dimer observables.

This monomer–defect correspondence is established knowledge — it follows from Coulomb-gas arguments and is confirmed by exact asymptotics — but it has never been derived constructively from the fermionic representation. In the transfer-matrix solution the microscopic monomer insertion is a nonlocal string operator (a Jordan–Wigner-type product over intervening sites), and Wilkins and Powell (2023) found that it cannot be bosonized by their approach; they instead wrote down the vortex operator in the continuum theory and checked that it reproduces the correct leading asymptotics. Powell (2026) likewise derives the full close-packed theory but contains no monomer machinery, stating that the partition function with inserted monomers can be expressed in terms of vertex-operator correlations while leaving a constructive derivation of this relationship to future work. Closing this gap is the problem audited here.

## Problem Statement

Starting from the exact Grassmann-integral (equivalently, free-fermion) representation of the close-packed classical dimer model on the square lattice — and, where the source framework applies, the honeycomb lattice — with a fixed set of monomer insertions (specified sites exempted from the close-packing constraint), derive constructively, through the continuum Dirac-fermion limit and bosonization, the correspondence between monomers and topological defects of the height field. Concretely, a solution must: (i) show how monomer insertions modify the Grassmann integral, including the nonlocal (Jordan–Wigner-type string) structure of the insertion operators and the treatment of the sum over fermion boundary phases (spin sectors) with their complex weights; (ii) carry out the bosonization of the resulting insertion operators so as to obtain, rather than assume, that each monomer acts as a vortex of the height field — a magnetic vertex operator dual to $e^{\pm 2\pi i h}$, with charge sign fixed by the monomer's sublattice — such that the partition function with monomers equals the corresponding height-theory vertex-operator correlator; and (iii) verify the result against exact lattice asymptotics: at minimum the Fisher–Stephenson decay $G_m \sim r^{-1/2}$ of the two-monomer distribution function for opposite-sublattice pairs, with the exact Fisher–Hartwig amplitude ($G_m(X,0)\sqrt{X} \to E/2 \approx 0.247372$ for same-row monomers on the isotropic square lattice), together with the exact vanishing of the two-monomer distribution for same-sublattice pairs. The source additionally suggests — without posing as a problem — that the same Grassmann-integral method may extend to non-translation-symmetric settings (quasicrystalline tilings, spatially disordered weights) and beyond perturbation theory for interacting dimers; progress on those extensions is adjacent to, but does not by itself answer, the question posed here.

The verification contract below evaluates answers to this statement. It does not narrow or redefine the research question.

Known solving difficulties:

- The microscopic monomer insertion is a nonlocal string operator in the fermion representation (in the transfer-matrix/Jordan–Wigner form, a product over all intervening sites), whereas standard bosonization identities apply to local fermion bilinears; bosonizing this string within path-integral bosonization is the central unsolved technical obstacle, which Wilkins and Powell (2023) explicitly could not overcome.
- Monomers violate the lattice Gauss law (flux constraint) that underlies the height mapping, so the derivation must track how a localized flux violation enters the continuum Dirac theory, whose known sources (flux density and VBS order parameter) map to a U(1) gauge field and a mass term rather than to vortices.
- The partition function with monomers involves a sum over fermion boundary phases (spin sectors) with complex weights; the height-theory counterpart (sum over height boundary tilts and vortex sectors) must be matched carefully, including phases and normalization constants.
- Reproducing the exact Fisher–Hartwig amplitude, not merely the exponent $r^{-1/2}$, requires controlling the normalization between the lattice and continuum theories (lattice spacing, compactification radius, operator normalizations).
- A fully constructive mapping may exist only at the level of asymptotics; establishing exact equivalence could require tools beyond standard bosonization, such as exact Fisher–Hartwig asymptotics or elliptic/theta-function identities, and a precise obstruction result (that no such derivation exists within a stated framework) may itself be difficult to formulate and prove.

## Current Progress

- Status: `ready`

Source fidelity (primary source inspected in full): S. Powell, arXiv:2606.17154 (v1 of 15 June 2026, the only version; LKM open-question node gcn_29239f63974b4237 from LKM paper 7474660877999497217) poses exactly one item as future work in its Discussion: monomers are known to correspond to topological defects of the height-field theory, the partition function with inserted monomers 'can be expressed in terms of correlations of a vertex operator dual to $e^{\pm 2\pi i h}$', but 'we leave a similar constructive derivation of this relationship to future work'. The LKM paraphrase's other items are stated differently by the source: interactions 'can in some cases be handled perturbatively' (the paper never poses a full non-perturbative treatment — that clause is the paraphrase's inference); the extension to non-translation-symmetric settings is hedged as 'It may also be possible...'; and anisotropic weights are described as understood, being 'equivalent to rescaling the spatial coordinates' (following Wilkins and Powell 2023), with the stiffness $\kappa=\pi$ stated to hold for any isotropic model described by free Dirac fermions. The audited open core is therefore the explicitly deferred constructive monomer derivation.

The correspondence itself is established knowledge. Monomers carry opposite effective magnetic charges on the two sublattices and interact via a logarithmic entropic Coulomb interaction; Fisher and Stephenson (1963) computed the monomer distribution function in the scaling limit, finding $r^{-1/2}$ decay for opposite-sublattice pairs. Fendley, Moessner, and Sondhi (2002) showed that on the triangular lattice monomer correlations instead decay exponentially (confinement), consistent with a relevant cosine perturbation of the height action.

Wilkins and Powell (2021) gave the exact lattice-level fermionic treatment of monomers: expressing Lieb's transfer matrix in terms of free fermions, the monomer distribution function becomes a Toeplitz determinant whose large-separation asymptotics they evaluated exactly via the Fisher–Hartwig conjecture, $G_m(X,0) \approx E/(2\sqrt{X})$ for odd separations $X$ along a row, with (in their Eq. (166)) $E \simeq ((1+\alpha^2)/2)^{-1/4} \times 0.494744$, i.e. $E = 0.494744$ at the isotropic point $\alpha=1$ and hence $G_m(X,0)\sqrt{X} \to E/2 \approx 0.247372$; and $G_m$ identically zero for even $X$ (same-sublattice pairs), reproducing Fisher–Stephenson. (Note: the same published equation also displays the algebraic form $E = 2^{2/3}e^{6\zeta'(-1)}(1+\alpha^2)^{-1/4}$, which evaluates to 0.49483 at $\alpha=1$; the two branches of Eq. (166) are inconsistent in the fourth significant digit, so the explicitly stated decimal 0.494744 is used as the anchor throughout.)

Wilkins and Powell (2023) derived the height field theory constructively by bosonizing the continuum limit of the transfer-matrix fermions. For monomers, however, they state that because the microscopic insertion is a nonlocal chain of fermion operators, they are 'not able to bosonize it using the same approach'; instead they constructed the corresponding magnetic vertex operator in the continuum theory and confirmed that it reproduces the correct leading-order asymptotics. The constructive step from microscopic monomer insertion to height-theory vortex operator was thereby explicitly left open, and the monomer's effective charge sign (opposite on the two sublattices) entered as an input to the construction rather than an output.

Powell (2026) provides the real-space Grassmann-integral route for the close-packed model on square and honeycomb lattices — continuum Dirac fermion, path-integral bosonization, stiffness $\kappa=\pi$, sources for the flux density and the VBS order parameter, spin-sector sums mapping to height tilts — but contains no monomer machinery and defers the monomer derivation to future work. As of 30 August 2026 the paper has zero citations on both Semantic Scholar and INSPIRE, and no revised version exists.

Systematic searches found no other work performing the constructive derivation. arXiv keyword searches return only the source paper itself for 'height field' + 'Grassmann', nothing for 'monomer' + 'vertex operator', and only combinatorial or unrelated works for 'dimer' + 'Grassmann' + 'monomer', 'bosonization' + 'dimer', and 'monomer correlation' + 'dimer'; the citation lists of Wilkins–Powell 2021 (6 citations) and 2023 (4 citations) contain no work on it (the citers concern Kasteleyn transitions in Heisenberg models, analog-digital simulators, tetramer-dimer thermodynamics, Mermin–Wagner theorems, and flux fractionalization).

Adjacent exact-solution literature does not close the gap: Allegra (2015) gave a complete fermionic (Pfaffian) solution of the square-lattice dimer model with an arbitrary number of monomers and compared the inferred scaling dimensions with the bosonic height theory, but without a constructive bosonization of the monomer insertions; Allegra and Fortin (2014) expressed the monomer-dimer partition function as a Grassmann integral (a product of two Pfaffians) without connecting it to the height field; Aizenman, Laínz Valcázar, and Warzel (2017) proved Pfaffian structure of boundary-monomer and order-disorder correlation functions; Ramirez Trino, Seifi MirJafarlou, and Rajabpour (2025) developed a generalized Grassmann-Pfaffian framework for monomer-dimer systems. The rigorous-probability bosonization literature (double-dimer bosonization and related spinor-observable results) likewise treats different observables in a different framework, not the constructive bosonization of the single-dimer monomer string. All treat the fermionic/combinatorial side; none derives the vertex-operator (vortex) mapping.

For the source's adjacent outlook items: interacting dimers are treated perturbatively within the bosonization framework by Falco (2013) and Wilkins–Powell (2023), while the rigorous constructive-renormalization-group program of Giuliani, Mastropietro, and Toninelli (2015; 2017) already gives non-perturbative results — height fluctuations of weakly interacting dimers converge to the Gaussian free field, with non-universal stiffness and critical exponents — though not a bosonized derivation of the height theory with defects. For aperiodic settings, Flicker, Simon, and Parameswaran (2020) showed that Penrose tilings admit no perfect matchings at all (maximum matchings carry a fixed monomer density $81-50\varphi \approx 0.098$, with $\varphi$ the golden ratio), and Singh and Flicker (2024) exactly solved the dimer model on the spectre aperiodic monotiling (finding deconfined test monomers); Caracciolo et al. (2021) studied disordered dimer models numerically. No Grassmann-to-height derivation exists for any of these settings.

Coverage and residual uncertainty: the core is a 2.5-month-old explicitly flagged future-work item with zero forward citations, so coverage of the forward direction is essentially complete; the residual uncertainty is limited to the possibility of an unindexed treatment (a thesis, lecture notes, or a passage inside a longer work) and to the judgment of what distinguishes a constructive derivation from the constructed-operator confirmation of Wilkins–Powell (2023). The problem statement pins this distinction: the microscopic insertion, including its string structure and the spin-sector sum, must be bosonized, and the exact asymptotics reproduced.

## Scientific Significance

Affected-field significance: `medium`.

Solving the problem would not change the known physics of monomers — their entropic Coulomb interaction, $r^{-1/2}$ correlations, and vortex interpretation are already established by exact calculations and Coulomb-gas arguments. The direct impact is methodological: it would complete the constructive bridge between the exact free-fermion (Grassmann) solution of the two-dimensional dimer model and its continuum height-field description, replacing a symmetry-plus-confirmation identification of the defect operator with an actual derivation. It would supply the first bosonization of a genuinely nonlocal microscopic insertion (a Jordan–Wigner-type string) into a magnetic vertex operator of the height field — a template for treating defects in other constrained classical and quantum systems (loop models, quantum dimer models at Rokhsar–Kivelson points) whose excitations are likewise represented by topological operators. This is clear, substantive progress with follow-on momentum for the constructive-field-theory program, but indirect with respect to the field's core empirical knowledge, hence medium.
