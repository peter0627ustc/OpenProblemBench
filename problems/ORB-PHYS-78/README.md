# ORB-PHYS-78: Arbitrary-width structure of the Potts partition-function-zero accumulation locus on self-dual square-lattice strips (the Chang–Shrock conjecture)

The $Q$-state Potts model partition function $Z(G,Q,v)$ (a Tutte-polynomial specialization) was computed exactly by Chang and Shrock on infinite-length self-dual cyclic square-lattice strips restricted to the self-dual manifold $Q=v^2$, for transverse widths $L_y\le 4$. Their exact zero-accumulation loci display a systematic pattern — real-axis crossings at the odd Beraha values $Q_{2\ell+1}=4\cos^2(\pi/(2\ell+1))$, a semi-infinite segment $4\le Q\le\infty$ present for odd $L_y$ and absent for even $L_y$, division of each plane into at least $2L_y$ regions with a fixed assignment of the dominant transfer-matrix eigenvalue to each region, confluence of all arcs at $Q=4$, and isolated zeros near the even Beraha values $Q_{2\ell}$ — which they conjectured to hold for arbitrary width $L_y$. The problem asks for a proof or disproof of that conjecture, for a determination of whether the locus crosses the real $Q$ axis outside $0\le Q\le 4$ for sufficiently wide strips, and for a rigorous general derivation of the noncompactness of the loci in the $v$ and $Q$ planes. The strongest later work (Jacobsen–Saleur's boundary Temperley–Lieb analysis) derives, at physics level of rigor, the real-axis crossing positions at every finite width but none of the global structure; a systematic forward-citation and author sweep found no proof, disproof, or extension to widths $L_y\ge 5$, so the problem is open.

## Background

The $Q$-state Potts model assigns to each vertex of a graph $G$ one of $Q$ spin states and couples neighboring spins with Boltzmann weight $v=e^{K}-1$, where $K$ is the dimensionless exchange. In the Fortuin–Kasteleyn cluster representation (an expansion over spanning subgraphs $G'=(V,E')$ of $G$, each weighted by $Q^{k(G')}\,v^{e(G')}$, with $k(G')$ the number of connected components and $e(G')$ the number of occupied edges) the partition function is a polynomial in $Q$ and $v$, equivalent to a specialization of the Tutte polynomial of $G$; at $v=-1$ it reduces to the chromatic polynomial counting proper $Q$-colorings of $G$. Because $Z$ is a polynomial, its zeros can lie off the real parameter axes, and the loci on which zeros accumulate in the thermodynamic limit (complex-temperature zeros, the complex-temperature analogue of the Lee–Yang zeros, which are the zeros of the Ising partition function in the complex magnetic field) encode the analytic structure of the free energy — in two dimensions, the complex-temperature phase diagram.

For a strip of a lattice, i.e. a graph of fixed transverse width $L_y$ and length $L_x$ with $L_x\to\infty$, the partition function is computable exactly by transfer matrices and takes the form $Z=\sum_j c_j(Q)\,\lambda_j(Q,v)^{L_x}$, a finite sum of eigenvalue powers with polynomial amplitudes $c_j$. The Beraha–Kahane–Weiss theorem then states that the zeros accumulate, as $L_x\to\infty$, exactly on the locus where two or more dominant (largest-modulus) terms are equal in magnitude, possibly supplemented by isolated points where a dominant amplitude $c_j$ vanishes. The accumulation locus $\mathcal{B}$ can therefore be determined exactly, without approximation, provided one can control which eigenvalues dominate where in the complex plane.

A distinguished submanifold for the square lattice is the self-dual manifold $Q=v^2$. Potts duality maps $v\to Q/v$, so this manifold is invariant under duality, just as the square lattice itself is self-dual. Chang and Shrock studied strips that preserve this property: the CDBC strip $G_{\rm CDBC}[L_y\times L_x]$ (self-dual cyclic boundary conditions) is an infinite-length strip with periodic longitudinal boundary conditions plus one extra external vertex connected to every vertex on one of the two sides running along the length (so the external vertex has degree $L_x$), which makes the graph self-dual (its vertex and face counts are equal and its average degree is 4, as on the square lattice). Restricting to $Q=v^2$, they computed the exact accumulation loci $\mathcal{B}_v$ and $\mathcal{B}_Q$ in the complex $v$ and $Q$ planes for widths $1\le L_y\le 4$. Their transfer-matrix analysis organizes the partition function as

$$Z\big(G_{\rm CDBC}[L_y\times L_x],Q,v\big)=Q\,v^{L_xL_y}\sum_{d=1}^{L_y+1}\sum_j\bar\kappa^{(d)}(Q)\,\big(\bar\lambda_{L_y,d,j}\big)^{L_x},$$

where the eigenvalues $\bar\lambda_{L_y,d,j}=v^{-L_y}\lambda_{L_y,d,j}$ are labeled by a sector number $d\in\{1,\ldots,L_y+1\}$ and an index $j$ within that sector, the amplitudes $\bar\kappa^{(d)}(Q)$ are $L_x$-independent polynomials in $Q$, and the single eigenvalue of the last sector is identically $\bar\lambda_{L_y,L_y+1}=1$; $\bar\lambda_{L_y,d,\max}$ denotes the eigenvalue of largest magnitude within sector $d$, with which member $j$ of the sector attains the maximum depending on the point of the complex plane under consideration. Already at $L_y=4$ there are 126 such eigenvalues, roots of high-degree polynomials with no closed form.

The exact loci for $L_y\le 4$ show a strikingly regular pattern. In the interval $-2\le v\le -1$ the locus crosses the negative real $v$ axis at $v=-2$ and at $v_{2\ell+1}=-2\cos(\pi/(2\ell+1))$ for $1\le\ell\le L_y$; equivalently $\mathcal{B}_Q$ crosses the positive real axis at the odd Beraha values $Q_{2\ell+1}=4\cos^2(\pi/(2\ell+1))$ for $0\le\ell\le L_y$, where $Q_1=4$ (the Beraha numbers $4\cos^2(\pi/r)$ have played a special role in the theory of chromatic polynomials since Beraha, Kahane and Weiss). For odd $L_y$ the locus also contains the semi-infinite real segments $-\infty\le v\le -2$ and $4\le Q\le\infty$; the even widths computed show no such segment. All curves of the locus meet at $v=-2$ (equivalently $Q=4$), where every transfer-matrix eigenvalue has unit modulus. Isolated zeros off the locus occur at $Q=0$ (from the explicit prefactor above, a zero proven by the authors for all widths) and near the even Beraha values $Q_{2\ell}$, $1\le\ell\le L_y+1$, where the amplitude of the dominant eigenvalue vanishes. Finally, the locus divides each plane into at least $2L_y$ regions, with a systematic rule for which eigenvalue dominates in each region. Chang and Shrock elevated these observations to a conjecture for arbitrary width (their Section V), and flagged two further issues: their properties 'do not preclude' real-axis crossings outside $0\le Q\le 4$ for sufficiently wide strips, and the noncompactness of $\mathcal{B}_v$ and $\mathcal{B}_Q$ (arcs running to complex infinity, i.e. passage through $1/v=0$ and $1/Q=0$) was established only for the widths they computed.

The mechanism behind part of this pattern is understood. Saleur's exact study of the two-dimensional antiferromagnetic Potts model showed that at Beraha values, transfer-matrix eigenvalues in adjacent sector numbers become equal in magnitude, reflecting the structure of the Temperley–Lieb algebra — the algebra generated by operators $U_i$ obeying $U_i^2=\sqrt{Q}\,U_i$, $U_iU_{i\pm1}U_i=U_i$, and $U_iU_j=U_jU_i$ for $|i-j|\ge 2$, whose representations merge when $q=e^{i\pi/r}$ is a root of unity. Jacobsen and Saleur's later analysis of the boundary chromatic polynomial — the Potts partition function on an annulus whose outer-rim spins see $Q_s$ colors instead of $Q$; the CDBC self-dual strip corresponds to $Q_s=Q-1$ with edge weight $v_e=\pm\sqrt{Q}$ — showed via the representation theory of the boundary Temperley–Lieb (blob) algebra (the boundary variant of the Temperley–Lieb algebra augmented by a marker operator acting on one rim) that dominant eigenvalues in the $L$- and $(L+2)$-leg sectors (loop-model sectors with $L$ non-contracted strands threading the annulus) cross exactly at the positions observed by Chang and Shrock for every finite width, with the number of crossings cut off by the width through $L\le 2W$ and the whole pattern terminating at $Q=4$, the end of the Berker–Kadanoff (massless antiferromagnetic) phase. This explains, at physics level of rigor, the real-axis crossing positions but not the global complex-arc structure, the region count, the dominance assignment away from the real axis, the odd/even parity of the semi-infinite segment, or the noncompactness.

## Problem Statement

For the $Q$-state Potts model on infinite-length CDBC (self-dual cyclic boundary conditions) strips of the square lattice, $G_{\rm CDBC}[L_y\times L_x]$ with $L_x\to\infty$ (periodic longitudinal boundary conditions plus one external vertex joined to all $L_x$ vertices of one longitudinal side, making the strip graph self-dual), restricted to the self-dual manifold $Q=v^2$:

Notation: on this manifold the partition function has the exact form $Z\big(G_{\rm CDBC}[L_y\times L_x],Q,v\big)=Q\,v^{L_xL_y}\sum_{d=1}^{L_y+1}\sum_j\bar\kappa^{(d)}(Q)\,\big(\bar\lambda_{L_y,d,j}\big)^{L_x}$, where the transfer-matrix eigenvalues $\bar\lambda_{L_y,d,j}=v^{-L_y}\lambda_{L_y,d,j}$ are labeled by the sector number $d\in\{1,\ldots,L_y+1\}$ and an index $j$ running over the eigenvalues of that sector, the amplitudes $\bar\kappa^{(d)}(Q)$ are $L_x$-independent polynomials in $Q$, and the single eigenvalue of sector $d=L_y+1$ is identically $\bar\lambda_{L_y,L_y+1}=1$. Throughout, $\bar\lambda_{L_y,d,\max}$ denotes the eigenvalue of largest magnitude within sector $d$; which member $j$ of the sector attains this maximum depends on the point of the complex plane under consideration.

(a) Prove or disprove the Chang–Shrock conjecture that, for every width $L_y\ge 1$, the accumulation loci $\mathcal{B}_v$ and $\mathcal{B}_Q$ of partition-function zeros in the complex $v$ and $Q$ planes have the following structure:

- $\mathcal{B}_v$ crosses the negative real axis at $v=-2$ and at $v_{2\ell+1}=-2\cos\big(\pi/(2\ell+1)\big)$ for $1\le\ell\le L_y$, and $\mathcal{B}_Q$ crosses the positive real axis at the odd Beraha values $Q_{2\ell+1}=4\cos^2\big(\pi/(2\ell+1)\big)$ for $0\le\ell\le L_y$, where $Q_1=4$;
- the semi-infinite real segments $-\infty\le v\le -2$ and $4\le Q\le\infty$ belong to the locus for odd $L_y$ and are absent for even $L_y$;
- all curves of $\mathcal{B}_v$ meet at $v=-2$ (equivalently, all curves of $\mathcal{B}_Q$ meet at $Q=4$), where every transfer-matrix eigenvalue has unit modulus;
- $\mathcal{B}_v$ divides the $v$ plane into at least $2L_y$ regions and $\mathcal{B}_Q$ divides the $Q$ plane into at least $2L_y$ regions, with the dominant eigenvalue assigned systematically across those regions: one member of sector $d=1$ (denoted $\bar\lambda_{L_y,1,\max}$ there) dominates in the regions extending to complex infinity, a different member of sector $d=1$ dominates in the region containing the interval $4\le Q\le\infty$ (for odd $L_y$, two members of sector $d=1$ are degenerate in magnitude on this interval, giving rise to its line segment on the locus), $\bar\lambda_{L_y,k+1,\max}$ dominates in the region containing the interval $Q_{2k+1}\le Q\le Q_{2k+3}$ for $1\le k\le L_y-1$ (this range is empty for $L_y=1$), and $\bar\lambda_{L_y,L_y+1}=1$ dominates in the region containing the interval $Q_{2L_y+1}\le Q\le Q_1=4$;
- besides the locus, the partition function has an isolated zero at $Q=0$ (proven by the authors for all widths) and, for each $1\le\ell\le L_y+1$, zeros close to the even Beraha value $Q=Q_{2\ell}$, which become isolated accumulation points at these values as $L_x\to\infty$ through the vanishing there of the amplitude of the dominant eigenvalue.

(b) Determine whether, for sufficiently large $L_y$, $\mathcal{B}_Q$ also crosses the real $Q$ axis at points outside the interval $0\le Q\le 4$ (i.e. with $Q<0$ or $Q>4$), and if so, how such crossings scale with $L_y$.

(c) Furnish a rigorous general derivation of the noncompactness of $\mathcal{B}_v$ and $\mathcal{B}_Q$ on the self-dual manifold for arbitrary $L_y$ — beyond the finite-width evidence — including a characterization of the possible types of arcs that run to complex infinity and their dependence on the boundary conditions.

These three items form one coherent objective: a complete structure theorem for the zero-accumulation locus of the self-dual Potts strip at arbitrary width, of which item (a) is the core, and items (b) and (c) the explicitly flagged boundary questions of the same locus.

The verification contract below evaluates answers to this statement. It does not narrow or redefine the research question.

Known solving difficulties:

- No closed forms: the CDBC transfer-matrix eigenvalues are roots of high-degree characteristic polynomials (already 126 eigenvalues at width $L_y=4$), so a proof cannot proceed by explicit eigenvalue comparison and must exploit structural or representation-theoretic properties.
- Global equimodular analysis: the region count and the dominance assignment require controlling which eigenvalue has maximal modulus throughout the complex $v$ and $Q$ planes, not only on the real axis.
- Representation-theoretic input: the Beraha-value crossing positions and the odd/even parity effect on the semi-infinite segment suggest that Temperley–Lieb and quantum-group ($U_q(\mathrm{sl}_2)$) degeneracy structure at roots of unity must be combined with finite-width transfer-matrix combinatorics, as in the boundary blob-algebra analysis of Jacobsen–Saleur.
- Asymptotics for noncompactness: a general proof requires sector-by-sector control of eigenvalue magnitudes as $|v|,|Q|\to\infty$ and a classification of the arcs running to complex infinity, together with their dependence on boundary conditions.
- The outside-$[0,4]$ question may demand exact or certified high-precision locus computations at widths $L_y\ge 5$, which have never been published and whose transfer-matrix dimension grows rapidly with width.
- The conjecture might simply be false at some larger width, so a valid resolution could equally take the form of an exhaustive finite-width counterexample computation rather than a proof.

## Current Progress

- Status: `ready`

Source and conjecture, verified against the arXiv full text (Shu-Chiuan Chang and Robert Shrock, Int. J. Mod. Phys. B 21, 1755 (2007), DOI 10.1142/S021797920703703x, arXiv:cond-mat/0602178, https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0602178; LKM open-question node gcn_0d34220baa374982): the authors computed the exact zero-accumulation loci on the self-dual manifold $Q=v^2$ for CDBC strips of widths $1\le L_y\le 4$ (plus free-longitudinal-boundary variants) and posed the Section V conjecture for arbitrary width reproduced in the problem statement. Their sentence 'These properties do not preclude the possibility that, for sufficiently wide strips, the locus $\mathcal{B}_Q$ may also cross the real $Q$ axis at points in the intervals $Q<0$ or $Q>4$' is an explicitly open question, not a claim. Source-fidelity corrections to the LKM paraphrase: (a) the semi-infinite segments $-\infty\le v\le -2$ and $4\le Q\le \infty$ are present for odd $L_y$ and absent for the even widths computed (the LKM phrase 'for even versus odd $L_y$' is ambiguous on this); (b) LKM item (iv), the deferral to other strip types, boundary conditions and lattices, is excluded from the open core because the authors delivered it themselves in their companion paper (next entry); (c) LKM item (iii), noncompactness, is a property demonstrated for the computed widths and asserted generally, but no proof for arbitrary $L_y$ or characterization of the arcs to infinity is given — a correctly inferred open item, though not posed as a numbered problem in the paper.

Companion paper, verified in full text (Chang and Shrock, J. Phys. A 39, 10277 (2006), DOI 10.1088/0305-4470/39/33/002, arXiv:cond-mat/0602440, https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0602440): extends the exact-locus analysis to square-lattice strips with free, cyclic, Möbius, cylindrical, toroidal and Klein-bottle boundary conditions and to triangular- and honeycomb-lattice strips on their ferromagnetic critical manifolds (e.g. $Q=v^2(v+3)$ for the triangular lattice), thereby delivering the deferred extensions of LKM item (iv); it poses analogous arbitrary-width conjectures of its own (for cyclic strips: real-axis crossings at $Q=4$ and at the even Beraha values $Q_{2\ell}$, $1\le\ell\le L_y$, with additional crossings on the negative real $Q$ axis appearing for widths $L_y\ge3$) and invokes Saleur's 1991 eigenvalue-degeneracy result to explain isolated accumulation at even Beraha values. Neither paper proves any arbitrary-width conjecture. The negative-real-axis crossings of the cyclic (non-self-dual) strips at $L_y\ge3$ sharpen item (b): crossings outside $0\le Q\le 4$ do occur in a neighboring strip family, while for the CDBC self-dual strips none were found up to $L_y=4$.

Underlying exact mechanism (H. Saleur, Nucl. Phys. B 360, 219 (1991), DOI 10.1016/0550-3213(91)90402-J, https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(91)90402-J): established the special role of Beraha numbers in the two-dimensional antiferromagnetic Potts model, including exact degeneracies in magnitude of transfer-matrix eigenvalues in adjacent sector numbers on cyclic strips at $Q=Q_{2\ell}$. This is the backbone for the isolated zeros at even Beraha values; it does not address the crossing arcs, the region count, or the dominance structure.

Strongest later progress (Jesper Lykke Jacobsen and Hubert Saleur, J. Stat. Phys. 132, 707 (2008), DOI 10.1007/s10955-008-9585-0, arXiv:0803.2665, https://arxiv.org/abs/0803.2665, verified in full text): their boundary chromatic polynomial / boundary Temperley–Lieb (blob) algebra analysis shows that dominant eigenvalues in the $L$- and $(L+2)$-leg sectors of finite-width annuli cross exactly at $r=(L+1)/(t-1)$ and that dominant amplitudes vanish at $r=L/(t-1)$ ($L$ even), by representation theory (merging representations at roots of unity, $U_q(\mathrm{sl}_2)$ quantum-group structure), with a width cutoff $L\le 2W$ and termination of the Berker–Kadanoff phase at $Q_c=4$. Their Fig. 6 reproduces the Chang–Shrock $W=3$ annulus data (the CDBC self-dual strip is the case $Q_s=Q-1$, $v_e=\pm\sqrt Q$) with what they call perfect agreement, and the real accumulation points agree for $W=4$ as well (the complete $W=4$ limiting curve was not computed). This derives the real-axis crossing positions and the special role of $Q=4$ at physics level of rigor, but does not address the complex-arc structure, the region count, the dominance assignment off the real axis, the odd/even parity of the semi-infinite segment, noncompactness, or crossings outside $[0,4]$. The conjecture as a whole remains unproven.

Confirmation of difficulty and adjacent results: J.-M. Billiot, F. Corset and E. Fontenas (J. Stat. Phys. 139, 960 (2010), DOI 10.1007/s10955-010-9977-9, arXiv:0905.2863, https://arxiv.org/abs/0905.2863) computed complex zeros for other self-dual graph families (double-edge triangle strip, wheel, multi-edge cycle) and explicitly note that for self-dual square-lattice strips the eigenvalues are roots of high-degree polynomials, making the degeneration curves of the dominant eigenvalue hard to locate; they resolve nothing for the square-lattice strips. Jesús Salas and Alan D. Sokal (J. Stat. Phys. 144, 1028 (2011), DOI 10.1007/s10955-011-0292-x, https://doi.org/10.1007/s10955-011-0292-x) studied extra-vertex boundary conditions on square strips, but on the chromatic line $v=-1$, a different manifold from $Q=v^2$ — relevant only as context for the boundary-condition family. Jacobsen, Richard and Salas (Nucl. Phys. B 743, 153 (2006), DOI 10.1016/j.nuclphysb.2006.02.033, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nuclphysb.2006.02.033) mapped the complex-temperature phase diagram that the strip regions approximate, without addressing the strip locus itself.

## Scientific Significance

Affected-field significance: `medium`.

A resolution would upgrade the Chang–Shrock finite-width exact computations into a complete structure theorem for the zero-accumulation locus of the Potts (Tutte) partition function on self-dual square-lattice strips at arbitrary width — the first such arbitrary-width locus structure for a nontrivial strip family — and would rigorously anchor the observed Beraha-value crossing pattern in Temperley–Lieb and quantum-group representation theory. Direct consequences within the partition-function-zeros subfield: the region count and dominance map fix the analytic structure of the infinite-strip free energy across the complex $Q$ plane; the noncompactness result fixes its large-$Q$ and large-$v$ asymptotics (equivalently, the failure of a Taylor expansion in $1/Q$); and the outside-$[0,4]$ question determines whether new real-axis singularities appear as the quasi-one-dimensional strip approaches two dimensions, which matters for how finite-width strip data are used to infer two-dimensional critical behavior. The impact is direct but specialized: it chiefly advances the exact-results and partition-function-zeros community rather than the broader study of phase transitions.
