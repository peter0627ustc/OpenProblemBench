# ORB-PHYS-47: Rigorous, assumption-free equivalence of the two contour prescriptions in superstring perturbation theory (low spacetime dimensions, stub-free intermediate-state sums, and controlled integral interchanges)

Superstring perturbation theory requires a prescription for deforming integration cycles to define divergent S-matrix amplitudes, and two such prescriptions are known: deformation of the moduli-space integration cycles into complexified moduli space (the string-theoretic $i\varepsilon$), and deformation of the loop-energy integration contours of superstring field theory Feynman diagrams. Sen (2017) proved the two prescriptions equivalent to all orders in perturbation theory, which establishes unitarity of the moduli-space prescription — but the proof explicitly assumes spacetime dimension $D>2$, inserts vertex 'stubs' to force convergence of the infinite sum over intermediate string states, and justifies key interchanges of integrals only by formal phase estimates. The open problem is to establish the same all-order equivalence while removing these three crutches: extend it to $D\le 2$ where infrared divergences of individual massless propagators are severe, control the sum over intermediate string states without stub suppression factors (or prove independence of the stub choice), and supply rigorous functional-analytic justification for the Schwinger-parameter/loop-momentum interchanges and contour deformations, including exceptional kinematic subspaces and higher-codimension boundary contributions in moduli space. A systematic audit of the citing literature (INSPIRE, Semantic Scholar) shows that subsequent work on Cutkosky rules, analyticity and crossing, two-dimensional string amplitudes, and explicit tree-level and one-loop contour constructions leaves all three gaps untouched.

## Background

In first-quantized string theory, an $n$-particle scattering amplitude at genus $g$ (i.e. at order $g$ in the topological expansion, where the world-sheet — the two-dimensional surface swept out by a propagating string — is a Riemann surface with $g$ handles) is computed as an integral over the moduli space of Riemann surfaces of genus $g$ with $n$ punctures (the space of inequivalent complex structures on the surface, with the punctures marking the positions of the external string vertex operators, the local insertions on the surface that create or absorb the external particles). When the total center-of-mass energy of the incoming particles exceeds the threshold for production of some final state — which, because string theory has states of arbitrarily small mass (the massless graviton, and in many backgrounds tachyons), happens generically — these integrals diverge along the real integration cycles, just as loop momentum integrals in quantum field theory develop infrared (long-distance, low-energy) singularities from massless particles.

Two systematic prescriptions repair this. The first, the string-theoretic analog of the Feynman $i\varepsilon$ (the infinitesimal displacement of propagator poles that makes loop integrals well-defined) proposed by Witten (2013), deforms the integration cycles over moduli space into the complexified moduli space, so that the divergent oscillatory integrals become absolutely convergent ones over deformed cycles. The second comes from superstring field theory (SFT), the second-quantized formulation in which the S-matrix is a sum of Feynman diagrams: because the SFT interaction vertices grow exponentially for large timelike momenta, the integration contours over the loop energies cannot lie along the real axis and must instead run from $-i\infty$ to $+i\infty$ while navigating around the propagator poles in the complex energy plane (Pius and Sen, 2016). Sen (2017) proved that these two prescriptions agree to all orders in perturbation theory, so that the unitarity (probability conservation) of the SFT-based amplitudes — where it follows from the standard Cutkosky-rule analysis (the Cutkosky rules determine the discontinuities of Feynman amplitudes across branch cuts and thereby express unitarity) — carries over to the world-sheet (moduli-space) prescription.

The proof of Sen (2017) proceeds by introducing a complex parameter $u$ that scales all loop energies, regarding the amplitude as the limit $u\to 1$ taken from within the first quadrant of the complex $u$-plane, replacing each propagator by a Schwinger-parameter integral (a $t$-integral representation of $(k^2+m^2)^{-1}$, where $t$ is a proper-time parameter), and showing that both prescriptions yield the same analytic function of $u$ there. The argument, however, rests on three explicit props. First, convergence of the large-$t$ regions uses the factor $t^{-D/2}$ from Gaussian loop-momentum integration, which suffices only in spacetime dimension $D>2$; for $D\le 2$ single massless propagators produce genuine divergences of the form $\int d^D k/k^2$, and the paper explicitly sets this regime aside. Second, the sum over intermediate string states is made convergent by adding 'stubs' to the vertices — lengths of cylinder inserted at each puncture, implemented as factors $(\lambda_i(y))^{m_i^2}$ multiplying the contribution of a state of mass $m_i$ — chosen small enough to beat the density of string states, which grows like $e^{c\,m}$ at mass level $m$; no first-principles derivation of this choice, or control of the undeformed sum, is given. Third, the manipulations that replace propagators by $t$-integrals and interchange them with the loop-momentum and moduli integrals are justified only by phase estimates and by the avoidance of pinch singularities (contours trapped between approaching poles) for $u$ in the first quadrant; loci such as internal energies $k_i^0=0$, where the damping factors vanish, are dismissed as measure zero, and possible boundary contributions when contours are deformed near higher-codimension faces of the compactified moduli space (the boundary strata added upon compactification, where the Riemann surface degenerates into long thin tubes — precisely the regions associated with propagators going on-shell) are not analyzed. The equivalence result underpins later all-order results in the same program — Cutkosky rules, unitarity, and analyticity/crossing symmetry (de Lacroix, Erbin, and Sen, 2019) — all of which inherit these assumptions. Standard references (Erbin's textbook String Field Theory: A Modern Introduction; the 2024 review by Sen and Zwiebach) present the equivalence as established in the critical-dimension, stub-based setting (the critical dimension, ten for superstrings, being the spacetime dimension at which the world-sheet theory is internally consistent) without treating the three gaps as open problems, while recent work on explicit contour constructions (Eberhardt–Mizera; Manschot–Wang) and on two-dimensional string amplitudes addresses related questions at tree level and one loop only.

## Problem Statement

Establish the equivalence of the two contour prescriptions in superstring perturbation theory — the deformation of moduli-space integration cycles into complexified moduli space (the string-theoretic $i\varepsilon$) and the deformation of loop-energy integration contours of superstring field theory Feynman diagrams — to all orders in perturbation theory, without the auxiliary assumptions on which the existing proof of Sen (2017) rests. Concretely, discharge the three explicitly flagged gaps, which jointly support this single objective of putting the all-order equivalence (and thereby the unitarity of the moduli-space prescription) on a sound footing:

1. Low dimensions: extend the equivalence to spacetime dimension $D\le 2$, where a single massless propagator produces genuine infrared divergences of the form $\int d^D k/k^2$ and the existing convergence argument, based on $t^{-D/2}$ suppression of the large-Schwinger-parameter regions, fails. Since the amplitudes themselves are ill-defined there in their naive form, this may require reorganizing the quantity being compared (for example, by resummation of massless exchanges, or by passing to inclusive observables — cross sections summed over undetected soft particles), provided the two prescriptions are applied to the same reorganized object and shown to agree for it.

2. Stub independence: control the infinite sum over intermediate string states, whose level density grows like $e^{c\,m}$, without the inserted stub factors $(\lambda_i(y))^{m_i^2}$ that are chosen small by hand to force convergence — either by proving convergence of the undeformed sum over all string states, by deriving the admissible class of stub choices from first principles, or by proving that the final amplitudes are independent of the stub choice.

3. Rigor of the interchanges: supply a rigorous functional-analytic justification for replacing propagators by Schwinger $t_i$-integrals and interchanging them with the loop-momentum and moduli integrations — including the treatment of the loci with vanishing internal energies $k_i^0=0$ currently dismissed as measure zero, of exceptional kinematic subspaces, and of possible boundary contributions arising when contours are deformed near higher-codimension faces of the compactified moduli space.

An answer that refutes extendability — for instance, an explicit demonstration that in some $D\le 2$ theory or in a stub-free loop computation the two prescriptions yield inequivalent amplitudes — also counts as a resolution, provided the discrepancy is established for the same quantity under both prescriptions.

The verification contract below evaluates answers to this statement. It does not narrow or redefine the research question.

Known solving difficulties:

- In $D\le 2$ the amplitudes are infrared divergent even at tree level through single massless propagators, so the objects to be compared must first be reorganized (e.g., by resumming massless exchanges or passing to inclusive cross sections), and the two prescriptions must then be shown to act on the same reorganized object — a restructuring of the question before any estimate can begin.
- The density of intermediate string states grows exponentially ($e^{c\,m}$ at mass level $m$), and the stub factors suppress it only by an unbounded over-all choice; removing them requires a new convergence mechanism for infinite-level sums at arbitrary loop order, or an independence argument that must hold uniformly over moduli space.
- Interchanging Schwinger $t_i$-integrals with loop-momentum and moduli integrals requires uniform convergence or dominated-convergence estimates for oscillatory integrands with complex phases, precisely near pinch surfaces and exceptional kinematic subspaces (such as $k_i^0=0$) where the exponential damping factors degenerate.
- Contour deformations near higher-codimension faces of the compactified moduli space can pick up boundary contributions that mix with infrared effects; controlling them needs geometric analysis beyond the standard toolbox of perturbative quantum field theory.
- No existing formalism combines the required functional analysis with superstring field theory Feynman rules and the geometry of moduli spaces, so the solution likely requires developing new mathematical infrastructure rather than applying a known technique.
- The problem is genuinely all-orders: any argument must handle arbitrary genus and arbitrary numbers of propagators simultaneously, since each assumption (dimension, stubs, interchanges) enters at every loop order.

## Current Progress

Sen (2017, arXiv:1610.00443) states the problem. All three claimed gaps appear verbatim in the paper — the $D>2$ restriction ('For $D\le 2$ we anyway expect infrared divergences ... Therefore we shall not worry about this case'), the role of stub factors $(\lambda_i(y))^{m_i^2}$ in taming the $e^{c\,m}$ growth of the density of intermediate string states, and the 'formal' status of the propagator-to-$t$-integral replacements and their interchanges with loop-momentum integrals, justified only by phase estimates and avoidance of pinch singularities for $u$ in the first quadrant.

Direct continuation of the program keeps the assumptions: Pius–Sen (arXiv:1604.01783) proved Cutkosky rules for superstring field theory amplitudes to all orders, and de Lacroix–Erbin–Sen (arXiv:1810.07197) proved analyticity and crossing symmetry to all orders — the latter still 'requires, as usual, explicitly subtracting / regulating the non-analyticities associated with massless particles'. Neither work addresses the $D\le 2$ regime, stub removal, or the formal status of the integral interchanges. Erbin's textbook (arXiv:2301.01686) and the Sen–Zwiebach review (arXiv:2405.19421) cite the equivalence as a consistency pillar for the standard (critical-dimension, stub-based) setting and do not list the three gaps as open problems.

Progress on the $D=2$ physics, but not on the equivalence: Sen (arXiv:2003.12076, 2012.11624, 1908.02782) and Balthazar–Rodriguez–Yin (arXiv:2201.05621) show that in two-dimensional (non-critical) string theories, world-sheet divergences can be tamed by string-field-theory-motivated contour choices, yielding finite complex amplitudes for real energies, and Sen (arXiv:2208.07385) constructs infrared-finite semi-inclusive cross sections in 2D type 0B string theory. These works demonstrate that contour-deformation logic survives in $D=2$ in specific tree-level and instanton-level settings, but none establishes — or formulates — the all-order equivalence of the moduli-space and loop-energy prescriptions in $D\le 2$.

A recent line of work makes the $i\varepsilon$/contour prescriptions explicit and computable at low genus: Eberhardt–Mizera (arXiv:2302.12733) evaluate one-loop open-string amplitudes at finite $\alpha'$ using Rademacher's fractal contour for the modular parameter; Eberhardt–Mizera (arXiv:2403.07051) engineer Lorentzian Pochhammer contours on genus-zero moduli spaces giving numerical tree-level amplitudes directly in physical kinematics for $n\le 9$; Arkani-Hamed–Eberhardt–Huang–Mizera (arXiv:2201.11575) prove tree-level unitarity of superstring amplitudes in $D\le 6$ directly from contour-integral representations; and Manschot–Wang (arXiv:2411.02517) prove that the string $i\varepsilon$-prescription for one-loop zero- and two-point amplitudes is equivalent to a regularization by generalized exponential integrals, in agreement with the Eberhardt–Mizera contour. All of these operate at tree level or one loop with explicit regulators and do not address the all-orders equivalence proof or its assumptions.

Related negative signal on automaticity of equivalence: Buoninfante (arXiv:2205.15348) shows that several contour prescriptions equivalent in local quantum field theories become inequivalent for string-inspired nonlocal vertices already at one loop (bubble diagram), reinforcing that the equivalence proven by Sen is a nontrivial result that must be re-established, not assumed, when the setting is changed.

On the stub question: Sen (arXiv:1902.00263) shows that string field theory serves as a world-sheet ultraviolet regulator and that tree-level results are independent of the auxiliary SFT choices; hyperbolic string field theory constructions (referenced in the Sen–Zwiebach review) build vertices with geodesic stubs integrated over. No work found proves convergence of the stub-free sum over intermediate string states at arbitrary loop order, derives the admissible stub choices from first principles, or proves amplitude-level stub independence at loop level.

No later work resolves, refutes, or even partially closes any of the three gaps.

The remaining question is precisely the removal of the proof's three explicit assumptions while preserving its objective — the all-order equivalence of the two contour prescriptions, and hence the unitarity of the moduli-space $i\varepsilon$ prescription, without dimension, stub, or formality crutches.

## Scientific Significance

Affected-field significance: `medium`.

Direct impact on the mathematical foundations of string perturbation theory: the result that the world-sheet (moduli-space) $i\varepsilon$ prescription is unitary would no longer rest on the crutches of $D>2$, hand-chosen stubs, and formal integral interchanges, extending its regime of validity to the low-dimensional and non-critical (away from the ten-dimensional consistency point) settings where two-dimensional string theory and its matrix-model dualities (exactly solvable quantum-mechanical dual descriptions) live, and giving the first stub-free or rigorously controlled account of intermediate-state sums. The accepted physical conclusions in the critical dimension would not change — the equivalence is already believed and proven under the stated assumptions — so the effect is a hardening and broadening of the rigorous foundation (an enabling capability for future formal work on string amplitudes) rather than a change in the field's core physical knowledge.
