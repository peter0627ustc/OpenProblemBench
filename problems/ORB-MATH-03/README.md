# ORB-MATH-03: Algebraicity of Weil classes on polarized abelian varieties of Weil type

The Hodge conjecture predicts that every rational $(p,p)$-cohomology class on a smooth complex projective variety is a rational combination of classes of algebraic subvarieties. A polarized abelian variety of Weil type is a triple $(A,\eta,h)$ in which $A$ is an abelian variety of even dimension carrying an action of a CM field $K$ whose eigenspaces on $H^{1}$ are balanced with respect to the Hodge decomposition; André Weil, and later Deligne in generality, associated to such a triple a distinguished space $HW(A,\eta)$ of Hodge classes, the Weil classes, which for a generic triple lie outside the algebra generated by divisor classes, so their algebraicity is exactly the new content of the Hodge conjecture for these varieties. The problem is to determine for which triples $(A,\eta,h)$ the Weil classes are algebraic. Work of Markman (2025) settled all abelian fourfolds of Weil type with $K$ imaginary quadratic and sixfolds of discriminant $-1$, which yields the Hodge conjecture for abelian fourfolds (indeed for all abelian varieties of dimension at most five, by reductions of Moonen–Zarhin and others); but sixfolds of nontrivial discriminant, all dimensions $\ge 8$, and all CM fields of degree $>2$ remain open. A full positive answer would imply the Hodge conjecture for all CM abelian varieties, Grothendieck's standard conjectures for all abelian varieties, and the Tate conjecture for abelian varieties over finite fields; a counterexample would refute the Hodge conjecture itself.

## Background

A rational cohomology class of a smooth complex projective variety is called a Hodge class if it has type $(p,p)$ in the Hodge decomposition; the Hodge conjecture predicts that every Hodge class is algebraic, i.e. a rational linear combination of cohomology classes of complex algebraic subvarieties of codimension $p$. For $p=1$ this is the Lefschetz $(1,1)$ theorem, which identifies divisor classes with the rational classes of type $(1,1)$, but already for abelian varieties of dimension $\ge 2$ and $p=2$ it is wide open in general. On an abelian variety $A$ the cohomology ring is the exterior algebra of $H^{1}(A,\mathbb{Q})$, and a polarization (the class $h$ of an ample divisor) singles out divisor classes in $H^{1,1}$ and induces the Rosati involution on the endomorphism algebra $\mathrm{End}^{0}(A)=\mathrm{End}(A)\otimes\mathbb{Q}$: the anti-involution $f\mapsto f^{\dagger}$ determined by $h$, in the same way a Hermitian form determines adjoint maps.

Let $K$ be a CM field, that is, a totally imaginary quadratic extension $K/F$ of a totally real number field $F$, and set $e=[K:\mathbb{Q}]$. A pair $(A,\eta)$ consisting of an abelian variety and an algebra embedding $\eta\colon K\to\mathrm{End}^{0}(A)$ is of Weil type if $H^{1}(A,\mathbb{Q})$ is free of rank $d$ over $K$ and the eigenspace decomposition of $H^{1}(A,\mathbb{C})$ under $\eta(K)$ is balanced: for every embedding $\sigma\colon K\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}$ one has $\dim H^{1,0}_{\sigma}=\dim H^{0,1}_{\sigma}=d/2$. Then $\dim A=ed/2$ is even, say $2n$. A polarization $h$ is compatible if the Rosati involution acts on $\eta(K)$ as complex conjugation; such an $h$ defines a $K$-valued Hermitian form $H$ on $H^{1}(A,\mathbb{Q})$, whose determinant class in $\mathbb{Q}^{\times}/\mathrm{Nm}(K^{\times})$ is the discriminant, a discrete invariant labelling the components of the moduli space of polarized Weil-type triples. The triple is of split Weil type if $H$ admits an isotropic subspace of half the dimension; discriminant $-1$ (trivial discriminant) is the prototypical case.

The space of Weil classes is
$$
HW(A,\eta)=\wedge^{d}_{K}H^{1}(A,\mathbb{Q})\;\subset\;H^{d/2,d/2}(A,\mathbb{Q}),
$$
a $K$-line, hence an $e$-dimensional $\mathbb{Q}$-vector space (two-dimensional over $\mathbb{Q}$ in Weil's classical imaginary-quadratic case). The balanced-eigenspace condition forces these classes to be of type $(d/2,d/2)$, and they are absolute Hodge, meaning they remain Hodge classes under every embedding of the coefficient field into $\mathbb{C}$ and correspond compatibly to étale classes - a rigidity property strictly weaker than algebraicity. Moonen and Zarhin proved that the Weil classes are exceptional: for a generic polarized triple of Weil type with $d\ge 4$, the Hodge ring (the graded algebra of rational Hodge classes) is generated by divisor classes together with $HW(A,\eta)$, and $HW(A,\eta)$ meets the subalgebra generated by divisor classes trivially. Their algebraicity is thus precisely the essential new content of the Hodge conjecture for abelian varieties.

The construction originates in Weil's 1977 note 'Abelian varieties and the Hodge ring', written after Mumford found abelian varieties whose rational $(p,p)$-classes are not generated by divisor classes; in a 1965 letter Weil wrote of the resulting 'abnormal classes' that he would 'rather attempt to show there is no such cycle'. Deligne generalized the construction to arbitrary CM fields, and Moonen–Zarhin systematized the theory, proving in particular that Weil classes on Weil-type varieties account for all exceptional Hodge (and Tate) classes on abelian varieties of dimension $\le 5$ up to divisor classes.

The significance of the problem is structural. André proved that every Hodge class on an abelian variety of CM type is a sum of pullbacks of Weil classes on abelian varieties of split Weil type, so the Hodge conjecture for all CM abelian varieties reduces to algebraicity of split Weil classes. Milne proved that, conditional on Weil-class algebraicity, the Tate conjecture for divisors (the statement that $\ell$-adic Tate classes on an abelian variety over a finite field are generated by algebraic cycles) and Grothendieck's standard conjectures (algebraicity of the Künneth components and of the Lefschetz operator's inverse) follow for large classes of abelian varieties, including products with CM elliptic factors and their reductions.

Algebraicity is known in an expanding family of cases: Schoen proved it for fourfolds of Weil type with $K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$ and, in an addendum, for arbitrary discriminant with that $K$ and for sixfolds of trivial discriminant; Koike treated $K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-1})$; Markman settled the generic fourfold of discriminant $1$ for all imaginary quadratic $K$ using algebraic cycles on the intermediate Jacobians (complex tori built from a variety's odd-degree cohomology, which receive classes of algebraic cycles) of hyper-Kähler varieties - simply connected projective varieties with trivial canonical bundle and, up to scalar, a unique holomorphic symplectic two-form - of generalized Kummer type, i.e. deformation-equivalent to a fibre of the summation map from a Hilbert scheme of points on an abelian surface; and in 2025 Markman proved the sixfold case of discriminant $-1$ for every imaginary quadratic $K$, implying every abelian fourfold of Weil type, for all discriminants, via a degeneration argument of Schoen. Floccari–Fu and van Geemen–Rapagnetta gave independent alternative proofs for fourfolds of trivial discriminant via hyper-Kähler geometry. The current frontier is Markman's secant-sheaf strategy: when an abelian variety $X$ has real multiplication by $F$, the even cohomology of $X$ realizes the half-spin representation (one of the two irreducible spin representations of the spin group of a quadratic space) and contains the spinorial variety (a component of the Grassmannian of maximal isotropic subspaces); a rational $K$-secant plane to it determines a Weil-type structure on $X\times\hat{X}$, and the normalized Chern character (the characteristic class summarizing the Chern classes of a coherent sheaf) of a suitable derived transform - an integral functor between derived categories of coherent sheaves - of sheaves with Chern characters in the secant plane remains of Hodge type under all Weil-type deformations. If the transformed object is semiregular (a condition ensuring that it deforms along with the Weil-type data), algebraicity of all Weil classes on the entire moduli component follows; this semiregularity has been verified only for $\dim X\le 3$, which forces $K$ imaginary quadratic.

## Problem Statement

Let $K$ be a CM field, i.e. a totally imaginary quadratic extension of a totally real field $F$, and set $e=[K:\mathbb{Q}]$. A polarized abelian variety of Weil type is a triple $(A,\eta,h)$ consisting of a complex abelian variety $A$, an algebra embedding $\eta\colon K\to\mathrm{End}^{0}(A)$ such that the rank-$d$ $K$-module $H^{1}(A,\mathbb{Q})$ is balanced ($\dim H^{1,0}_{\sigma}=\dim H^{0,1}_{\sigma}=d/2$ for every embedding $\sigma\colon K\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}$), and an ample class $h$ compatible with $\eta$ in that the Rosati involution acts on $\eta(K)$ as complex conjugation. The space of Weil classes
$$
HW(A,\eta)=\wedge^{d}_{K}H^{1}(A,\mathbb{Q})\;\subset\;H^{d/2,d/2}(A,\mathbb{Q})
$$
is a $K$-line, $e$-dimensional over $\mathbb{Q}$, and consists of Hodge (indeed absolute Hodge) classes.

Determine for which polarized abelian varieties of Weil type the Weil classes are algebraic, i.e. spanned over $\mathbb{Q}$ by cohomology classes of algebraic cycles. Equivalently, settle Question 1.1 of Markman's survey (arXiv:2509.23403): does $HW(A,\eta)$ consist of algebraic classes for every triple $(A,\eta,h)$ of Weil type? A complete answer may take the form of (i) a proof of algebraicity for all triples; (ii) an explicit triple together with a rigorous proof that one of its Weil classes is not algebraic, which would refute the Hodge conjecture; or (iii) an exact characterization, in terms of the CM field $K$, the dimension, and the discriminant, of the triples for which algebraicity holds. The problem is posed in its natural generality - arbitrary CM field, arbitrary even dimension, arbitrary discriminant - and any answer must account for the established boundary cases: algebraicity is already proved for every abelian fourfold of Weil type with $K$ imaginary quadratic (all discriminants) and for abelian sixfolds of Weil type of discriminant $-1$ with $K$ imaginary quadratic, while sixfolds of nontrivial discriminant, all dimensions $\ge 8$, and all CM fields with $[K:\mathbb{Q}]>2$ are open.

The verification contract below evaluates answers to this statement. It does not narrow or redefine the research question.

Known solving difficulties:

- Semiregularity bottleneck: the only currently unconditional route (Markman's secant-sheaf strategy) requires an object that deforms with the Weil-type data over a positive-dimensional moduli component, and semiregularity of that object has been proved only in dimension at most 3, which forces $K$ imaginary quadratic.
- Absence of geometric cycle sources: all known proofs ultimately import cycles from hyper-Kähler geometry (generalized Kummer or OG6 type) or from curves with automorphisms; no comparable source is known for CM fields of degree $>2$.
- CM rigidity: at CM points the Weil-type moduli collapses to zero dimensions, so every deformation-based method fails precisely where explicit new examples are easiest to write down (Mostaed's three compounding obstructions).
- Discriminant control: Schoen's degeneration transfers algebraicity across discriminants only in dimension four; no analogue is known for sixfolds of nontrivial discriminant or for higher dimensions.
- Construction of secant sheaves: even producing sheaves with Chern characters in the required secant plane for abelian varieties with real multiplication by a general totally real field is open, and the weaker algebraicity criterion proposed in the survey (Question 11.4) is unverified for every proposed higher-dimensional example.
- Refutation is equally hard: Weil expected a counterexample and the tropical-geometric search program (Kontsevich's idea as recorded by Zharkov) has produced none; proving non-algebraicity of a specific Hodge class requires a motivic or cycle-theoretic obstruction that no current technique supplies.

## Current Progress

- Status: `ready`

Source fidelity: the LKM formulation was checked against the primary sources and found faithful in substance. Its three records correspond to Markman's secant-sheaf strategy (arXiv:2509.23079 and arXiv:2509.23403), the obstruction analysis of Mostaed (arXiv:2603.20268), and Milne's conditional theorems (arXiv:2112.12815), whose main statements indeed assume algebraicity of Weil classes (for example: if the Weil classes on $(A\times B^{m},\mathbb{Q})$ are algebraic, then the Hodge, Tate, and standard conjectures hold for the corresponding products and their reductions). One correction to the paraphrase: the space of Weil classes is one-dimensional over $K$ but $[K:\mathbb{Q}]$-dimensional over $\mathbb{Q}$ (two-dimensional over $\mathbb{Q}$ in Weil's classical imaginary-quadratic setting), not one-dimensional over $\mathbb{Q}$.

Origins: Weil introduced abelian varieties of Weil type and their exceptional classes in a 1977 note (DOI: 10.1007/978-1-4757-1705-1_123), after Mumford found that rational $(p,p)$-classes on abelian varieties need not be generated by divisor classes; in a 1965 letter Weil wrote that he would rather attempt to show that no algebraic cycle represents the new classes. Deligne (DOI: 10.1007/BFb0091629) generalized the construction to arbitrary CM fields, and Moonen–Zarhin (DOI: 10.1515/crll.1998.034) proved that the Weil classes are the exceptional Hodge (and, over number fields, Tate) classes: for a generic polarized triple with $d\ge 4$ the Hodge ring is generated by divisor classes and the Weil classes, which intersect the divisor algebra trivially.

Conditional significance: André (1992) proved that every Hodge class on an abelian variety of CM type is a sum of pullbacks of Weil classes on abelian varieties of split Weil type, reducing the Hodge conjecture for CM abelian varieties to the algebraicity of split Weil classes. Milne (arXiv:2112.12815) proved that, assuming Weil-class algebraicity, the Tate and standard conjectures follow for large classes of abelian varieties (products with CM elliptic factors and their reductions over finite fields); his expository paper (arXiv:2010.08857) records that the Hodge conjecture for abelian varieties would follow from the standard Lefschetz-type conjecture together with the CM case.

Partial algebraicity results before 2025: Schoen proved algebraicity for fourfolds of Weil type with $K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$ (1988), extended in his 1998 addendum to arbitrary discriminant for that field and to sixfolds of trivial discriminant, via degeneration of families of self-products with automorphisms; Koike (2004) handled $K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-1})$; Markman (JEMS 2023) proved the generic fourfold of discriminant $1$ for all imaginary quadratic $K$ using cycles on intermediate Jacobians of hyper-Kähler varieties of generalized Kummer type; van Geemen (arXiv:2108.02087) analyzed which Hodge classes on products of abelian surfaces deform to Weil classes on fourfolds.

Breakthrough: Markman (arXiv:2502.03415, Theorem 1.5.1) proved that the Hodge-Weil classes are algebraic for all polarized abelian sixfolds of Weil type of discriminant $-1$, for every imaginary quadratic $K$, by constructing a semiregular reflexive sheaf on $X\times\hat{X}$ ($X$ the Jacobian of a non-hyperelliptic genus 3 curve) that deforms over the entire 9-dimensional moduli space. Via Schoen's degeneration argument this implies algebraicity for every abelian fourfold of Weil type, all imaginary quadratic $K$, all discriminants (Corollary 1.6.1), and hence the Hodge conjecture for abelian fourfolds; the February 2026 survey (arXiv:2509.23403, Corollary 1.3) extends the consequence to all abelian varieties of dimension $\le 5$, combining results of Moonen–Zarhin and others. Independent alternative proofs for fourfolds of trivial discriminant were given by Floccari–Fu (arXiv:2504.13607, via singular hyper-Kähler varieties of OG6 type, i.e. deformations of O'Grady's six-dimensional exceptional hyper-Kähler variety, also proving the Hodge and Tate conjectures for OG6-type varieties) and by van Geemen–Rapagnetta (arXiv:2607.18341, via hyper-Kähler sixfolds of K3$^{[3]}$ type, i.e. deformations of the Hilbert scheme of three points on a K3 surface).

Conditional reduction and its limit: Markman's strategy (arXiv:2509.23079; arXiv:2509.23403, Section 4) reduces the algebraicity of all Weil classes on a connected component of the Weil-type moduli space to one input: a coherent sheaf $E$ on $X\times\hat{X}$ whose normalized Chern character stays of Hodge type under all Weil-type deformations and which is semiregular; semiregularity then forces $E$ to deform along the component and a nonzero Weil class to be algebraic. Semiregularity has been verified only when $\dim X\le 3$, which forces $K$ imaginary quadratic; for general CM fields only examples of sheaves with Chern characters in the required secant plane are available, some with the algebraicity criterion still unchecked, and the semiregularity of the general object 'has not been addressed yet' (arXiv:2509.23079). Section 12 of the survey leaves dimensions $\ge 8$ and $[K:\mathbb{Q}]>2$ entirely open, proposing only speculative secant constructions whose criterion is yet to be verified.

Evidence of genuine obstruction: Mostaed (arXiv:2603.20268) studies the intersection of McMullen's compact Kobayashi-geodesic curve with the Weil locus inside the Hilbert modular sixfold attached to $\mathbb{Q}(\cos(\pi/21))$ and proves this intersection is finite, with every point CM; the intersection is possibly empty, and its non-emptiness is reduced to 2816 explicit algebraic equations for the prime 43. For any point of the intersection, the Weil classes in $H^{3,3}$ are absolute Hodge but inaccessible to all existing algebraicity methods, for three compounding reasons: the points are CM-isolated (no positive-dimensional Weil-type deformation family, so deformation and semiregularity arguments collapse), the Weil classes arise from the CM type of a degree-12 CM field rather than from secant geometry, and the discriminant is uncontrolled. The paper lists the remaining open computational and theoretical steps toward a new case of the Hodge conjecture for abelian sixfolds.

Refutation direction: Weil himself expected the classes to be non-algebraic, and Zharkov (arXiv:2002.02347) records a tropical-geometry program of Kontsevich to search for counterexamples by degenerating Weil classes in tropical abelian varieties; no counterexample has been found.

Coverage and residual uncertainty: the audit searched the arXiv listing (four query families), zbMATH and Crossref, and the forward citation graph of arXiv:2502.03415 (11 citing works through August 2026) and arXiv:2509.23403; no work claims general algebraicity or a counterexample, and the most recent papers (February–July 2026) uniformly treat everything beyond fourfolds and sixfolds of discriminant $-1$ with imaginary quadratic $K$ as open. Residual uncertainties: Mostaed's original finiteness results sit in a single 2026 preprint and were not independently verified (his account of Markman's theorem was checked against Markman's own papers and matches); the exact relation between 'split Weil type' and 'discriminant $-1$' for sixfolds is taken from the sources rather than independently derived; journal-only literature was inspected through metadata (Crossref, zbMATH) rather than full texts.

## Scientific Significance

Affected-field significance: `high`.

The impact is direct and on core knowledge of algebraic cycles and motives. A positive answer in full generality would establish the Hodge conjecture for all abelian varieties of CM type (by André's reduction of their Hodge classes to split Weil classes), which in turn implies Grothendieck's standard conjectures for all abelian varieties and, by Milne's theorems, the Tate conjecture for abelian varieties over finite fields and a workable theory of abelian motives in mixed characteristic. A counterexample would refute the Hodge conjecture itself. Even partial progress on the open core (sixfolds of nontrivial discriminant, dimensions at least 8, CM fields of degree above 2) directly enlarges the class of abelian varieties for which the Tate and standard conjectures hold unconditionally.
