# ORB-PHYS-61: Explicit identification of the local and Fourier inverse Kasteleyn matrices for the critical Z-invariant Ising model on periodic isoradial graphs

The critical Z-invariant Ising model on an isoradial graph $G$ maps, via Fisher's correspondence, to a dimer model on a decorated Fisher graph $\mathcal{G}^D$. All dimer, hence Ising, correlations are Pfaffians of the inverse Kasteleyn matrix $K^{-1}$, and two independent exact formulas exist for its coefficients: a Fourier double-integral formula, valid when $G$ is $\mathbb{Z}^2$-periodic (Boutillier–de Tilière 2010), and a local contour-integral formula requiring no periodicity (Boutillier–de Tilière 2011). Uniqueness of the decaying inverse guarantees that the two coincide, but the authors state that they have 'not yet been able to understand the identity by an explicit computation', except on the square lattice. This problem asks for a direct, coefficient-level identification of the two formulas for every $\mathbb{Z}^2$-periodic isoradial graph. The analogous Fourier-to-local passage has since been carried out explicitly for the massive Laplacian Green function (2017) and for bipartite elliptic dimers on minimal graphs (2023), supplying the technique, but the non-bipartite Fisher-graph case remains open.

## Background

The Ising model on a finite graph $G$ assigns spins $\sigma_v\in\{-1,+1\}$ to vertices with probability proportional to $\exp(\sum_{e=uv}J_e\sigma_u\sigma_v)$, where $J_e$ are coupling constants. A planar graph is *isoradial* if it is embedded so that every face is inscribed in a circle of common radius, with circumcenters inside the faces; joining each circumcenter to the vertices of its face yields the *diamond graph*, all of whose faces are rhombi of side length one, and each edge $e$ of $G$ is the diagonal of exactly one rhombus, whose half-angle $\theta_e$ is attached to $e$. Requiring the Ising coupling constants to be invariant under star-triangle (Yang–Baxter) transformations of the underlying graph (*Z-invariance*) and to satisfy a generalized self-duality forces the *critical* values $J(\theta_e)=\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{1+\sin\theta_e}{\cos\theta_e}$ (equivalently $\sinh(2J_e)=\tan\theta_e$); the resulting critical Z-invariant Ising model, introduced by Baxter, contains the critical square, triangular, and honeycomb-lattice models as special cases.

Fisher's correspondence maps the Ising model on $G$ to a dimer model on a decorated graph $\mathcal{G}^D$ (the *Fisher graph*), obtained by replacing each vertex of $G$ by a ring of triangles; perfect matchings (dimer configurations) of $\mathcal{G}^D$ correspond to polygonal contour configurations of the Ising model. Dimer statistics are encoded in the *Kasteleyn matrix* $K$: a weighted, oriented adjacency matrix of $\mathcal{G}^D$ whose orientation is clockwise odd around every face, with edge weights $\nu_e$ explicit functions of the angles $\theta_e$. Correlations (and hence Ising observables such as spin and energy correlations) are Pfaffians of submatrices of an inverse $K^{-1}$ — a Pfaffian being a signed sum over pairings of indices, computable as a square root of a determinant — so explicit formulas for the coefficients of $K^{-1}$ are the central computational object of the theory.

Two independent routes to $K^{-1}$ exist. When $G$ is $\mathbb{Z}^2$-periodic, Boutillier–de Tilière computed $K^{-1}$ by Fourier transform: writing $\widehat K(z,w)$ for the finite matrix of the Kasteleyn operator restricted to $(z,w)$-quasi-periodic functions over the fundamental domain and $P(z,w)=\det\widehat K(z,w)$ for the characteristic polynomial (its zero set $P(z,w)=0$ being the *spectral curve* of the periodic dimer model), the inverse is given by the double integral over the unit torus $\mathbb{T}^2$,

$$K^{-1}_{(v,x,y),(v',x',y')}=\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^2}\iint_{\mathbb{T}^2}\frac{\operatorname{Cof}(\widehat K(z,w))_{v',v}}{P(z,w)}\,z^{x'-x}w^{y'-y}\,\frac{dz}{z}\frac{dw}{w},$$

where $v,v'$ index vertices of the fundamental domain and $(x,y),(x',y')\in\mathbb{Z}^2$ are translation labels; the same paper proves that the inverse Kasteleyn matrix whose coefficients decrease at infinity exists and is unique (its Proposition 5). Independently, with no periodicity assumption, Boutillier–de Tilière proved that $K^{-1}$ is given by the local formula

$$K^{-1}_{x,y}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^2}\oint_{C_{x,y}}f_x(\lambda)\,f_y(-\lambda)\,e_{x,y}(\lambda)\,\log\lambda\,d\lambda+C_{x,y},$$

where $e_{x,y}$ is the discrete exponential function introduced by Mercat and Kenyon (defined from the angles of the rhombi crossed by a path of the diamond graph from $x$ to $y$), $f_x,f_y$ depend only on the local geometry at $x$ and $y$, $C_{x,y}$ is a contour separating the poles of the integrand and avoiding a half-line determined by the pair $(x,y)$, and the additive constant $C_{x,y}$ equals $\pm\frac{1}{4}$ for nearby vertices and $0$ otherwise. The formula is *local*: $K^{-1}_{x,y}$ depends only on an edge-path of $G$ between the vertices naturally associated to $x$ and $y$, so changing the graph away from this path does not change the value. This extends Kenyon's local formulas for the critical Green function and for critical dimers on bipartite isoradial graphs, which do not apply here because $\mathcal{G}^D$ is neither bipartite nor isoradial.

In the periodic case both formulas are available and, by the uniqueness result, they coincide. Yet, as the authors state in the introduction of the locality paper, 'the proofs are nevertheless totally different in spirit, and we have not yet been able to understand the identity by an explicit computation, except in the case where $G=\mathbb{Z}^2$.' One structural input is known: the dimer characteristic polynomial is an explicit constant multiple of the critical Laplacian characteristic polynomial, $P(z,w)=2^{|V_1|}\prod_{e\in E(G_1)}(\cot^2\frac{\theta_e}{2}-1)\,P_\Delta(z,w)$, so the spectral data on the two sides is the same. Later work in the same research line made the analogous Fourier-to-local passage explicit in two neighboring settings: for the massive Laplacian Green function (Boutillier–de Tilière–Raschel 2017, by residue evaluation plus a change of variable through the elliptic parametrization of the spectral curve, the authors noting that this relation 'was not understood' in the earlier critical papers, including the locality paper), and for bipartite elliptic dimers on minimal graphs (Boutillier–Cimasoni–de Tilière 2023, again by residues plus a change of variable). For the Ising Fisher graph itself, the massive successor paper (Boutillier–de Tilière–Raschel 2019) still identifies its local and Fourier inverses only through the uniqueness argument, and a February 2026 classification paper by de Tilière–Rey does not address the question. The explicit, coefficient-level identification for the non-bipartite Fisher graph therefore remains to be written down.

## Problem Statement

Prove, by explicit direct computation rather than by the uniqueness of the decaying inverse, that the local and Fourier formulas for the inverse Kasteleyn matrix of the critical Z-invariant Ising model coincide on every periodic isoradial graph.

Precisely: let $G$ be an infinite $\mathbb{Z}^2$-periodic isoradial graph with critical coupling constants $J(\theta)=\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{1+\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}$, let $\mathcal{G}^D$ be its Fisher graph with the critical dimer weight function, and let $K$ be the Kasteleyn matrix of $\mathcal{G}^D$. Show, for every pair of vertices $x,y$ of $\mathcal{G}^D$ (covering all decoration vertex types and all translate pairs), that the coefficient $K^{-1}_{x,y}$ given by the local contour-integral formula (Theorem 1 in the introduction, stated precisely as Theorem 4 in Section 4, of Boutillier–de Tilière, 'The critical Z-invariant Ising model via dimers: locality property') equals the coefficient given by the Fourier double-integral formula (Equation (6) of Boutillier–de Tilière, 'The critical Z-invariant Ising model via dimers: the periodic case'), where the two papers' conventions — Kasteleyn orientations, weight normalizations, branches of $\log\lambda$, and integration contours — are matched explicitly.

The identification must be established by direct evaluation, for example by computing one of the two integrals of the Fourier formula by residues and performing an explicit change of variable in the remaining one, along the parametrization of the spectral curve $P(z,w)=2^{|V_1|}\prod_{e\in E(G_1)}(\cot^2\frac{\theta_e}{2}-1)\,P_\Delta(z,w)$, so that the mechanism by which the locality property emerges from the spectral data is made transparent. The uniqueness statement (Proposition 5 of the periodic-case paper) may be invoked only as a final consistency check, never as the step that identifies the two matrices. A proof of the corresponding identity for the massive (elliptic) Z-invariant Ising model of Boutillier–de Tilière–Raschel, followed by an explicit and controlled limit as the elliptic modulus tends to zero, also constitutes a valid solution.

The verification contract below evaluates answers to this statement. It does not narrow or redefine the research question.

Known solving difficulties:

- The Fourier formula's numerator involves the cofactor matrix of the finite fundamental-domain matrix $\widehat K(z,w)$; matching its entries with the local functions $f_x(\lambda)f_y(-\lambda)$ times the discrete exponential $e_{x,y}(\lambda)$ is a nontrivial algebraic bridge that must be performed for arbitrary fundamental domains.
- At criticality the spectral curve $P(z,w)$, an explicit constant multiple of the critical Laplacian characteristic polynomial, is a degenerate genus-zero limit of the elliptic genus-one curve for which the change-of-variable technique is proven; the degeneration is singular (exponential decay of the massive coefficients degenerates to inverse-distance decay), so either the critical computation is done directly with trigonometric parametrizations or the massive identity is proved first and the limit controlled explicitly.
- Branch and contour bookkeeping: the local formula uses $\log\lambda$ with contours $C_{x,y}$ avoiding a half-line determined by the vertex pair, while the Fourier formula integrates over the unit torus with residue selections; the two must be matched exactly, including the additive constants $C_{x,y}=\pm\frac{1}{4}$ for nearby vertex pairs and the exceptional vertex-type cases.
- The Fisher graph is non-bipartite and its decoration vertices come in several types; the identity must cover every vertex-type pair and every translate pair for all periodic isoradial graphs, not just example lattices.
- The two source papers use different Kasteleyn orientations and weight normalizations; these conventions must be explicitly translated before the two formulas can be compared term by term.

## Current Progress

- Status: `ready`

Phys. 301, 2011, DOI 10.1007/s00220-010-1151-3), verified against the paper's LaTeX source. The exact passage reads: the two expressions for the inverse Kasteleyn matrix coincide by the uniqueness statement (Proposition 5 of the periodic-case paper), 'the proofs are nevertheless totally different in spirit, and we have not yet been able to understand the identity by an explicit computation, except in the case where $G=\mathbb{Z}^2$'. The candidate's formulation, attribution, and scope are correct; no misreading or conflation of adjacent results was detected. The reference [BdT08] is confirmed to be the periodic-case paper (Probability Theory and Related Fields 147, 2010, DOI 10.1007/s00440-009-0210-1).

The two sides of the identity are precisely identified in the sources. The periodic-case paper (PTRF 147, 2010, DOI 10.1007/s00440-009-0210-1, arXiv:0812.3848) defines the inverse by the Fourier double integral over the unit torus involving the cofactor matrix of $\widehat K(z,w)$ and the characteristic polynomial $P(z,w)$ (its Equation (6)) and proves uniqueness of the inverse with coefficients decreasing at infinity (Proposition 5). The locality paper (CMP 301, 2011, DOI 10.1007/s00220-010-1151-3, arXiv:0902.1882) proves the local contour-integral formula (Theorem 1 in the introduction, stated precisely as Theorem 4 in Section 4), its asymptotic expansion, and the explicit constant relating $P(z,w)$ to the critical Laplacian characteristic polynomial (its Corollary 2); the square lattice is the only case in which the authors verified the identity by hand.

Boutillier–de Tilière–Raschel (Inventiones Mathematicae 208, 2017, DOI 10.1007/s00222-016-0687-z, arXiv:1504.00792) resolved the analogous question for the massive Laplacian Green function on periodic isoradial graphs: in Section 5.5 they recover the local formula from the Fourier double integral by an explicit residue computation followed by a change of variable through the elliptic parametrization of the genus-one spectral curve, and note explicitly that this relation 'was not understood' in Kenyon's 2002 paper, the 2011 locality paper, and de Tilière's quadri-tiling paper. This establishes the technique relevant to the present problem but does not treat the Fisher-graph Kasteleyn operator.

Boutillier–de Tilière–Raschel (PTRF 174, 2019, 'The Z-invariant Ising model via dimers', DOI 10.1007/s00440-018-0861-x, arXiv:1612.09082), the elliptic (massive) generalization of the source paper, prove the equality of their local inverse Kasteleyn operator and the Fourier inverse in the periodic case (the proposition stating equality of the two inverse operators in their Section 5) by the same uniqueness argument, citing Proposition 5 of the periodic-case paper. A remark in the same section sketches an explicit residue-plus-change-of-variable computation in the spirit of the 2017 massive Laplacian paper, but only for the free energy (recovering Baxter's local formula), not for the coefficients of the inverse. The coefficient-level explicit identification thus remains open also in the massive model, whose critical limit contains the present problem.

de Tilière (Electronic Journal of Probability 26, 2021, 'The Z-Dirac and massive Laplacian operators in the Z-invariant Ising model', DOI 10.1214/21-EJP601, arXiv:1801.00207) relates the inverse Kasteleyn matrices of the Fisher graph and of an associated bipartite graph; all periodic-case identifications in that paper likewise go through the uniqueness of the inverse decreasing at infinity rather than an explicit computation.

Boutillier–Cimasoni–de Tilière (Communications in Mathematical Physics 400, 2023, 'Elliptic dimers on minimal graphs and genus 1 Harnack curves', DOI 10.1007/s00220-022-04612-6, arXiv:2007.14699) prove the bipartite genus-one analogue explicitly: the Fourier-transform inverse expressions are shown to coincide with the local inverse by 'the explicit evaluation of one of the integrals via residues, and a change of variable in the remaining integral' using the spectral-curve parametrization together with a lemma showing that the denominator-Jacobian combination is a trivial holomorphic form; they note that locality of the ergodic Gibbs measures in the periodic case was previously known only in genus zero. The Fisher (non-bipartite) case of the Ising model is not covered.

de Tilière–Rey (arXiv:2602.13526, February 2026, classification of genus-one Ising models on isoradial graphs) continues this research program and poses different open questions (full probabilistic descriptions with local formulas for the classified, possibly frustrated, models); it does not address the explicit Fourier-to-local identification. Coverage of this audit: all 67 citing works of the locality paper and all 66 citing works of the periodic-case paper (Semantic Scholar, cross-checked against OpenAlex and arXiv full-text searches for 'inverse Kasteleyn' combined with 'isoradial' and 'Fisher graph') were examined at the title/abstract level, with full-source inspection of the 2017/2019/2021 Boutillier–de Tilière–Raschel line, the 2023 elliptic-dimers paper, the 2026 classification paper, and adjacent works (George's spectral transform for the Ising model, Melotti's free-fermion eight-vertex paper, Bošković's Newton-polygon paper for non-bipartite dimers). No work performing the coefficient-level explicit identification for the Fisher graph was found. Remaining uncertainty: theses and non-indexed write-ups were not exhaustively searched, and the paywalled published text of the locality paper was verified through its author-final arXiv version; the persistence of the uniqueness argument across four successive papers of the same group (2017–2026) strongly indicates no public explicit proof exists.

## Scientific Significance

Affected-field significance: `medium`.

Field: exactly solvable two-dimensional statistical mechanics (dimer and Ising models on isoradial graphs, discrete complex analysis). The truth of the identity is already known via uniqueness, so the impact of solving it is methodological rather than a change of core knowledge: an explicit computation would make the emergence of the locality property from the Fourier/spectral-curve side transparent for the non-bipartite Fisher case, would complete the explicit Fourier-to-local program already achieved for the massive Laplacian Green function (2017) and for bipartite elliptic dimers (2023), and would provide a practical two-way bridge converting spectral data of periodic isoradial graphs into local computations of Ising correlations and conversely. The impact is direct but confined to this specialist community, hence medium rather than high.
