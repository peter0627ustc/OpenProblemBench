# ORB-PHYS-67: Proof of the inhomogeneous eigenvalue conjecture for the supersymmetric eight-vertex model on a strip with open boundary conditions (Hagendorf–Liénardy Conjecture 5.1)

At the supersymmetric point of the eight-vertex model, where the vertex weights obey $(a^2+ab)(b^2+ab)=(c^2+ab)(d^2+ab)$ (equivalently, crossing parameter $\eta=\pi/3$), Hagendorf and Liénardy proved in 2020 that the homogeneous transfer matrix of the model on a strip with open boundary conditions and specific non-diagonal $K$-matrices possesses the remarkably simple, non-degenerate, largest eigenvalue $\Lambda_L=(a+b)^{2L}\operatorname{tr}(K^+K^-)$. Their Conjecture 5.1 extends this to the inhomogeneous model, in which each of the $L$ vertical lines carries its own spectral parameter $u_j$: the double-row transfer matrix is conjectured to have eigenvalue $\Lambda_L=\operatorname{tr}(K^+(u)K^-(u))\prod_{j=1}^{L}(a(u+u_j)+b(u+u_j))(a(u-u_j)+b(u-u_j))$. The conjecture was verified numerically for small $L$ in the trigonometric limit $p\to0$, and that limit was subsequently proven using boundary quantum Knizhnik–Zamolodchikov equations; however, the conjecture itself — the elliptic regime at generic nome $p$ — remains unproven, as does its periodic-boundary counterpart dating from 2010–2013. This problem asks for a rigorous proof of Conjecture 5.1, which is the stated route to rigorous eigenvector analysis, sum rules, exact finite-size correlation functions, and connections to alternating-sign-matrix combinatorics and the Painlevé VI equation.

## Background

The eight-vertex model is a statistical-mechanical model on the square lattice in which arrows on edges are conserved at each vertex, leaving eight local configurations with weights $a$, $b$, $c$, $d$. Its row-to-row transfer matrix $T$ builds the partition function row by row, and its largest eigenvalue determines the free energy per site. Baxter introduced a parametrisation of the weights by Jacobi theta functions depending on a spectral parameter $u$, a crossing parameter $\eta$, and an elliptic nome $p$ with $0<p<1$; the limit $p\to0$ is the trigonometric (six-vertex) limit. On a strip with $L$ vertical lines and open boundary conditions, the transfer matrix is of Sklyanin double-row type, $T(u)=\operatorname{tr}_0 K_0^+(u)\,U_{0,[1,L]}(u)\,K_0^-(u)\,\bar U_{0,[1,L]}(u)$, where $U$ and $\bar U$ are ordered products of the eight-vertex $R$-matrix (the local vertex weights in operator form) between an auxiliary space and the $L$ physical spaces, and the $K$-matrices are $2\times2$ solutions of the reflection equation that encode the boundary weights. In the inhomogeneous model the $j$-th vertical line carries its own spectral parameter $u_j$, so the $R$-matrices in $U$ and $\bar U$ are evaluated at $u\pm u_j$.

A distinguished locus is the supersymmetric eight-vertex model, where the weights obey $(a^2+ab)(b^2+ab)=(c^2+ab)(d^2+ab)$; in the theta-function parametrisation this corresponds to $\eta=\pi/3$. The name comes from a dynamic lattice supersymmetry of the associated open XYZ spin chain — the anisotropic Heisenberg spin-$1/2$ chain with boundary magnetic fields on the matching anisotropy line. Its supercharge $Q$ (a nilpotent local operator) and adjoint $Q^\dagger$ define supersymmetry singlets, the common vectors annihilated by $Q$ and $Q^\dagger$; for the relevant parameters these singlets are the ground states of the chain.

In 2001 Stroganov conjectured that, for periodic boundary conditions and an odd number $L=2n+1$ of vertical lines, the homogeneous transfer matrix of this model possesses the remarkably simple, doubly degenerate eigenvalue $\Theta_n=(a+b)^{2n+1}$. This conjecture stimulated work connecting the model to enumerative combinatorics (alternating sign matrices, the objects enumerated by the ground-state components), to functional equations, and to the Painlevé VI equation (certain polynomials appearing at special spectral values are tau functions of Painlevé VI). Hagendorf and Liénardy proved Stroganov's conjecture in Part I of their work (2018) using the supersymmetry, and showed that $\Theta_n$ is the largest eigenvalue for positive weights.

In Part II (2020) the same authors extended the analysis to open boundary conditions. For $K^\pm$ built from a specific non-diagonal $K$-matrix (containing all three Pauli matrices) with its boundary parameter fixed at $t=\pi/6$, they proved that the homogeneous transfer matrix possesses the non-degenerate eigenvalue $\Lambda_L=(a+b)^{2L}\operatorname{tr}(K^+K^-)$, that its eigenspace is the space of supersymmetry singlets of the associated open XYZ chain, and that for positive weights it is the largest eigenvalue, thereby fixing the free energy per site $f=-\ln(a+b)$ and the boundary free energy. Their proof rests on a commutation relation between the supercharge and the transfer matrix that is special to the homogeneous point.

The open question is the inhomogeneous generalisation, stated as Conjecture 5.1 of that paper: for $\eta=\pi/3$ and the same $K$-matrices, the inhomogeneous transfer matrix $T(u|u_1,\dots,u_L)$ is conjectured to possess the eigenvalue obtained by replacing each homogeneous factor $(a+b)^2$ by $(a(u+u_j)+b(u+u_j))(a(u-u_j)+b(u-u_j))$, multiplied by $\operatorname{tr}(K^+(u)K^-(u))$. The conjecture was verified numerically for small $L$ in the trigonometric limit $p\to0$ and checked for compatibility with the functional equations obeyed by the transfer matrix. An analogous conjecture for the periodic inhomogeneous model is due to Razumov and Stroganov (2010) and Zinn-Justin (2013); Zinn-Justin used it as input to derive a sum rule for the ground-state components that had been observed by Bazhanov and Mangazeev, and Brasseur and Hagendorf developed this conditional framework further. Proving these conjectures is the stated route to a rigorous investigation of the eigenvectors of the supersymmetric eight-vertex model, with expected payoffs in alternating-sign-matrix combinatorics, exact finite-size correlation functions, and finite-chain Painlevé VI theory.

## Problem Statement

Prove Conjecture 5.1 of Hagendorf–Liénardy (2020). Let $\eta=\pi/3$ and let $K(u)$ be the $K$-matrix

$$K(u)=\mathbf{1}+\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3}\frac{\vartheta_1(u,p)}{\vartheta_{5-\alpha}(u,p)}\,\mu_\alpha\,\sigma^\alpha$$

with the coefficients $\mu_\alpha$ given by eq. (111) of that paper, evaluated at the boundary parameter $t=\pi/6$. (In the source paper's conventions, the $K$-matrix (101) and the coefficients (111) are expressed through Jacobi theta functions of nome $p$, whereas the vertex weights $a(u),b(u),c(u),d(u)$ of eq. (96) are theta functions of nome $p^2$; both conventions are retained here exactly as in the source.) Show that, for every $L>1$ and arbitrary inhomogeneity parameters $u_1,\dots,u_L$, the double-row transfer matrix

$$T(u|u_1,\dots,u_L)=\operatorname{tr}_0\,K_0^+(u)\,U_{0,[1,L]}(u|u_1,\dots,u_L)\,K_0^-(u)\,\bar U_{0,[1,L]}(u|u_1,\dots,u_L)$$

with $K^-(u)=K(u)$, $K^+(u)=K(u+2\eta)$, $U_{0,[1,L]}=R_{0L}(u+u_L)\cdots R_{01}(u+u_1)$, $\bar U_{0,[1,L]}=R_{01}(u-u_1)\cdots R_{0L}(u-u_L)$, and $R(u)$ the eight-vertex $R$-matrix with weights parametrised as in eq. (96) of the source paper, possesses the eigenvalue

$$\Lambda_L=\operatorname{tr}\bigl(K^+(u)K^-(u)\bigr)\prod_{j=1}^{L}\bigl(a(u+u_j)+b(u+u_j)\bigr)\bigl(a(u-u_j)+b(u-u_j)\bigr).$$

The proof must hold in the elliptic regime of generic nome $0<p<1$; answers restricted to the trigonometric limit $p\to0$ (already proven via boundary quantum Knizhnik–Zamolodchikov equations) or to the homogeneous point $u_1=\cdots=u_L$ (already proven in the source paper) do not discharge the conjecture. The proof may proceed by any rigorous technique — for example an explicit construction of the eigenvector with $T\Psi=\Lambda_L\Psi$, functional $TQ$-type relations, separation of variables, elliptic extensions of the boundary quantum Knizhnik–Zamolodchikov equations, or supersymmetry arguments. A natural strengthening, envisaged but not required by the source, is the characterisation of the corresponding eigenspace, expected to generalise the space of supersymmetry singlets of the associated open XYZ spin chain; the acceptance target is the eigenvalue formula itself.

The verification contract below evaluates answers to this statement. It does not narrow or redefine the research question.

Known solving difficulties:

- The homogeneous proof hinges on a commutation relation between the transfer matrix and the lattice-supersymmetry supercharge of the associated open XYZ chain, together with cohomology arguments on the space of supersymmetry singlets; these tools are special to the homogeneous point and do not directly extend to inhomogeneous transfer matrices.
- The boundary $K$-matrices at $t=\pi/6$ are non-diagonal (all three Pauli components are present), which blocks the standard algebraic Bethe ansatz; the source paper notes that even for the homogeneous model, off-diagonal Bethe ansatz and separation-of-variables methods have not yielded closed finite-$L$ eigenvalue expressions.
- The only successful attack so far — the proof of the trigonometric limit — uses contour-integral solutions of boundary quantum Knizhnik–Zamolodchikov equations; no elliptic counterpart of these integral representations is known, and constructing one is itself a recognised open problem.
- Functional $TQ$-type relations for the open eight-vertex model have a complicated analytic structure in the elliptic regime and have so far not isolated the conjectured eigenvalue.
- The periodic-case sibling conjecture has been open since 2010 despite sustained work by several groups (Razumov–Stroganov, Zinn-Justin, Bazhanov–Mangazeev, Brasseur–Hagendorf, Rosengren), indicating a deep structural difficulty rather than a merely technical gap.

## Current Progress

- Status: `ready`

Source fidelity: the candidate formulation was checked verbatim against the primary source (arXiv:1911.03348, J. Stat. Mech. 2020, 033104). Conjecture 5.1 states exactly that for $\eta=\pi/3$, with the $K$-matrix of eq. (101) with coefficients (111) evaluated at $t=\pi/6$, the inhomogeneous transfer matrix (153) possesses the eigenvalue (155); it was supported by numerical checks for small $L$ in the trigonometric limit $p\to0$ and by compatibility with functional equations. The LKM summary is faithful; the periodic-case sibling conjecture cited as refs [2,4] of the source is Razumov–Stroganov (2010) and Zinn-Justin (2013). Apart from one transcription slip — the elliptic nome of the $K$-matrix, written $p^2$ in the candidate but $p$ in eq. (101) of the source (corrected at review; the coefficients (111) are likewise theta functions of nome $p$, while the vertex weights (96) use nome $p^2$) — no misreading or conflation of adjacent results was found. (LKM provenance: node gcn_ac8f45e325394ae2.)

Hagendorf and Liénardy (2021, J. Stat. Mech. 013104; arXiv:2008.03220) constructed the ground-state eigenvector of the inhomogeneous open XXZ chain at $\Delta=-1/2$ — the trigonometric limit $p\to0$ of the supersymmetric eight-vertex model — with $q=\beta=e^{2\pi i/3}$, via contour-integral solutions of the boundary quantum Knizhnik–Zamolodchikov equations, and stated that the eigenvalue expression obtained there, evaluated with arbitrary inhomogeneities $z_1,\dots,z_N$, proves the trigonometric limit of the conjectured expression. This resolves the $p\to0$ regime of Conjecture 5.1 only; the 2022 erratum to that paper corrects a typographical error in a different conjecture (the totally-symmetric-alternating-sign-matrix sum rule) and does not affect this result.

Brasseur and Hagendorf (2021, J. Stat. Mech. 023102; arXiv:2009.14077) investigated the periodic inhomogeneous case conditionally on Zinn-Justin's Conjecture 2.1 and state in their conclusion that the proof of that periodic conjecture remains a challenge, and that for the open-boundary conjecture of the source paper a characterisation of its eigenspace, analogous to Conjecture 2.1, is still to be found — i.e. both inhomogeneous eight-vertex conjectures were open as of 2021.

Hagendorf and Rosengren (2024, Commun. Math. Phys. 405; arXiv:2208.13533), in their study of nearest-neighbour correlation functions for the supersymmetric XYZ chain (periodic case), identify as a major obstruction that the ground-state eigenvectors are not known explicitly, and note that integral formulas extending to the inhomogeneous eight-vertex model would presumably complete the proof of many ground-state properties described by Bazhanov–Mangazeev, Brasseur–Hagendorf, Razumov–Stroganov, and Zinn-Justin — confirming that the inhomogeneous eight-vertex eigenvalue/eigenvector problem remains open and is the bottleneck of the programme.

Liénardy and Hagendorf (2025, arXiv:2509.09209) proved the totally-symmetric-alternating-sign-matrix conjecture (Conjecture 5.5 of the boundary-qKZ paper); this continues the trigonometric ($p\to0$) programme and leaves the elliptic Conjecture 5.1 untouched.

Coverage and search: forward citations of the source were enumerated via Crossref (3), Semantic Scholar (4), and INSPIRE (2) and all were inspected; the complete arXiv author listings of Hagendorf (27 papers, newest September 2025) and Liénardy (7 papers) contain no proof of the elliptic conjecture; arXiv searches on 'supersymmetric eight-vertex', 'eight-vertex inhomogeneous', 'XYZ spin chain supersymmetric', and 'Razumov-Stroganov' found no other candidate resolution. A web-search hit suggesting a 2023–2024 'proof of the eigenvalue conjecture' by Hagendorf and a co-author could not be confirmed in any bibliographic database and was judged spurious. The audit leaves a precise nonempty open core: a rigorous proof of Conjecture 5.1 at generic elliptic nome $p$, with the original generality (all $L>1$, arbitrary inhomogeneities) retained; the proven trigonometric limit is recorded above as partial progress, not as a narrowing.

## Scientific Significance

Affected-field significance: `medium`.

Directly affects the research programme on the supersymmetric eight-vertex model and Razumov–Stroganov-type problems in exactly solvable lattice models. A proof would convert conditional results — Zinn-Justin's periodic sum-rule derivation, Brasseur–Hagendorf's sum rules and component formulas, and the eigenvector properties listed by Hagendorf–Rosengren — into unconditional theorems, and would provide the first rigorous handle on inhomogeneous eight-vertex eigenvectors, the currently missing ingredient for exact finite-size correlation functions, refined alternating-sign-matrix enumeration, and the finite-chain Painlevé VI connections. The impact is direct and enabling within this subfield; it does not by itself change the core methods or knowledge of the broader fields of statistical mechanics or integrable systems.
