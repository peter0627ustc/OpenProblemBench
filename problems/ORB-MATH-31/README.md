# ORB-MATH-31: Is Axiom A dense in the $C^1$ topology among diffeomorphisms of compact surfaces? (Smale's problem / $C^1$ Palis density conjecture, surface case)

The source paper (C. G. Moreira, Acta Mathematica 2011) proved that there are no $C^1$-stable intersections of regular Cantor sets — any pair of dynamically defined Cantor sets can be approximated in the $C^1$ topology by a pair with empty intersection — and that a generic $C^1$ surface diffeomorphism has no tangencies between the stable foliation of one horseshoe and the unstable foliation of another. The author presented this as evidence toward the classical question of whether hyperbolicity is dense in the $C^1$ topology for surface diffeomorphisms. This audit reconstructs the literature lineage: the weaker Palis dichotomy (approximation by a hyperbolic map or one with a homoclinic tangency) was proved for $C^1$ surfaces by Pujals–Sambarino; Crovisier–Pujals reduced the problem to the gap between essential hyperbolicity and genuine Axiom A; the toy-model family of Benedicks–Carleson (a one-parameter family of Hénon-like planar maps) satisfies $C^1$ density of Axiom A; and in dimension at least three Axiom A is provably not $C^1$-dense, so surfaces are the one remaining case. No work through August 2026 proves or refutes $C^1$ density of Axiom A on compact surfaces: the problem remains open with a precise, nonempty open core.

## Background

Let $M$ be a compact surface and let $\mathrm{Diff}^1(M)$ be the space of $C^1$ diffeomorphisms of $M$ endowed with the $C^1$ topology. A diffeomorphism $f$ satisfies Smale's Axiom A if its non-wandering set $\Omega(f)$ — the set of points every neighbourhood of which returns to intersect itself under some positive iterate of $f$ — is uniformly hyperbolic, meaning the tangent bundle over $\Omega(f)$ splits into two invariant directions, one uniformly contracted and one uniformly expanded by the derivative, and periodic points are dense in $\Omega(f)$. Together with the no-cycle condition (no cyclic intersections between the invariant manifolds of distinct basic pieces), Axiom A characterizes $\Omega$-stable dynamics by the Palis–Smale theorem, so the question of whether such systems are dense is a question about how far one can push arbitrary dynamics toward the best-understood class. The density question goes back to Smale, who introduced Axiom A in his 1967 survey, and he later posed the general problem 'In which class of systems is hyperbolicity dense?' explicitly in his 1998 list of mathematical problems for the next century; it became a central open problem of smooth dynamics. A homoclinic tangency — a non-transverse intersection between the stable and unstable manifolds of a hyperbolic periodic point — is the archetypal local obstruction: a diffeomorphism with a homoclinic tangency cannot be Axiom A, and tangencies generate rich non-hyperbolic behavior. A heterodimensional cycle is the analogous obstruction built from periodic points whose stable manifolds have different dimensions; it can only occur in ambient dimension at least three, since all saddle periodic points of a surface diffeomorphism have one-dimensional stable manifolds.

For surface diffeomorphisms the answer depends dramatically on the regularity. In the $C^2$ topology Axiom A is not dense: Newhouse constructed persistent ('wild') non-hyperbolic $C^2$ surface diffeomorphisms, and related Newhouse phenomena give open sets of $C^2$ surface diffeomorphisms with infinitely many coexisting sinks. The mechanism is fractal. A horseshoe — a hyperbolic invariant Cantor set of a surface map — meets a transverse section in regular Cantor sets (compact sets dynamically defined by a $C^r$ expanding map of finitely many intervals). Newhouse's thickness is a fractal invariant of such Cantor sets, continuous and positive in the $C^2$ (indeed $C^{1+\alpha}$) topology, and when the product of the thicknesses of two regular Cantor sets exceeds one and their convex hulls overlap, the two sets intersect stably, i.e. the intersection persists under perturbation; stably intersecting stable and unstable Cantor sets of horseshoes produce persistent tangencies. Moreira and Yoccoz later proved that generic pairs of $C^2$-regular Cantor sets whose Hausdorff dimensions sum to more than one even have translations with stable intersection.

The $C^1$ world is different. Ures showed that thickness is 'terribly discontinuous' in the $C^1$ topology — generic $C^1$-regular Cantor sets have zero thickness — and Moreira, in the source paper of this candidate, proved that there are no $C^1$-stable intersections of regular Cantor sets at all: any pair $(K,K')$ of regular Cantor sets can be approximated arbitrarily well in the $C^1$ topology by pairs $(\tilde K,\tilde K')$ with empty intersection, and generic pairs of $C^1$-regular Cantor sets have an arithmetic difference $K-K'=\{x-y:x\in K,y\in K'\}$ with empty interior. The same paper shows that a residual set of $C^1$ surface diffeomorphisms has no tangencies between the stable foliation of one horseshoe and the unstable foliation of another. Since stable Cantor-set intersections are the main known obstruction to hyperbolicity on surfaces, Moreira's result gives, in the author's words, 'some hope of proving density of hyperbolicity in the $C^1$ topology for diffeomorphisms of surfaces' — a question he attributes to Smale.

The question sits inside the Palis density conjecture, which predicts that every diffeomorphism can be approximated by one that is hyperbolic or exhibits a homoclinic tangency or a heterodimensional cycle. On surfaces, where heterodimensional cycles between saddles cannot occur, Pujals and Sambarino proved this dichotomy affirmatively in the $C^1$ topology: every $C^1$ surface diffeomorphism can be $C^1$-approximated by one that is hyperbolic or displays a homoclinic tangency. Crovisier and Pujals proved the analogue in arbitrary dimension: every diffeomorphism is $C^1$-approximated by one exhibiting a tangency or a heterodimensional cycle, or by an essentially hyperbolic one (finitely many hyperbolic attractors and repellers whose basins form an open dense subset of $M$). In ambient dimension at least three the strong density statement fails outright: Bonatti and Díaz constructed $C^1$-robust homoclinic tangencies using blenders (hyperbolic sets whose unstable set behaves as if it had larger dimension than its unstable leaves, creating robust intersections with curves and surfaces), so there are $C^1$-open sets of diffeomorphisms containing no Axiom A element; subsequent work extended these robust-tangency mechanisms, including $C^1$-stable intersections of higher-dimensional regular Cantor sets (Asaoka) and uncountably many $C^1$-robust tangencies unfolding from coindex-one heterodimensional cycles (Li). These results delimit the problem to dimension two, where Moreira's theorem kills the classical obstruction but, as the citation trail confirms, does not settle density: the surface case of the density question remains open.

## Problem Statement

Are Axiom A diffeomorphisms dense in $\mathrm{Diff}^1(M)$ equipped with the $C^1$ topology, for every compact surface $M$? Equivalently: given any $C^1$ diffeomorphism $f$ of a compact surface $M$ and any $\varepsilon>0$, does there exist a diffeomorphism $g$ with $d_{C^1}(f,g)<\varepsilon$ whose non-wandering set is uniformly hyperbolic and has periodic points dense (Axiom A; one may equivalently demand Axiom A together with the no-cycle condition, i.e. $\Omega$-stability, since the no-cycle condition is accessible within the Axiom A class)? A negative answer amounts to exhibiting, for some compact surface $M$, a non-empty $C^1$-open subset of $\mathrm{Diff}^1(M)$ containing no Axiom A diffeomorphism. This is the two-dimensional, $C^1$-topology case of the Palis density conjecture and is often called Smale's problem for surface diffeomorphisms. Moreira's theorem that stable intersections of regular Cantor sets — the classical Newhouse obstruction — do not exist in the $C^1$ topology removes the main known mechanism obstructing density on surfaces, but, as the later literature confirms, it does not by itself settle the question.

The verification contract below evaluates answers to this statement. It does not narrow or redefine the research question.

Known solving difficulties:

- The density must be achieved for an arbitrary given $C^1$ surface diffeomorphism, so a positive proof must eliminate all tangencies and all sinks/sources phenomena simultaneously and at all scales, whereas Moreira's theorem handles tangencies between two prescribed horseshoes and its genericity statement is residual, not open-and-dense; converting genericity statements into approximations of every individual system is not automatic.
- The $C^1$ category lacks bounded distortion of the iterates of the defining expanding maps — the very mechanism that kills $C^1$-stable Cantor intersections also destroys the classical distortion-based toolkit, so new perturbation techniques are required.
- The gap between Crovisier–Pujals essential hyperbolicity (finitely many hyperbolic attractors with a dense union of basins) and genuine Axiom A remains: essential hyperbolicity permits non-hyperbolic recurrent behavior in the complement of the basins.
- Non-hyperbolic phenomena accumulate on themselves (Newhouse cascades of sinks — infinite sequences of attracting periodic orbits born at successive bifurcations near a tangency — and tangencies generating further tangencies), so a perturbation that restores hyperbolicity at one scale may create new obstructions at a smaller scale; a proof needs a global induction over all horseshoe pairs at once.
- The critical-set program (Pujals–Rodriguez Hertz; Crovisier–Pujals 2026) gives a criterion for a dominated splitting (a continuous invariant decomposition of the tangent bundle over an invariant set into two subbundles, one uniformly dominant over the other), namely absence of critical points, but controlling the recurrence of critical orbits for an arbitrary $C^1$ surface diffeomorphism is unresolved.
- A negative resolution faces the opposite difficulty: constructing a $C^1$-robust non-hyperbolic mechanism on a surface, which must bypass Moreira's theorem since stable Cantor-set intersections are unavailable in the $C^1$ topology.

## Current Progress

Moreira (arXiv:0901.3131; Acta Mathematica, DOI 10.1007/s11511-011-0064-0) proves that any pair of regular Cantor sets can be $C^1$-approximated by a disjoint pair (answering a question of Bonatti), that generic pairs of $C^1$-regular Cantor sets have an arithmetic difference with empty interior, and — in the published version — that a residual set of $C^1$ surface diffeomorphisms has no tangencies between the stable and unstable foliations of any two horseshoes. The density question is presented by the author as motivation ('stable intersections of Cantor sets are the main known obstructions to density of hyperbolicity for diffeomorphisms of surfaces... this gives some hope of proving density of hyperbolicity in the $C^1$ topology for diffeomorphisms of surfaces'), and in his later survey as 'a well-known question by Smale'. The horseshoe-tangency elimination result appears in the published paper and as Theorem 2.6 of Moreira's ICM survey.

The dichotomy (weak) form of the problem was settled before the source paper: Pujals and Sambarino (Annals of Mathematics, 2000, DOI 10.2307/121127, https://doi.org/10.2307/121127) proved the $C^1$ Palis conjecture for surfaces — every $C^1$ surface diffeomorphism can be $C^1$-approximated by one that is hyperbolic or exhibits a homoclinic tangency. This makes the surviving open core precise: eliminating the tangency branch, i.e. achieving full density of hyperbolicity itself.

Crovisier and Pujals (Inventiones mathematicae, 2015, DOI 10.1007/s00222-014-0553-9, https://doi.org/10.1007/s00222-014-0553-9) proved that in arbitrary dimension every diffeomorphism is $C^1$-approximated by one exhibiting a homoclinic tangency or a heterodimensional cycle, or by an essentially hyperbolic diffeomorphism (finitely many hyperbolic attractors/repellers with an open dense union of basins). On surfaces this reduces the problem to the still-open gap between essential hyperbolicity and Axiom A; Crovisier's 2014 survey (arXiv:1405.0305, https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.0305) additionally notes that Moreira's theorem discharges the 'robustness' step of the program (there are no $C^1$-robust homoclinic tangencies on surfaces) while the localization and local-dichotomy steps remain open.

Matheus, Moreira and Pujals (Ann. Sci. ENS, 2013, DOI 10.24033/asens.2204, https://doi.org/10.24033/asens.2204), using Moreira's theorem, proved that Axiom A is $C^1$-dense inside the Benedicks–Carleson toy-model family (a one-parameter family of dissipative Hénon-like planar maps introduced by Benedicks and Carleson as a simplified model of Hénon dynamics, a family that exhibits $C^2$-open Newhouse phenomena), providing explicit evidence toward Smale's conjecture, but only for that restricted family, not for $\mathrm{Diff}^1(M)$.

In ambient dimension at least three the strong density statement is false: Bonatti and Díaz (Trans. AMS, 2012, DOI 10.1090/S0002-9947-2012-05445-6, https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-2012-05445-6) constructed $C^1$-robust homoclinic tangencies via blender-horseshoes on every manifold of dimension $\geq 3$, so Axiom A is not $C^1$-dense there. Later work sharpened the contrast with the surface case: Asaoka (2019, arXiv:1911.08091, https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.08091) constructed $C^1$-stable intersections of regular Cantor sets in higher dimension, showing Moreira's theorem has no higher-dimensional analogue, and Li (2024, arXiv:2406.12500, https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12500) produced uncountably many $C^1$-robust tangencies from coindex-one heterodimensional cycles (cycles between periodic points whose stable manifold dimensions differ by one). These papers — the main line citing Moreira's theorem — delimit the problem to dimension two without touching it.

The problem is still presented as open in the authoritative surveys and in current research: Crovisier's 2014 survey (arXiv:1405.0305, https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.0305) lists Smale's problem for $C^1$ surface diffeomorphisms among the guiding open questions and decomposes it into open intermediate steps; Moreira's ICM 2018 survey (DOI 10.1142/9789813272880_0028, https://doi.org/10.1142/9789813272880_0028) reiterates that $C^1$ density of hyperbolicity on surfaces is open, with the toy-model result as the main evidence; Crovisier and Pujals's 2026 paper on the critical set for surface diffeomorphisms (arXiv:2601.08208, https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.08208; an intrinsic notion of critical points via the projective action of the derivative, with a dominated splitting iff no critical points under a non-degeneracy assumption, and a two-dimensional Misiurewicz class, i.e. surface diffeomorphisms whose critical orbits are non-recurrent) continues this program without claiming any density of Axiom A.

Results announced only in seminars cannot be excluded, but the published literature through August 2026 leaves a precise open core: whether Axiom A diffeomorphisms are $C^1$-dense in $\mathrm{Diff}^1(M)$ for every compact surface $M$.

## Scientific Significance

Affected-field significance: `high`.

This is the remaining open case of the Palis density conjecture in the lowest non-trivial dimension and regularity, and a direct descendant of the founding question of Smale's hyperbolic program. A positive resolution would show that every $C^1$ surface diffeomorphism is approximable by $\Omega$-stable (Axiom A) systems, making uniform hyperbolicity the $C^1$-dense background of all surface dynamics and directly changing the field's core knowledge of what obstructs hyperbolicity at low regularity; a negative resolution would exhibit a genuinely new robust non-hyperbolic mechanism on surfaces, necessarily different from the thickness-based Newhouse mechanism that Moreira's theorem eliminates. Either answer would also settle the delimitation of the Palis program against the known dimension $\geq 3$ counterexamples (Bonatti–Díaz), so the impact on the field's core questions is direct rather than incremental.
