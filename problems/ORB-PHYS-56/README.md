# ORB-PHYS-56: Exact fixed-magnetization partition functions and Helmholtz fluctuation-induced forces beyond one dimension and beyond the Gaussian fixed point

The one-dimensional Ising chain in the fixed-magnetization (canonical) ensemble was solved exactly by Dantchev and Rudnick in 2022, who derived closed-form hypergeometric partition functions for periodic, antiperiodic, and Dirichlet boundary conditions, used them to define and compute the Helmholtz force — the canonical-ensemble counterpart of the critical Casimir force — and explicitly noted that the Ising model with fixed magnetization is unsolved in every dimension higher than one. Later work by the same group rigorously completed the one-dimensional case (all boundary conditions, Legendre relation between the ensembles, exact $\ln N/N$ finite-size corrections), solved the Nagle–Kardar chain and, exactly, the three-dimensional Gaussian model in the canonical ensemble, while mean-field, $\epsilon$-expansion, functional-renormalization-group, and Monte Carlo studies had already established that canonical and grand-canonical critical forces genuinely differ. What remains open is the exact canonical treatment of genuinely interacting and higher-dimensional models: most prominently the two-dimensional Ising model with fixed magnetization — whose partition function is a magnetization sector of the unsolved field-dependent model — together with the interacting (non-Gaussian) fixed points and the solvable models (spherical model in any dimension, Potts and spin-1 chains) explicitly flagged as open exact targets in the 2026 Gaussian-model study. This record states that surviving open core, the structural obstruction behind it, and the verification standards (finite-lattice enumeration, limit identities, pinned numerical protocols) that an answer must meet.

## Background

The Ising model — spins $s_i=\pm 1$ on a lattice coupled through a nearest-neighbor interaction $J$, with dimensionless coupling $K=\beta J$ ($\beta=1/k_BT$) — is the reference model of statistical mechanics. It is exactly solved in one dimension at arbitrary temperature and magnetic field, and in two dimensions only at zero field. In the grand-canonical ensemble (GCE) one fixes the external field $h$; in the canonical ensemble (CE), also called the fixed-$M$ ensemble, one instead fixes the total magnetization $M=\sum_i s_i$, i.e. the difference between the numbers of up and down spins. The CE is the rigorous description of any closed system with a conserved scalar order parameter — a binary alloy or lattice gas of fixed composition, a fluid film of fixed total mass — and the two ensembles are inequivalent for finite systems.

Near a critical point, confined order-parameter fluctuations generate the critical Casimir force between the confining boundaries: minus the derivative, with respect to the film thickness $L$, of the excess free energy (the free energy minus the bulk contribution) of a film of geometry $\infty^{d-1}\times L$. Dantchev and Rudnick (2022) introduced the canonical counterpart, which they named the Helmholtz force: minus the $L$-derivative of the excess Helmholtz free energy at fixed magnetization, with the total magnetization $M$ or the magnetization density $m=M/N$ held constant during the differentiation. Their starting observation was that the Ising chain in the fixed-$M$ ensemble — unlike the field ensemble — had never been solved: they noted that the solution of the Ising model with fixed magnetization 'is not available' in one or any higher dimension, and they filled that gap for the finite chain under periodic, antiperiodic, and Dirichlet (free-end) boundary conditions.

The one-dimensional solution rests on domain-wall combinatorics. A domain wall is a bond between two adjacent unlike spins; with periodic boundary conditions domain walls occur in pairs, so a configuration of $2k$ alternating domains has energy cost $4kK$ relative to the aligned state. Counting the arrangements of $N_+=(N+M)/2$ up spins and $N_-=(N-M)/2$ down spins into $k$ domains of each sign produces products $\prod_{p=1}^{k-1}[(N-2p)^2-M^2]$, which sum to a Gauss hypergeometric function $_2F_1$. The resulting closed form is exact when $N$ and $M$ have the same parity and $|M|<N$ (the formula smoothly interpolates otherwise, and $M=\pm N$ is trivial). From it one finds that the Helmholtz force of the chain can be attractive or repulsive depending on temperature and $m$ — whereas the one-dimensional Casimir force is only attractive — and that the fixed-$M$ free energy contains a logarithmic contribution from the translational degeneracy of the domain-wall positions, a non-scaling term that violates naive finite-size scaling (the statement that the excess free energy of a critical system is a universal function of $L/\xi$, with $\xi$ the correlation length).

Two obstructions block the extension of this exact program to interacting and higher-dimensional systems. First, in the two-dimensional Ising model the exact solution (Onsager, Kaufman) exists only at $h=0$: the fixed-$M$ partition function $Z(N_x,N_y,M,K)$ is precisely the magnetization-sector (Fourier) component, in $h$, of the field-dependent partition function, and extracting it requires either solving the model in a nonzero field — a famous open problem — or summing interacting Peierls contours (closed domain-boundary loops on the dual lattice) with fixed enclosed area and fixed energy, for which no technique is known. Second, in the continuum Ginzburg–Landau–Wilson (GLW) field theory of criticality, the Gaussian (free-field) approximation remains exactly solvable under the fixed-order-parameter constraint, because the constraint merely fixes the zero-momentum mode; but at the interacting Wilson–Fisher fixed point that governs real critical behavior, only mean-field, $\epsilon=4-d$ expansion, functional-renormalization-group, and Monte Carlo results for canonical critical forces exist. Prior to the 2022 Letter, the Dietrich group had already established, at mean-field, $\epsilon$-expansion, and three-dimensional-Ising-Monte-Carlo level, that canonical and grand-canonical critical Casimir forces in films genuinely differ (for example, a sign reversal under strong adsorption); the recent review by Dantchev and Dietrich (Physics Reports 1005 (2023)) surveys exact critical Casimir results. What remained, and remains, open is exact treatment.

## Problem Statement

Extend the exact solution of the fixed-magnetization (canonical) ensemble — and with it the exact theory of the Helmholtz fluctuation-induced force — beyond the one-dimensional Ising chain and beyond the free Gaussian fixed point, to at least one genuinely interacting or higher-dimensional model, doing for that model what the 2022 solution did for the chain. Admissible target models are any short-range statistical-mechanical model with a finite-temperature critical point in spatial dimension $d\ge 2$ or with genuinely interacting (non-Gaussian) critical behavior: the flagship instance is the two-dimensional nearest-neighbor Ising model on a finite $N_x\times N_y$ lattice with periodic boundary conditions and fixed total magnetization $M$ (other standard boundary conditions admissible); the models explicitly flagged as feasible exact targets in the follow-up literature — the spherical model in any $d$, the $q$-state Potts chain, the spin-1 Ising chain — are equally admissible. For the chosen model the answer must deliver, in exact form:

1. The fixed-$M$ partition function $Z(N,M,K)$, with $N$ the total number of spins of the chosen finite system (for the two-dimensional flagship, $Z(N_x,N_y,M,K)$ on the $N_x\times N_y$ lattice) — a closed form in standard special functions, an exact finite sum, or an equivalently explicit exact representation — covering the full range of the coupling $K$ (not only the critical point) and the parity and $|M|=N$ limit cases.

2. The Helmholtz force derived from it: minus the derivative with respect to system size of the excess Helmholtz free energy at fixed $M$ (or fixed $m=M/N$), including its finite-size scaling function and the leading non-scaling finite-size corrections (the analog of the one-dimensional $\ln N/N$ terms).

3. The precise comparison with the grand-canonical Casimir force of the same model and boundary conditions: where the two forces coincide, where and why they differ, and how the canonical and grand-canonical free energies are related in both the thermodynamic limit and the finite-size regime.

These three deliverables form one chain — partition function, free energy and force, ensemble comparison — and a solution is complete only when the whole chain is exact for the chosen model.

The verification contract below evaluates answers to this statement. It does not narrow or redefine the research question.

Known solving difficulties:

- The two-dimensional fixed-$M$ problem embeds the unsolved field-dependent two-dimensional Ising model: the fixed-$M$ partition function is the magnetization-sector (Fourier) component of $Z(h)$, for which no closed form exists, so a solution must either make unexpected progress on the model in a field or bypass it entirely.
- The one-dimensional solution rests on exact enumeration of non-interacting domain-wall configurations with fixed numbers of up and down spins; in $d\ge 2$ the analogous objects are strongly interacting Peierls contour (domain-boundary loop) configurations with fixed enclosed area and energy, for which no summation technique is known.
- At interacting (non-Gaussian) critical fixed points the magnetization constraint couples to the full field dependence of the renormalized free energy, which is not known exactly; existing canonical results use mean-field theory, the $\epsilon=4-d$ expansion, the functional renormalization group, or Monte Carlo, each limited in precision or order.
- Even for exactly solvable candidate models (spherical, Potts, spin-1 chains), the global magnetization constraint must be combined with the model's own solution structure (for example, together with the spherical constraint), and the fixed-$M$ finite-size free energy contains non-scaling logarithmic terms that must be tracked exactly rather than asymptotically.
- The Helmholtz force is a derivative of an excess free energy that subtracts a bulk reference at fixed magnetization, so a solution must control the finite system and the bulk simultaneously with enough precision to extract the subleading terms in which the force lives.

## Current Progress

The source Letter (Dantchev and Rudnick, Physical Review E 106, L042103 (2022)) obtained the exact closed-form fixed-$M$ partition function of the one-dimensional Ising chain under periodic, antiperiodic, and Dirichlet boundary conditions (Gauss hypergeometric functions), defined the Helmholtz force, showed that it can be attractive or repulsive where the one-dimensional Casimir force is only attractive, identified the $\ln N$ translational-degeneracy term, and explicitly stated that the Ising model with fixed magnetization is unsolved in every dimension higher than one and that the ensemble dependence of fluctuation-induced forces extends, in principle, to any model of interest.

Field-theoretic Ginzburg–Landau–Wilson treatments in the canonical ensemble already exist. The Letter itself cites canonical-ensemble critical-Casimir studies that predate it — Gross, Vasilyev, Gambassi, and Dietrich (Physical Review E 94, 022103 (2016)) found a sign reversal and slower decay of the canonical critical Casimir force under strong adsorption, via mean-field theory and three-dimensional Ising Monte Carlo at fixed magnetization; Gross, Gambassi, and Dietrich (Physical Review E 96, 022135 (2017)) computed canonical critical Casimir forces to leading order in the $\epsilon=4-d$ expansion for periodic, Dirichlet, and Neumann films; Rohwer, Squarcini, Vasilyev, Dietrich, and Gross (Physical Review E 99, 062103 (2019)) extended this to the ordinary (Dirichlet) surface universality class. These works are approximate; the genuinely open part was, and is, exact treatment.

Dantchev, Tonchev, and Rudnick (Annals of Physics 459, 169533 (2023)) re-derived the general-$M$ one-dimensional results rigorously via the transfer-matrix method and Chebyshev polynomials (earlier transfer-matrix results were limited to $M=0$ with $N$ even), proved that the canonical and grand-canonical free energies are related by Legendre transformation in the thermodynamic limit, established the exact leading $\ln N/N$ finite-size correction of the canonical free energy, and derived the exact Helmholtz-force scaling functions for periodic and antiperiodic chains. This substantially closes the related direction about rigorous characterization of the general-$M$, parity-inconsistent, and boundary-limit cases — which the source paper itself had described as handled by interpolation and as trivial, respectively.

Dantchev, Tonchev, and Rudnick (Annals of Physics 464, 169647 (2024)) completed the one-dimensional program with Dirichlet (free) boundary conditions, proving analytically that the Casimir force is attractive for all temperatures and fields while the Helmholtz force can be attractive or repulsive. All three standard boundary conditions of the chain are now exactly solved in both ensembles.

The Nagle–Kardar model — a one-dimensional Ising chain with nearest-neighbor couplings plus equivalent-neighbor long-range ferromagnetic interactions of strength $J_l/N$, possessing a genuine critical line ending at a tricritical point — was treated in the fixed-$M$ ensemble by Dantchev, Tonchev, and Rudnick (Symmetry 17, 22 (2024); European Physical Journal Special Topics 235, 3111 (2025)): the canonical and grand-canonical ensembles remain inequivalent even in the thermodynamic limit, and the Helmholtz force is independent of the long-range coupling, coinciding with that of the short-range chain. This supplies the 'other statistical models' direction at the level of a one-dimensional model with a finite-temperature transition, but not in higher dimension.

Dantchev and Rudnick (Annals of Physics 494, 170623 (2026); arXiv:2603.16030 — the published title is Helmholtz versus Casimir forces in the Gaussian model: Exact results) solved the canonical ensemble of the three-dimensional Gaussian model exactly for Dirichlet–Dirichlet, Neumann–Dirichlet, Neumann–Neumann, and periodic boundary conditions: both forces obey finite-size scaling; they coincide under periodic and Neumann–Neumann conditions but differ under Dirichlet–Dirichlet and Neumann–Dirichlet ones. This completes the exact free-field (Gaussian) GLW canonical program. Its conclusion explicitly states that the ensemble-dependence issue 'is by no means limited to the Gaussian model, or to the canonical ensemble' and names the spherical model in any $d$, Potts models, spin-1 Ising chains, and microcanonical forces as feasible open exact targets.

For the genuinely interacting two- and three-dimensional Ising universality classes at fixed magnetization, only approximate treatments exist: Rose, Rançon, and Balog (Physical Review E 113, 054106 (2026)) computed the constraint effective action and fixed-$M$ critical correlation functions via the functional renormalization group at second order of the derivative expansion, benchmarked against Monte Carlo; Balog and Rançon (Physical Review E 112, 054127 (2025)) solved the one-dimensional constraint correlation functions exactly in the scaling limit and identified the failure of standard derivative-expansion schemes to capture the magnetization-induced oscillations. No exact fixed-$M$ partition function or Helmholtz force exists in $d\ge 2$.

The computational-complexity perspective (Carlson, Davies, Kolla, and Perkins, arXiv:2111.03033 (2021)) shows that approximating the fixed-magnetization Ising partition function on bounded-degree graphs is intractable in part of the low-temperature phase (no fully polynomial randomized approximation scheme — FPRAS — exists below the regular-tree threshold for small magnetizations) and tractable elsewhere — a hardness result on general graphs that does not settle whether an exact solution for the two-dimensional lattice exists.

The quantum and experimental extensions remain open: Wang, Assaad, and Parisen Toldin (Physical Review E 96, 042131 (2017)) developed canonical-ensemble quantum Monte Carlo for fermions and proved an exact formula for the leading canonical finite-size free-energy correction, verified on the one-dimensional classical fixed-$M$ Ising chain, but no exact fixed-magnetization Helmholtz force is known for any quantum model, and no experimental observation of Helmholtz forces in one-dimensional or quasi-one-dimensional materials has been reported. The survey literature (Dantchev, Entropy 26, 499 (2024)) frames Helmholtz forces as a basic open chapter of fluctuation-induced interactions in micro- and nano-systems.

The two-dimensional fixed-$M$ Ising partition function remains unsolved in the literature considered at curation; the structural obstruction is that it is a magnetization-sector (Fourier) component of the field-dependent partition function, itself unsolved in two dimensions. Exact critical-point magnetization-distribution results for the two-dimensional scaling limit (conformal-field-theory moment relations), even where they exist, would not constitute the all-temperature fixed-$M$ free energy required for the force.

## Scientific Significance

Affected-field significance: `high`.

Solving this problem would produce the first exact fixed-magnetization partition function and Helmholtz fluctuation-induced force for an interacting or higher-dimensional model, directly changing core knowledge in the statistical mechanics of finite-size effects and fluctuation-induced forces. It would settle, at the level of exact formulas rather than the existing mean-field, $\epsilon$-expansion, functional-renormalization-group, and Monte Carlo evidence, whether and how canonical and grand-canonical critical forces differ at a non-Gaussian fixed point, and it would determine whether the non-scaling logarithmic finite-size terms found in one dimension persist in higher dimension. In the flagship two-dimensional Ising case it would amount to the first exact magnetization-sector result for the two-dimensional Ising model beyond zero field — a long-standing gap in the most studied model of statistical mechanics. The impact is direct for the theory of critical Casimir forces, whose experiments on confined critical mixtures involve closed films of fixed composition that are intrinsically canonical systems, and for the exactly-solved-models literature; it is indirect for nanoscale force engineering in one-dimensional and quasi-one-dimensional materials.
