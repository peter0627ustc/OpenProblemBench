# ORB-PHYS-58: Exact solution and exact critical temperature of the spin-one Ising model on the honeycomb lattice

The spin-one Ising model on the honeycomb lattice — spins $\sigma_i\in\{-1,0,+1\}$ with pure bilinear nearest-neighbor exchange $H=J\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}(1-\sigma_i\sigma_j)$, equivalent to the Blume–Capel model at zero crystal field — is the simplest two-dimensional lattice spin model with integer spin that has never been solved exactly: neither its free energy nor even its critical temperature is known in closed form, in contrast to the exactly solved spin-one-half honeycomb Ising model. The state of the art is exact enumeration of the integer density of states on $L\times2L$ honeycomb lattices up to $L=14$, from which the ferromagnetic critical point $y_c=\mathrm{e}^{-\beta_cJ}=0.42191(1)$ (i.e. $\beta_cJ\approx0.8630$) is inferred by finite-size extrapolation of partition-function zeros, consistent with the 1973 low-temperature-series estimate $0.4217(10)$. The problem is to obtain an exact solution of the model — a closed-form per-site free energy from which the exact critical point and complex-temperature singularity structure follow — or, at minimum, a rigorous exact determination of the critical temperature. A systematic literature audit confirms the problem is open: all known exact results for honeycomb spin-one models hold only on special temperature-dependent-coupling subspaces of the generalized (bilinear-plus-biquadratic-plus-crystal-field) Hamiltonian that provably do not contain the pure bilinear model.

## Background

The Ising model places a classical spin on each site of a lattice, with nearest-neighbor pairs coupled by an exchange interaction. Its spin-one-half version ($\sigma_i=\pm1$) is the paradigm of an exactly solved phase transition: Onsager (1944) obtained the free energy of the square-lattice model at zero magnetic field, and Houtappel (1950) extended the exact solution to the honeycomb lattice, whose critical coupling is $\beta_cJ=\frac{1}{2}\ln(2+\sqrt{3})$ in the convention $H=-J\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}\sigma_i\sigma_j$ — equivalently $y_c=2-\sqrt{3}$ in the low-temperature variable $y=\mathrm{e}^{-\beta J}$ used below — with the spontaneous magnetization computed by Naya (1954). These solutions rest on a free-fermion structure — a representation of the partition function as a determinant describing noninteracting ("free") fermions, the Kac–Ward/Onsager algebra — of the $2\times2$ bond-weight matrices, together with planar duality.

The spin-one Ising model upgrades the local spin to $\sigma_i\in\{-1,0,+1\}$ while keeping only bilinear exchange, in the convention of the source paper $H=J\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}(1-\sigma_i\sigma_j)$ with $J>0$, so bond energies are $0$, $J$, or $2J$. Because the $\sigma=0$ state costs no single-site energy, this is exactly the Blume–Capel model (the spin-one Ising model with a crystal-field term $D\sum_i\sigma_i^2$) at $D=0$: the $\sigma=0$ sites act as annealed vacancies, meaning vacant sites in thermal equilibrium with the occupied ones rather than frozen quenched defects. It is the minimal integer-spin lattice model with annealed dilution, and it orders ferromagnetically at a finite temperature on the honeycomb lattice.

In two dimensions the spin-one Ising model has never been solved exactly on any standard lattice — a standing contrast with the spin-one-half case. The obstruction is structural: the $3\times3$ bond-weight matrix of a $\{-1,0,+1\}$ spin is not reducible to the free-fermion form underlying all spin-one-half solutions, and tracing out one honeycomb sublattice (the honeycomb lattice is bipartite with coordination number 3) produces a spin-one model with three-spin interactions on the triangular lattice rather than a solvable model. No duality that pins the critical point is known either.

What exact results do exist concern the generalized honeycomb spin-one Hamiltonian that adds a biquadratic exchange term $K\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}\sigma_i^2\sigma_j^2$ (coupling the squared spins of neighboring sites) and a single-ion crystal-field term $\Delta\sum_i\sigma_i^2$ (distinguishing the energy of the $\sigma=0$ state from that of the $\sigma=\pm1$ states) — the Blume–Emery–Griffiths form. Horiguchi (1986) discovered a solvable case, clarified by F. Y. Wu (1986) and worked out by X. N. Wu and F. Y. Wu (1988): under the constraint $K=-\ln\cosh J$ on the dimensionless couplings, the model maps exactly onto the spin-one-half Ising ferromagnet, yielding its free energy and spontaneous magnetization. Urumov (1987) obtained the exact magnetization along a line in the coupling–temperature plane, and Kolesík and Šamaj (1992) systematized such transformations, recovering the known and finding new solvable subspaces of the general model. Crucially, these solvable loci are temperature-dependent coupling relations: at the pure bilinear point $K=0$ the constraint $K=-\ln\cosh J$ collapses to the trivial $\beta J=0$, so none of them solves the model at hand. Rosengren and Häggkvist (1989) claimed a rigorous solution of a two-dimensional Blume–Emery–Griffiths model, which F. Y. Wu (1990) disputed; no accepted exact solution of the pure model resulted.

The numerical and rigorous state of the art for the pure model is as follows. Fox and Guttmann (1973) derived low-temperature series for the $S=1$ Ising model on several two- and three-dimensional lattices, estimating the honeycomb critical point $y_c=0.4217(10)$ in the low-temperature variable $y=\mathrm{e}^{-\beta J}$ and finding critical exponents consistent with the spin-one-half values. Warnaar, Nienhuis and Seaton (1992) showed that the solvable restricted solid-on-solid dilute-$A_3$ model (an exactly solvable lattice height model of the $A_3$ Dynkin-diagram family) is related to the two-dimensional critical spin-one Ising model in a magnetic field, fixing the critical-isotherm exponent $\delta=15$; the exponents are thus settled by integrable and conformal-field-theory relatives, but the critical temperature is not. Braga, Ferreira and Sá Barreto (1994) derived rigorous upper bounds on the critical temperature of the two-dimensional spin-one Blume–Emery–Griffiths model from correlation inequalities. Most recently, Kim (2025) enumerated the exact integer density of states $\Omega(E)$ of the pure model on $L\times2L$ honeycomb lattices ($N_s=2L^2$ sites, $N_b=3L^2-2L$ bonds) up to $L=14$, constructed the exact partition-function polynomials $Z(y)=\sum_E\Omega(E)y^E$, and studied the Fisher zeros — the zeros of $Z$ in the complex temperature plane, whose accumulation in the thermodynamic limit locates the singularities of thermodynamic functions. Bulirsch–Stoer extrapolation (a rational-function finite-size extrapolation) of the zero closest to the positive real axis gives $y_c=0.42191(1)$, i.e. $\beta_cJ\approx0.8630$, with thermal scaling exponent $y_t=1$ as expected for the two-dimensional Ising universality class, and locates inferred complex-temperature singularities at $y_1=-0.398(6)$, $y_2\approx-0.5+0.2i$, $y_3\approx0.563+0.826i$. All of these are inferences from exact finite-lattice data; nothing about the infinite honeycomb lattice is established exactly.

## Problem Statement

Determine, exactly, the thermodynamics of the spin-one Ising model on the honeycomb lattice: the ferromagnet with Hamiltonian $H=J\sum_{\langle i,j\rangle}(1-\sigma_i\sigma_j)$, where each spin takes values $\sigma_i\in\{-1,0,+1\}$ and $J>0$, on the honeycomb lattice in the thermodynamic limit at zero magnetic field. The complete objective is an exact solution — a closed-form expression for the per-site free energy (equivalently the partition function per site) valid at all temperatures, from which the exact ferromagnetic critical point $y_c=\mathrm{e}^{-\beta_cJ}$, the exact critical behavior, and the exact complex-temperature (Fisher-zero) singularity structure all follow; the antiferromagnetic critical point, in the same variable $y=\mathrm{e}^{-\beta J}$ with antiferromagnetic coupling $J<0$ (so $y>1$), is $1/y_c$ by the bipartite symmetry of the honeycomb lattice. The minimal standalone objective, stated explicitly by the source, is the exact critical temperature, currently bracketed numerically at $y_c=0.42191(1)$ (low-temperature series: $0.4217(10)$) but not known in closed form. These two deliverables are nested — the critical point is a component of the full solution — and jointly serve one objective: replacing finite-size inference by exact knowledge of this model. An acceptable answer must establish the claimed results as exact by rigorous derivation; a high-precision numerical enclosure, however tight, does not answer the question, and neither do exact results for the generalized model on special temperature-dependent coupling loci (such as $K=-\ln\cosh J$), which are adjacent but different problems.

The verification contract below evaluates answers to this statement. It does not narrow or redefine the research question.

Known solving difficulties:

- The $3\times3$ bond-weight matrix of a $\{-1,0,+1\}$ spin is not reducible to the free-fermion (Kac–Ward/Onsager) form that underlies every exactly solved two-dimensional spin-one-half model, so the standard toolbox of two-dimensional exact solutions does not apply.
- Tracing out either honeycomb sublattice (bipartite, coordination number 3) generates a spin-one model with three-spin interactions on the triangular lattice rather than a solvable model, and no planar duality pins the critical point as it does for the spin-one-half triangular–honeycomb pair.
- The known decoration and star-triangle mappings (local transformations replacing a site or a bond triangle by an equivalent cluster with renormalized couplings) onto the spin-one-half Ising model exist only on the temperature-dependent locus $K=-\ln\cosh J$ of the generalized (biquadratic) Hamiltonian; at the pure bilinear point $K=0$ the constraint collapses to $\beta J=0$, and extending these transformations to the fixed pure Hamiltonian has resisted work since 1986 (Horiguchi, Wu, Kolesík–Šamaj).
- Any exact solution would require a genuinely new mechanism — for example a hidden integrable structure of the spin-one transfer matrix, a novel duality, or a rigorous characterization of the limiting Fisher-zero locus — with high novelty cost, since the conventional approaches have been exhaustively explored over four decades.
- The sub-goal of an exact critical temperature alone may be unreachable except through, or alongside, the full solution, because no known quantity or symmetry pins it exactly.
- Answer validation is demanding: a claimed closed form must reproduce exact finite-lattice data (integer densities of states, polynomial partition functions up to $L=14$, series coefficients) and the inferred complex-temperature singularity structure, so incomplete or heuristic derivations will be detectable, while correct derivations will be long.

## Current Progress

- Status: `ready`

Source verification: the primary source (Kim 2025, Entropy 27(12), 1258, doi:10.3390/e27121258, inspected in full via PubMed Central) defines the model exactly as the pure bilinear spin-one Ising model $H=J\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}(1-\sigma_i\sigma_j)$, $\sigma_i\in\{-1,0,+1\}$, and states that it 'has never been solved exactly in spite of its simplicity' and that 'even its exact critical temperature is not known', in two dimensions generally and on the honeycomb lattice in particular. The LKM paraphrase is faithful to the source; no conflation of adjacent results was found, and the audit keeps the problem at its source generality.

The spin-one-half honeycomb Ising model has been exactly solved since Houtappel (1950, doi:10.1016/0031-8914(50)90130-3) — critical coupling $\beta_cJ=\frac{1}{2}\ln(2+\sqrt{3})$ in the convention $H=-J\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}\sigma_i\sigma_j$, i.e. $y_c=2-\sqrt{3}$ in the low-temperature variable $y=\mathrm{e}^{-\beta J}$; spontaneous magnetization by Naya (1954, doi:10.1143/PTP.11.53) — the benchmark against which the spin-one case has stood unsolved for roughly seven decades.

The only exact results for honeycomb spin-one models concern the generalized Hamiltonian with biquadratic exchange $K$ and crystal field $\Delta$: Horiguchi (1986, doi:10.1016/0375-9601(86)90665-1) found the first solvable case; F. Y. Wu (1986, doi:10.1016/0375-9601(86)90142-8) clarified it; X. N. Wu and F. Y. Wu (1988, doi:10.1007/BF01022986) showed that under the constraint $K=-\ln\cosh J$ (dimensionless couplings) the model maps exactly onto the spin-one-half Ising ferromagnet, obtaining its free energy and spontaneous magnetization; Urumov (1987, doi:10.1088/0022-3719/20/31/004) derived the exact magnetization along a line in the coupling–temperature plane; Kolesík and Šamaj (1992, doi:10.1142/S0217979292000724) systematized these transformations and found new solvable subspaces of the general model; Tucker (1991, doi:10.1063/1.348793) compared cluster effective-field-theory phase boundaries with these exact subspace results. All of these solvable loci impose temperature-dependent biquadratic coupling; at the pure bilinear point $K=0$ the constraint degenerates to $\beta J=0$, so none of them touches the model of this problem.

Rosengren and Häggkvist (1989, doi:10.1103/PhysRevLett.63.660) claimed a rigorous solution of a two-dimensional Blume–Emery–Griffiths model; F. Y. Wu (1990, doi:10.1103/PhysRevLett.64.700) published a comment disputing the claim and the authors replied (1990, doi:10.1103/PhysRevLett.64.701). The full texts of this exchange were not retrievable (publisher paywalls), but its outcome is legible from the subsequent literature: Gwa and Wu (1991, doi:10.1103/PhysRevB.43.13755) treated the honeycomb Blume–Emery–Griffiths critical surface by compact expressions validated against numerical data rather than as a solved model, and Braga, Ferreira and Sá Barreto (1994, doi:10.1007/BF02188687) derived rigorous upper bounds on the critical temperature of the two-dimensional spin-one Blume–Emery–Griffiths model — work that would be unnecessary if an exact critical point were known. No accepted exact solution of the pure model resulted from the 1989 claim.

Numerical determinations: Fox and Guttmann (1973, doi:10.1088/0022-3719/6/5/020) derived low-temperature series for the $S=1$ Ising model on several lattices and estimated the honeycomb critical point $y_c=0.4217(10)$ in the variable $y=\mathrm{e}^{-\beta J}$, with critical exponents indistinguishable from the spin-one-half values. Kim (2025, doi:10.3390/e27121258) enumerated the exact integer density of states on $L\times2L$ honeycomb lattices up to $L=14$, constructed the exact polynomial partition functions, and by Bulirsch–Stoer extrapolation of the Fisher zeros obtained $y_c=0.42191(1)$ (i.e. $\beta_cJ\approx0.8630$), consistent with the series value, together with the inferred complex-temperature singularities $y_1=-0.398(6)$, $y_2\approx-0.5+0.2i$, $y_3\approx0.563+0.826i$. These remain finite-size inferences from exact data: no thermodynamic-limit quantity of the pure model is established exactly.

Critical exponents, but not the critical point, are settled by integrable relatives: Warnaar, Nienhuis and Seaton (1992, doi:10.1103/PhysRevLett.69.710) related the two-dimensional critical spin-one Ising model in a magnetic field to the solvable restricted solid-on-solid dilute-$A_3$ model and obtained $\delta=15$ without scaling relations; the zero-field model is in the two-dimensional Ising universality class (thermal exponent $y_t=1$), as confirmed by both the series analysis and the zero extrapolations.

Forward coverage: Kim (2025, doi:10.3390/e27121258) has zero recorded citations (Crossref, OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar, checked 2026-08-30). Crossref sweeps of 2023–2026 literature for exact solutions of spin-one Ising and Blume–Capel honeycomb models found nothing; current work on the two-dimensional Blume–Capel family remains numerical (transfer-matrix and Monte Carlo studies). No work claims an exact critical temperature for any two-dimensional spin-one Ising model, and no narrower surviving core has been resolved: the problem stands at full source generality.

Coverage limits: the arXiv API was unreachable from this environment and general web search returned no usable results, so very recent preprints are covered only through Crossref/OpenAlex/Semantic Scholar; the content of the 1989–1990 Physical Review Letters exchange could not be read directly. Neither gap affects the conclusion: every verifiable indicator, from the 1973 series to the December 2025 primary source (Kim, Entropy 27(12), 1258, doi:10.3390/e27121258), treats the exact critical temperature of the pure spin-one Ising model on the honeycomb lattice as unknown, and the exact solution as nonexistent to date.

## Scientific Significance

Affected-field significance: `medium`.

Solving this problem would deliver the first exact solution and first exactly known critical temperature of any two-dimensional spin-one Ising model — the zero-crystal-field Blume–Capel model — extending exact two-dimensional lattice statistics beyond the spin-one-half (free-fermion) family. The impact would be direct for the theory of exactly solvable models in statistical mechanics: whichever mechanism produced the solution (a new integrability structure, duality, or rigorous characterization of the limiting Fisher-zero locus) would be a new capability, plausibly transferable to the square and triangular versions and to the wider Blume–Capel/Blume–Emery–Griffiths family, whose two-dimensional phase boundaries are otherwise known only numerically. It would also provide an exact reference point for the physics of annealed vacancies and tricritical behavior in two dimensions, and would sharpen the Kolesík–Šamaj classification of solvable subspaces of honeycomb spin-one models. The impact on the broader theory of critical phenomena would be indirect, since the model's universality class and critical exponents ($\nu=1$, $\alpha=0$; $\delta=15$) are already established by series analysis and by integrable (dilute-$A_3$) and conformal-field-theory results; what changes is exact solvability and exact reference data, not the qualitative theory of the transition.
