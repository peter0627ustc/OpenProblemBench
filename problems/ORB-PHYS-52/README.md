# ORB-PHYS-52: Almost-everywhere classical simulation of quantum query algorithms: the quantum analogue of average-case derandomization of query algorithms (Aaronson–Ambainis simulation conjecture)

In the query model, every randomized $q$-query algorithm can be simulated on almost all inputs by a deterministic $O(q/\varepsilon)$-query algorithm (Fact 1.1 of Blanc–Lange–Tan 2019, from the easy direction of Yao's lemma). Whether the analogous statement holds for quantum query algorithms — that every $t$-query quantum algorithm has its acceptance probabilities approximated on almost all inputs by a deterministic classical algorithm making only $\mathrm{poly}(t)$ queries — has been open since 1999 or earlier (Conjecture 4 of Aaronson–Ambainis 2014) and is the explicitly highlighted major open problem of Blanc–Lange–Tan's work on constructive derandomization of query algorithms. The standard sufficient route is the Aaronson–Ambainis conjecture: every polynomial $p:\{\pm1\}^n\to[0,1]$ of degree at most $t$ has a coordinate with influence at least $\mathrm{poly}(\mathrm{Var}(p)/t)$. As of August 2026 the general conjecture is open: the best general bound remains the $\exp(t)$ dependence of Dinur–Friedgut–Kindler–O'Donnell, and confirmed cases cover only restricted classes (symmetric functions; completely bounded block-multilinear forms; homogeneous Fourier completely bounded polynomials; random restrictions; and, very recently, parallel/low-round quantum algorithms with $t^{O(d^2)}$ classical queries). The formulation is verified against the primary sources; the surviving open core is the general, arbitrarily adaptive case, and it is the subject of this problem.

## Background

In the query model (decision-tree model) of computation, an algorithm accesses an input $x\in\{0,1\}^N$ only by querying coordinates $x_i$, and its cost is the number of queries; a randomized query algorithm additionally flips coins. A classical result that Blanc, Lange and Tan (2019) isolate as their Fact 1.1 — an easy consequence of the easy direction of Yao's min-max lemma, the averaging principle that some fixed setting of the algorithm's random bits performs on average (over uniform inputs) as well as the randomized algorithm itself — shows that any randomized $q$-query algorithm $R$ can be derandomized on average: for every $\varepsilon$ there is a deterministic $O(q/\varepsilon)$-query algorithm $D$ with $\mathbb{E}_{x}\big[(D(x)-\mathbb{E}_{r}[R(x,r)])^2\big]\le\varepsilon$, i.e. $D$ approximates $R$'s mean output on almost all inputs at only a constant-factor query overhead. Blanc–Lange–Tan made this and related statements (Nisan's STOC 1989 theorem that bounded-error randomized $q$-query algorithms for total decision problems — Boolean functions $f:\{0,1\}^N\to\{0,1\}$ defined on every input — admit exact deterministic $O(q^3)$-query algorithms, and Beals–Buhrman–Cleve–Mosca–de Wolf's Journal of the ACM 2001 result giving deterministic $O(Q(f)^6)$-query algorithms from quantum ones) constructive: e.g. a deterministic procedure running in time $\mathrm{poly}(N)\cdot 2^{O(q/\varepsilon)}$ that outputs an $O(q/\varepsilon)$-query simulator.

A quantum query algorithm $Q$ making $t$ queries applies interleaved arbitrary unitary transformations and $t$ invocations of a quantum oracle unitary (which maps $|i,b\rangle|x\rangle\mapsto|i,b\oplus x_i\rangle|x\rangle$ on the query register), then measures. Its acceptance probability $p_Q(x)$ is a function of the input. By the polynomial method (Beals–Buhrman–Cleve–Mosca–de Wolf, 2001), $p_Q:\{0,1\}^N\to[0,1]$ is a multilinear polynomial of degree at most $2t$; this is the basis of quantum lower bounds and of the connection to Fourier analysis on the Boolean cube (the expansion of such functions as multilinear polynomials in the orthogonal basis of parity functions).

The quantum analogue of Fact 1.1 — whether a $t$-query quantum algorithm can be simulated on almost all inputs by a deterministic classical query algorithm of polynomially related query complexity — is attributed by Aaronson and Ambainis to folklore dating back to 1999 or earlier, and is Conjecture 4 of their paper “The need for structure in quantum speedups” (Theory of Computing, 2014); it is often called the simulation conjecture. Aaronson–Ambainis reduced a sufficient form of it to a now-famous purely Fourier-analytic statement, the Aaronson–Ambainis conjecture: for a polynomial $p:\{\pm1\}^n\to[0,1]$ of degree at most $t$, define the influence of coordinate $i$ as $\mathrm{Inf}_i(p)=\mathbb{E}_x\,|p(x)-p(x^{\oplus i})|$ (where $x^{\oplus i}$ flips the $i$-th bit) and the variance $\mathrm{Var}(p)$ over uniform $x$; then some coordinate satisfies $\mathrm{Inf}_i(p)\ge\mathrm{poly}(\mathrm{Var}(p)/t)$. Under this conjecture the simulation is even constructive, with an efficient meta-algorithm conditional on $\mathsf{P}=\mathsf{P}^{\#\mathsf{P}}$ (here $\#\mathsf{P}$ is the counting complexity class). Despite almost two decades of work, the best general influence bound remains the $\exp(t)$ dependence implied by the Fourier-tails result of Dinur–Friedgut–Kindler–O'Donnell (STOC 2006), and all known proof techniques (which rely on hypercontractivity — a family of inequalities controlling the noise-sensitivity of low-degree polynomials, for which the exponential dependence on degree is inherent) stall at that barrier. Confirmed special cases include symmetric functions (Aaronson–Ambainis 2014); completely bounded block-multilinear forms — the norm-constrained class of multilinear polynomials that, by Arunachalam–Briët–Palazuelos (SIAM Journal on Computing, 2019), exactly characterizes the acceptance polynomials of quantum query algorithms — proven by Bansal–Sinha–de Wolf (2022) using free probability (asymptotic operator-algebra techniques), yielding almost-everywhere classical simulation for algorithms including $k$-fold Forrelation (a canonical unstructured black-box problem with a maximal quantum-over-classical query separation); homogeneous Fourier completely bounded polynomials (Escudero Gutiérrez, 2023); a non-negligible fraction of random restrictions (each coordinate fixed at random with a small probability; Bhattacharya, 2024); and, very recently, a noncommutative analogue for Hamiltonians built from anti-commuting Pauli strings (tensor products of the elementary Pauli matrices; Slote–Volberg–Zhang, 2026).

The question matters beyond quantum query complexity. A positive answer would give broad justification for the thesis that exponential quantum speedups require structured inputs (the “need for structure” principle); would rule out the query-complexity approach to separating $\mathsf{BPP}$ (bounded-error randomized polynomial time) from $\mathsf{BQP}$ (bounded-error quantum polynomial time) relative to a random oracle (an oracle whose answers are independent random bits; open since Fortnow–Rogers 1999); and would make unconditional the certifiable-randomness protocol (a protocol whose outputs are accompanied by a proof of statistical randomness) of Yamakawa–Zhandry (Journal of the ACM, 2024), whose security is currently proven only under the Aaronson–Ambainis conjecture or against shallow-query adversaries (Khurana–Roberts–Tal, 2026).

## Problem Statement

Prove or refute the quantum analogue of average-case derandomization of query algorithms (the simulation conjecture, Conjecture 4 of Aaronson–Ambainis, highlighted as the major open problem in Remark 1 of Blanc–Lange–Tan): for every quantum query algorithm $Q$ making $t$ queries to an input $x\in\{0,1\}^N$, there exists — even just non-constructively — a deterministic classical query algorithm $D$ making $\mathrm{poly}(t)$ queries whose output approximates the acceptance probability $p_Q(x)$ of $Q$ on almost all inputs, e.g. $\Pr_{x\sim\{0,1\}^N}\big[|D(x)-p_Q(x)|\le\tfrac13\big]\ge0.99$ with query complexity $\mathrm{poly}(t)$ (with $\mathrm{poly}(t,1/\delta)$ queries for a $1-\delta$ success guarantee). An admissible and standard sufficient route is a proof of the Aaronson–Ambainis influence conjecture: every polynomial $p:\{\pm1\}^n\to[0,1]$ of degree at most $t$ has some coordinate $i$ with $\mathrm{Inf}_i(p)=\mathbb{E}_x|p(x)-p(x^{\oplus i})|\ge\mathrm{poly}(\mathrm{Var}(p)/t)$. A valid proof must cover all $t$-query quantum algorithms — not merely restricted classes such as nonadaptive or low-round (parallel) algorithms (whose quantum oracle calls are grouped into few synchronized rounds), symmetric functions, or completely bounded block-multilinear/homogeneous special cases — though a proof exploiting the completely bounded structure of quantum acceptance polynomials is admissible if it applies to every quantum query algorithm. Constructive solutions (an efficient deterministic procedure outputting the simulator, as Blanc–Lange–Tan achieved for the classical randomized case) are welcome strengthenings, consistent with the source framing, but non-constructive existence already answers the stated question. A refutation consists of an explicit family of $t$-query quantum algorithms whose acceptance probabilities cannot be approximated within additive error $1/3$ on a $0.99$ fraction of inputs by any deterministic classical algorithm of $\mathrm{poly}(t)$ query complexity, for infinitely many $t$.

The verification contract below evaluates answers to this statement. It does not narrow or redefine the research question.

Known solving difficulties:

- Two-decade technical barrier: all known influence and Fourier-tail bounds for bounded low-degree polynomials on the Boolean cube saturate at exp(t) dependence because they rely on hypercontractivity, for which the exponential loss in degree is inherent; beating poly(t) requires genuinely new techniques.
- Confirmed cases each needed distinct machinery and cover only restricted classes — symmetric functions (Aaronson–Ambainis), completely bounded block-multilinear forms via free probability (Bansal–Sinha–de Wolf), homogeneous Fourier completely bounded polynomials (Escudero Gutiérrez), random restrictions (Bhattacharya) — so no unified attack is known even for natural subclasses.
- On the quantum side, adaptivity is the core obstacle: the simulation conjecture was settled only for parallel/low-round algorithms (t^{O(d^2)} classical queries, Blanc–Docter–Strassle–Tan 2026), and handling arbitrarily interleaved oracle calls apparently requires new structural information about query-weight distributions beyond the polynomial (acceptance-probability) abstraction.
- A refutation would require an explicit family of bounded low-degree polynomials (or quantum algorithms) with all influences superpolynomially small in the degree, consistent with every confirmed special case and with the exp(t) general bound — no candidate family is known.
- Constructive resolutions additionally require efficient deterministic procedures for building the simulator with near-optimal parameters (query O(q/ε), runtime poly(N)·2^{O(q/ε)}), matching the classical constructive results of Blanc–Lange–Tan.

## Current Progress

- Status: `ready`

Source fidelity verified against the primary source (arXiv:1912.03042 HTML). The candidate's direction (i) is Remark 1 of Blanc–Lange–Tan verbatim: obtaining a quantum analogue of Fact 1.1 — even just non-constructively — is called a major open problem, attributed to Conjecture 4 of Aaronson–Ambainis (2014), folklore since 1999 or earlier; the Aaronson–Ambainis influence conjecture is stated to remain a major open problem in the analysis of Boolean functions. The paper itself contributes Lemma 2.1: the influence conjecture holds for mean functions of randomized query algorithms (some influence at least $\mathrm{Var}/q$), which underlies its constructive classical derandomizations.

Two further directions highlighted by the source paper (arXiv:1912.03042) remain unaddressed in the literature I found: (ii) a single unified constructive algorithm combining the $O(q/\varepsilon)$ query cost and $\mathrm{poly}(N)\cdot2^{O(q/\varepsilon)}$ runtime of Theorem 1, the instance-optimality of Theorem 2, and the online bounds of Theorem 4/Theorem 11 is explicitly left as future work; (iii) the uniform dequantization of Corollary 2 incurs $\mathrm{poly}(2^m)\cdot\mathrm{quasipoly}(n)$ preprocessing in the quantum-space parameter $m$, a stated limitation (the LKM record slightly overstates it as an explicitly flagged open question). Neither affects the identity of the core open problem, which is direction (i).

Aaronson–Ambainis (Theory of Computing, 2014, DOI 10.4086/toc.2014.v010a006) formulated the simulation conjecture and reduced it (in sufficient form) to the influence conjecture; they proved the conjecture for symmetric functions. Dinur–Friedgut–Kindler–O'Donnell (STOC 2006, DOI 10.1145/1132516.1132580) on Fourier tails of bounded functions yields an $\exp(t)$-query simulation; per the August 2026 literature this remains the best general bound, with all known proofs blocked at $\exp(t)$ by their reliance on hypercontractivity.

Arunachalam–Briët–Palazuelos (SIAM Journal on Computing, 2019, DOI 10.1137/18m117563x) characterized quantum query algorithms exactly as completely bounded forms, enabling structural attacks: Bansal–Sinha–de Wolf (arXiv:2203.00212, 2022) proved the influence conjecture for completely bounded block-multilinear polynomials (via free probability), giving almost-everywhere $\mathrm{poly}(t)$-query classical simulation for a class of quantum algorithms including $k$-fold Forrelation; Escudero Gutiérrez (arXiv:2304.06713, 2023) proved it for homogeneous Fourier completely bounded polynomials and gave a simpler proof of the 2022 result. These are genuine partial resolutions but cover only special subclasses of the acceptance-probability polynomials.

Bhattacharya (arXiv:2402.13952, 2024) proved the influence conjecture holds after applying a random restriction with survival probability $\log(d)/(C_1 d)$ to a non-negligible fraction of restrictions — evidence for, not a resolution of, the general conjecture. Slote–Volberg–Zhang (arXiv:2608.04411, 2026) proved a noncommutative/quantum analogue of the Aaronson–Ambainis conjecture for Hamiltonians built on anti-commuting Pauli strings, together with tightness results and counterexamples for adjacent inequalities (Bohnenblust–Hille for $d$-local Hamiltonians, quantum Fourier entropy–influence); the commutative Boolean-cube conjecture is untouched.

The conjecture's downstream stakes rose: Yamakawa–Zhandry (Journal of the ACM, 2024, DOI 10.1145/3658665) proved security of a certifiable-randomness / verifiable-quantum-advantage construction conditional on the Aaronson–Ambainis conjecture; Khurana–Roberts–Tal (2025–2026, arXiv:2608.24832) made the security unconditional only against adversaries of $o(\log\lambda)$ adaptive quantum query depth, explicitly referring to the still-unproven Aaronson–Ambainis conjecture (as of 25 August 2026).

Most decisive for current status: Blanc–Docter–Strassle–Tan (arXiv:2608.19158, 19 August 2026), by the same group as the source paper, settle the simulation conjecture only for parallel quantum algorithms — every $t$-query $d$-round quantum algorithm is simulated on most inputs with $t^{O(d^2)}$ classical queries — state that the general case for highly adaptive algorithms remains open, propose a new (weaker, still unproven) 'query weights' conjecture that would suffice, and confirm the $\exp(t)$ hypercontractivity barrier for the influence formulation. The arbitrarily adaptive case therefore survives as a precise open core.

## Scientific Significance

Affected-field significance: `high`.

A positive resolution would directly establish a polynomial relationship between quantum and classical query complexity on average — core knowledge about where quantum speedups are and are not possible, formalizing the 'need for structure' principle for black-box computation. It would simultaneously settle a 25-year-old open problem in the analysis of Boolean functions (influences of bounded low-degree polynomials), directly close the query-complexity route to separating BPP from BQP relative to a random oracle, and make unconditional the certifiable-randomness and verifiable-quantum-advantage applications of Yamakawa–Zhandry whose security currently rests on the unproven conjecture. A refutation would be equally transformative, revealing an average-case quantum-classical query gap for unstructured problems and reopening these applications. The impact is direct on quantum query complexity, discrete Fourier analysis, and quantum cryptography.
