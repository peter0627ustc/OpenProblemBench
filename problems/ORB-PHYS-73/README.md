# ORB-PHYS-73: Exact critical coupling of the q-state Potts model on the kagome lattice

The ferromagnetic q-state Potts model on the kagome lattice - the Archimedean lattice of corner-sharing triangles - has resisted exact solution for more than forty-five years; Wu's 1982 review called the kagome lattice "the simplest lattice on which an exact solution has not yet been found." The critical coupling is known exactly on the square, triangular, and honeycomb lattices and for the Ising case $q=2$ ($v_c=\sqrt{3+2\sqrt{3}}-1$), but for $q\ne 2$ on the kagome lattice only conjectures and extraordinarily precise numerics exist. Wu's 1979 sixth-order polynomial conjecture was refuted at 95-205 standard deviations, Tsallis' 1982 alternative was rigorously disproved in its percolation case, and the 2011 internal-energy strip-crossing conjecture of Baek, Mäkelä, Minnhagen, and Kim was disproved by the authors' own $L=3$ transfer-matrix calculation, which showed that finite-size corrections are nonzero for $q\ne 2$. This audit verifies source fidelity against the primary literature, reconstructs the citation lineage, and isolates the surviving open core: an exact closed-form expression for the critical coupling $v_c(q)$ as a function of $q$ - including the $q\to 1$ limit, the kagome bond-percolation threshold, known numerically to seventeen digits ($p_c=0.52440499916744820(1)$) but with no closed form. The problem remains open.

## Background

The q-state Potts model is a lattice spin model in which each site carries a variable $\sigma_i$ taking one of $q$ discrete values and each nearest-neighbor bond contributes an energy $-J\,\delta(\sigma_i,\sigma_j)$ that favors alignment, with $J>0$ the ferromagnetic coupling. Its critical behavior is governed by the dimensionless coupling $K=\beta J$ (with $\beta$ the inverse temperature), or equivalently by $v=e^K-1$, which grows from 0 at infinite temperature to infinity at zero temperature. In the Fortuin-Kasteleyn (FK) representation the model is rewritten as a bond-percolation ensemble on the same lattice, in which each bond is independently occupied with probability $p=1-e^{-K}$ and every connected cluster carries a combinatorial weight $q$; setting $q=1$ recovers ordinary bond percolation, so the percolation threshold is the $q\to 1$ limit of the Potts critical point, with $p=v/(1+v)$.

The kagome lattice is the Archimedean (vertex-transitive) tiling of the plane by alternating triangles and hexagons, denoted $(3,6,3,6)$, in which every site has four nearest neighbors (coordination number 4); it is the lattice of corner-sharing triangles and the medial lattice of the honeycomb lattice. Its planar dual (obtained by placing a site in each face and joining sites of adjacent faces) is the dice lattice, and the critical couplings of the two lattices are related by the exact duality relation $v_c\,\tilde v_c=q$. On the square lattice the ferromagnetic Potts transition is known to be continuous for $q\le 4$ and first order for $q>4$, and this is believed to hold on any two-dimensional lattice.

Exact Potts critical points are known only on a restricted class of planar lattices: the square lattice (self-dual), and the triangular and honeycomb lattices, whose solution combines duality with the star-triangle transformation - an exact local equivalence between a triangle of three couplings and a three-legged star, which is the algebraic mechanism underlying solvability. For the kagome lattice the Ising case $q=2$ was solved exactly by Kano and Naya in 1953, giving $v_c(2)=\sqrt{3+2\sqrt{3}}-1\approx 1.542459756$; the Ising model is special, being solvable by duality and star-triangle arguments on essentially any planar lattice. For $q\ne 2$ no exact solution is known on the kagome lattice. Wu's 1982 review of the Potts model describes the kagome lattice as "the simplest lattice on which an exact solution has not yet been found" - a characterization still current in the recent literature - and two closed-form conjectures long stood: Wu (1979) proposed that the kagome critical manifold is the zero locus of the sixth-order algebraic curve
$$
v^6+6v^5+9v^4-2qv^3-12qv^2-6q^2v-q^3=0,
$$
derived from exact equivalences plus a non-rigorous homogeneity assumption, and Tsallis (1982) proposed an alternative form. Both are now refuted: Jacobsen and Scullard (2012) showed that numerical critical values rule out Wu's curve at a confidence level of 95 (respectively 205) standard deviations for $q=3$ (respectively $q=4$), identifying it as exactly the smallest (6-edge) approximation in a convergent sequence with no exact status, and Wierman, Yu, and Huang (2015) rigorously disproved Tsallis' conjecture in its bond-percolation case.

Baek, Mäkelä, Minnhagen, and Kim (2011) attacked the problem with a transfer-matrix method. A transfer matrix propagates the statistical weight of a spin configuration from one slice of a semi-infinite strip of width $L$ to the next; its largest eigenvalue yields the strip free energy, and derivatives with respect to $\beta$ yield observables such as the internal energy per spin $u_L$. From exact $L=1$ and $L=2$ calculations performed in arbitrary-precision arithmetic, they observed that curves of $u_L$ versus $K$ for different widths appear to cross at a width-independent point, and combined this crossing with the kagome-dice duality relation to conjecture the exact critical couplings $K_c(q=3)=1.0565094269290$ and $K_c(q=4)=1.1493605872292$. They also searched for a sixth-order polynomial with small integer coefficients having their value $v_c(q=3)=1.876313463895$ as a root, finding only a near miss (a polynomial whose root is $1.876313463898$) and concluding that no such polynomial exists in the tested coefficient ranges, and they posed the exact $q$-dependence of $K_c(q)$, encompassing the $q=1$ percolation case, as the open problem.

The width-invariance premise was disproved within two years. Baek and Mäkelä (2013) computed the $L=3$ transfer matrix exactly in factorized form and found $u_{L=3}=-1.62949\ldots$ at the conjectured critical point, deviating from the conjectured $-1.6295437063996$: finite-size corrections are nonzero for $q\ne 2$. Jacobsen and Scullard (2012) reached the same conclusion independently, noting that exact internal-energy crossings hold only for the square-lattice Potts model (by duality) and for the Ising model on general lattices, and that the 2011 values are incompatible with their own numerics. The 2011 "exact" values are therefore refuted; the refined analysis of Baek and Mäkelä yields the bounds $1.0560<K_c(q=3)<1.0565615$.

The state of the art is the critical-polynomial (graph-polynomial) method of Jacobsen and Scullard. For a finite basis $B$ - a chosen cluster of edges of the lattice - one computes, by the contraction-deletion recursion satisfied by the FK weight, a bivariate polynomial $P_B(q,v)$ whose zero locus approximates the critical manifold; Wu's conjecture is precisely the 6-edge basis result. Larger bases give systematically improving approximations that converge to the numerically observed critical points, and a reformulation in terms of eigenvalue identities in the periodic Temperley-Lieb algebra - the algebra of non-crossing loop operators describing the FK clusters - makes bases of up to $n=16$ unit cells tractable. The resulting values are extraordinarily precise but still not exact: the extrapolations $v_c(q=3)=1.876459(2)$ and $v_c(q=4)=2.156252(2)$ (Jacobsen-Scullard 2012), and, for $q=1$ bond percolation, the kagome threshold $p_c=0.52440499916744820(1)$ (Scullard-Jacobsen 2020) with rigorous bounds $0.522551<p_c<0.526490$. Scullard and Jacobsen emphasize that exact solutions remain available only on a restricted class of lattices, the kagome lattice being among the unsolved Archimedean lattices. One further hint of possible exact structure: Anglès d'Auriac (1999) presented random-matrix-theory evidence that the critical point of the three-state kagome Potts model is integrable, but no algebraic solution has been derived from it since.

## Problem Statement

Determine the exact critical coupling of the ferromagnetic q-state Potts model on the infinite kagome lattice. In the variable $v=e^K-1$ (with $K=\beta J$), the goal is an exact closed-form expression for the critical value $v_c(q)$ as a function of the state count $q$ - for instance an explicit algebraic curve $P(q,v)=0$ whose physical branch gives $v_c(q)$ - together with a proof that the expression is exactly the critical point of the model on the infinite lattice. The primary targets are $q=3$ and $q=4$, for which sharp numerical anchors exist but no closed form is known: $v_c(3)=1.876459(2)$ and $v_c(4)=2.156252(2)$. A complete answer also fixes the general integer-$q$ behavior and the $q\to 1$ limit, which is the bond-percolation threshold of the kagome lattice, $p_c=v_c(1)/(1+v_c(1))$, known numerically to $p_c=0.52440499916744820(1)$ but not exactly; at minimum, an acceptable answer must settle $q=3$ and $q=4$ exactly and state precisely what it establishes for general integer $q$ and for the $q\to 1$ limit. The Ising case $q=2$ is already solved exactly, $v_c(2)=\sqrt{3+2\sqrt{3}}-1$ (Kano-Naya), and is not part of the open problem. An answer may alternatively consist of a rigorous exact determination of $v_c(q)$ by other means - for example, proving the infinite-basis limit of the kagome critical polynomials $P_B(q,v)$, or exhibiting a new exact solvability (integrable structure) of the kagome Potts model - or of a proof that no algebraic equation of the conjectured Wu-Tsallis type (low degree in $v$, small integer coefficients) has $v_c(q)$ as a root. This is the problem behind Wu's 1982 characterization of the kagome lattice as "the simplest lattice on which an exact solution has not yet been found".

The verification contract below evaluates answers to this statement. It does not narrow or redefine the research question.

Known solving difficulties:

- The kagome lattice lies outside the restricted class of lattices with exact Potts solutions (square, triangular, honeycomb): a solution would require either a new integrable structure of Yang-Baxter type or a fundamentally new exact-criticality criterion, and neither is known.
- Both historical closed-form conjectures (Wu 1979, Tsallis 1982) were refuted, and the 2011 'exact' values were disproved by their own authors' next calculation; the problem has repeatedly punished plausible guesses, so acceptance demands proof rather than numerical agreement.
- The critical-polynomial method converges only in the infinite-basis limit (empirically with exponent $w\approx 6.35$ for three-fold-symmetric lattices), no finite basis is exact on the kagome lattice for $q\ne 2$, and identifying the limit in closed form is itself unsolved.
- Transfer-matrix state spaces grow as $q^{3L}$ with strip width $L$, and finite-size corrections are provably nonzero for $q\ne 2$ (Baek-Mäkelä 2013), so exact values cannot be extracted from any finite strip.
- The random-matrix evidence for integrability of the $q=3$ critical point (Anglès d'Auriac 1999) has resisted conversion into an algebraic solution for over twenty-five years.
- Proving the complementary negative result - that no low-degree, small-integer-coefficient algebraic equation has $v_c(q)$ as a root - lacks standard techniques, since degrees and coefficient magnitudes are unbounded a priori.
- Rigorous threshold bounds from duality and substitution arguments converge too slowly to identify a closed form.

## Current Progress

- Status: `ready`

Source fidelity: the candidate derives from LKM open question gcn_c16978791561474c attached to Baek, Mäkelä, Minnhagen, and Kim (2011). The full text (arXiv:1106.1296) was inspected directly, and all four bundled issues are present in the paper: (i) the call for "a complete analytic argument" behind the observed strip-width independence of the internal energy at criticality; (ii) extension of the transfer-matrix calculation to widths $L\ge 3$ (the state-space dimension grows as $q^{3L}$); (iii) the failed search for a sixth-order integer-coefficient polynomial with root $v_c(q=3)=1.876313463895$ (best fit a near miss at $1.876313463898$, with coefficient ranges $c_6=1$, $c_5,c_4\in[-25,25]$, others in $[-102,102]$); and (iv) the exact $q$-dependence of $K_c(q)$, including the $q=1$ percolation case, where the strip method is inapplicable because the internal energy is $K$-independent there. Attribution is correct, no adjacent results were conflated, and the LKM summary is faithful to the source.

Wu's 1979 conjecture (the sixth-order critical curve) is definitively refuted: Jacobsen and Scullard (2012; arXiv:1204.0622; J. Phys. A: Math. Theor. 45, 494003) showed their numerical critical values are incompatible with it at 95 standard deviations for $q=3$ and 205 for $q=4$, and identified the conjecture as exactly the 6-edge-basis critical polynomial - the smallest approximation in a convergent sequence, with no exact status.

Tsallis' 1982 alternative conjecture is also refuted: Wierman, Yu, and Huang (2015; Electron. J. Combin. 22 (2), Paper P2.52; DOI: 10.37236/5117) rigorously disproved its bond-percolation threshold prediction for the kagome lattice using exact substitution-method bounds.

The Baek et al. (2011) strip-crossing conjecture was disproved by its own authors: Baek and Mäkelä (2013; arXiv:1401.1566; J. Korean Phys. Soc. 63, 1167) computed the $L=3$ transfer matrix exactly and found $u_{L=3}=-1.62949\ldots$ at their conjectured $K_c$, versus the conjectured $-1.6295437063996$, concluding that "the absence of finite-size corrections assumed in Ref. [4] is not true in general except for $q=2$." The 2011 exact values $K_c(q=3)=1.0565094269290$ and $K_c(q=4)=1.1493605872292$ are thereby refuted; the same authors give corrected bounds $1.0560<K_c(q=3)<1.0565615$, consistent with the Jacobsen-Scullard estimate $K_c=1.0565600(7)$. This resolves the source question's sub-issues (i) and (ii) negatively: width-invariance fails for $q\ne 2$, and the $L=3$ calculation was the decisive test.

Jacobsen and Scullard (2012; arXiv:1204.0622) independently concluded that internal-energy crossings exhibit finite-size corrections for $q\ne 2$ on the kagome lattice (exact crossings hold only for the square-lattice Potts model by duality, and for the Ising model on any lattice), stated that the 2011 values are incompatible with their numerics, and provided the current best ferromagnetic anchors $v_c(3)=1.876459(2)$ and $v_c(4)=2.156252(2)$ from critical-polynomial extrapolation.

The $q=1$ percolation case is pinned numerically far beyond conjecture-testing needs, but with no closed form: Scullard and Jacobsen (2016; arXiv:1511.04374) obtained $p_c=0.52440499917(1)$ from critical polynomials on bases up to $6\times 6$ unit cells (convergence exponent $w\approx 6.35$ for three-fold-symmetric lattices); Jacobsen (2015; arXiv:1507.03027) obtained $p_c=0.524404999167439(4)$ from eigenvalue identities in periodic Temperley-Lieb algebras; and Scullard and Jacobsen (2020; arXiv:1910.12376; Phys. Rev. Research 2, 012050) reached $p_c=0.52440499916744820(1)$ with bases up to $n=16$, alongside rigorous bounds $0.522551<p_c<0.526490$. All of these are extrapolations, not exact solutions; the 2020 paper states that exact solutions are available only on a restricted class of lattices, the kagome lattice remaining among the unsolved Archimedean lattices.

A standing hint of possible exact structure: Anglès d'Auriac (1999; Europhys. Lett. 48 (1), 15; DOI: 10.1209/epl/i1999-00107-2) gave random-matrix-theory evidence that the critical point of the three-state kagome Potts model is integrable; no Bethe-ansatz or Yang-Baxter-type solution has been derived from it in the subsequent quarter century.

Formulation correction and surviving core: because the width-invariance premise was disproved, the audited problem is stated on the surviving core - the exact critical coupling $v_c(q)$ (closed form, exact characterization by other means, or proof of non-existence of the conjectured algebraic type), including the $q=1$ percolation limit - rather than on the 2011 bundle's premise. The polynomial-search sub-issue survives with corrected numerics: the 2011 near-miss search targeted the now-refuted value $1.876313463895$, whereas the current anchor is $v_c(3)=1.876459(2)$, so the question of whether $v_c(q)$ is algebraic in $q$ is intact and sharpened.

Coverage: the forward citation chain of the 2011 paper was followed through Semantic Scholar (four of five indexed citing works examined; the fifth could not be retrieved before the OpenAlex quota was exhausted), and the adjacent literature (Jacobsen-Scullard 2012; Jacobsen 2015; Scullard-Jacobsen 2016 and 2020; Baek-Mäkelä 2013; Wierman-Yu-Huang 2015; Anglès d'Auriac 1999) was traced and inspected directly. No traced work claims an exact solution of the kagome-lattice Potts model for $q\ne 2$; the problem is repeatedly described as open. The audit outcome is therefore: open.

## Scientific Significance

Affected-field significance: `high`.

An exact $v_c(q)$ would directly change core knowledge of exactly solvable two-dimensional models: it would close the problem Wu's 1982 review described as "the simplest lattice on which an exact solution has not yet been found," move the kagome lattice into the exactly solved class (currently only the square, triangular, and honeycomb lattices), and yield the first exact bond-percolation threshold beyond the duality-solvable cases (the $q\to 1$ limit), replacing the numerically pinned $p_c=0.52440499916744820(1)$ with a closed form. The impact on critical-point knowledge is direct; it would also likely be methodologically transformative, since a solution would require either a new integrable structure or a proof that the critical-polynomial sequence converges to an identifiable limit - capabilities transferable to the remaining unsolved Archimedean lattices and to the classification of solvable Potts lattices.
