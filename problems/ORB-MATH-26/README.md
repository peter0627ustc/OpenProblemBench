# ORB-MATH-26: One-dimensionality of stable Allen-Cahn solutions with bounded energy density in dimensions 5 through 7

The stable De Giorgi classification conjecture for the Allen-Cahn equation predicts that every stable solution $u:\mathbb{R}^n\to[-1,1]$ with bounded energy density is one-dimensional in all dimensions $n\le 7$. This is known for $n=2$ (1998), $n=3$ (2000-2001), and, since 2025, for $n=4$; the cases $n=5,6,7$ remain open. The recent $n=4$ proof of Florit-Simon and Serra identifies a precise quantitative obstruction to extending their multi-scale improvement-of-flatness argument: the available Wang-Wei mean-curvature estimates, rescaled near a high-curvature center, are too weak exactly when $n\ge 5$. The open problem is to complete the classification in the remaining range $5\le n\le 7$, most plausibly by exploiting the Toda-type system governing the interaction between transition layers to obtain sharper mean-curvature control at critical scales. A positive answer would also settle, via known reductions, the Marques-Neves multiplicity-one and Morse index conjectures for Allen-Cahn approximations of minimal hypersurfaces in those dimensions.

## Background

The Allen-Cahn equation $-\Delta u + W'(u)=0$ in $\mathbb{R}^n$, with $W(u)=\frac{1}{4}(1-u^2)^2$ a double-well potential, is the basic PDE model of phase transitions: bounded solutions interpolate between the pure phases $\pm 1$ across interfaces of thickness $O(1)$. A solution is called stable if the second variation of the energy $\int(\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^2+W(u))\,dx$ is nonnegative on all compactly supported perturbations. Stability is implied by monotonicity in one coordinate direction or by local minimality, and by itself rules out nontrivial bounded solutions other than $\pm1$ only under additional hypotheses. The one-dimensional solutions are $u\equiv\pm1$ and the planar layers $u(x)=\tanh((e\cdot x-s_0)/\sqrt2)$ for a unit vector $e$ and a shift $s_0\in\mathbb{R}$. The energy of $u$ in a ball $B_r$ is $\mathcal{E}_1(u,B_r)=\int_{B_r}\big(\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^2+W(u)\big)\,dx$, and the energy density is $\mathbf{M}_r(u)=r^{1-n}\mathcal{E}_1(u,B_r)$; by Modica's monotonicity formula $r\mapsto\mathbf{M}_r(u)$ is nondecreasing, so uniform boundedness of $\mathbf{M}_r$ in $r$ amounts to $\mathbf{M}_\infty(u)<\infty$ and is equivalent to the energy growing at most like $r^{n-1}$ (cubic growth in the four-dimensional setting of the source paper); it holds automatically for one-dimensional solutions. De Giorgi's 1978 conjecture concerned monotone solutions, i.e. solutions nondecreasing in one coordinate direction, and was proved for $n=2$ by Ghoussoub and Gui (1998), for $n=3$ by Ambrosio-Cabré (2000) with the general-nonlinearity version by Alberti-Ambrosio-Cabré (2001), and for $n\le 8$ under an additional limits-at-infinity hypothesis by Savin (2009). The stable version asks the same classification for stable solutions, where the expected threshold is $n\le 7$: by the analogy with the Bernstein problem for minimal hypersurfaces (the question whether entire minimal graphs must be affine, recently settled affirmatively for stable minimal hypersurfaces in $\mathbb{R}^4$ by Chodosh and Li, 2024), and because Pacard-Wei (2013) and Liu-Wang-Wei (2017) constructed stable (even globally minimizing) non-one-dimensional Allen-Cahn solutions built on minimal cones in dimensions $n\ge 8$. Under the bounded-energy-density assumption, Wang and Wei (2019) developed a regularity theory for solutions with several nearly parallel flat interfaces — including their description as unions of $C^{2,\vartheta}$ graphs (the sheeting theory) and mean-curvature estimates governed at main order by a Toda-type integrable system of ODEs describing the interaction between neighboring layers — and this reduces the classification to a precise conjecture: Conjecture 1.2 of Florit-Simon and Serra (2025) asserts that stable solutions with bounded energy density are one-dimensional for every $n\le 7$. Before that paper the conjecture was known only for $n=2$ and $n=3$, both results over 25 years old. Florit-Simon and Serra proved the case $n=4$, introducing a continuous-induction scheme on the energy density at infinity, a tangential form of the stability inequality, and a new monotonicity-type formula for the height excess; the authors state that the paper introduces ideas that may prove useful for addressing the remaining cases $n=5,6,7$. Their Section 3.4 pinpoints why the same strategy stalls for $5\le n\le 7$: near an isolated high-curvature center, combining the rescaled Wang-Wei mean-curvature bound $O((\widetilde R_k\widetilde\varepsilon_k)^{-1})$ with the sharp flatness lower bound $\widetilde\varepsilon_k^2\gtrsim \widetilde R_k^{-(n-3)}$ leaves mean curvature too large for the improvement-of-flatness iteration (the iterative scheme that successively straightens interfaces already close to flat) precisely when $n\ge 5$; the authors note that overcoming this seems to require extracting finer information from the Toda-type system behind the Wang-Wei estimates rather than using them as a black box. The classification has geometric consequences beyond the equation itself: by results of Wang-Wei and Chodosh-Mantoulidis (2020), a positive answer in dimension $n$ implies robust curvature estimates for stable phase transitions and the Marques-Neves (2016) multiplicity-one and Morse index conjectures for Allen-Cahn min-max approximations of minimal hypersurfaces in closed $n$-manifolds (min-max hypersurfaces are produced by a variational sweepout construction; the conjectures predict that the Allen-Cahn min-max hypersurfaces arise with multiplicity one and that their Morse index — the number of independent directions in which the second variation is negative — matches the number of sweepout parameters); the $4\le n\le 7$ cases of those conjectures are, per the literature, bottlenecked exactly on this classification. The problem is stated here for the full remaining range $5\le n\le 7$; the stronger stable De Giorgi conjecture without the bounded-density hypothesis is a strictly harder question that remains open even in $n=3$ and is not part of this problem.

## Problem Statement

Prove or refute Conjecture 1.2 of Florit-Simon-Serra (2025) in the remaining dimensions $5\le n\le 7$: must every stable solution $u:\mathbb{R}^n\to[-1,1]$ of the Allen-Cahn equation $-\Delta u+W'(u)=0$ (with $W(u)=\frac14(1-u^2)^2$) whose energy density $\mathbf{M}_r(u)=r^{1-n}\mathcal{E}_1(u,B_r)$, where $\mathcal{E}_1(u,B_r)=\int_{B_r}\big(\frac12|\nabla u|^2+W(u)\big)\,dx$, is uniformly bounded in $r$ be one-dimensional, i.e. either $u\equiv\pm1$ or $u(x)=\tanh\big((e\cdot x-s_0)/\sqrt2\big)$ for some unit vector $e\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}$ and $s_0\in\mathbb{R}$? A positive resolution must deliver the classification in all three dimensions $n=5,6,7$ (the dimensions may be treated separately, but the problem is settled only when the whole range is covered); a refutation must exhibit, in some single $5\le n\le 7$, a stable non-one-dimensional solution with uniformly bounded energy density, which disproves the conjecture as stated. A solution that proceeds through the Wang-Wei multi-layer theory is expected to require, and should include as an identified ingredient, sharper mean-curvature control at the critical scales $\rho\sim\widetilde\varepsilon_k\widetilde R_k$ than the black-box $C^{2,\vartheta}$ estimates provide - for instance via the Toda-type system governing inter-layer interactions - since the known estimates provably stall for $n\ge5$.

The verification contract below evaluates answers to this statement. It does not narrow or redefine the research question.

Known solving difficulties:

- The known multi-scale strategy provably stalls for $n\ge5$: after rescaling near an isolated high-curvature center, the Wang-Wei mean-curvature estimates combine with the sharp flatness lower bound to leave too much curvature for the improvement-of-flatness iteration, so genuinely new input (most plausibly quantitative exploitation of the Toda-type system governing inter-layer interaction, or sharper intermediate Wang-Wei estimates) is required.
- The $n=4$ proof's key tools - continuous induction on the energy density at infinity, the tangential stability inequality, and the monotonicity-type formula for the height excess - were tuned to dimension four, and their constants and density-pinching step degrade with $n$; extending them demands reengineering the whole scheme, not just one estimate.
- The intrinsic minimal-surface route (Schoen-Simon-Yau style Simons/Schoen inequalities), which settled the stable Bernstein problem in $n=4,5,6$, appears inapplicable to the diffuse-interface setting, so no off-the-shelf alternative method exists.
- Handling the possible coexistence of several bad high-curvature centers and the global varifold/density-pinching bookkeeping in higher dimensions adds substantial technical overhead beyond the single-center idealized obstruction already identified.
- A refutation, if that is the truth, would require constructing a genuinely new type of stable bounded-density solution below the cone threshold $n=8$, itself a hard gluing/Lyapunov-spectral problem; the problem could also be true in some of $n=5,6,7$ and false in others, so each dimension may need separate treatment.

## Current Progress

Florit-Simon and Serra (arXiv:2509.02739, 2025) discuss Conjectures 1.1–1.2 and the higher-dimensional obstruction in Section 3.4. The paper proves Conjecture 1.2 in $n=4$ under the bounded-energy-density hypothesis, states the previously known cases are only $n=2$ (Ghoussoub-Gui 1998) and $n=3$ (Ambrosio-Cabré 2000; Alberti-Ambrosio-Cabré 2001), identifies the quantitative obstruction (rescaled mean curvature $O((\widetilde R_k\widetilde\varepsilon_k)^{-1})$ versus the sharp lower bound $\widetilde\varepsilon_k^2\gtrsim \widetilde R_k^{-(n-3)}$, insufficient for $n\ge5$), and suggests the Toda-type layer-interaction system as the plausible route around it. The remaining cases of Conjecture 1.2 are $n=5,6,7$. The density-free strong conjecture is a broader question, and the optimal-exponent questions are related directions.

Lineage before the source: the stable classification with bounded energy density was open in every dimension $n\ge4$ for over 25 years after the $n=2$ (1998) and $n=3$ (2000-2001) results; the threshold $n\le7$ is known to be sharp through the cone-type stable solutions of Pacard-Wei (2013) and Liu-Wang-Wei (2017) in $n\ge8$. Wang-Wei (2019) supplied the multi-layer regularity theory (sheeting, $C^{2,\vartheta}$ and mean-curvature estimates from a Toda-type system) that the modern attack relies on, and the Wang-Wei/Chodosh-Mantoulidis reduction showed that the classification in dimension $n$ implies the Marques-Neves multiplicity-one and Morse index conjectures for Allen-Cahn approximations in that dimension, making $4\le n\le7$ a recognized bottleneck.

After the source paper: Florit-Simon's follow-up arXiv:2602.03136 (3 Feb 2026) proves that every finite-Morse-index solution with bounded energy density in $\mathbb{R}^4$ is one-dimensional and states the same for all $4\le n\le7$ 'conditionally on the classification of stable solutions' - direct evidence from the same author, five months later, that the stable classification in $n=5,6,7$ was still open as of February 2026.

Fernández-Real, Florit-Simon, and Serra's arXiv:2605.11110 (11 May 2026) develops a flexible improvement-of-flatness scheme in annuli and mentions variants employed in Allen-Cahn settings; it supplies methodological input toward the higher-dimensional program but does not claim or contain a resolution of the stable classification in $n=5,6,7$.

Ferla's arXiv:2603.29060 (2026) addresses a complementary regime: it proves one-dimensionality for solutions in $n\ge9$ possessing $n-7$ (or, without minimality, $n-2$) monotonicity directions; it does not touch the stable bounded-density classification in $5\le n\le7$, which needs no monotonicity assumption.

Section 3.4 identifies the quantitative obstruction in Eq. (41): rescaled mean curvature $O((\widetilde R_k\widetilde\varepsilon_k)^{-1})$ versus the flatness lower bound $\widetilde\varepsilon_k^2\ge c\widetilde R_k^{-(n-3)}$. The Chodosh–Mantoulidis work relevant here is 'Minimal surfaces and the Allen-Cahn equation on 3-manifolds: index, multiplicity, and curvature estimates', Ann. of Math. (2) 191 (2020), 213–328, DOI 10.4007/annals.2020.191.1.4. The $n=5,6,7$ cases remain unresolved in the literature considered at the August 2026 curation cutoff.

## Scientific Significance

Affected-field significance: `high`.

Direct impact on elliptic PDE and geometric analysis: a positive answer completes the stable De Giorgi classification of Allen-Cahn solutions with bounded energy density in the full sharp range $n\le7$ (false for $n\ge8$), closing a question open since 1998 beyond dimension three. Through the established Wang-Wei and Chodosh-Mantoulidis reductions it would simultaneously settle the Marques-Neves multiplicity-one and Morse index conjectures for Allen-Cahn approximations of minimal hypersurfaces in closed manifolds of dimensions $5$ to $7$ - currently bottlenecked exactly on this classification - and yield robust curvature estimates for stable phase transitions in those dimensions. A refutation would be equally significant, revealing a new stable multi-layer or cone-like phenomenon below the minimal-surface threshold $n=8$.
