# ORB-PHYS-79: Pair-correlation functions of the two-dimensional integrable N-state chiral Potts model

For the two-dimensional integrable N-state chiral Potts model - the archetypal Yang-Baxter-solvable lattice model beyond the free-fermion class, with Boltzmann weights parametrized on a higher-genus spectral curve - Perk and Au-Yang noted in 2006 that only the order parameter (one-point function) had been proved, while the pair-correlation (two-point) functions at finite spin separation remained unsolved. This audit verifies that statement against the primary source and finds the problem still open. Later work produced a Z-invariance strategy reducing general-model correlations to superintegrable-chain matrix elements (Au-Yang and Perk, 2010), exact finite-length data and a nearest-neighbour conjecture (Fabricius and McCoy, 2010), factorized matrix elements with only partially known normalizations (Iorgov et al., 2010), finite-lattice determinant form-factor representations (Grosjean, Maillet and Niccoli, 2015), and a proven finite-volume symmetry property (Zhu, 2026); none evaluates the two-point function at arbitrary separation in the thermodynamic limit. The problem asks for explicit, systematically evaluable two-point functions for arbitrary N, whose infinite-separation limit is the proved order parameter and whose N=2 specialization reproduces the known Ising correlations, thereby unlocking wavevector-dependent susceptibility computations for chiral Potts-type systems.

## Background

The two-dimensional Ising model set the standard for what 'exactly solved' means in equilibrium statistical mechanics: after Onsager's 1944 computation of the free energy, its spin-spin correlation functions were also obtained explicitly (Wu, McCoy, Tracy and Barouch, 1976), and these two-point functions are the input from which the wavevector-dependent susceptibility $\chi(\mathbf{q})$ - the Fourier transform of the pair correlation - can be computed to arbitrary precision. Extending such correlation-function results to integrable models that are not of free-fermion type (that is, whose correlations do not reduce to determinants or Pfaffians of Clifford-algebra operators) has been a recognized open frontier ever since. The integrable chiral Potts model (Au-Yang, McCoy, Perk, Tang and Yan, 1987; Baxter, Perk and Au-Yang, 1988) is the most prominent such model. Its spins take the $N$ values $\omega^\sigma$, $\sigma=0,\ldots,N-1$, $\omega=e^{2\pi i/N}$, on a square lattice, with interaction weights that break the spin-inversion symmetry of the ordinary Potts model (whence 'chiral'). The weights satisfy the star-triangle (Yang-Baxter) equation, the algebraic condition making the transfer matrices commute and the model solvable; it was the first solved model whose spectral parameters ('rapidities') live on an algebraic curve of genus larger than one. Because the weights are single-valued only on this higher-genus curve and do not depend on rapidity differences, the corner transfer matrix - Baxter's device that represents one quadrant of the lattice and yields one-point functions as its matrix elements - fails here, and the model became the recognized hard case of the discipline. The order parameter (the one-point function $\langle s^n\rangle$, nonzero only in the ordered phase, also called the spontaneous magnetization) was nonetheless conquered: Albertini, McCoy, Perk and Tang conjectured in 1989 that $M_r=(1-k^2)^{r(N-r)/(2N^2)}$ for $r=1,\ldots,N-1$, with $k$ the modulus of the rapidity curve, and Baxter proved this in 2005 by functional-relation methods; algebraic proofs via the Onsager algebra - the infinite-dimensional Lie algebra generated by the two parts of the superintegrable transfer matrix, first appearing in Onsager's 1944 paper - followed (Au-Yang and Perk; Iorgov et al.). Here 'superintegrable' denotes the special submanifold of the full integrable model (Baxter, 1988), equivalent to the $Z_N$-symmetric quantum chain of von Gehlen and Rittenberg with an Onsager-algebra spectrum, which reduces to the Ising model at $N=2$. Pair correlations are another matter. A two-point function $\langle s_A^{(n)} s_B^{(n)}\rangle$ measures the joint statistics of two spins at sites $A$ and $B$ separated by a finite distance; its large-distance limit factorizes into order parameters, so the solved one-point problem is only the asymptotic tip. By $Z$-invariance - the consequence of commuting transfer matrices that correlations are unchanged under deformations of the rapidity lattice - a bulk pair correlation of the general integrable model depends only on the $2m$ rapidity lines passing between the two spins, through universal functions $g_{2m}$; hence general-model correlations can be reconstructed from ground-state expectations $\langle Z_0^r Z_R^{\dagger r}\rangle$ of the Weyl ('clock') operators $Z$ at separation $R$ in the superintegrable chain. Perk and Au-Yang (2006) stated the resulting open problem: the pair-correlation functions of the two-dimensional integrable $N$-state chiral Potts model 'have not yet been solved', and detailed pair correlations for the eight-vertex model, the full (incommensurate-phase) chiral Potts model, and three-dimensional Yang-Baxter integrable models were likewise unavailable - the missing input, they noted, for extending their exact wavevector-dependent-susceptibility program for $Z$-invariant Ising models (including quasiperiodic pentagrid/Penrose-tiling systems) to these harder integrable models. Three partially successful routes to the chiral Potts correlations exist. The Onsager-algebra route constructs the eigenvectors of the superintegrable chain in 'Onsager sectors' and factorized matrix elements of the spin operators between them (matrix elements of local operators between eigenstates are called form factors). The functional-relation route proved the order parameter but does not directly give finite-separation behavior. The separation-of-variables (SOV) route of the quantum inverse scattering method reconstructs all local operators on finite lattices and expresses their form factors as finite sums of determinants. Each route stops short of the summed, thermodynamic-limit two-point function at finite separation. Two further terms from the source's context: the 'incommensurate' phases of the full chiral Potts model are phases in which the ordering wavevector varies continuously instead of being fixed by the lattice, and a 'Lifshitz point' would be a multicritical point where such a phase meets the commensurate ordered and disordered phases (Huse and Fisher, 1982); whether the classical two-dimensional model possesses one was numerically contested at the time of the source.

## Problem Statement

Obtain the pair-correlation (two-point) functions of the two-dimensional integrable $N$-state chiral Potts model at arbitrary finite separation between the two spins, in the thermodynamic limit, for arbitrary $N$ (the case $N=2$ reduces to the exactly solved Ising model and serves as the consistency limit). In the superintegrable formulation this is the ground-state two-point function $\langle Z_0^r Z_R^{\dagger r}\rangle$ of the superintegrable chiral Potts chain at arbitrary separation $R$ and $r=1,\ldots,N-1$; by $Z$-invariance it determines the universal functions $g_{2m}$ of the $2m$ rapidity lines passing between the two spins for the general integrable model, including the full chiral Potts model with its incommensurate phases. 'Obtain' is meant in the sense established for the Ising model: explicit closed-form, multiple-integral, or systematically convergent series representations from which numerical values at any prescribed separation and rapidity configuration can be computed and large-distance asymptotics extracted. An accepted answer must (i) reduce, at infinite separation in the ordered phase, to the proved order parameter $M_r=(1-k^2)^{r(N-r)/(2N^2)}$, the correlation factorizing into the product of the two one-point functions; (ii) reproduce the known Ising two-point functions at $N=2$; and (iii) agree with exact finite-size data, such as the published length-3, 4 and 5 chain results for $N=3$, whose conjectured nearest-neighbour correlation is a concrete first sub-target. The resulting two-point functions are precisely the input required for the wavevector-dependent susceptibility computations in chiral Potts-type systems that the source paper envisaged.

The verification contract below evaluates answers to this statement. It does not narrow or redefine the research question.

Known solving difficulties:

- The model lacks the rapidity-difference property: its Boltzmann weights are single-valued only on a higher-genus algebraic curve, which defeats the corner-transfer-matrix and simple inversion-relation techniques that produced correlation functions for Ising-like and six-vertex-like models.
- For $N\geq3$ there is no free-fermion structure, so no Pfaffian or determinantal reduction of correlation functions exists; every route passes through genuinely interacting form-factor sums over excited states.
- The spectral data are only partially explicit: Onsager-sector eigenvectors and factorized spin-operator matrix elements are known, but their normalizing scalar factors are known only in ground-state sectors, while the two-point function needs the contribution of all eigenvectors not orthogonal to the maximum eigenvectors.
- The thermodynamic and homogeneous limits of the finite-lattice determinant form factors are themselves unsolved - even rederiving the order parameter this way for general homogeneous representations is an announced but unpublished task.
- The general integrable model is reachable only through $Z$-invariance bookkeeping over the $2m$ rapidity lines passing between the two spins, adding a further analytic layer on top of the superintegrable computation.
- Candidate answers face tight simultaneous consistency requirements - the order-parameter limit at infinite separation, the Ising limit at $N=2$, and finite-size data - which restrict the admissible analytic forms and demand uniform control of limiting regimes.

## Current Progress

- Status: `ready`

Source fidelity: the primary source was inspected in full (arXiv:math-ph/0606046v1, Section 2.6). Perk and Au-Yang state verbatim that for the two-dimensional integrable N-state chiral Potts model 'the pair-correlation functions have not yet been solved for this model; only the conjecture for its order parameters (one-point functions) has been proved recently by Baxter', that the superintegrable model lies on a submanifold of the commensurate phase of the more general chiral Potts model, and that Jin's numerical results did not seem to support a Lifshitz point in the classical two-dimensional model. The LKM paraphrase (source record gcn_7b01dc028f694434) is faithful; the bundled items about the eight-vertex model, three-dimensional models, deferred publication details, and Lifshitz points expand the source's closing remarks.

One bundled item was resolved shortly after the source appeared: the deferred computational and combinatorial details of the pentagrid Ising $\chi(\mathbf{q})$ calculation were published as Au-Yang and Perk, J. Stat. Phys. 127 (2007) 221-264, and the three-dimensional overlapping-unit-cell construction as J. Phys. A 39 (2006) 9035-9044; the source's publication-status concern is therefore closed, while the scientific core (chiral Potts pair correlations) is not.

Au-Yang and Perk (2010, published 2011) made the main strategic advance: they showed that $Z$-invariance reduces the pair correlation in the general integrable chiral Potts model to matrix elements computable from superintegrable eigenvectors, and rederived the order parameter as the infinite-distance limit of that pair correlation. They explicitly left the separation dependence open: it would require inner products of all eigenvectors not orthogonal to the maximum eigenvectors, and they wrote that 'it will be very hard to work out all necessary details this way', calling instead for a more combinatorial route.

Fabricius and McCoy (2010) computed exact ground-state two-point functions of the three-state superintegrable chiral Potts chain for lengths 3, 4 and 5 only, and from these data conjectured the form of the nearest-neighbour correlation; they also observed that half-chain correlations are real for even length. The nearest-neighbour conjecture remains unproven; the reality observation was proven by Zhu (2026).

Iorgov, Pakuliak, Shadura, Tykhyy and von Gehlen (2010) proved Baxter's conjectured factorized form of spin-operator matrix elements between arbitrary Onsager sectors of the superintegrable chain, but the overall scalar factors are known only for ground-state sectors; taking the thermodynamic limit of the ground-state matrix elements yields the order parameter only, not finite-separation correlations.

Grosjean, Maillet and Niccoli (2015) solved the quantum inverse problem for general cyclic representations of the six-vertex Yang-Baxter algebra, covering the Bazhanov-Stroganov model and the chiral Potts model: all local operators are reconstructed in Sklyanin's separation-of-variables framework, and the form factors of any local operator are expressed as finite sums of determinants on finite lattices. The homogeneous and thermodynamic limits - needed even to rederive the order parameter for general representations, let alone to sum the form-factor series into two-point functions - were announced for a future paper that has not appeared in the literature located by this audit.

Zhu (2026, arXiv:2603.27930) proved the exact finite-volume symmetry formula $\langle Z_0^r Z_R^{\dagger r}\rangle^{*}=\langle Z_0^r Z_{L-R}^{\dagger r}\rangle$ for every translation eigensector of the periodic superintegrable chain, resolving the Fabricius-McCoy reality conjecture. Its introduction states that 'finite-distance correlation functions in the chiral Potts setting remain far less explicit' than in the Ising case - the most recent direct statement of openness located.

Baxter's 2010 review of exact solutions since 1944 confirms the picture from the solver's side: the superintegrable magnetization is expressed as a Cauchy-like determinant and evaluated in the thermodynamic limit, the chiral Potts model is flagged as the most challenging case because it lacks the rapidity-difference property, and no pair-correlation results are reported.

Adjacent strands of the source bundle, checked for completeness. Eight-vertex model: the nearest-neighbour two-point function of the $Z$-invariant eight-vertex model was computed via free-field representations (Lashkevich and Pugai, 1998), qKZ/free-field representations of correlation functions exist from the 1990s, and determinant form-factor representations with a multiple-integral generating function for a two-point function were obtained for the cyclic eight-vertex solid-on-solid model (Levy-Bencheton and Terras, 2013); explicit finite-distance two-point functions of the general eight-vertex model are nevertheless still unavailable. Three-dimensional Yang-Baxter (tetrahedron-equation) models: only combinatorial special cases with equal-weight configurations have nontrivial spin correlations computed (Korepanov, 2025). Lifshitz points: Nyckees, Colbois and Mila (2021), using corner-transfer-matrix renormalization-group numerics on the classical two-dimensional three-state chiral Potts model, found strong evidence that a Lifshitz point does exist, separating a Pokrovsky-Talapov transition line from a line of direct continuous order-disorder transitions - contrary to the earlier numerical evidence reported in the source - but this remains numerical, not exact, and no classification for the broader family exists.

## Scientific Significance

Affected-field significance: `high`.

Solving this problem would directly change the core capabilities of exactly-solved-model theory: it would deliver the first exact finite-distance correlation functions for a Yang-Baxter-integrable two-dimensional model outside the free-fermion class, on a higher-genus spectral curve, completing for the chiral Potts model what the Wu-McCoy-Tracy-Barouch results did for Ising. The impact is direct in three specific ways: (i) exact two-point functions and their asymptotics would fix correlation lengths and provide exact benchmarks for the $Z_N$-parafermionic critical behavior of the chiral Potts universality class, currently accessible only numerically or through conformal-field-theory arguments; (ii) they would supply the missing input for computing wavevector-dependent susceptibilities of (quasiperiodic) chiral Potts-type systems, the program the source paper was built around; (iii) the techniques required to sum finite-lattice form factors into thermodynamic-limit correlations would extend the separation-of-variables and Onsager-algebra form-factor machinery itself to the model where it is currently blocked. Indirectly, conformal-field-theory and numerical studies that use the chiral Potts model as a reference point would gain exact benchmarks.
