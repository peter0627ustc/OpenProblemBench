# ORB-PHYS-62: Universal validity of Pauling's residual entropy estimate for regular graphs with growing degree

An Eulerian orientation of a graph $G$ directs every edge so that each vertex has equal in-degree and out-degree; the number $\mathrm{EO}(G)$ of such orientations is the partition function of an ice-type (six-vertex) model, and the per-vertex logarithm $\rho(G)$ is the residual entropy. Pauling's 1935 heuristic predicts $\rho(G)\approx\widehat{\rho}(G)=\log\binom{d}{d/2}-\frac{d}{2}\log 2$ for even degree $d$. The open question, stated as Conjecture 1.1 of arXiv:2509.20671 (the regular special case of Conjecture 2.2 of Isaev-McKay-Zhang): is $\rho(G)=\widehat{\rho}(G)+o(1)$ for every sequence of simple $d$-regular graphs with even $d\to\infty$? This is known only under auxiliary hypotheses — bounds on short closed trails, eigenvalue concentration, growing girth, and Cartesian-product structure — and the boundary between graph families on which Pauling's estimate is and is not asymptotically valid is undetermined. This record reconstructs the verified literature status of the conjecture and its partial results.

## Background

In 1935, Pauling estimated the residual entropy of water ice — the entropy that remains at zero temperature because the hydrogen bonds admit many arrangements consistent with the local ice rule. On a graph, the same question is purely combinatorial. An orientation of an undirected graph $G=(V,E)$ assigns a direction to each edge; the orientation is Eulerian if every vertex has in-degree equal to out-degree, which is possible only when all vertex degrees are even. The number $\mathrm{EO}(G)$ of Eulerian orientations is the partition function of the ice-type (six-vertex) model of statistical mechanics placed on $G$, and the normalized logarithm $\rho(G)=\frac{1}{|V|}\log\mathrm{EO}(G)$ (natural logarithm) is called the residual entropy. For a $d$-regular graph with even $d$, Pauling's independence heuristic — each vertex chooses which $d/2$ of its $d$ incident edges point outward, with a global correction factor $2^{-|E|}$ for double counting — yields the estimate $\widehat{\rho}=\log\binom{d}{d/2}-\frac{d}{2}\log 2$ per vertex, which at $d=4$ equals $\log(3/2)$, Pauling's classical value for ice. Lieb's exact solution of square ice on the two-dimensional square lattice gives per-vertex entropy $\frac{3}{2}\log\frac{4}{3}$, so at fixed degree the estimate is close but not exact; the open question is whether it becomes exact as the degree grows.

Three regimes are known for general graphs. First, $\rho(G)\ge\widehat{\rho}(G)$ for every graph of even degree (Lieb-Wu; Schrijver). Second, upper bounds are only partial: for connected $d$-regular multigraphs of girth $g$ (the length of a shortest cycle), Las Vergnas proved $\rho(G)\le\widehat{\rho}(G)+\frac{\log d}{2g}+O(1/n)$, and Isaev-McKay-Zhang proved that simple graphs (no loops or parallel edges) of minimum degree $d_{\min}\to\infty$ satisfy $\rho(G)\le\widehat{\rho}(G)+\log 2+O(\log^{2}d_{\min}/d_{\min})$, with sharper constants for regular graphs. Third, Isaev-McKay-Zhang conjectured (their Conjecture 2.2) that $\rho(G)=\widehat{\rho}(G)+o(1)$ for every sequence of simple graphs of even degrees whose harmonic mean degree tends to infinity, and verified it for sufficiently dense expanding graphs, for random graphs with given degrees in two broad ranges, and for cycles of cliques; they also recorded that simplicity is essential — for multigraphs the gap $\rho-\widehat{\rho}$ is not uniformly bounded — and that regularity cannot be dropped (a disjoint union of a large clique and triangles has gap tending to $\frac{1}{6}\log 2$). A stronger conjectured upper bound of Schrijver from 1983, $\mathrm{EO}(G)\le\prod_{v}\mathrm{RT}(d_{v}+1)^{1/(d_{v}+1)}$ where $\mathrm{RT}(m)$ is the number of regular tournaments (Eulerian orientations of the complete graph on $m$ vertices), would imply the regular case with gap $O(\log d/d)$ but has remained open for over forty years.

The source paper (Hasheminezhad-Isaev-McKay-Zhang) proves the regular case — their Conjecture 1.1 — under four sufficient hypotheses: (i) a bound $c_{\ell}(G)\le C e^{-(\ell+1)}d^{\ell-1}n$ on the number of closed trails of length $\ell$ (a closed trail is a closed walk that uses no edge more than once; trails differing only in starting point or direction of traversal are identified), for all $3\le\ell\le\ell_{\max}$ with $\ell_{\max}=\omega(\log d)$; (ii) a spectral condition, namely that at most $n\,d^{-\omega(1)}$ adjacency eigenvalues of $G$ lie outside $[-d^{1-\delta},d^{1-\delta}]$ for some constant $\delta>0$; (iii) girth tending to infinity; and (iv) iterated Cartesian products of regular graphs, including the hypercubes (the $1$-skeletons of $d$-dimensional cubes). Whether the universal statement holds without such hypotheses, and where the boundary between valid and invalid graph families lies, is left open.

## Problem Statement

Let $G$ be a finite simple graph (no loops, no parallel edges) with all vertex degrees even. An orientation of $G$ is Eulerian if every vertex has in-degree equal to out-degree; write $\mathrm{EO}(G)$ for the number of Eulerian orientations and $\rho(G)=\frac{1}{|V(G)|}\log\mathrm{EO}(G)$ (natural logarithm) for the residual entropy. For a $d$-regular graph with even $d$, Pauling's estimate is $\widehat{\rho}(G)=\log\binom{d}{d/2}-\frac{d}{2}\log 2$ per vertex.

Primary question. For every sequence $G=G(n)$ of simple $d(n)$-regular graphs on $n$ vertices such that $d(n)$ is even and $d(n)\to\infty$, does $\rho(G)=\widehat{\rho}(G)+o(1)$ hold as $n\to\infty$? (This is Conjecture 1.1 of Hasheminezhad-Isaev-McKay-Zhang, arXiv:2509.20671, the regular special case of Conjecture 2.2 of Isaev-McKay-Zhang, doi:10.4171/aihpd/207.) By the known universal bounds, the gap satisfies $0\le\rho(G)-\widehat{\rho}(G)\le\log 2+o(1)$ along every such sequence, so the question is whether the gap must tend to zero. A resolution is either a proof of the universal statement, or a counterexample: an explicit sequence of simple $d(n)$-regular graphs with even $d(n)\to\infty$ and a constant $\varepsilon>0$ such that $\rho(G)-\widehat{\rho}(G)\ge\varepsilon$ for all large $n$.

Secondary question (the boundary, as posed in the source). The source paper derives the conclusion of the primary question only under auxiliary hypotheses: the short-closed-trail bound $c_{\ell}(G)\le C e^{-(\ell+1)}d^{\ell-1}n$ for all $3\le\ell\le\ell_{\max}$ with $\ell_{\max}=\omega(\log d)$; the eigenvalue condition that at most $n\,d^{-\omega(1)}$ adjacency eigenvalues of $G$ lie outside $[-d^{1-\delta},d^{1-\delta}]$ for some constant $\delta>0$; girth tending to infinity; and iterated Cartesian products of regular graphs. Determine which of these hypotheses are necessary and which can be weakened or removed: for instance, exhibit simple even-$d$-regular sequences with $d\to\infty$ that violate a hypothesis yet still satisfy $\rho=\widehat{\rho}+o(1)$, or prove that removing some hypothesis admits sequences with a positive limiting gap. More generally, characterize the boundary between the families of simple even-$d$-regular graphs with $d\to\infty$ on which Pauling's estimate is asymptotically valid and those on which it fails.

The verification contract below evaluates answers to this statement. It does not narrow or redefine the research question.

Known solving difficulties:

- The upper-bound side is the obstruction: the known uniform bound leaves a $\log 2$ gap, and every technique that reaches $o(1)$ (switching arguments, cumulant expansions, spectral methods) so far requires structural hypotheses such as short-trail control or eigenvalue concentration.
- The natural vehicle — Schrijver's 1983 conjectured upper bound via regular-tournament counts — has been open for over forty years; the source paper's switching argument reaches it only under the short-trail hypothesis.
- A counterexample is not obviously easier: all examined families (expanders, random regular graphs, cliques and disjoint unions of cliques, Cartesian products and hypercubes, high-girth sequences) satisfy the conjecture, and the gap is pinched in $[0,\log 2+o(1)]$, so a violating family must be exotic.
- Fixed-degree techniques (Benjamini-Schramm convergence, exact lattice solutions) do not transfer: at fixed even $d$ the estimate already fails (the square lattice), so any proof must exploit degree growth $d\to\infty$.

## Current Progress

Pauling (J. Am. Chem. Soc. 57 (1935) 2680-2684, doi:10.1021/ja01315a102) proposed the estimate; Lieb (Phys. Rev. 162 (1967) 162-172, doi:10.1103/PhysRev.162.162) computed the exact square-lattice value $\frac{3}{2}\log\frac{4}{3}$ per vertex, proving the estimate inexact at fixed degree $d=4$ (gap approximately $0.026$).

For every graph of even degree, $\rho\ge\widehat{\rho}$ (Lieb-Wu, as recorded in arXiv:2509.20671 and arXiv:2409.04989; proved combinatorially by Schrijver, Combinatorica 3 (1983) 375-380, doi:10.1007/BF02579193). Las Vergnas (Combinatorica 10 (1990) 61-65, doi:10.1007/BF02122696) proved $\rho\le\widehat{\rho}+\frac{\log d}{2g}+O(1/n)$ for connected regular multigraphs of girth $g$, so girth growing faster than $\log d$ implies the conjecture. Schrijver's 1983 conjectured upper bound $\mathrm{EO}\le\prod_{v}\mathrm{RT}(d_{v}+1)^{1/(d_{v}+1)}$ in terms of regular-tournament counts remains open and would imply the regular conjecture with gap $O(\log d/d)$.

Isaev-McKay-Zhang (Ann. Inst. Henri Poincare D (2025), doi:10.4171/aihpd/207; arXiv:2409.04989) stated Conjecture 2.2 for simple graphs with harmonic mean degree tending to infinity; proved $\rho\le\widehat{\rho}+\log 2+O(\log^{2}d_{\min}/d_{\min})$ when $d_{\min}\to\infty$ (with constants $21/22$ for regular and $5/6$ for regular triangle-free simple graphs); verified the conjecture for sufficiently dense expanding graphs ($d\gg\log^{8}n$), for random graphs with given degrees in two ranges, and for cycles of cliques; and recorded both the unbounded multigraph gap and a non-regular counterexample family (a clique plus triangles, gap tending to $\frac{1}{6}\log 2$ at harmonic mean degree $4+o(1)$).

Hasheminezhad-Isaev-McKay-Zhang (arXiv:2509.20671, v2 November 2025) prove Conjecture 1.1 under four sufficient hypotheses — the short-closed-trail bound, the eigenvalue-concentration condition, growing girth, and iterated Cartesian products including hypercubes — establishing the regular case for the first time outside the expansion and random-graph regimes.

Isaev-McKay-Zhang (J. Combin. Theory Ser. B 172 (2025) 263-314, doi:10.1016/j.jctb.2025.01.002) developed the cumulant-expansion tail bound for counting Eulerian orientations that underlies the source paper's analysis of long closed trails.

Bencs-Borbenyi-Csikvari (arXiv:2409.18012) proved convergence of $\frac{1}{|V|}\ln\varepsilon(G)$ along Benjamini-Schramm convergent bounded-degree Eulerian sequences (local weak convergence of rooted neighborhoods) — the fixed-degree regime, which does not address $d\to\infty$.

No resolution of the conjecture was identified at the August 2026 curation cutoff. Recent work on arborescences, cumulant expansions, graph limits, degree-sequence enumeration, counting complexity, and planar maps addresses related questions.

## Scientific Significance

Affected-field significance: `medium`.

Resolving the conjecture would settle when Pauling's ninety-year-old estimate for ice-type residual entropy becomes asymptotically exact at the level of generality of all simple regular graphs: it would close the $\log 2$ gap left by the known uniform bounds, and on the upper-bound side it would necessarily resolve or bypass Schrijver's 1983 conjectured bound on Eulerian orientations, a recognized open problem in extremal combinatorics. The boundary strand maps which graph families' ice-type partition functions are predicted by local entropy estimates, connecting extremal graph theory (Eulerian orientations, regular tournaments, graph spectra, subgraph counts) with the statistical mechanics of exactly solved ice-type models.
