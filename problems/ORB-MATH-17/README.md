# ORB-MATH-17: The optimal universal constant in linear lower bounds for the crossing number of composite knots

For oriented knots $K_1,\ldots,K_n$ with crossing numbers $c(K_i)$, Qiu and Wang proved that every nontrivial connected sum satisfies $c(K_1\#\cdots\#K_n)>\frac{1}{16}\sum_i c(K_i)$, improving Lackenby's 2009 bound with constant $\frac{1}{152}$. The paper leaves open the quantitative sharpness of this bound: what is the optimal universal constant $\alpha^{*}=\inf\, c(K_1\#\cdots\#K_n)/\sum_i c(K_i)$? Currently $\frac{1}{16}\le\alpha^{*}\le 1$, the trivial upper bound coming from $c(K_1\#K_2)\le c(K_1)+c(K_2)$. The conjectured value $\alpha^{*}=1$ is the decades-old crossing-number additivity conjecture, one of the most famous open problems in knot theory; even the weaker inequality $c(K_1\#K_2)\ge c(K_1)$ remains unproved. The problem is to push the universal constant above $\frac{1}{16}$ — ultimately to $1$ — or to show by explicit knot families that it cannot be pushed that far.

## Background

A knot is a closed embedded circle in the 3-sphere $S^3$, considered up to continuous deformation, and its crossing number $c(K)$ is the minimum number of double points in any planar projection (diagram) of it. The connected sum $K_1\#K_2$ is formed by cutting each knot open and splicing the ends together, the knot-theoretic analogue of multiplying integers; every knot factors uniquely into prime knots under this operation (Schubert's prime decomposition, 1949). Since placing two diagrams side by side and fusing gives $c(K_1\#K_2)\le c(K_1)+c(K_2)$, the hard direction is the lower bound: it is a very old conjecture that the crossing number is additive, $c(K_1\#K_2)=c(K_1)+c(K_2)$. Additivity is known for alternating knots (Murasugi, Kauffman, Thistlethwaite, 1987), adequate knots (a wider class of diagrams, including alternating ones), and Diao's zero-deficiency knots, which include torus knots (knots lying on the surface of a standardly embedded torus in $S^3$). These class-specific additivity results do not provide a general crossing-number certificate from the Jones polynomial. For arbitrary knots no invariant is known to certify the crossing number, and the first general result was Lackenby's 2009 theorem, proved with normal surface theory (a technique that studies how essential 2-spheres intersect a handle decomposition of the knot complement in controlled 'normal' pieces), giving $c(K_1\#\cdots\#K_n)\ge\frac{1}{152}\sum_i c(K_i)$. Ito (2022) gave a complementary bound depending on the braid indices of the summands (the braid index of a knot is the minimum number of strands needed to present it as a closed braid). Qiu and Wang (arXiv:2407.10467, v2 2026) adapted the normal-surface strategy to a handle structure built directly from a minimal diagram $D$ of $K=K_1\#\cdots\#K_n$: they put the decomposition spheres into a normal form and count crossings of induced diagrams $D_i$ of the summands region by region, proving $c(D_1)+\cdots+c(D_n)<16\,c(D)$ and hence their $\frac{1}{16}$ bound. The constant $16$ arises from worst-case configurations of normal disk types per region, and the paper does not claim any optimality for it; it also notes that even whether $c(K_1\#K_2)\ge c(K_1)$ always holds is unknown, and that the same circle of ideas applies to Lackenby's satellite-knot bound $c(K)\ge c(L)/10^{13}$ (2014). One recent preprint bears directly on the endpoint: Kawauchi (reference 7) claims the full additivity identity $c(K_1\#K_2)=c(K_1)+c(K_2)$ for knots and links via 4D topology, but the claim is unrefereed, uncited, and unverified; it does not constitute an established resolution, and a citation to it is not an independent proof, so the additivity question — and with it the value of $\alpha^{*}$ — remains open as stated.

## Problem Statement

Let $c(K)$ denote the crossing number of a knot $K$, and let $K_1\#\cdots\#K_n$ denote the connected sum of nontrivial oriented knots. Define the optimal universal constant
$$
\alpha^{*}\;=\;\inf_{n\ge 2}\;\inf_{K_1,\ldots,K_n}\;\frac{c(K_1\#\cdots\#K_n)}{c(K_1)+\cdots+c(K_n)}.
$$
The theorem of Qiu and Wang gives $\alpha^{*}\ge\frac{1}{16}$ (indeed $c(K_1\#\cdots\#K_n)>\frac{1}{16}\sum_i c(K_i)$ always), while the trivial diagram construction gives $\alpha^{*}\le 1$. Determine $\alpha^{*}$: the task is resolved exactly when $\alpha^{*}$ is pinned to a single value, that is, when a lower bound $\alpha^{*}\ge\alpha$ and a matching upper bound $\alpha^{*}\le\alpha$ are proved for one and the same explicit $\alpha$. The lower-endpoint possibility $\alpha^{*}=\frac{1}{16}$ would be established by explicit knot families whose ratio $c(K_1\#\cdots\#K_n)/\sum_i c(K_i)$ approaches $\frac{1}{16}$, with the Qiu--Wang bound as the matching lower bound; the upper endpoint $\alpha^{*}=1$ is the additivity conjecture $c(K_1\#K_2)=c(K_1)+c(K_2)$ for all knots. Additionally open are whether a lower bound $c(K_1\#\cdots\#K_n)\ge\alpha\sum_i c(K_i)$ holds for some explicit $\alpha>\frac{1}{16}$, what a refutation of the additivity conjecture would give (by itself only $\alpha^{*}<1$), and the weaker open sub-question, which the source paper explicitly singles out, of whether even $c(K_1\#K_2)\ge c(K_1)$ always holds.
