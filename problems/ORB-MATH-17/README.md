# ORB-MATH-17: The optimal universal constant in linear lower bounds for the crossing number of composite knots

For oriented knots $K_1,\ldots,K_n$ with crossing numbers $c(K_i)$, Qiu and Wang proved that every nontrivial connected sum satisfies $c(K_1\#\cdots\#K_n)>\frac{1}{16}\sum_i c(K_i)$, improving Lackenby's 2009 bound with constant $\frac{1}{152}$. The paper leaves open the quantitative sharpness of this bound: what is the optimal universal constant $\alpha^{*}=\inf\, c(K_1\#\cdots\#K_n)/\sum_i c(K_i)$? Currently $\frac{1}{16}\le\alpha^{*}\le 1$, the trivial upper bound coming from $c(K_1\#K_2)\le c(K_1)+c(K_2)$. The conjectured value $\alpha^{*}=1$ is the decades-old crossing-number additivity conjecture, one of the most famous open problems in knot theory; even the weaker inequality $c(K_1\#K_2)\ge c(K_1)$ remains unproved. The problem is to push the universal constant above $\frac{1}{16}$ — ultimately to $1$ — or to show by explicit knot families that it cannot be pushed that far.

## Background

A knot is a closed embedded circle in the 3-sphere $S^3$, considered up to continuous deformation, and its crossing number $c(K)$ is the minimum number of double points in any planar projection (diagram) of it. The connected sum $K_1\#K_2$ is formed by cutting each knot open and splicing the ends together, the knot-theoretic analogue of multiplying integers; every knot factors uniquely into prime knots under this operation (Schubert's prime decomposition, 1949). Since placing two diagrams side by side and fusing gives $c(K_1\#K_2)\le c(K_1)+c(K_2)$, the hard direction is the lower bound: it is a very old conjecture that the crossing number is additive, $c(K_1\#K_2)=c(K_1)+c(K_2)$. Additivity holds for the classes where the span of the Jones polynomial (a quantum invariant introduced in 1985, whose span equals $2c(K)$ for these classes) certifies minimality: alternating knots (Murasugi, Kauffman, Thistlethwaite, 1987), adequate knots (a wider class of diagrams, including alternating ones, for which the same span certificate still applies), torus knots (knots lying on the surface of a standardly embedded torus in $S^3$), and Diao's zero-deficiency knots (a combinatorial diagram class in which the span is again forced to equal the crossing number). For arbitrary knots no invariant is known to certify the crossing number, and the first general result was Lackenby's 2009 theorem, proved with normal surface theory (a technique that studies how essential 2-spheres intersect a handle decomposition of the knot complement in controlled 'normal' pieces), giving $c(K_1\#\cdots\#K_n)\ge\frac{1}{152}\sum_i c(K_i)$. Ito (2022) gave a complementary bound depending on the braid indices of the summands (the braid index of a knot is the minimum number of strands needed to present it as a closed braid). Qiu and Wang (arXiv:2407.10467, v2 2026) adapted the normal-surface strategy to a handle structure built directly from a minimal diagram $D$ of $K=K_1\#\cdots\#K_n$: they put the decomposition spheres into a normal form and count crossings of induced diagrams $D_i$ of the summands region by region, proving $c(D_1)+\cdots+c(D_n)<16\,c(D)$ and hence their $\frac{1}{16}$ bound. The constant $16$ arises from worst-case configurations of normal disk types per region, and the paper does not claim any optimality for it; it also notes that even whether $c(K_1\#K_2)\ge c(K_1)$ always holds is unknown, and that the same circle of ideas applies to Lackenby's satellite-knot bound $c(K)\ge c(L)/10^{13}$ (2014).

## Problem Statement

Let $c(K)$ denote the crossing number of a knot $K$, and let $K_1\#\cdots\#K_n$ denote the connected sum of nontrivial oriented knots. Define the optimal universal constant
$$
\alpha^{*}\;=\;\inf_{n\ge 2}\;\inf_{K_1,\ldots,K_n}\;\frac{c(K_1\#\cdots\#K_n)}{c(K_1)+\cdots+c(K_n)}.
$$
The theorem of Qiu and Wang gives $\alpha^{*}\ge\frac{1}{16}$ (indeed $c(K_1\#\cdots\#K_n)>\frac{1}{16}\sum_i c(K_i)$ always), while the trivial diagram construction gives $\alpha^{*}\le 1$. Determine $\alpha^{*}$. In particular: (1) prove a bound $c(K_1\#\cdots\#K_n)\ge\alpha\sum_i c(K_i)$ for some explicit $\alpha>\frac{1}{16}$ — for instance by sharpening the per-region normal-disk counting through which the factor $16$ currently arises — or show that no constant larger than $\frac{1}{16}$ works by exhibiting explicit knot families whose ratio approaches $\frac{1}{16}$; and (2) settle the conjectural endpoint $\alpha^{*}=1$, i.e. prove or refute the additivity conjecture $c(K_1\#K_2)=c(K_1)+c(K_2)$ for all knots; a refutation should exhibit explicit knots with certified minimal crossing numbers. A weaker open sub-question, which the source paper explicitly singles out, is whether even $c(K_1\#K_2)\ge c(K_1)$ always holds.

The verification contract below evaluates answers to this statement. It does not narrow or redefine the research question.

Known solving difficulties:

- The additivity conjecture has resisted all techniques since the 1980s; every known method loses large constant factors, and even the much weaker monotonicity statement $c(K_1\#K_2)\ge c(K_1)$ is unproved.
- The current bound's constant 16 emerges from worst-case per-region configurations of normal disks in a handle structure adapted to a minimal diagram; pushing it below 16 requires finer control of which normal-disk types can appear simultaneously, a delicate combinatorial-geometric refinement of the Qiu–Wang case analysis.
- No known knot invariant certifies the crossing number of an arbitrary knot, so an invariant-based proof of additivity (in the spirit of the Jones-polynomial span proof for alternating knots) is not available outside special classes such as alternating, adequate, and torus knots.
- A counterexample strategy would require certified non-minimal diagrams or certified crossing-number computations for explicit composites; additivity is consistent with all tabulated knots, making this direction appear implausible and unsupported by any current heuristic.
- The problem sits inside a family of related open quantitative questions (the satellite bound $c(K)\ge c(L)/10^{13}$, the conjecture $c(K)\ge c(L)$), suggesting that genuinely new ideas about knot diagrams, rather than incremental counting improvements, may be needed to reach the optimal constant.

## Current Progress

- Status: `ready`

Source fidelity was verified against the arXiv abstract page and the full text of v2 of Qiu–Wang (arXiv:2407.10467): Theorem 1.1 states exactly $c(K)>\frac{1}{16}(c(K_1)+\cdots+c(K_n))$ for a nontrivial knot $K=K_1\#\cdots\#K_n$ with oriented summands, improving Lackenby's $\frac{1}{152}$. The candidate's paraphrase is faithful; the only corrections are minor: the theorem is stated for a nontrivial connected sum of oriented knots (trivial summands contribute zero crossings) and the inequality is strict. The paper poses no numbered open problem, but its introduction explicitly frames full additivity as unsolved, notes that even $c(K_1\#K_2)\ge c(K_1)$ is unknown, and reduces the bound to the per-region counting estimate $c(D_1)+\cdots+c(D_n)<16\,c(D)$, so the sharpness of the constant is the natural quantitative frontier the paper leaves open.

Lackenby (Journal of Topology 2, 2009, DOI 10.1112/jtopol/jtp028, arXiv:0805.4706) established the first general linear lower bound, $c(K_1\#\cdots\#K_n)\ge\frac{1}{152}\sum_i c(K_i)$, via normal surfaces, and his abstract states the additivity equality as a very old conjecture; this remained the best universal constant for fifteen years. His 2014 satellite-knot result $c(K)\ge c(L)/10^{13}$ (Algebraic & Geometric Topology 14, DOI 10.2140/agt.2014.14.2379) is the companion statement for satellite knots (where the conjectured optimal bound is $c(K)\ge c(L)$, Kirby's problem list 1.67), and Qiu–Wang state that their methods improve that constant too, though it would remain very large.

Ito (Journal of Topology 15, 2022, DOI 10.1112/topo.12259) proved a different lower bound for $c(K_1\#K_2)$ depending on the braid indices of $K_1$ and $K_2$, stronger than the linear bound when the braid indices are small; it leaves the universal-constant question untouched. Additivity itself was previously settled only for special classes: alternating knots (Murasugi 1987, Topology 26, DOI 10.1016/0040-9383(87)90058-9, via the Jones polynomial span, together with the parallel work of Kauffman and Thistlethwaite), adequate and torus knots, and Diao's zero-deficiency knots (Journal of Knot Theory and Its Ramifications 13, 2004, DOI 10.1142/s0218216504003524).

Retrieval for later work (audit date 2026-08-26): arXiv full-text and title searches ("composite knots crossing number", "crossing number connected sum", author searches), Semantic Scholar's citation index (zero citations of arXiv:2407.10467 recorded), and Crossref (no published journal version of Qiu–Wang found) surfaced no refereed or preprint work improving the constant $\frac{1}{16}$, determining the optimal constant, or proving additivity. Qiu–Wang v2, posted 2026-07-31, remains the state of the art, and the coverage is as complete as these routes allow (web search engines returned no usable results this session; Google Scholar was unavailable).

One unverified recent claim must be flagged: Akio Kawauchi's preprint arXiv:2608.13789 (posted August 13, 2026, two weeks before this audit), "The crossing numbers of knots and links via 4D topology", claims as Theorem 1.4 the full additivity $c(k_1\#k_2)=c(k_1)+c(k_2)$ for any knots or links, via circular rigid disk-chord diagrams of spun ribbon torus-links in the 4-sphere. The preprint is unrefereed, uncited, and undiscussed; the same reference list cites the author's companion claim of the classical Poincaré conjecture "via 4D topology" in an unrefereed obscure journal, which the mathematical community has not accepted. This claim therefore does not constitute an established resolution, and the literature status of the problem remains open; if the claim were to survive expert scrutiny it would give $\alpha^{*}=1$ and subsume the constant question entirely.

Coverage and certainty: the open core — determining the optimal universal constant, in particular any improvement of the Qiu–Wang constant $\frac{1}{16}$ (arXiv:2407.10467) toward $1$, with $\alpha^{*}=1$ (additivity) as the conjectural endpoint and the weaker $c(K_1\#K_2)\ge c(K_1)$ as an open sub-question — is precise and, on the evidence above, nonempty. Uncertainty is limited to the possibility of very recent uncited preprints not indexed by the available routes, and the unresolved status of the Kawauchi claim.

## Scientific Significance

Affected-field significance: `high`.

Directly changes core knowledge in low-dimensional topology and knot theory. Determining $\alpha^{*}$ settles exactly how the most classical knot invariant — the crossing number — behaves under the most basic operation on knots, the connected sum; the endpoint $\alpha^{*}=1$ is the decades-old additivity conjecture, and a value $\alpha^{*}<1$ (via certified examples) would be equally foundational. The answer directly affects knot tabulation and the certification of minimal diagrams, bears on the satellite-knot conjecture $c(K)\ge c(L)$ (Kirby's problem list 1.67), to which the same methods apply, and would calibrate the strength of the normal-surface and diagrammatic techniques that currently produce all known bounds.
