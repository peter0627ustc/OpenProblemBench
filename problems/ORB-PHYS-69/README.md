# ORB-PHYS-69: Isomorphism classification of three-generator Sklyanin algebras over fields of characteristic 3 and over fields without a nontrivial cubic root of unity

The Sklyanin algebra $Q_{p,q,r}$ over a field $\mathbb{K}$ is the quadratic algebra on three generators $x,y,z$ with the relations $pyz+qzy+rxx=0$, $pzx+qxz+ryy=0$, $pxy+qyx+rzz=0$. Iyudu and Shkarin (arXiv:2108.06290) determined exactly which of these algebras are isomorphic, provided the ground field has characteristic different from 3 and contains a nontrivial cubic root of unity $\theta$: the answer is given by the orbits of a 24-element group isomorphic to $SL_2(\mathbb{Z}_3)$ (the group of $2\times 2$ matrices of determinant 1 over the three-element field) acting by fractional transformations on the normalized parameter surface, together with explicitly described degenerate and quantum-polynomial strata. The authors state explicitly that they did not treat the two remaining regimes: fields of characteristic 3, and fields of characteristic different from 3 containing no nontrivial cubic root of unity (such as the rationals, the reals, or $\mathbb{F}_p$ with $p\equiv 2 \pmod{3}$). The present problem asks for the explicit parameter-space classification of the isomorphism classes in those two regimes. Both are genuinely different from the solved case: in characteristic 3 the root-of-unity substitution collapses and the degeneracy stratification itself changes shape ($p^3=q^3=r^3$ reduces to $p=q=r$), while over fields without $\theta$ the classification over the algebraic closure does not automatically descend, raising the question of whether distinct $\mathbb{K}$-forms of Sklyanin algebras — algebras that become isomorphic only after extending the ground field — exist.

## Background

The three-generator Sklyanin algebras form one of the most studied families of graded algebras in noncommutative algebra. Recall that a quadratic algebra is a graded algebra $A(V,R)$ presented by a finite-dimensional vector space $V$ of degree-one generators and a subspace $R\subseteq V\otimes V$ of homogeneous quadratic relations; its Hilbert series $H_A(t)=\sum_{n\ge 0}\dim A_n\,t^n$ is its basic size invariant. The Sklyanin algebra $Q_{p,q,r}$ over a field $\mathbb{K}$, introduced by E. K. Sklyanin in connection with the Yang–Baxter equation (the consistency condition for the factorized scattering operators of exactly solvable models in statistical mechanics and quantum field theory), is the quadratic algebra on generators $x,y,z$ with the three relations $pyz+qzy+rxx=0$, $pzx+qxz+ryy=0$, $pxy+qyx+rzz=0$, where $p,q,r\in\mathbb{K}$. Multiplying the triple $(p,q,r)$ by a common nonzero scalar does not change the algebra, so the parameters are naturally points of a projective plane; nevertheless, non-proportional triples can give isomorphic algebras, and describing exactly when this happens is a delicate question.

The landmark results are geometric. Artin and Schelter classified graded algebras of global dimension 3 that are Artin–Schelter regular (the noncommutative analogues of polynomial rings); Artin, Tate and Van den Bergh then showed, over an algebraically closed ground field, that $Q_{p,q,r}$ with at least two of $p,q,r$ nonzero and $p^3,q^3,r^3$ not all equal is Artin–Schelter regular: it is Koszul (its trivial module admits a free resolution generated in matching degrees) and has the same Hilbert series $(1-t)^{-3}$ as a commutative polynomial ring in three variables (a PHS-algebra, for polynomial Hilbert series). Their method attaches to $Q_{p,q,r}$ geometric data — a smooth elliptic curve $E\subset\mathbb{P}^2$ parametrizing the point modules of the algebra (a point module is a cyclic graded module all of whose graded components are one-dimensional — the noncommutative analogue of a point of $\mathbb{P}^2$) together with a translation automorphism — and shows two Sklyanin algebras are isomorphic precisely when their geometric data agree, a criterion that is not explicit in the parameters $(p,q,r)$. Odesskii and Feigin placed the family inside the wider theory of elliptic algebras; Polishchuk and Positselski proved that a generic $Q_{p,q,r}$ over $\mathbb{C}$ is Koszul but not a PBW-algebra, i.e. no choice of bases of generators and relations turns the relations into a noncommutative Gröbner basis with respect to a monomial ordering (the noncommutative analogue of a Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt basis); Smith showed that the degenerate Sklyanin algebras, those failing the regularity criterion, become monomial after a linear change of generators; Walton determined the dimensions of the irreducible representations of the regular members of the family and their polynomial-identity degree in the module-finite case. All of these treatments work over algebraically closed fields, in practice of characteristic other than 2 or 3.

Iyudu and Shkarin re-established and extended these facts by purely algebraic, combinatorial means. Their 2017 paper constructed Gröbner bases for Sklyanin algebras indirectly, through a one-sided module; the 2021 paper, the source of the present problem, found a linear substitution after which the leading monomials of a reduced Gröbner basis of the ideal of relations of $Q_{p,q,1}$ itself can be read off. The substitution uses a nontrivial cubic root of unity, an element $\theta\in\mathbb{K}$ with $\theta^3=1\neq\theta$ (which exists in every algebraically closed field of characteristic different from 3 and in no field of characteristic 3). After scaling and a chain of substitutions the algebra is brought to the potential algebra with potential $x^3-(xyz)^{\mathrm{cyc}}+\alpha(yyz)^{\mathrm{cyc}}-(\gamma-\alpha^2)z^3$, where $u^{\mathrm{cyc}}$ denotes the cyclic symmetrization of a word $u$ (the sum of all cyclic shifts of $u$) and a potential algebra is one whose relations are the formal partial derivatives of such a cyclically invariant potential; an induction then shows the Gröbner basis is either an explicitly described infinite family with a regular pattern or a finite one. This yields a short proof of the classification theorem (valid over every field of characteristic different from 3, with or without $\theta$ in it, since Hilbert series and Koszulity are unchanged by extending the ground field within the same characteristic): $Q_{p,q,r}$ is always Koszul, and it is a PHS-algebra if and only if at least two of $p,q,r$ are nonzero and $p^3=q^3=r^3$ fails.

The same paper then settles the isomorphism problem in that setting. First, a general observation valid over any field: if two quadratic algebras are isomorphic as algebras they are already isomorphic as graded algebras, that is, by a linear substitution of the generators, so 'isomorphic' is unambiguous. Assuming $\theta\in\mathbb{K}$ and characteristic different from 3, the classification reads: the degenerate algebras reduce to the free algebra and two monomial algebras (with relations $xy=zx=yz=0$ and $xx=yy=zz=0$); the parameter stratum where $(p+q)^3+r^3=0$ consists of quantum polynomial algebras $B_\alpha$ with relations $xy=\alpha yx$, $zx=\alpha xz$, $yz=\alpha zy$, where $B_\alpha\cong B_\beta$ if and only if $\alpha=\beta$ or $\alpha\beta=1$; and on the remaining main stratum, normalized to $r=1$, two algebras $Q_{a,b,1}$ and $Q_{a',b',1}$ are isomorphic exactly when $(a,b)$ and $(a',b')$ lie in the same orbit of a 24-element group of fractional transformations isomorphic to $SL_2(\mathbb{Z}_3)$, the group of $2\times 2$ matrices of determinant 1 over the three-element field, generated by $(a,b)\mapsto(\theta a,\theta b)$ and $(a,b)\mapsto\bigl((\theta a+\theta^2 b+1)/(a+b+1),(\theta^2 a+\theta b+1)/(a+b+1)\bigr)$.

Every ingredient of this description breaks in the two regimes the authors explicitly set aside. In characteristic 3 a nontrivial cubic root of unity cannot exist (there $t^3-1=(t-1)^3$), so neither the substitution nor the orbit formulas even make sense; moreover the parameter-space geometry itself changes, because the Frobenius map $u\mapsto u^3$ is injective in characteristic 3, so the degeneracy equation $p^3=q^3=r^3$ collapses to $p=q=r$ and $x^3+y^3+z^3=(x+y+z)^3$, whence the degenerate strata and the elliptic-curve data behave differently from all other characteristics. In characteristic different from 3 but without $\theta$ (for instance $\mathbb{Q}$, $\mathbb{R}$, or $\mathbb{F}_p$ with $p\equiv 2 \pmod{3}$), the Hilbert-series and Koszulity statements survive by passing to the algebraic closure, but isomorphism does not: two algebras with $\mathbb{K}$-rational parameters may be joined by a linear substitution only over the quadratic extension $\mathbb{K}(\theta)$, and whether a substitution over $\mathbb{K}$ itself must exist — a Galois-descent question, with room for genuine twist phenomena — distinct $\mathbb{K}$-forms, that is, algebras that become isomorphic only after extending the ground field — at special parameters whose automorphism group is larger than the generic one, is not known. The open problem is to complete the classification in precisely these two regimes.

## Problem Statement

Let $\mathbb{K}$ be a field. The three-generator Sklyanin algebra with parameters $(p,q,r)\in\mathbb{K}^3$ is the quadratic algebra $Q_{p,q,r}$ over $\mathbb{K}$ on generators $x,y,z$ with the three defining relations $pyz+qzy+rxx=0$, $pzx+qxz+ryy=0$, $pxy+qyx+rzz=0$; multiplying the triple $(p,q,r)$ by a common nonzero scalar does not change the algebra. Determine, explicitly in terms of the parameter triples, necessary and sufficient conditions for $Q_{p,q,r}$ and $Q_{p',q',r'}$ to be isomorphic as $\mathbb{K}$-algebras (equivalently, by a general observation of Iyudu–Shkarin valid for quadratic algebras over any field, as graded algebras via a linear substitution of the generators), in each of the two regimes not covered by the existing classification:

(i) $\mathrm{char}\,\mathbb{K}=3$;

(ii) $\mathrm{char}\,\mathbb{K}\neq 3$ and $\mathbb{K}$ contains no element $\theta$ with $\theta^3=1\neq\theta$ (for example $\mathbb{Q}$, $\mathbb{R}$, or $\mathbb{F}_p$ with $p\equiv 2 \pmod{3}$).

A complete solution should, in each regime: identify the degenerate strata of the parameter space — the loci where the algebra ceases to have the polynomial Hilbert series $(1-t)^{-3}$, which for characteristic different from 3 are exactly the triples with at least two of $p,q,r$ zero or with $p^3=q^3=r^3$ (in characteristic 3 the locus $p^3=q^3=r^3$ collapses to the diagonal $p=q=r$, so this stratification must itself be re-derived); treat any intermediate stratum analogous to the $(p+q)^3+r^3=0$ quantum-polynomial family (algebras with relations of the shape $xy=\alpha yx$, $zx=\alpha xz$, $yz=\alpha zy$); give the orbit or group-theoretic description of the main stratum over $\mathbb{K}$; and, in regime (ii), settle the descent question of whether two $\mathbb{K}$-rational parameter triples lying in a common orbit over the algebraic closure are always joined by an isomorphism defined over $\mathbb{K}$, or whether genuinely distinct $\mathbb{K}$-forms occur — algebras that become isomorphic only after extending the ground field — in particular at special parameters whose stabilizer or automorphism group is nontrivial. The expected form of the answer is a theorem of the same explicit shape as the 24-element $SL_2(\mathbb{Z}_3)$-orbit classification available when $\theta\in\mathbb{K}$, and any answer must reduce to that classification when specialized to algebraically closed fields of characteristic different from 3 containing $\theta$.

The verification contract below evaluates answers to this statement. It does not narrow or redefine the research question.

Known solving difficulties:

- Collapse of the method in characteristic 3: the normal-form reduction and every orbit formula of the known classification use a nontrivial cubic root of unity $\theta$, which cannot exist in characteristic 3; since the Frobenius map is injective there, the degeneracy locus $p^3=q^3=r^3$ collapses to the diagonal $p=q=r$ and $x^3+y^3+z^3=(x+y+z)^3$, so the strata, normal forms, and the entire case analysis must be rebuilt from scratch.
- Galois-descent analysis in the root-of-unity-free regime: isomorphism over the algebraic closure does not imply isomorphism over $\mathbb{K}$; a complete answer must decide, stratum by stratum, whether the isomorphism torsor (the variety of isomorphisms between the two algebras, on which the automorphism group of either acts freely and transitively) has $\mathbb{K}$-rational points, with possible nontrivial twist obstructions at special parameters (extra automorphisms of the associated elliptic curve, finite-order translations).
- Completeness direction: proving that no isomorphisms exist beyond those exhibited requires controlling all algebra isomorphisms, equivalently all invertible linear substitutions carrying one relation space onto the other, which fragments into many parameter subcases across the degenerate, quantum-polynomial, and main strata.
- Field-dependence of the answer: the classification may bifurcate between algebraically closed, real, finite, and general fields within each regime, and a uniform statement must track these dependences correctly.
- In characteristic 3 the degenerate strata must first be identified before they can be classified, since the known degeneracy criterion (valid for characteristic different from 3) no longer applies.

## Current Progress

Source problem (Iyudu–Shkarin, arXiv:2108.06290, 2021): the paper proves, over any field of characteristic different from 3, that every $Q_{p,q,r}$ is Koszul and that $Q_{p,q,r}$ has polynomial Hilbert series if and only if at least two of $p,q,r$ are nonzero and $p^3=q^3=r^3$ fails (Theorem 1.1; the proof passes to the algebraic closure, where a nontrivial cubic root of unity $\theta$ exists, so no assumption on $\theta$ being in the ground field is needed for this part). Under the additional assumption $\theta\in\mathbb{K}$ it then classifies isomorphisms completely: degenerate algebras reduce to the free algebra and two monomial algebras; the stratum with $(p+q)^3+r^3=0$ consists of quantum polynomial algebras $B_\alpha$ with $B_\alpha\cong B_\beta$ iff $\alpha=\beta$ or $\alpha\beta=1$; and on the main stratum (normalized to $r=1$) isomorphism classes are the orbits of a 24-element group isomorphic to $SL_2(\mathbb{Z}_3)$. In Section 6.2 the authors state explicitly that they did not determine which Sklyanin algebras are isomorphic in two cases: when the characteristic of the ground field equals 3, and when the characteristic is different from 3 but the field possesses no nontrivial cubic root of 1.

Geometric lineage: Artin–Schelter (“Graded algebras of global dimension 3”, Advances in Mathematics 66 (1987), DOI 10.1016/0001-8708(87)90034-X) and Artin–Tate–Van den Bergh (“Some Algebras Associated to Automorphisms of Elliptic Curves”, The Grothendieck Festschrift I, 1990, DOI 10.1007/978-0-8176-4574-8_3; “Modules over regular algebras of dimension 3”, Inventiones mathematicae 106 (1991), DOI 10.1007/bf01243916) established the regularity criterion and the classification of three-dimensional Sklyanin algebras through geometric data (an elliptic curve in $\mathbb{P}^2$ with a translation automorphism) over algebraically closed fields, observing that isomorphism of algebras is equivalent to isomorphism of the geometric data — a criterion not explicit in the parameters. Odesskii–Feigin (“Sklyanin elliptic algebras”, Functional Analysis and Its Applications 23 (1989), DOI 10.1007/bf01079526) and Odesskii's survey (“Elliptic algebras”, Russian Mathematical Surveys 57 (2002), DOI 10.1070/rm2002v057n06abeh000573) treat the elliptic algebras in the same algebraically closed setting; Polishchuk–Positselski (Quadratic Algebras, University Lecture Series 37, 2005, DOI 10.1090/ulect/037) prove generic Sklyanin algebras are Koszul but not PBW over $\mathbb{C}$; Smith (“‘Degenerate’ 3-dimensional Sklyanin algebras are monomial algebras”, Journal of Algebra 358 (2012), DOI 10.1016/j.jalgebra.2012.01.039) showed the degenerate Sklyanin algebras become monomial after a change of basis; Walton (“Representation theory of three-dimensional Sklyanin algebras”, Nuclear Physics B 860 (2012), DOI 10.1016/j.nuclphysb.2012.02.015, arXiv:1107.2953) determined the irreducible-representation dimensions under the standing assumption of an algebraically closed field of characteristic other than 2 or 3. None of these treats characteristic 3 or non-algebraically-closed fields without a cubic root of unity.

The authors' own adjacent work does not cover the two regimes: the 2017 Gröbner-basis paper (Iyudu–Shkarin, “Three dimensional Sklyanin algebras and Gröbner bases”, Journal of Algebra 470 (2017), 379–419, DOI 10.1016/j.jalgebra.2016.08.023, arXiv:1601.00564) works in characteristic different from 3 and classifies generalized nine-parameter Sklyanin algebras with polynomial Hilbert series, not the isomorphism classes of the standard family; the potential-algebras classification (Iyudu–Shkarin, “Potential algebras with few generators”, Glasgow Mathematical Journal 62 (2020), S28–S76, DOI 10.1017/S0017089520000233, arXiv:1806.06829) and the PBW-algebras classification (Iyudu–Shkarin, “Classification of quadratic and cubic PBW algebras on three generators”, arXiv:1806.06844) both carry the standing assumption that the ground field is algebraically closed of characteristic other than 2 or 3.

Rogalski's 2023 survey (arXiv:2307.03174) recounts the Gröbner-based recovery of the regularity classification without addressing the isomorphism question. The authors' potential-algebras paper (arXiv:1806.06829; Glasgow Mathematical Journal 62 (2020), S28–S76) also does not settle it. Related work remains within the standard algebraically closed framework. Matsuno classifies quadratic AS-regular algebras of Type EC (Canadian Mathematical Bulletin 64 (2021), 123–141, DOI 10.4153/S0008439520000302; arXiv:1912.05167) and studies twisted geometric algebras (2022–2025). Herrera–Higuera–Rubiano (arXiv:2507.19719) assume a primitive cube root of unity and use the $\theta$-dependent isomorphism lemmas without extending them. Mizomov (Journal of Algebra and Its Applications, 2024, DOI 10.1142/S0219498825502184) studies an isomorphism between four-dimensional Sklyanin and elliptic algebras. No treatment of characteristic 3 or of fields such as $\mathbb{Q}$, $\mathbb{R}$, or $\mathbb{F}_p$ ($p\equiv 2 \pmod{3}$) was found; the arXiv preprint also has no published journal version at curation.

The reductions and parameter formulas of Iyudu–Shkarin, Theorem 1.1 (arXiv:2108.06290), hold over every field of characteristic different from 3, with or without $\theta$: Hilbert series and Koszulity are insensitive to same-characteristic field extension. What is missing without a primitive cube root of unity is specifically the isomorphism classification of Section 5. The $(\Pi_k)$/$(\Sigma_k)$ Gröbner-basis dichotomy belongs to the proof of Theorem 1.1: the basis is either a patterned infinite family or a finite one, and both cases are handled there. A separate classification of the parameter subvarieties is not posed as an open problem in the paper. The problem is therefore the paper's explicit one, the isomorphism classification in the two regimes; the Gröbner-basis boundary locus is recorded here as a secondary, non-posed aspect.

What remains open (relative to Iyudu–Shkarin, arXiv:2108.06290, whose Section 6.2 states the two untreated cases explicitly): both regimes are entirely untreated in the literature found, and both have genuine mathematical content rather than being routine extensions. In characteristic 3 the degeneracy locus changes shape (injectivity of the Frobenius map makes $p^3=q^3=r^3$ equivalent to $p=q=r$, and $x^3+y^3+z^3=(x+y+z)^3$), the root-of-unity substitution and the $SL_2(\mathbb{Z}_3)$ orbit machinery collapse, and even the paper's proof of the Koszulity/PHS classification uses characteristic different from 3 essentially (though that classification is not part of the posed problem). In the root-of-unity-free regime the classification over the algebraic closure does not automatically descend, and whether genuine $\mathbb{K}$-forms of Sklyanin algebras exist — a Galois-descent question, most delicate at special parameters with nontrivial stabilizers such as those carrying extra automorphisms of the associated elliptic curve — is unknown.

## Scientific Significance

Affected-field significance: `medium`.

Directly completes the explicit parameter-space classification of a classical, much-studied family in noncommutative algebra and noncommutative projective geometry — the three-generator Sklyanin algebras — by extending it to the base-field regimes (characteristic 3; fields without a nontrivial cubic root of unity, including the rationals, the reals, and most finite fields) that every existing treatment excludes. A solution would add new core knowledge about this standard family: whether genuinely distinct $\mathbb{K}$-forms of Sklyanin algebras exist — algebras that become isomorphic only after extending the ground field — (a Galois-descent phenomenon never analyzed for this family) and how the degeneracy stratification and the associated elliptic-curve geometry reshape in characteristic 3. The impact on the field's mainstream machinery, which is developed over algebraically closed fields of characteristic zero, is indirect, hence medium rather than high.
