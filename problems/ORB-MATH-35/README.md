# ORB-MATH-35: Is every line graph $\{1\star(\chi'(G)-3),\,3\}$-choosable?

The List Colouring Conjecture (LCC) predicts that the line graph $L(G)$ of every graph $G$ is $\chi'(G)$-choosable, where $\chi'(G)$ is the chromatic index; it is open in general, even for complete graphs of even order. The framework of $\lambda$-choosability refines the scale between $k$-colourability and $k$-choosability by constraining how list entries are distributed across a partitioned palette: for a multiset $\lambda=\{k_1,\dots,k_q\}$, a $\lambda$-list assignment partitions the colour set into classes $C_1,\dots,C_q$ with each vertex's list containing at least $k_i$ colours from $C_i$. In this framework it is known that $L(G)$ is $\lambda$-choosable whenever $\lambda$ partitions $\chi'(G)$ into parts of size at most 2, and Zhu and Zhu posed the natural next question: is $L(G)$ always $\{1\star(\chi'(G)-3),3\}$-choosable, with one palette class of size 3 and $\chi'(G)-3$ singleton classes? This audit verified the question against the primary sources and swept the 2020–2026 follow-up literature (forward citation chains of the source paper and of the foundational refinement paper, the complete zbMATH corpus on $\lambda$-choosability, arXiv sweeps, and a June 2026 dynamic survey on list colouring): the question remains open. When $\chi'(G)=3$ the multiset degenerates to $\{3\}$, so the question specialises exactly to the LCC for graphs of chromatic index 3.

## Background

List colouring extends ordinary graph colouring by assigning each vertex $v$ its own set $L(v)$ of permissible colours and requiring a proper colouring with $f(v)\in L(v)$ for every $v$. A graph is $k$-choosable if such a colouring exists for every assignment of lists of size at least $k$, and the choice number $\operatorname{ch}(G)$ is the least such $k$; always $\operatorname{ch}(G)\ge\chi(G)$. A graph with $\operatorname{ch}(G)=\chi(G)$ is called chromatic-choosable. The most famous conjecture in this area is the List Colouring Conjecture (LCC), stated in several equivalent forms in the 1970s (see the 2026 dynamic survey of Vasudevan, Somasundaram and Narayanan): line graphs are chromatic-choosable. The line graph $L(G)$ has the edges of $G$ as vertices, two of them adjacent when the corresponding edges share an endpoint; edge colouring of $G$ is vertex colouring of $L(G)$, so the conjecture says $\operatorname{ch}(L(G))=\chi(L(G))=\chi'(G)$, where the chromatic index $\chi'(G)$ is the minimum number of colours in a proper edge colouring of $G$. Equivalently, every edge-$k$-colourable graph is edge-$k$-choosable. Galvin confirmed the conjecture for bipartite multigraphs, and various further classes are verified (snarks, i.e. bridgeless cubic graphs that are not 3-edge-colourable; odd complete graphs; series-parallel graphs; and others), but the conjecture is open in general — for instance it is unknown for complete graphs of even order, as Xuding Zhu's 2020 paper records.

The framework of $\lambda$-choosability, introduced by Xuding Zhu in 2020, interpolates between colourability and choosability. For a multiset $\lambda=\{k_1,\dots,k_q\}$ of positive integers with $k_\lambda=\sum_i k_i$ (writing $1\star m$ for $m$ copies of the integer 1), a $\lambda$-list assignment of $G$ is a list assignment $L$ whose colour set $\bigcup_v L(v)$ can be partitioned into disjoint classes $C_1\cup\dots\cup C_q$ such that $|L(v)\cap C_i|\ge k_i$ for every vertex $v$ and every $i$; $G$ is $\lambda$-choosable if it is $L$-colourable for every $\lambda$-list assignment $L$. If $\lambda=\{k\}$ this is ordinary $k$-choosability, and if $\lambda=\{1\star k\}$ it is equivalent to $k$-colourability; every $\lambda$-choosable graph is $k_\lambda$-colourable, and every $k_\lambda$-choosable graph is $\lambda$-choosable, since a $\lambda$-list assignment is a $k_\lambda$-list assignment with extra structure. The partitions of $k$ thus form a strict hierarchy of colourability strengths between $k$-colourability and $k$-choosability.

For line graphs the frontier of this hierarchy is sharp. Zhu proved that if $\lambda$ partitions $\chi'(G)$ and every part of $\lambda$ has size at most 2, then $L(G)$ is $\lambda$-choosable: the proof decomposes a proper $\chi'(G)$-edge-colouring into groups of at most two colour classes, each inducing matchings, paths, and even cycles — structures whose line graphs are choosable from the corresponding palette class by classical results — and colours each group from its own class, the classes being disjoint. He also proved that for even $n$, the complete graph $K_n$ is edge-$\lambda$-choosable for every partition of $n-1=\chi'(K_n)$ into parts of size at most 3. A palette class of size 3, however, groups three colour classes of the decomposition into a subgraph of maximum degree 3 that is 3-edge-colourable, and whether such subgraphs are edge-3-choosable is exactly the LCC in the chromatic-index-3 case — the obstacle the open question confronts. The question was recorded by Jialu Zhu and Xuding Zhu in their work on minimum non-chromatic-$\lambda$-choosable graphs, the $\lambda$-analogue of the study of $\varphi(k)$, the minimum order of a $k$-chromatic non-$k$-choosable graph (Ohba's conjecture, confirmed by Noel, Reed and Wu, asserts $\varphi(k)\ge 2k+2$); having determined $\varphi(\lambda)$ for all $\lambda$, they explicitly recorded the line-graph question as a natural unresolved problem.

## Problem Statement

Let $G$ be a graph of chromatic index $\chi'(G)\ge 3$ and let $L(G)$ be its line graph. For the multiset $\lambda=\{1\star(\chi'(G)-3),\,3\}$, that is, $\chi'(G)-3$ copies of the integer 1 together with one integer 3 (so $k_\lambda=\chi'(G)$ and $\lambda$ has $\chi'(G)-2$ parts), is $L(G)$ always $\lambda$-choosable?

Explicitly: whenever each edge $e$ of $G$ receives a list $L(e)$ of colours and the colour set $\bigcup_{e\in E(G)}L(e)$ can be partitioned into disjoint classes $C_1\cup\dots\cup C_{\chi'(G)-2}$ such that every edge's list contains at least one colour from each of $C_1,\dots,C_{\chi'(G)-3}$ and at least three colours from $C_{\chi'(G)-2}$, must $G$ admit a proper edge colouring in which each edge $e$ receives a colour from $L(e)$?

This is the question posed by Zhu and Zhu. When $\chi'(G)=3$ the multiset is $\{3\}$ and the question specialises to: is every 3-edge-colourable graph edge-3-choosable — the List Colouring Conjecture for the class of graphs with chromatic index 3. For $\chi'(G)\ge 4$ the statement is strictly weaker than the LCC for $G$, sitting between $\chi'(G)$-edge-colourability (which always holds) and $\chi'(G)$-choosability of $L(G)$ (the LCC).

The verification contract below evaluates answers to this statement. It does not narrow or redefine the research question.

Known solving difficulties:

- When $\chi'(G)=3$ the question coincides with the List Colouring Conjecture for all 3-edge-colourable graphs (class-I subcubic graphs), a decades-old open case; any proof must at minimum settle that entire class.
- The known part-size-2 proof works because groups of at most two colour classes of an optimal edge colouring induce matchings, paths, and even cycles, whose line graphs are choosable by classical characterisations of 2-choosable graphs; a palette class of size 3 instead produces class-I subcubic subgraphs, whose edge-3-choosability is exactly the open LCC instance, so the standard decomposition method stalls precisely at the crux.
- Galvin's kernel-perfect orientation method — the tool behind the LCC's principal verified case, bipartite multigraphs — exploits the bipartite row/column structure of the line graph and has no known extension to general graphs.
- Algebraic methods (Alon-Tarsi numbers, the Combinatorial Nullstellensatz) certify full $k$-choosability of line graphs in restricted cases and are not known to degrade to $\lambda$-choosability with one part of size 3.
- A counterexample would require constructing a graph whose line graph defeats a heavily constrained palette; the known non-$\lambda$-choosable constructions in the literature concern planar graphs and squares of graphs rather than line graphs, and none transfers.
- The refinement order among partitions gives no leverage: the partition $\{1\star(\chi'(G)-3),3\}$ is incomparable in the $\lambda$-order with the covered partitions having all parts of size at most 2, so no monotonicity argument reduces one to the other.

## Current Progress

- Status: `ready`

Source fidelity verified: the question appears verbatim in the introduction of Jialu Zhu and Xuding Zhu, 'Minimum non-chromatic-$\lambda$-choosable graphs' (arXiv:2208.02050, 2022; Journal of Graph Theory 110 (2025) 283-289), posed immediately after, and contrasted with, the known positive result (their reference [21]) that $L(G)$ is $\lambda$-choosable whenever $\lambda$ partitions $\chi'(G)$ into parts of size at most 2. The LKM paraphrase, the attribution, and the contrast are all accurate; no conflation of adjacent results was found.

Framework origin: Xuding Zhu, 'A refinement of choosability of graphs' (Journal of Combinatorial Theory, Series B 141 (2020) 143-164), introduced $\lambda$-choosability and proved the part-size-2 theorem for line graphs via decomposition of an optimal edge colouring, plus the stronger fact that $K_n$ for even $n$ is edge-$\lambda$-choosable for every partition of $n-1$ into parts of size at most 3. The same paper posed an asymptotic weakening of the LCC (its Conjecture 2: for each $s$ there is $k(s)$ such that every edge-$k$-colourable graph with $k\ge k(s)$ is edge-$\lambda$-choosable for every partition of $k$ into parts of size at most $s$) and recorded that the LCC is open for complete graphs of even order. The audited question is the extreme instance of this programme with a single part of size 3 and no lower bound on $k$.

The source paper's own contribution — determining $\varphi(\lambda)$, the minimum order of a $k_\lambda$-chromatic non-$\lambda$-choosable graph, for all $\lambda$, extending the Ohba/Noel-Reed-Wu theory — concerns small complete-multipartite-type graphs and does not touch line graphs; the EUROCOMB'23 extended abstract has the same content. The line-graph question was posed as open in 2022 and no later version, erratum, or follow-up note narrows it.

Forward citation sweep (as of 2026-08-28): the published source paper has one recorded citation (Semantic Scholar) — Fang and Xu, 'Codegree Thresholds for $\lambda$-Choosability of Graphs' (arXiv:2608.16740, 2026), which proves that graphs of large minimum $q$-codegree fail to be $\lambda$-choosable and never mentions line graphs. The foundational 2020 JCTB paper has 24 recorded citations, all inspected by title and the relevant ones by abstract or full text: Gu-Zhu (strictness of the $\lambda$-hierarchy in high girth), Zhu-Zhu ($\lambda$-paintability and $\varphi(\lambda)$ bounds), Gu-Jiang-Wood-Zhu (a refined list version of Hadwiger's conjecture), Leonard (strictly $k$-colorable graphs and sharpness of the Four Colour Theorem on the $\lambda$-scale), Enami-Yokoi (the partial order of $\lambda$-choosability for planar graphs), and various signed-colouring and planar-list-colouring papers. None addresses $\lambda$-choosability of line graphs.

The June 2026 dynamic survey of Vasudevan, Somasundaram and Narayanan on list colouring and chromatic-choosability covers line graphs and the LCC in detail; the verified classes it lists (bipartite multigraphs via Galvin's theorem, snarks, $K_{2n+1}$, series-parallel graphs, graphs with no circuit of size at least 4, and others) do not include all graphs of chromatic index 3, and the survey does not mention the $\lambda$-choosable weakening of the LCC at all — consistent with the question being unresolved as of mid-2026.

Partial positive evidence only: Galvin's theorem gives the full LCC, hence the question, for bipartite multigraphs; the 2020 part-size-2 theorem's second half gives it for complete graphs of even order; the confirmed Ohba conjecture gives it for line graphs on at most $2\chi'(G)+1$ vertices (very small graphs); every other LCC-verified class transfers similarly. No counterexample is known (no graph is known whose line graph defeats a $\{1\star(\chi'(G)-3),3\}$-assignment), and no technique extending beyond these classes is known.

Audit observation (derived during this audit, not found in the literature, and not counted toward closure): the question appears to be equivalent to the List Edge Colouring Conjecture restricted to graphs of chromatic index at most 3, i.e. to 'every 3-edge-colourable (class-I subcubic) graph is edge-3-choosable'. Specialising the question to $\chi'(G)=3$ gives the multiset $\{3\}$, which is exactly the LCC for $G$. Conversely, given any $\{1\star(\chi'(G)-3),3\}$-assignment, group a proper $\chi'(G)$-edge-colouring into $\chi'(G)-3$ single colour classes (matchings, colourable from the singleton palette classes) and one triple of colour classes spanning a subgraph of chromatic index at most 3, colourable from the 3-class by the assumed chromatic-index-3 LCC; disjointness of the palette classes makes the composed colouring proper. If this elementary reduction is correct, the open core of the audited question is precisely the chromatic-index-3 case of the LCC — a famous open problem — which corroborates that the swept literature contains no resolution.

Coverage and residual uncertainty: both papers' citation chains were exhausted (Semantic Scholar), the zbMATH corpus on '$\lambda$-choosable' is complete and fully reviewed, arXiv was swept through August 2026, and the 2026 survey was read; the question is recorded as open. The Wiley full text of the JGT 2025 published version was inaccessible (paywall), so the verbatim presence of the question in the published PDF rests on the arXiv version (the only preprint version) together with the matching published abstract and the EUROCOMB'23 extended abstract. One web-search result alleging a related 'Kim-Oum-Zhu' paper on $\lambda$-choosable graphs was discarded as unverifiable (no such record exists in arXiv, zbMATH, or Crossref).

## Scientific Significance

Affected-field significance: `high`.

A positive answer would, already at $\chi'(G)=3$, settle the List Colouring Conjecture for every graph of chromatic index 3 — the entire class of 3-edge-colourable (class-I subcubic) graphs, for which no general theorem is known — and would additionally give, for every graph, the first $\lambda$-choosability theorem for line graphs beyond the part-size-2 barrier of Zhu's 2020 theorem. This is a direct change to core knowledge in list edge colouring: the chromatic-index-3 case is one of the LCC's central open instances, and any proof of it would necessarily introduce transferable technique. A negative answer would either refute the LCC on a chromatic-index-3 graph (if the counterexample has $\chi'(G)=3$) or exhibit the first known graph whose line graph fails even this weakened refined choosability, exposing a new obstruction between edge-colourability and the LCC. For the chromatic-choosability programme (Ohba-type minimum-order questions and the $\lambda$-choosability hierarchy) the line-graph case is the framework's flagship open instance, so the impact there is direct.
