# ORB-MATH-15: The 11/8-conjecture (Matsumoto): $b_2\ge\frac{11}{8}|\sigma|$ for closed smooth spin 4-manifolds

The 11/8-conjecture of Matsumoto asserts that every closed smooth spin 4-manifold $X$ satisfies $b_2(X)\ge\frac{11}{8}|\sigma(X)|$, where $b_2$ is the second Betti number and $\sigma$ the signature; equivalently, the even indefinite unimodular lattice $2pE_8\oplus qH$ occurs as the intersection form of such a manifold only when $q\ge 3p$, the borderline being realized by connected sums of the K3 surface. It is the last missing piece of the geography problem for simply connected smooth 4-manifolds. The best general bound remains Furuta's 10/8 theorem ($b_2\ge\frac{10}{8}|\sigma|+2$), refined residue-class-wise by Furuta-Kametani and by Hopkins-Lin-Shi-Xu, whose "10/8+4" theorem also proves that the stable-homotopy (Furuta-Mahowald) route is capped at $\frac{10}{8}|\sigma|+4$. The conjecture is equivalent to a lower bound on the bounding genus of homology 3-spheres with vanishing Rokhlin invariant. A systematic audit of the literature through August 2026 finds the conjecture open.

## Background

For a closed, oriented, smooth 4-manifold $X$, the cup product makes $H^2(X;\mathbb{Z})$ modulo torsion into a unimodular (determinant $\pm 1$) symmetric bilinear lattice $Q_X$, called the intersection form; its rank is the second Betti number $b_2(X)$ and its signature (number of positive minus negative eigenvalues) is $\sigma(X)$. The geography problem asks which unimodular forms arise as $Q_X$ for closed simply connected smooth 4-manifolds. Freedman's topological classification shows that in the topological category an even form is realized exactly when the Kirby-Siebenmann invariant (the obstruction to a topological 4-manifold admitting any smooth structure) equals $\sigma/8$ modulo 2, by a unique simply connected manifold; in the smooth category, Donaldson's diagonalizability theorem forces every definite form realized by a closed simply connected smooth 4-manifold to be $\pm I$. By the classical Hasse-Minkowski classification of indefinite unimodular forms, the remaining indefinite forms are the odd ones (diagonal $\pm 1$, all smoothly realized by connected sums of $\mathbb{C}P^2$ and $\overline{\mathbb{C}P}^2$) and the even ones (a unimodular form is even when $Q(x,x)$ is even for every $x$, and odd otherwise), which are exactly $kE_8\oplus lH$: here $E_8$ is the positive-definite even unimodular lattice of rank 8 and signature 8, and $H$ is the hyperbolic plane $\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$ of rank 2 and signature 0.

An oriented 4-manifold is spin when its second Stiefel-Whitney class $w_2\in H^2(X;\mathbb{Z}/2)$ (the characteristic class obstructing a spin structure) vanishes; by Wu's formula this is equivalent to $Q_X$ being even, and Rohlin's theorem forces $\sigma(X)\equiv 0\pmod{16}$ for any closed smooth spin 4-manifold. So the intersection form of a closed smooth spin 4-manifold is $2pE_8\oplus qH$ up to sign, with $p\ge 0$. The realization direction is easy: the K3 surface, a simply connected complex surface with intersection form $2E_8\oplus 3H$, $b_2=22$ and $\sigma=-16$, shows that the connected sum $\#_p K3\#(q-3p)(S^2\times S^2)$, i.e. $p$ copies of the K3 surface together with $q-3p$ copies of $S^2\times S^2$, realizes $2pE_8\oplus qH$ whenever $q\ge 3p$.

Matsumoto's 11/8-conjecture, posed in 1982 in the language of the bounding genus of homology 3-spheres, asserts that this construction is optimal: the form $2pE_8\oplus qH$ with $p\ge 1$ is realized by a closed smooth spin 4-manifold only if $q\ge 3p$. Since $b_2=16p+2q$ and $|\sigma|=16p$ for such a form, this is equivalent to the inequality $b_2(X)\ge\frac{11}{8}|\sigma(X)|$ for every closed smooth spin 4-manifold $X$ (no simple connectivity is needed), with the K3 surface attaining equality. Together with Freedman's and Donaldson's theorems, the conjecture is the last missing piece of the simply connected smooth geography problem: it would determine exactly which even indefinite forms are smoothly realizable.

The known partial results come from gauge theory. Donaldson proved the case $p=1$ (a single $E_8$ pair) under the assumption that $H_1(X;\mathbb{Z})$ has no 2-torsion, and that assumption was removed by Kronheimer (unpublished 1994 lecture, as documented by Hopkins-Lin-Shi-Xu) using Pin(2)-symmetries of the Seiberg-Witten equations; here Pin(2), the normalizer of the circle subgroup in the unit quaternions, is an extra symmetry group of the monopole equations on a spin 4-manifold. Furuta's 10/8 theorem, proved by finite-dimensional approximation of the monopole equations (replacing the Seiberg-Witten map by a Pin(2)-equivariant map between finite-dimensional representation spheres, the Bauer-Furuta invariant), gives $b_2\ge\frac{10}{8}|\sigma|+2$, equivalently $q\ge 2p+1$. Furuta and Kametani obtained the first residue-class refinements. Hopkins, Lin, Shi and Xu then completely solved Furuta's problem on Pin(2)-equivariant stable maps (via the Pin(2)-equivariant Mahowald invariant) and proved the full refinement: for $p\ge 2$, $2pE_8\oplus qH$ is spin-realizable only if $q\ge 2p+2$ when $p\equiv 1,2,5,6\pmod 8$, $q\ge 2p+3$ when $p\equiv 3,4,7\pmod 8$, and $q\ge 2p+4$ when $p\equiv 0\pmod 8$; in particular every closed simply connected smooth spin 4-manifold other than $S^4$, $S^2\times S^2$ and the K3 surface satisfies $b_2\ge\frac{10}{8}|\sigma|+4$. Crucially, the same paper proves that this is the limit of the method: using only the existence of the relevant Furuta-Mahowald classes, no bound stronger than $\frac{10}{8}|\sigma|+4$ can be obtained, so a proof of the 11/8-conjecture requires finer geometric or analytic properties of the Seiberg-Witten map beyond its equivariant stable homotopy class; Furuta, Kametani, Matsue and Minami had earlier shown that nilpotency phenomena in the Bauer-Furuta invariants obstruct any proof of 11/8 from that theory alone.

There is an equivalent formulation on the 3-dimensional side. An integral homology 3-sphere is a closed 3-manifold $Y$ with the same integral homology as $S^3$; its Rokhlin invariant $\mu(Y)\in\mathbb{Z}/2$ is $\sigma(W)/8$ modulo 2 for any compact spin 4-manifold $W$ with boundary $Y$. Matsumoto's bounding genus $|Y|$ is the minimal $n$ such that $Y$ bounds a homologically 1-connected (i.e. $H_1=0$) spin 4-manifold with intersection form $nH$ (finite exactly when $\mu(Y)=0$); Tange's invariant $\mathfrak{ds}(Y)$ is the maximum of $b_2(W)/8$ over definite spin boundings $W$ of $Y$. Tange proved that Matsumoto's conjecture is equivalent to the statement $|Y|\ge\frac{3}{2}\mathfrak{ds}(Y)$ for every homology 3-sphere with $\mu(Y)=0$ and $\mathfrak{ds}(Y)<\infty$. Bauer proposed attacking the simply connected case by cutting a hypothetical counterexample along homology 3-spheres; Manolescu's $\kappa$-invariant of homology 3-spheres, constructed from Pin(2)-equivariant Seiberg-Witten Floer K-theory, and his Floer $K_G$-split condition (with $G=\mathrm{Pin}(2)$; a splitting property of the equivariant Seiberg-Witten Floer $K$-theory of a homology 3-sphere, defined in his paper) give the sharpest obstruction in this direction: no counterexample can admit such a decomposition with all cutting homology spheres Floer $K_G$-split. However, many Brieskorn spheres (links of complex surface singularities, such as $\Sigma(2,3,11)$, forming a standard explicit family of homology 3-spheres) are provably not $K_G$-split, so this route also remains open.

## Problem Statement

Prove or refute the 11/8-conjecture of Matsumoto in its standard full generality: every closed, smooth, oriented, spin 4-manifold $X$ satisfies

$$b_2(X)\;\ge\;\frac{11}{8}\,\left|\sigma(X)\right|.$$

Equivalently: for every $p\ge 1$ and $q\ge 0$, the even indefinite unimodular form $2pE_8\oplus qH$ arises as the intersection form of a closed smooth spin 4-manifold if and only if $q\ge 3p$ (the "if" direction being realized by $\#_p K3\#(q-3p)(S^2\times S^2)$). Equivalently, in the homology-3-sphere form (Tange): every integral homology 3-sphere $Y$ (a closed 3-manifold with the same integral homology as $S^3$) with Rokhlin invariant $\mu(Y)=0$ and $\mathfrak{ds}(Y)<\infty$ satisfies $|Y|\ge\frac{3}{2}\mathfrak{ds}(Y)$; here $\mu(Y)=\sigma(W)/8\pmod 2$ for any compact spin 4-manifold $W$ with $\partial W=Y$, $|Y|$ is the bounding genus (the minimal $n$ such that $Y$ bounds a homologically 1-connected, i.e. $H_1=0$, spin 4-manifold with intersection form $nH$), and $\mathfrak{ds}(Y)$ is the maximum of $b_2(W)/8$ over definite spin boundings $W$ of $Y$. A resolution consists of either a proof of the inequality for all closed smooth spin 4-manifolds, or an explicit closed smooth spin 4-manifold $X$ with $b_2(X)<\frac{11}{8}|\sigma(X)|$ (equivalently, with intersection form $2pE_8\oplus qH$ and $q\le 3p-1$). Partial bounds such as Furuta's 10/8 theorem or the residue-refined 10/8+4 theorem do not resolve the problem.

The verification contract below evaluates answers to this statement. It does not narrow or redefine the research question.

Known solving difficulties:

- The established route (Bauer-Furuta stable homotopy invariants and Furuta-Mahowald classes) is provably capped at $\frac{10}{8}|\sigma|+4$ by Hopkins-Lin-Shi-Xu, so a proof requires genuinely new geometric or analytic input on the Seiberg-Witten map beyond its Pin(2)-equivariant stable homotopy class.
- The decomposition strategy along homology 3-spheres (Bauer) requires the cutting spheres to be Floer $K_G$-split for Manolescu's $\kappa$-bounds to yield a contradiction, but explicit Brieskorn spheres are not $K_G$-split; the strategy needs new topological conditions guaranteeing splitness or a sharpened invariant.
- A counterexample must evade all known gauge-theoretic constraints (10/8, residue-class refinements, 10/8+4); no current construction technique (handlebody calculus, complex or symplectic geometry, surgery and gluing) produces closed spin manifolds with $b_2$ below the K3 threshold.
- The problem sits at the interface of Seiberg-Witten analysis, Pin(2)-equivariant stable homotopy theory, and the homology-cobordism topology of homology 3-spheres; even the relative (manifolds-with-boundary) versions are only partially controlled, and historical progress has required major new machinery each time.

## Current Progress

- Status: `ready`

Source fidelity: the candidate formulation matches the primary sources. Matsumoto's conjecture appeared in his 1982 paper on the bounding genus of homology 3-spheres; the lattice version ($2pE_8\oplus qH$ realized only if $q\ge 3p$) and the Betti-number version ($b_2\ge\frac{11}{8}|\sigma|$) are stated as equivalent by Hopkins-Lin-Shi-Xu (Conjectures 1.7 and 1.9, with Remark 1.8 noting that no simple connectivity is assumed), and Tange states and proves the equivalence with the homology-3-sphere version $|Y|\ge\frac{3}{2}\mathfrak{ds}(Y)$ (Conjectures 1.1-1.2 and Proposition 1.2). Three provenance defects in the LKM lead were found and corrected: (a) the claim node citing "Intersection Forms of Spin Four-Manifolds" (arXiv:1211.7092) is Stefan Bauer's withdrawn preprint, a claimed proof of the full conjecture posted in November 2012 and withdrawn in December 2012 ("Proposition 5.2 is wrong", the error pointed out by Tom Mrowka); it is a withdrawn claimed proof, not a status witness, though Bauer's decomposition strategy for the conjecture is real and is documented in Manolescu's paper; (b) the $|Y|\ge\frac{3}{2}\mathfrak{ds}(Y)$ formulation is due to Tange (Topology and its Applications, 2016), not Manolescu, whose own paper appeared in Mathematische Annalen 359 (2014), not "J. reine angew. Math. 2014" as the LKM record states, and contributes the $\kappa$-invariant and the Floer $K_G$-split obstruction; (c) Furuta's own 10/8 theorem gives $q\ge 2p+1$ (in the $2pE_8\oplus qH$ notation), not "$l\ge 2|k|+2$"; the stronger residue-class bounds are due to Furuta-Kametani and Hopkins-Lin-Shi-Xu.

Lineage of partial results: Donaldson proved the $p=1$ case under a no-2-torsion hypothesis on $H_1$, which Kronheimer removed (unpublished 1994 lecture, as documented by Hopkins-Lin-Shi-Xu). Furuta (Math. Res. Lett. 2001) proved the 10/8 theorem $b_2\ge\frac{10}{8}|\sigma|+2$ (equivalently $q\ge 2p+1$) via finite-dimensional approximation of the Seiberg-Witten monopole equations and their Pin(2)-symmetry. Furuta-Kametani (preprint math/0502511, 2005) proved the first residue-class refinements, including $q\ge p+3$ when $p\equiv 0\pmod 8$ in the $p(-E_8)\oplus qH$ normalization (as documented by Lin, who gave an alternative proof). Hopkins-Lin-Shi-Xu (arXiv 2018; Comm. Amer. Math. Soc. 2 (2022) 22-132) completely solved Furuta's question on Pin(2)-equivariant stable maps via the Pin(2)-equivariant Mahowald invariant, proved the full residue-class refinement ($q\ge 2p+2$, $2p+3$, or $2p+4$ according to $p\bmod 8$ for $p\ge 2$) and the 10/8+4 theorem for closed simply connected spin 4-manifolds other than $S^4$, $S^2\times S^2$, K3, and proved in Remark 1.23 that the Furuta-Mahowald method is capped at $\frac{10}{8}|\sigma|+4$; Furuta-Kametani-Matsue-Minami (Geom. Topol. Monogr. 2007) had earlier shown that nilpotency of the Bauer-Furuta invariants is exactly why that theory cannot prove the 11/8-conjecture. None of these reaches $\frac{11}{8}$ in general.

Decomposition and relative attacks: Bauer proposed proving the simply connected case by cutting a putative counterexample $2r(-E_8)\oplus(3r-1)H$ along homology 3-spheres (via Freedman-Taylor). Manolescu (Math. Ann. 2014) introduced the $\kappa$-invariant of homology 3-spheres from Pin(2)-equivariant Seiberg-Witten Floer K-theory, satisfying $\kappa(Y_1)+q\ge\kappa(Y_0)+p-1$ for spin cobordisms with form $p(-E_8)\oplus qH$, strengthened to $+p+1$ when $Y_0$ is Floer $K_G$-split, and proved (Theorem 1.6) that no counterexample can decompose with all cutting spheres Floer $K_G$-split; however, explicit Brieskorn spheres $\pm\Sigma(2,3,12n-1)$ and $\pm\Sigma(2,3,12n-5)$ are not $K_G$-split, so the strategy requires new conditions guaranteeing splitness. Lin (AGT 2015) developed Pin(2)-equivariant KO-theory bounds for spin cobordisms between homology spheres; Tange (Topology Appl. 2016) proved the equivalence with the bounding-genus formulation and computed $\mathfrak{ds}$ and $E_8$-genera for families of Brieskorn spheres. These works sharpen the relative theory but leave the closed conjecture untouched.

Adjacent results that do not close the core: Konno-Taniguchi (Invent. Math. 2020) extended 10/8-type inequalities to end-periodic 4-manifolds with positive-scalar-curvature ends; Baraglia (AGT 2021) proved families versions of Donaldson diagonalizability and the 10/8 theorem; Donald-Vafaee (Proc. AMS 2016) derived a smooth knot slicing obstruction from the 10/8 theorem; Aceto-Golla-Larson (J. Topology 2017) compute embedding numbers of 3-manifolds into $\#_n(S^2\times S^2)$ only conditionally on the 11/8-conjecture, an explicit acknowledgment that the conjecture is open and load-bearing. None of these proves or refutes $b_2\ge\frac{11}{8}|\sigma|$.

Current status (August 2026): the most recent inspected works still apply Furuta's 10/8 theorem and the Hopkins-Lin-Shi-Xu 10/8+4 theorem as the state of the art: Liu-Piccirillo (arXiv 2024) bound Dehn surgery numbers by 10/8, and Aceto-Golla (arXiv:2607.09398, July 2026) constrain smooth line arrangements via "Furuta's 10/8-Theorem". A systematic search (arXiv full-text phrase searches for "11/8-conjecture", "11/8 conjecture", "Furuta 11/8", "10/8 theorem"; zbMATH Open; a forward-citation scan of Hopkins-Lin-Shi-Xu via Semantic Scholar, none of whose citing works claims a resolution; and LKM claim-graph retrieval) found no proof, counterexample, or refutation of the general conjecture. The precise surviving open core is the full inequality: for general $p$ the best known necessary conditions ($q\ge 2p+2$ to $2p+4$ depending on $p\bmod 8$) fall short of the conjectured $q\ge 3p$, and the established homotopy-theoretic method is provably insufficient to close the gap. Coverage limits: Semantic Scholar citation data for Hopkins-Lin-Shi-Xu is partial, and the paywalled 2025 monograph "Monopole Maps and L^2-cohomology" (Kato-Nakamura, Springer) was not inspected; neither affects the conclusion. A WebSearch tool suggestion of a claimed "10/8+1/40" improvement could not be verified on arXiv or zbMATH and was discarded.

## Scientific Significance

Affected-field significance: `high`.

A proof would complete the geography problem for closed simply connected smooth 4-manifolds: combined with Freedman's topological classification, Donaldson's diagonalizability theorem, and the Hasse-Minkowski classification, it would give the exact criterion $q\ge 3p$ for an even indefinite unimodular form to be the intersection form of a smooth spin 4-manifold, directly changing the field's core knowledge (which simply connected topological 4-manifolds admit spin smooth structures, and where the smooth and topological categories diverge). A counterexample would be equally transformative, exhibiting a smooth spin manifold below the K3 threshold and a fundamentally new smooth-structure phenomenon. The impact is direct on 4-dimensional topology and on gauge-theoretic and equivariant stable-homotopy methods; the conjecture is also load-bearing elsewhere, with results such as the exact embedding numbers of 3-manifolds into $\#_n(S^2\times S^2)$ currently known only conditionally on it.
