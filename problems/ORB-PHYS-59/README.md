# ORB-PHYS-59: Complete classification of maximum-velocity quantum circuits: the two-qubit conjecture and the general local-dimension problem


Brickwork quantum circuits built from a two-site unitary gate $U$ on a local Hilbert space of dimension $q$ have a strict causal light cone of velocity 1, and the butterfly velocity $v_B$ of out-of-time-order correlators satisfies $v_B \le 1$. Claeys and Lamacraft (2020) called circuits with $v_B=1$ maximum-velocity circuits (MVCs) and reduced their study to the spectrum of a folded column transfer matrix $T_1$: maximal velocity requires $T_1$ to have additional unit-eigenvalue eigenoperators beyond those guaranteed by unitarity. They proved that dual-unitary circuits and a one-parameter family of kicked-XY circuits are MVCs — so dual unitarity is sufficient but not necessary — and found numerically that for $q=2$ every two-qubit gate passing the $T_1$ criterion is dual-unitary or gauge-equivalent to the kicked-XY family, leaving a rigorous classification open. Later work settled a converse for a different velocity (maximal entanglement velocity implies dual unitarity; Zhou and Harrow 2022), proved that entangling hierarchical generalized dual-unitary circuits also have $v_B=1$ and hence provide additional non-dual-unitary MVC mechanisms at every local dimension (Yu, Wang, Kos 2024; Foligno, Kos, Bertini 2024), and produced complete qubit-gate classifications under neighboring solvability criteria (Singh and Dooley 2026; Hübner and Kim 2026) — but no proof or refutation of the two-qubit MVC conjecture and no complete classification for general $q$ exists. The problem: rigorously and completely characterize the set of maximum-velocity circuits — prove or refute the $q=2$ conjecture, and give an exhaustive description for arbitrary $q$, accounting for the known constructions and any further MVC families.

## Background

A brickwork (brick-wall) quantum circuit is a discrete-time model of many-body dynamics: qudits — quantum systems with a $q$-dimensional local Hilbert space — sit on a one-dimensional chain, and each time step applies a two-site unitary gate $U$ on all even bonds and then on all odd bonds. Local causality implies that operators can spread by at most one site per time step, so every diagnostic of operator spreading has a strict light cone of velocity 1. The standard diagnostic of quantum information scrambling is the out-of-time-order correlator (OTOC) at infinite temperature, $C_{\alpha\beta}(x,t)=\langle\sigma_\alpha(0,t)\,\sigma_\beta(x,0)\,\sigma_\alpha(0,t)\,\sigma_\beta(x,0)\rangle$, where $\sigma_\alpha$ and $\sigma_\beta$ are local operators; the front of this correlator moves at the butterfly velocity $v_B\le 1$. Generic chaotic circuits have $v_B<1$ (for circuits with Haar-random gates the butterfly velocity was found, on average, to be $v_B=(q^2-1)/(q^2+1)$), so circuits with $v_B=1$ — maximum-velocity circuits (MVCs) — are extremal scramblers.

Claeys and Lamacraft expressed the long-time OTOC at a fixed distance $n$ behind the light-cone edge as a matrix element of powers of a column transfer matrix $T_n$: the $q^{4n}\times q^{4n}$ linear map on the space of $n$-site operators obtained by folding a column of $n$ gates, i.e. contracting a tensor network built from $U$ and its complex conjugate $U^*$. Unitarity of $U$ always supplies certain trivial unit-modulus eigenoperators of $T_n$, but these only reproduce the OTOC value outside the light cone. A maximal butterfly velocity $v_B=1$ requires at least one additional eigenoperator of the shallowest transfer matrix $T_1$ with eigenvalue one and nonzero overlap with the boundary vectors that carry the inserted operators. This supplies a necessary gate-level spectral condition for the classification of MVC gates.

Two mechanisms are known from the source paper. First, dual-unitary gates: $U$ is unitary and remains unitary after a space-time swap, meaning the matrix $\tilde U$ with elements $\tilde U_{ab,cd}=U_{db,ca}$ (the gate read as an evolution along the spatial direction) is also unitary. Every two-qubit gate is, up to single-qubit unitaries on its four legs, of the form $\exp[i(J_x\sigma_x\otimes\sigma_x+J_y\sigma_y\otimes\sigma_y+J_z\sigma_z\otimes\sigma_z)]$ with real Cartan entangling angles $J_x,J_y,J_z$, and dual unitarity fixes two of the three angles at $\pi/4$. Dual-unitary circuits have dynamical correlations only on the light cone, are exactly solvable, and are MVCs at every local dimension $q$. Second, kicked-XY circuits, $U[J]=(K\otimes\mathbb{1})\,e^{iJ\sigma_z\otimes\sigma_z}\,e^{i\frac{\pi}{4}\sigma_y\otimes\sigma_y}$ with $K=e^{i\frac{\pi}{4}\sigma_x}$, a Trotterization of a kicked XY spin chain: these have $v_B=1$ for all $J$ yet are not dual-unitary except at $|J|=\pi/4$, so dual unitarity is not necessary for maximal velocity. Circuit gauge equivalence uses single-site changes of basis that cancel on every internal wire. Precisely, label successive brickwork layers by $s$, and assign $g_x(s)\in U(q)$ to site $x$ at each layer boundary. A gate on the bond $(x,x+1)$ in layer $s$ transforms as
$$
U'_{x,s}=\bigl(g_x(s+1)\otimes g_{x+1}(s+1)\bigr)\,U\,\bigl(g_x(s)^\dagger\otimes g_{x+1}(s)^\dagger\bigr).
$$
The same $g_x(s)$ must be used by the two gates meeting at that internal wire; for equivalence of homogeneous circuits, the transformed gates must all equal the same $U'$ up to irrelevant overall phases. With $G_s=\bigotimes_xg_x(s)$, successive layers then telescope to an initial and a final product of single-site basis changes. This preserves local-operator supports and the butterfly velocity when the inserted operators are transformed with those bases. For an isolated two-qubit gate, arbitrary independent unitaries on its four legs instead define ordinary local-unitary equivalence, which is classified by Cartan invariant data. Those data are necessary invariants of a circuit gauge, but equality of them alone is not sufficient for circuit gauge equivalence.

For $q=2$, numerical exploration over the full two-qubit parameter space suggests that the dual-unitary family and the kicked-XY gauge class together exhaust all gates passing the $T_1$ criterion; no proof exists. For $q>2$ there is not even a complete parametrization of two-qudit gates, and the known dual-unitary constructions (Gutkin et al.) rest on complex Hadamard matrices — $q\times q$ unitary matrices whose entries all have modulus $1/\sqrt{q}$ — whose own classification is an open problem in mathematics (Tadej and Życzkowski). Post-2020 work added a third mechanism: hierarchical generalized dual-unitary circuits, defined by the hierarchy of tensor-network identities of Yu, Wang, and Kos; Foligno, Kos, and Bertini proved that the entangling gates in this hierarchy also have $v_B=1$.

## Problem Statement

The problem is to characterize completely and rigorously the set of maximum-velocity circuits (MVCs): brickwork circuits built from a homogeneous two-site unitary gate $U$ on a local Hilbert space of dimension $q$ whose butterfly velocity is maximal, $v_B=1$.

Following the source's gate-level formulation, call a two-site gate admissible if the folded column transfer matrix $T_1$ (of dimension $q^4$, built from $U$ and $U^*$) has at least one additional unit-eigenvalue eigenoperator beyond the trivial ones guaranteed by unitarity, with nonzero overlap with the OTOC boundary vectors so that it can support $v_B=1$.

1. Base case, $q=2$. Prove or refute: every admissible two-qubit gate is either dual-unitary or circuit-gauge-equivalent, in the cancelling-wire sense defined above, to a kicked-XY gate $U[J]=(K\otimes\mathbb{1})\,e^{iJ\sigma_z\otimes\sigma_z}\,e^{i\frac{\pi}{4}\sigma_y\otimes\sigma_y}$ with $K=e^{i\frac{\pi}{4}\sigma_x}$. The existing support for this statement is numerical only.

2. General $q$. Determine the complete MVC gate set for arbitrary local dimension. The known dual-unitary and entangling hierarchical constructions, and the explicit kicked-XY family at $q=2$, are examples to be accounted for by the characterization. A higher-dimensional kicked-XY extension, if used, must be defined explicitly and its MVC property proved; the complete characterization must cover all MVC gates whether or not such an extension exists.

2.1 Complete characterization. For every integer $q\ge2$, give an exhaustive characterization of the homogeneous two-site unitary gates with $v_B=1$, with a proof of membership and completeness under the circuit gauge equivalence above. If the characterization is expressed using $T_1$ admissibility, establish that the stated conditions are sufficient for maximal butterfly velocity, or identify and prove any further conditions needed; the necessary spectral condition alone is not an exhaustion proof.

2.2 Further families. Exhibit and characterize any additional MVC families needed to exhaust the gate set in 2.1. A single gate outside a proposed list disproves that list's exhaustiveness, but does not by itself characterize the remaining gates or complete the general-$q$ classification.

2.3 Dual-unitary subset at $q>2$. Decide whether the complex-Hadamard-based dual-unitary constructions of Gutkin et al. (Ref. 12) exhaust the dual-unitary gates for $q>2$. This question does not require a complete classification of complex Hadamard matrices.

Both parts concern an exhaustive, rigorous description of MVC gates at arbitrary local dimension. A proof or counterexample in part 1 settles the two-qubit exhaustion conjecture; the complete general-$q$ characterization, including its further-family requirement, and the dual-unitary construction question are also required. Scalable alternatives to constructing and diagonalizing $T_n$ may aid this classification but are not separate required results.
