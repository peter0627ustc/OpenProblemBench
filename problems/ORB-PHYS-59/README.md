# ORB-PHYS-59: Complete classification of maximum-velocity quantum circuits: the two-qubit conjecture and the general local-dimension problem

Brickwork quantum circuits built from a two-site unitary gate $U$ on a local Hilbert space of dimension $q$ have a strict causal light cone of velocity 1, and the butterfly velocity $v_B$ of out-of-time-order correlators satisfies $v_B \le 1$. Claeys and Lamacraft (2020) called circuits with $v_B=1$ maximum-velocity circuits (MVCs) and reduced their study to the spectrum of a folded column transfer matrix $T_1$: maximal velocity requires $T_1$ to have additional unit-eigenvalue eigenoperators beyond those guaranteed by unitarity. They proved that dual-unitary circuits and a one-parameter family of kicked-XY circuits are MVCs — so dual unitarity is sufficient but not necessary — and found numerically that for $q=2$ every two-qubit gate passing the $T_1$ criterion is dual-unitary or gauge-equivalent to the kicked-XY family, leaving a rigorous classification open. This audit confirms the question is still open. Later work settled a converse for a different velocity (maximal entanglement velocity implies dual unitarity; Zhou and Harrow 2022), proved that all hierarchical generalized dual-unitary circuits also have $v_B=1$ and hence provide additional non-dual-unitary MVC mechanisms at every local dimension (Yu, Wang, Kos 2024; Foligno, Kos, Bertini 2024), and produced complete qubit-gate classifications under neighboring solvability criteria (Singh and Dooley 2026; Hübner and Kim 2026) — but no proof or refutation of the two-qubit MVC conjecture and no complete classification for general $q$ exists. The problem: rigorously and completely characterize the set of maximum-velocity circuits — prove or refute the $q=2$ conjecture, and determine whether the dual-unitary, kicked-XY-type, and hierarchical families exhaust all MVC mechanisms for arbitrary $q$.

## Background

A brickwork (brick-wall) quantum circuit is a discrete-time model of many-body dynamics: qudits — quantum systems with a $q$-dimensional local Hilbert space — sit on a one-dimensional chain, and each time step applies a two-site unitary gate $U$ on all even bonds and then on all odd bonds. Local causality implies that operators can spread by at most one site per time step, so every diagnostic of operator spreading has a strict light cone of velocity 1. The standard diagnostic of quantum information scrambling is the out-of-time-order correlator (OTOC) at infinite temperature, $C_{\alpha\beta}(x,t)=\langle\sigma_\alpha(0,t)\,\sigma_\beta(x,0)\,\sigma_\alpha(0,t)\,\sigma_\beta(x,0)\rangle$, where $\sigma_\alpha$ and $\sigma_\beta$ are local operators; the front of this correlator moves at the butterfly velocity $v_B\le 1$. Generic chaotic circuits have $v_B<1$ (for circuits with Haar-random gates the butterfly velocity was found, on average, to be $v_B=(q^2-1)/(q^2+1)$), so circuits with $v_B=1$ — maximum-velocity circuits (MVCs) — are extremal scramblers.

Claeys and Lamacraft expressed the long-time OTOC at a fixed distance $n$ behind the light-cone edge as a matrix element of powers of a column transfer matrix $T_n$: the $q^{4n}\times q^{4n}$ linear map on the space of $n$-site operators obtained by folding a column of $n$ gates, i.e. contracting a tensor network built from $U$ and its complex conjugate $U^*$. Unitarity of $U$ always supplies certain trivial unit-modulus eigenoperators of $T_n$, but these only reproduce the OTOC value outside the light cone. A maximal butterfly velocity $v_B=1$ requires at least one additional eigenoperator of the shallowest transfer matrix $T_1$ with eigenvalue one and nonzero overlap with the boundary vectors that carry the inserted operators. This turns the physical question into a concrete gate-level classification problem: find all two-site unitary gates $U$ for which $T_1$ acquires such an additional unit eigenvalue.

Two mechanisms are known from the source paper. First, dual-unitary gates: $U$ is unitary and remains unitary after a space-time swap, meaning the matrix $\tilde U$ with elements $\tilde U_{ab,cd}=U_{db,ca}$ (the gate read as an evolution along the spatial direction) is also unitary. Every two-qubit gate is, up to single-qubit unitaries on its four legs, of the form $\exp[i(J_x\sigma_x\otimes\sigma_x+J_y\sigma_y\otimes\sigma_y+J_z\sigma_z\otimes\sigma_z)]$ with real Cartan entangling angles $J_x,J_y,J_z$, and dual unitarity fixes two of the three angles at $\pi/4$. Dual-unitary circuits have dynamical correlations only on the light cone, are exactly solvable, and are MVCs at every local dimension $q$. Second, kicked-XY circuits, $U[J]=(K\otimes\mathbb{1})\,e^{iJ\sigma_z\otimes\sigma_z}\,e^{i\frac{\pi}{4}\sigma_y\otimes\sigma_y}$ with $K=e^{i\frac{\pi}{4}\sigma_x}$, a Trotterization of a kicked XY spin chain: these have $v_B=1$ for all $J$ yet are not dual-unitary except at $|J|=\pi/4$, so dual unitarity is not necessary for maximal velocity. Gauge equivalence — dressing the legs of the gate by single-qubit unitaries that cancel between adjacent gates — leaves the circuit dynamics unchanged and defines the equivalence classes entering the classification; equivalence of two-qubit gates is decided by their local invariants, i.e. the Cartan coordinates above, which are unchanged by such dressings.

For $q=2$, numerical exploration over the full two-qubit parameter space suggests that the dual-unitary family and the kicked-XY gauge class together exhaust all gates passing the $T_1$ criterion; no proof exists. For $q>2$ there is not even a complete parametrization of two-qudit gates, and the known dual-unitary constructions (Gutkin et al.) rest on complex Hadamard matrices — $q\times q$ unitary matrices whose entries all have modulus $1/\sqrt{q}$ — whose own classification is an open problem in mathematics (Tadej and Życzkowski). Post-2020 work added a third mechanism: hierarchical generalized dual-unitary circuits (Yu, Wang, Kos), whose space-time swapped gate is proportional to an isometry onto a subspace rather than a unitary; Foligno, Kos, and Bertini proved that these also have $v_B=1$.

## Problem Statement

The problem is to characterize completely and rigorously the set of maximum-velocity circuits (MVCs): brickwork circuits built from a homogeneous two-site unitary gate $U$ on a local Hilbert space of dimension $q$ whose butterfly velocity is maximal, $v_B=1$.

Following the source's gate-level formulation, call a two-site gate admissible if the folded column transfer matrix $T_1$ (of dimension $q^4$, built from $U$ and $U^*$) has at least one additional unit-eigenvalue eigenoperator beyond the trivial ones guaranteed by unitarity, with nonzero overlap with the OTOC boundary vectors so that it can support $v_B=1$.

1. Base case, $q=2$. Prove or refute: every admissible two-qubit gate is either dual-unitary or gauge-equivalent (up to single-qubit dressings of its legs) to a kicked-XY gate $U[J]=(K\otimes\mathbb{1})\,e^{iJ\sigma_z\otimes\sigma_z}\,e^{i\frac{\pi}{4}\sigma_y\otimes\sigma_y}$ with $K=e^{i\frac{\pi}{4}\sigma_x}$. The existing support for this statement is numerical only.

2. General $q$. Determine the mechanism landscape of MVCs for arbitrary local dimension: establish whether the union of (a) dual-unitary gates, (b) kicked-XY-type gates, and (c) hierarchical generalized dual-unitary gates (proven to reach $v_B=1$) exhausts the admissible gates at each $q$; exhibit and characterize any further families; in particular, decide whether admissible gates exist outside all three classes, and whether the complex-Hadamard-based constructions exhaust even the dual-unitary subset for $q>2$.

The two parts share one scientific objective — an exhaustive, rigorous description of the admissible/MVC gate set — with part 1 its base case and part 2 its generalization; part 1 is settled either by an exhaustion proof or by an explicit admissible two-qubit gate provably outside both classes, which would refute the conjecture and itself constitute a new MVC mechanism. Supporting computational issues raised in the same source — scalable alternatives to the exponentially costly construction and diagonalization of $T_n$ — are means toward this classification, not separate required deliverables; the completeness of the complex Hadamard matrix classification is a neighboring open mathematical problem that bounds one construction route but is not itself part of the completion boundary.

The verification contract below evaluates answers to this statement. It does not narrow or redefine the research question.

Known solving difficulties:

- For $q=2$ the $T_1$ criterion imposes coupled nonlinear (trigonometric) constraints on the full two-qubit parameter space; an exhaustion proof requires a complete case analysis with many degenerate branches, quotiented by the gauge orbit via local invariants (Cartan coordinates), and measure-zero exceptional families can escape numerics.
- The classification cannot piggyback on dual-unitary parametrizations: the known MVC mechanisms are qualitatively different (the space-time swapped gate of a hierarchical gate is an isometry onto a subspace, not a unitary), so new structural arguments are needed to rule out — or find — further mechanisms.
- For $q>2$ no exhaustive parametrization of two-qudit gates exists, and completeness statements about Hadamard-based dual-unitary constructions are entangled with the open classification of complex Hadamard matrices.
- The transfer matrix $T_n$ has dimension $q^{4n}$, so brute-force numerical exploration of the criterion becomes exponentially expensive, and a rigorous computer-assisted enumeration requires interval-arithmetic or symbolic-elimination covering arguments.
- Exhibiting a genuinely new MVC family requires both constructing unit-eigenoperators of $T_1$ with the required boundary overlaps (to establish $v_B=1$) and proving inequivalence to all known families via local invariants.

## Current Progress

The primary source (arXiv:2003.01133, Conclusions) poses the question — "is it possible to completely characterize the set of MVCs, starting with the case $q=2$?" — states the $T_1$ unit-eigenoperator condition as a necessary condition for $v_B=1$, and presents the $q=2$ exhaustion claim ("either dual unitary or gauge-equivalent to the kicked XY models") explicitly as numerical evidence, not a theorem. Attribution to Claeys and Lamacraft (Phys. Rev. Research 2, 033032 (2020)). The question of qualitatively different MVC families for general $q$ is addressed in part by the 2024 hierarchical-circuit results below.

Zhou and Harrow (Phys. Rev. B 106, L201104 (2022); arXiv:2204.10341, https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10341) proved a converse rigidity result for a different velocity: a maximal entanglement velocity ($v_E=1$, the rate of entanglement growth after a quench) implies dual-unitarity, robustly — approximate maximality implies approximate dual-unitarity. This settles the entanglement-velocity analogue but not the butterfly-velocity classification: kicked-XY circuits and, later, hierarchical circuits show that $v_B=1$ does not force dual-unitarity.

Yu, Wang, and Kos (Quantum 8, 1260 (2024); DOI: 10.22331/q-2024-02-20-1260, https://doi.org/10.22331/q-2024-02-20-1260) introduced a hierarchical generalization of dual unitarity in which the first level is dual-unitarity and higher levels impose progressively weaker conditions on the space-time swapped gate; they gave a complete parametrization of the level-2 (and level-3) qubit gate families — level-2 qubit gates are local dressings of the Ising entangler $e^{i\frac{\pi}{4}\sigma_z\otimes\sigma_z}$ with constrained single-qubit gates — together with generalized-CNOT and Clifford-based constructions for local dimensions $q>2$.

Foligno, Kos, and Bertini (Phys. Rev. Lett. 132, 250402 (2024); arXiv:2312.02940, https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.02940) proved that all levels of this hierarchy have $v_B=1$, by explicitly constructing nontrivial fixed points of the transfer matrix $T_1$ — exactly the criterion of the source paper — with the boundary overlaps needed for a nonvanishing on-cone OTOC. Consequence: non-dual-unitary maximum-velocity circuits provably exist for every local dimension, so dual-unitary and kicked-XY-type gates do not exhaust MVC mechanisms for $q>2$; this partially resolves the source's "other families" sub-question in the affirmative. A possible connection, not established in the cited literature: the qubit level-2 gates all carry the same Cartan entangling point $(0,0,\pi/4)$ as the $J=0$ kicked-XY gate, so they plausibly lie inside the kicked-XY gauge class and do not refute the $q=2$ conjecture; the literature itself has not made this connection, and the observation is reported here as a scope remark only.

Singh and Dooley (arXiv:2605.23622 (2026), preprint) proved a neighboring rigidity result under an information-transfer criterion: for qubit chains, the one-qudit channel $\Phi_1$ governing state evolution along the light cone has peripheral (unit-modulus) eigenvalues only if the gate is dual-unitary, whereas for two-qudit subsystems or local dimension $d\ge 3$ non-dual-unitary gates can have peripheral spectra. This concerns lossless information transfer along the light cone, not the MVC criterion, and does not settle the classification.

Hübner and Kim (arXiv:2606.12538 (2026), preprint) developed a systematic theory of "influence-solvability" (circuits whose influence matrix — the self-generated bath of a subsystem — is a matrix product state of finite bond dimension) and completely classified the $\chi=1$ (Markovian) case for quantum gates of local dimension $d=2$: such gates are either dual-unitary or control gates satisfying a level-2-hierarchy-like condition depending on the initial state, with further new solvable circuits at $d\ge 3$. This is a complete classification under a different solvability criterion; the paper does not treat butterfly velocity or the MVC question.

The construction and local-equivalence theory of dual-unitary gates themselves advanced (Rather, Aravinda, and Lakshminarayan, PRX Quantum 3, 040331 (2022); Borsi and Pozsgay, Phys. Rev. B 106, 014302 (2022); Rampp, Rather, and Claeys, SciPost Phys. 18, 182 (2025), on biunitary and multi-unitary generalizations), but none of these claims a complete classification of dual-unitary gates for $q>2$, let alone of MVCs. The complex Hadamard matrix classification (Tadej and Życzkowski, Open Syst. Inf. Dyn. 13, 133 (2006)) remains open, leaving the Hadamard-based route to exhausting even the dual-unitary subset of MVCs at $q>2$ contingent on an open mathematics problem.

No scalable algorithmic alternative to exhaustive construction and diagonalization of $T_n$ (dimension $q^{4n}$) for deciding the $|\lambda|=1$ eigenoperator condition at larger $q$ is described in the literature discussed here; the solvable-circuits program (dual-unitary, hierarchical, biunitary, and influence-solvable families) bypasses the transfer-matrix search by imposing structure by hand rather than deciding it in general.

The $q=2$ MVC classification conjecture and a complete general-$q$ classification remain open in the literature considered at curation. The surviving open core is precise and nonempty: the rigorous $q=2$ classification (prove or refute dual-unitary $\cup$ kicked-XY-gauge exhaustion of the $T_1$-admissible gates) and the general-$q$ mechanism landscape.

## Scientific Significance

Affected-field significance: `medium`.

Directly affects the theory of exactly solvable models of non-equilibrium quantum many-body dynamics. A complete MVC classification would fix the exact boundary of the class of quantum circuits with maximal butterfly velocity — determining which nonintegrable circuits admit exact OTOC and correlation-function solutions, whether $v_B=1$ always reflects hidden space-time (dual-unitary or biunitary-like) structure, and whether the known dual-unitary, kicked-XY, and hierarchical families are the only maximal-scrambling building blocks available for exact solvability. The impact on the broader fields of quantum thermalization and scrambling is real but mostly indirect: it changes which models can be solved exactly and how new solvable models are constructed, rather than the phenomenology of generic systems.
