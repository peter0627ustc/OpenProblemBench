# ORB-MATH-25: The strong Weinstein conjecture: existence of null-homologous Reeb links on closed contact manifolds

Audit of the open question recorded from Deschamps–Maier–Stalljohann, 'On the contact type conjecture for exact magnetic systems' (arXiv:2508.01113), whose Question 1 asks whether the Reeb flow of every closed contact manifold admits at least one null-homologous periodic orbit, presented in the paper as a strengthening of the Weinstein conjecture (its Remark 1.10 also refers to 'the strong Weinstein conjecture' by name, citing Geiges–Zehmisch). Inspection of the primary sources shows that the strong Weinstein conjecture in the literature (formulated through Abbas–Cieliebak–Hofer and stated definitionally by Geiges–Zehmisch) asks for a null-homologous Reeb LINK — a finite collection of periodic orbits, possibly multiply covered, whose total homology class vanishes — and that the strictly stronger single-orbit version of the source paper is refuted by a classical published computation: on the 3-torus with the standard tight contact form $\cos\theta\,dx+\sin\theta\,dy$, every periodic Reeb orbit represents a nonzero class $(0,a,b)$ in the torsion-free group $H_1(T^3;\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}^3$. The record is therefore aligned with the standard authoritative link formulation: for every contact form on a closed contact manifold, does there exist a null-homologous Reeb link? This conjecture, which implies the still-open higher-dimensional Weinstein conjecture, remains open in general; it is confirmed for planar contact structures, subcritically Stein fillable and other cobordism classes (i.e. boundaries of restricted classes of filling symplectic manifolds), and certain connected sums. The magnetic-system application that motivated the source paper is documented as context.

## Background

A contact form on a closed (compact, without boundary) smooth manifold $M$ of dimension $2n+1$ is a 1-form $\alpha$ such that $\alpha\wedge(d\alpha)^n$ is a volume form; its kernel $\xi=\ker\alpha$ is a contact structure. The Reeb vector field $R_\alpha$ of $\alpha$ is uniquely determined by $\iota_{R_\alpha}d\alpha=0$ and $\alpha(R_\alpha)=1$; its periodic orbits (Reeb orbits) are the central dynamical object of contact topology. A closed curve in $M$ is called null-homologous if it represents the zero class in the singular homology group $H_1(M;\mathbb{Z})$.

Weinstein (1978) conjectured that the Reeb flow of any contact form on a closed contact manifold has at least one periodic orbit. Hofer (1993) proved this for closed overtwisted 3-manifolds (overtwisted being the 'flexible' class of contact structures, characterized by containing a model disk) and for tight (i.e. not overtwisted) $S^3$, in both cases through a contractible — hence null-homologous — orbit; Taubes (2007), via Seiberg–Witten gauge theory (an elliptic equation theory counting solutions of the Seiberg–Witten equations, imported from four-manifold topology), proved it for all closed contact 3-manifolds, but with no constraint on the homology class of the orbit produced. In dimensions at least 5 the Weinstein conjecture itself remains open in general.

A Reeb link for a contact form $\alpha$ is a finite collection of periodic orbits of $R_\alpha$ whose components may be taken with positive integer multiplicities (i.e. multiply covered); the link is null-homologous when the sum of the homology classes of its components, counted with multiplicity, is zero in $H_1(M;\mathbb{Z})$. The strong Weinstein conjecture — introduced through the work of Abbas–Cieliebak–Hofer on planar contact structures (contact structures supported by an open book, i.e. a decomposition of the manifold into surfaces ('pages') that spiral around an embedded binding link, all pages here being planar) and stated definitionally by Geiges–Zehmisch — predicts that for every contact structure $\xi$ on a closed manifold and every contact form $\alpha$ defining $\xi$, there exists a null-homologous Reeb link. It implies the ordinary Weinstein conjecture, and Suhr–Zehmisch explicitly note that it is not covered by Taubes' theorem.

The link formulation is not a technicality: the seemingly stronger single-orbit version, asking for one null-homologous periodic orbit, is false in this generality. On $T^3=(\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z})\times(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})^2$ with the standard tight contact form $\lambda_1=\cos\theta\,dx+\sin\theta\,dy$, the Reeb field is $R=\cos\theta\,\partial_x+\sin\theta\,\partial_y$; every torus $\{\theta\}$ is invariant, periodic orbits exist exactly for $\tan\theta\in\mathbb{Q}\cup\{\infty\}$, and every embedded periodic orbit represents a class $(0,a,b)\in H_1(T^3;\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}^3$ with $a,b$ coprime — never zero, and never zero under multiple covers, since the group is torsion-free (this computation is due to Hutchings–Sullivan in their calculation for $T^3$ using embedded contact homology, a Floer-theoretic homology theory for contact 3-manifolds whose chain complex is generated by finite collections of Reeb orbits). Null-homologous Reeb links do exist on this example: an orbit of class $(0,a,b)$ paired with an orbit of class $(0,-a,-b)$, exactly the 'orbit sets' that generate the degree-zero embedded contact homology chain complex.

The question carries a mechanical-system motivation, which is how it surfaced in the source paper. Deschamps–Maier–Stalljohann (2025) confirm, for their class of 'magnetic systems of strong geodesic type' (exact magnetic systems $(M,g,d\alpha)$ possessing a distinguished null-homologous periodic magnetic geodesic of maximal $\alpha$-length), a 1990s question known as the contact type conjecture: that the energy hypersurfaces of an exact magnetic system below the lowest Mañé critical value (the energy threshold below which the Lagrangian action functional behaves non-compactly) fail to be of contact type. Their Corollary H builds such systems on any closed contact manifold whose Reeb form has a null-homologous periodic orbit, so the homological theory of Reeb orbits directly feeds the magnetic conjecture.

## Problem Statement

The strong Weinstein conjecture (standard formulation). Let $(M,\xi)$ be a closed cooriented contact manifold (cooriented meaning the hyperplane field $\xi$ is globally expressible as $\ker\alpha$ for a 1-form $\alpha$ on $M$, which fixes an orientation of $\xi$). Is it true that for every contact form $\alpha$ with $\ker\alpha=\xi$ there exists a null-homologous Reeb link — a finite collection $\{\gamma_i\}_{i=1}^k$ of periodic orbits of the Reeb vector field $R_\alpha$ (the unique vector field determined by $\alpha(R_\alpha)=1$ and $\iota_{R_\alpha}d\alpha=0$), taken with positive integer multiplicities $m_i$, such that $\sum_{i=1}^k m_i[\gamma_i]=0$ in $H_1(M;\mathbb{Z})$?

The conjecture is open in general, in every dimension $\ge 3$, and it implies the Weinstein conjecture in all dimensions, which is itself open in dimensions at least 5. A strictly stronger single-orbit variant — Question 1 of Deschamps–Maier–Stalljohann (arXiv:2508.01113), asking whether every such Reeb flow has at least one null-homologous periodic orbit — is refuted by the classical example $(T^3,\cos\theta\,dx+\sin\theta\,dy)$ described in the background (all orbits represent nonzero classes in a torsion-free homology group) and must not be confused with the conjecture above; on classes where a single null-homologous or contractible orbit is known to exist, such as the Liouville case of the Geiges–Zehmisch cobordism theorem, the conjecture holds a fortiori.

The verification contract below evaluates answers to this statement. It does not narrow or redefine the research question.

Known solving difficulties:

- Any proof must at least overcome the long-standing obstructions to the higher-dimensional Weinstein conjecture, since the strong conjecture implies it; no general tool exists for arbitrary (non-fillable, non-planar) contact manifolds in dimensions at least 5.
- All known positive results depend on fillability or cobordism hypotheses via pseudoholomorphic-curve methods (Hofer's technique on the symplectization, i.e. the cylinder $\mathbb{R}\times M$ with the canonical symplectic form built from the contact form; Geiges–Zehmisch's capped cobordisms; Suhr–Zehmisch's polyfold arguments, a polyfold being an infinite-dimensional generalization of a manifold designed to make transversality rigorous in symplectic field theory), or in dimension 3 on Seiberg–Witten and embedded contact homology techniques that currently produce orbits without homological control of the kind needed.
- The link requirement is essential and subtle: Reeb orbit classes can be nonzero and even non-torsion (as on $T^3$), so a proof must produce cancelling combinations of distinct orbits rather than a single orbit; the orbit-set bookkeeping that handles this (embedded contact homology) is presently available only in dimension 3.
- A counterexample would need a Reeb flow on a closed contact manifold whose periodic-orbit homology classes admit no vanishing positive-integer combination — for instance classes confined to an open half-space of $H_1(M;\mathbb{R})$ — whereas the known integrable (Morse–Bott, i.e. degenerate with critical submanifolds, or Besse, i.e. all orbits periodic) constructions produce torsion fiber classes whose multiples are null-homologous, or cancelling pairs of classes as on $T^3$.
- The statement quantifies over all contact forms defining the structure, including degenerate forms, for which the usual perturbation-based existence machinery does not directly apply.
- Progress is fragmented across dimension regimes (dimension 3 gauge-theoretic versus dimensions at least 5 holomorphic-curve methods) with different technical obstructions, and no uniform argument is in sight.

## Current Progress

- Status: `ready`

Source fidelity: the candidate originates from the open-questions list of Deschamps–Maier–Stalljohann (arXiv:2508.01113, v3 April 2026, no journal reference yet), whose Question 1 asks whether the Reeb flow of every closed contact manifold admits at least one null-homologous periodic orbit, and which the paper introduces as a strengthening of the Weinstein conjecture (its Remark 1.10 separately refers to 'the strong Weinstein conjecture' as a named statement, citing Geiges–Zehmisch, doi:10.1017/s0305004112000163). Inspection of the primary sources shows that the strong Weinstein conjecture in the literature — introduced via Abbas–Cieliebak–Hofer (doi:10.4171/cmh/34) and stated definitionally by Geiges–Zehmisch ('we say that $(M,\xi)$ satisfies the strong Weinstein conjecture if for every $\alpha$ defining $\xi$ there exists a nullhomologous Reeb link'), and similarly by Suhr–Zehmisch (doi:10.1016/j.aim.2016.06.030) — asks for a null-homologous finite collection of Reeb orbits, not a single orbit. The LKM paraphrase faithfully reports the paper's Question 1; the paper does not explicitly define the named conjecture, and reading Question 1 as its formulation would be an imprecision — this record therefore states the standard link version as the named conjecture and records the correction explicitly.

The single-orbit reading is refuted by classical external evidence, not by any new construction of this audit: on $(T^3,\cos\theta\,dx+\sin\theta\,dy)$ with the standard tight contact structure, every periodic Reeb orbit lies in a class $(0,a,b)\in H_1(T^3;\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}^3$ with $a,b$ coprime, hence nonzero with all its multiples, as computed in Hutchings' lecture notes (arXiv:1303.5789, Section 4.2, following Hutchings–Sullivan, Geom. Topol. 10 (2006), doi:10.2140/gt.2006.10.169); the degree-zero embedded contact homology generators there are precisely the two-orbit sets $e_{a,b}e_{-a,-b}$, i.e. null-homologous Reeb links. Consequently the paper's Question 1, taken literally as a question about every closed contact manifold, has a negative answer, and its positive-class discussion in the paper (overtwisted 3-manifolds and tight $S^3$ via Hofer, plus higher-dimensional classes) is consistent with never having claimed the torus case.

Positive classes for the link-formulation conjecture: Hofer (1993, doi:10.1007/BF01232679) obtained contractible orbits for overtwisted closed 3-manifolds and tight $S^3$; Abbas–Cieliebak–Hofer (2005, doi:10.4171/cmh/34) established the strong conjecture for planar contact structures in dimension three; Geiges–Zehmisch (2012, doi:10.1017/s0305004112000163) proved it for contact manifolds arising as concave boundaries of cappable semipositive symplectic cobordisms — including contact-type hypersurfaces in subcritical Stein manifolds and in cotangent bundles over products $Q\times S^1$ — with a single contractible orbit in their Liouville case; Suhr–Zehmisch (2017, doi:10.1016/j.aim.2016.06.030) generalized this via polyfold technology to concave boundaries of cobordisms admitting essential local foliations by holomorphic spheres; Geiges–Zehmisch (2016, doi:10.1093/imrn/rnv124) obtained contractible orbits for non-trivial contact connected sums under nontrivial fundamental group or torsion-free homology. The source paper lists several further higher-dimensional classes; these were not independently inspected during this audit.

Status and adjacent results: the ordinary Weinstein conjecture was proved in dimension 3 by Taubes (2007) with no homological constraint, and Suhr–Zehmisch state explicitly that the strong (link) version 'is not covered by Taubes' result'. Acu (arXiv:1710.07724) extended the planar result to iterated planar open books for the ordinary conjecture. arXiv title/abstract sweeps for 'strong Weinstein conjecture' and 'null-homologous Reeb' over 2012–2026 surface no paper claiming the link conjecture in general and no counterexample to it; the most recent treatment found (the 2025/2026 source paper itself) still treats the homologically constrained statement as unresolved. Coverage is strongest via the Geiges–Zehmisch–Suhr lineage and embedded contact homology; no systematic MathSciNet/zbMATH forward-citation pass was possible with the available routes, so a closure claim hidden in a journal-only paper cannot be fully excluded, though the 2026 source paper's own literature discussion supports openness.

Dependency in the source paper (arXiv:2508.01113): Deschamps–Maier–Stalljohann's Corollary H constructs, on any closed contact manifold for which a single null-homologous periodic Reeb orbit exists, an infinite-dimensional family of Riemannian metrics making the magnetic system $(M,g,d\alpha)$ of strong geodesic type and thereby confirming the contact type conjecture for those systems. Since the single-orbit property fails on the standard tight $T^3$ (see entry 2), this corollary is non-vacuous exactly on classes where a single orbit is known (e.g. the Liouville case of the Geiges–Zehmisch theorem); the genuinely open problem behind it, corrected to the literature's formulation, is the link version stated in this record.

## Scientific Significance

Affected-field significance: `high`.

A proof would directly change core knowledge in contact topology and Hamiltonian dynamics: it would imply the Weinstein conjecture in all dimensions — open in general in dimensions at least 5 — and would additionally force, on every closed contact manifold and for every contact form, a homologically trivial collection of closed Reeb orbits, a constraint currently available only for planar, fillable/cobordism, and connected-sum classes. The impact is direct, with downstream consequences for symplectic capacity-type invariants defined through Reeb link actions (Geiges–Zehmisch derive one from their theorem) and for magnetic-system dynamics, since the source paper's Corollary H converts single-orbit instances into infinite-dimensional families of exact magnetic systems resolving the contact type conjecture. A counterexample would equally reshape the field by exhibiting the first closed contact manifold whose contact forms can avoid all homologically trivial orbit collections, showing Taubes-type existence cannot be homologically strengthened.
