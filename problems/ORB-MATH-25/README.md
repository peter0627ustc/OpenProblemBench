# ORB-MATH-25: The strong Weinstein conjecture: existence of null-homologous Reeb links on closed contact manifolds


This task asks whether the strong Weinstein conjecture holds: for every contact form $\alpha$ on a closed cooriented contact manifold $(M,\xi)$ with $\ker\alpha=\xi$, does there exist a null-homologous Reeb link — a nonempty finite collection of periodic orbits of the Reeb vector field $R_\alpha$, taken with positive integer multiplicities, whose total homology class vanishes in $H_1(M;\mathbb{Z})$?

## Background

A contact form on a closed (compact, without boundary) smooth manifold $M$ of dimension $2n+1$ is a 1-form $\alpha$ such that $\alpha\wedge(d\alpha)^n$ is a volume form; its kernel $\xi=\ker\alpha$ is a contact structure. The Reeb vector field $R_\alpha$ of $\alpha$ is uniquely determined by $\iota_{R_\alpha}d\alpha=0$ and $\alpha(R_\alpha)=1$; its periodic orbits (Reeb orbits) are the central dynamical object of contact topology. A closed curve in $M$ is called null-homologous if it represents the zero class in the singular homology group $H_1(M;\mathbb{Z})$.

Weinstein (1978) conjectured that the Reeb flow of any contact form on a closed contact manifold has at least one periodic orbit. Hofer (1993) proved this for closed overtwisted 3-manifolds (overtwisted being the 'flexible' class of contact structures, characterized by containing a model disk) and for tight (i.e. not overtwisted) $S^3$, in both cases through a contractible — hence null-homologous — orbit; Taubes (2007), via Seiberg–Witten gauge theory (an elliptic equation theory counting solutions of the Seiberg–Witten equations, imported from four-manifold topology), proved it for all closed contact 3-manifolds, but with no constraint on the homology class of the orbit produced. In dimensions at least 5 the Weinstein conjecture itself remains open in general.

A Reeb link for a contact form $\alpha$ is a nonempty finite collection of periodic orbits of $R_\alpha$ whose components may be taken with positive integer multiplicities (i.e. multiply covered); the link is null-homologous when the sum of the homology classes of its components, counted with multiplicity, is zero in $H_1(M;\mathbb{Z})$. The strong Weinstein conjecture — introduced through the work of Abbas–Cieliebak–Hofer on planar contact structures (contact structures supported by an open book, i.e. a decomposition of the manifold into surfaces ('pages') that spiral around an embedded binding link, all pages here being planar) and stated definitionally by Geiges–Zehmisch — predicts that for every contact structure $\xi$ on a closed manifold and every contact form $\alpha$ defining $\xi$, there exists a null-homologous Reeb link. It implies the ordinary Weinstein conjecture, and Suhr–Zehmisch explicitly note that it is not covered by Taubes' theorem.

The link formulation is not a technicality: the seemingly stronger single-orbit version, asking for one null-homologous periodic orbit, is false in this generality. On $T^3=(\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z})\times(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})^2$ with the standard tight contact form $\lambda_1=\cos\theta\,dx+\sin\theta\,dy$, the Reeb field is $R=\cos\theta\,\partial_x+\sin\theta\,\partial_y$; every torus $\{\theta\}$ is invariant, periodic orbits exist exactly for $\tan\theta\in\mathbb{Q}\cup\{\infty\}$, and every embedded periodic orbit represents a class $(0,a,b)\in H_1(T^3;\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}^3$ with $a,b$ coprime — never zero, and never zero under multiple covers, since the group is torsion-free (this computation is due to Hutchings–Sullivan in their calculation for $T^3$ using embedded contact homology, a Floer-theoretic homology theory for contact 3-manifolds whose chain complex is generated by finite collections of Reeb orbits). Null-homologous Reeb links do exist on this example: an orbit of class $(0,a,b)$ paired with an orbit of class $(0,-a,-b)$, exactly the 'orbit sets' that generate the degree-zero embedded contact homology chain complex.

The question carries a mechanical-system motivation. Deschamps–Maier–Stalljohann (2025) confirm, for their class of 'magnetic systems of strong geodesic type' (exact magnetic systems $(M,g,d\alpha)$ possessing a distinguished null-homologous periodic magnetic geodesic of maximal $\alpha$-length), a 1990s question known as the contact type conjecture: that the energy hypersurfaces of an exact magnetic system below the lowest Mañé critical value (the energy threshold below which the Lagrangian action functional behaves non-compactly) fail to be of contact type. Their Corollary H builds such systems on any closed contact manifold whose Reeb form has a null-homologous periodic orbit, so the homological theory of Reeb orbits directly feeds the magnetic conjecture.

## Problem Statement

The strong Weinstein conjecture (standard formulation). Let $(M,\xi)$ be a closed cooriented contact manifold (cooriented meaning the hyperplane field $\xi$ is globally expressible as $\ker\alpha$ for a 1-form $\alpha$ on $M$, which fixes an orientation of $\xi$). Is it true that for every contact form $\alpha$ with $\ker\alpha=\xi$ there exists a null-homologous Reeb link — a nonempty finite collection $\{\gamma_i\}_{i=1}^k$ of periodic orbits of the Reeb vector field $R_\alpha$ (the unique vector field determined by $\alpha(R_\alpha)=1$ and $\iota_{R_\alpha}d\alpha=0$), taken with positive integer multiplicities $m_i$ ($k\ge 1$), such that $\sum_{i=1}^k m_i[\gamma_i]=0$ in $H_1(M;\mathbb{Z})$?

The conjecture is open in general, in every dimension $\ge 3$, and it implies the Weinstein conjecture in all dimensions, which is itself open in dimensions at least 5. A strictly stronger single-orbit variant — Question 1 of Deschamps–Maier–Stalljohann (arXiv:2508.01113), asking whether every such Reeb flow has at least one null-homologous periodic orbit — is refuted by the classical example $(T^3,\cos\theta\,dx+\sin\theta\,dy)$ described in the background (all orbits represent nonzero classes in a torsion-free homology group) and must not be confused with the conjecture above; on classes where a single null-homologous or contractible orbit is known to exist, such as the Liouville case of the Geiges–Zehmisch cobordism theorem, the conjecture holds a fortiori.
