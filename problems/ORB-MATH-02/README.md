# ORB-MATH-02: Griffiths' 1969 positive-polynomial problem at the level of Chern forms: pointwise (weak) positivity of Schur forms for Griffiths-positive vector bundles

For a Griffiths-positive Hermitian holomorphic vector bundle $(E,h)$, Chern–Weil theory produces closed differential forms $c_k(E,h)$ representing the Chern classes. Since 1969 Griffiths has asked which weighted-homogeneous symmetric polynomials in these Chern forms are pointwise nonnegative as differential forms, and since Fulton–Lazarsfeld (1983) the expected answer is the form-level analogue of their cohomological description: precisely the nonnegative combinations of Schur polynomials with positive discriminant. Despite substantial 2020–2026 progress — pointwise Gysin and push-forward formulae, positivity for the stronger Nakano and dual Nakano curvature conditions, a reduction to an open statement on double mixed discriminants of positive linear maps, and a complete affirmative answer when the rank and the base dimension are both three (Wan, 2026) — the question remains open for Griffiths-positive bundles of general rank over bases of general dimension, as does the strong-positivity counterpart and any complete characterization of the form-level positive cone. This record audits the literature status of that problem in its natural, unrestricted generality.

## Background

A Hermitian holomorphic vector bundle $(E,h)$ over a complex manifold $X$ carries a unique Chern connection, whose curvature $\Theta_E$ is a $(1,1)$-form with values in the endomorphisms of $E$. The bundle is Griffiths-positive (resp. Griffiths semipositive) if for every point $x \in X$, every tangent vector $v \in T_xX$, and every vector $\xi \in E_x$, the Hermitian form $\langle i\Theta_E(v,\bar v)\xi,\xi\rangle$ is positive (resp. nonnegative); this is the differential-geometric analogue of ampleness. Stronger curvature conditions include Nakano positivity and dual Nakano positivity, where $i\Theta_E$ is positive as a Hermitian form on all of $T_xX \otimes E_x$ or on $T_xX \otimes E_x^*$, respectively. Via Chern–Weil theory the curvature determines closed $(k,k)$-forms $c_k(E,h)$ — the Chern forms — representing the Chern classes $c_k(E)$ in de Rham cohomology, and more generally $P(c_1(E,h),\dots,c_r(E,h))$ for any symmetric weighted-homogeneous polynomial $P$.

Griffiths (1969) introduced two nested notions of pointwise positivity for $(p,p)$-forms: a form is strongly positive if it lies in the cone generated by wedge products $\frac{i}{2}dz_{j_1}\wedge d\bar z_{j_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\frac{i}{2}dz_{j_p}\wedge d\bar z_{j_p}$ in local coordinates, and weakly positive if it pairs nonnegatively against every strongly positive $(n-p,n-p)$-form of complementary degree. Strong positivity implies weak positivity, and the two coincide only for $p \leq 1$; on bundles the strong cone is the natural target because wedge products of strongly positive forms are again strongly positive. Griffiths asked which polynomials $P$ give pointwise (weakly or strongly) nonnegative forms $P(c(E,h))$ whenever $(E,h)$ is Griffiths-positive — the characteristic-function analogue of the classical fact that the curvature form of a positive line bundle is a positive $(1,1)$-form.

A complete answer is known cohomologically. The Schur polynomials $s_\lambda$ are the determinantal symmetric polynomials indexed by partitions $\lambda$ (given by the Jacobi–Trudi identities in the complete symmetric functions of the Chern roots); they form a linear basis of the weighted-homogeneous symmetric polynomials. Fulton and Lazarsfeld (1983) proved that on a smooth projective variety, a polynomial is numerically positive on every ample vector bundle (positive degree on all positive-dimensional subvarieties) if and only if it is a nonnegative linear combination of Schur polynomials $s_\lambda$ over partitions with positive discriminant, the combinatorial condition $\sum_i(\lambda'_i-\lambda_i) > 0$ on the partition and its conjugate. This is a statement in cohomology and says nothing about the pointwise behavior of the differential forms $s_\lambda(c(E,h))$, because the techniques it relies on — notably the splitting principle and degeneration to Schubert cycles — have no pointwise analogue: the Chern forms of a bundle cannot be simultaneously diagonalized as differential forms. Diverio and Fagioli (2020) describe Griffiths' question explicitly as the pointwise, hermitianized version of the Fulton–Lazarsfeld theorem.

The pointwise question is nontrivial already in low degree: weak positivity of $c_1(E,h)$ is immediate, weak positivity of $c_2(E,h)$ holds in rank two classically and in any rank by a reduction recorded by Fagioli (2020), and weak positivity of $c_3(E,h)$ for Griffiths-positive bundles was established only in 2026 by Wan, whose proof passes through a purely operator-theoretic statement — positivity of a double mixed discriminant (a multilinear determinant-like invariant) for positive linear maps between spaces of $3\times 3$ complex matrices — that Finski (2020) had identified as the obstruction in general. Wan (2026) also proved that all Schur forms of a Griffiths-positive bundle of rank three over a complex threefold are weakly positive, giving a complete affirmative answer to Griffiths' question in the case where both the rank and the dimension equal three. For the stronger Nakano and dual Nakano curvature conditions, Finski (2020) proved that all Schur forms are positive as forms, and Wan (2023) extended this to his intermediate notions of strongly decomposable positivity of types I and II. Complementing these positive results, Finski (2020) proved inverse-problem negatives: already over complex surfaces, neither Griffiths positivity nor ampleness can be characterized by positivity of Schur polynomials, even after passing to all quotients of the bundle — so the exact form-level positive cone is not a priori forced to match the Fulton–Lazarsfeld cone, and the naive form-level conjecture is a one-directional expectation (Griffiths positivity $\Rightarrow$ Schur-form weak positivity), not an equivalence.

## Problem Statement

For a Hermitian holomorphic vector bundle $(E,h)$ of rank $r$ that is Griffiths-positive (or Griffiths semipositive) over a complex manifold $X$ of dimension $n$, with Chern forms $c_k(E,h)$ given by Chern–Weil theory from the curvature of $h$: determine, without restriction on $r$ or $n$, the weighted-homogeneous symmetric polynomials $P$ such that the differential form $P(c_1(E,h),\dots,c_r(E,h))$ is pointwise weakly nonnegative — resp. strongly nonnegative — on every such bundle. The conjectural answer, forming the pointwise analogue of the Fulton–Lazarsfeld theorem, is that at least every Schur form $s_\lambda(c_1(E,h),\dots,c_r(E,h))$ is weakly positive for Griffiths semipositive bundles, and that the pointwise positive cone should contain the cone of nonnegative combinations of Schur polynomials with positive discriminant; whether this cone description is exact at the form level is part of the question. Known as of 2026: the case $r=1$ is classical, weak positivity of $c_1$, $c_2$ (any rank) and $c_3$ for Griffiths-positive bundles is established, the Nakano and dual Nakano (and strongly decomposable) cases are fully settled, and the complete affirmative answer is known when $r = n = 3$. The problem asks for the general statement — arbitrary rank and arbitrary base dimension, in both the weak-positivity and strong-positivity forms — or a characterization/counterexample delimiting the form-level positive cone.

The verification contract below evaluates answers to this statement. It does not narrow or redefine the research question.

Known solving difficulties:

- Multi-linear algebra of mixed discriminants: Finski's reduction leaves open the positivity of double mixed discriminants for positive linear maps on the cone of positive definite matrices beyond third-order settings, and no general tool for these operator-theoretic inequalities is currently known.
- Failure of the splitting principle at the form level: the Chern forms of a bundle cannot be simultaneously diagonalized as differential forms, so the cohomological techniques behind Fulton–Lazarsfeld (degeneration to Schubert cycles, ambient Grassmannians) do not transfer pointwise.
- Gap between weak and strong positivity: strong positivity of even low-degree Chern forms is delicate, with general criteria for (2,2)-forms obtained only in 2025 and only for special families; the strong cone is not closed under the natural operations available in the weak theory.
- Possible mismatch of the cone description: Finski's inverse results show that positivity of Schur forms cannot characterize Griffiths positivity or ampleness, so the exact form-level positive cone may deviate from the Fulton–Lazarsfeld cone, and any complete characterization must handle this asymmetry.
- No induction on rank or dimension: Wan's rank-three/threefold proof relies on the classification-level analysis of positive linear maps between 3×3 matrix spaces, and no mechanism is known to bootstrap it to higher rank or dimension.

## Current Progress

Griffiths (1969) posed the problem in its original form: determine the invariant polynomials that give positive forms on positive vector bundles, introducing the weak and strong positivity cones for (p,p)-forms that define the target notion; the problem has driven the study of characteristic forms of positive bundles since. (Primary source: DOI 10.1515/9781400871230-011.)

Fulton and Lazarsfeld (1983) settled the cohomological analogue completely: for ample bundles, the numerically positive polynomials are exactly the nonnegative combinations of Schur classes over partitions of positive discriminant. This fixed the conjectural shape of the form-level answer while leaving the pointwise question — the one Griffiths actually asked about differential forms — untouched, since the splitting principle and degeneration arguments have no pointwise counterpart. (DOI: 10.2307/2006953.)

Xiao (2020; published in Science China Mathematics 65 (2022), 51–62, DOI: 10.1007/s11425-020-1868-7, arXiv:2007.12425) proved a form-level statement for ample bundles in the top nontrivial degree: on a smooth projective n-fold, every nonzero Schur class of an ample bundle in bidegree $(n-1,n-1)$ admits a representative that is a strictly positive smooth form — evidence for Griffiths' prediction at the level of representatives rather than of the Chern forms of a given metric.

Diverio and Fagioli (2020, published 2022) proved that the universal Gysin (push-forward) formulae of Darondeau–Pragacz type hold pointwise at the level of Chern forms on flag bundles, and deduced positivity of several previously unknown families of polynomials in the Chern forms of Griffiths (semi)positive bundles. Fagioli (2022) extended the pointwise push-forward machinery to all universal bundles on flag bundles, yielding further positive families and a differential-form version of the Jacobi–Trudi identity; Fagioli (2020, Differential Geometry and its Applications 2022) additionally proved weak positivity of $c_1c_2 - c_3$ for rank-3 Griffiths semipositive bundles, recorded the reduction of $c_2$ positivity to the rank-2 case, and surveyed the state of the art with open questions. These give sub-cones of the expected positive cone, not a characterization. (Identifiers: Diverio–Fagioli, DOI: 10.2422/2036-2145.202011_021, arXiv:2009.14587; Fagioli, DOI: 10.1002/mana.70061, arXiv:2210.11157; Fagioli, DGA, DOI: 10.1016/j.difgeo.2022.101848, arXiv:2012.12815.)

Finski (2020, JLMS 2022) proved that Schur polynomials in the Chern forms of Nakano-positive and dual-Nakano-positive bundles are positive as differential forms; for Griffiths-positive bundles he reduced the weak positivity of Schur forms to an open statement on positivity of a 'double mixed discriminant' for linear maps preserving the cone of positive definite matrices. He also proved inverse-negative results: over complex surfaces, Griffiths positivity and even ampleness cannot be characterized by positivity of Schur polynomials, even using all quotients — showing the form-level cone question is genuinely one-directional and its exact shape is not settled by the cohomological theory. (DOI: 10.1112/jlms.12605, arXiv:2009.13107.)

Wan (2023, Forum of Mathematics Sigma 2024) introduced strongly decomposable positivity of types I and II — conditions intermediate between Griffiths and (dual) Nakano positivity — and proved that Schur forms are weakly positive, resp. positive, in the two types, giving an algebraic proof of Finski's Nakano case and answering Griffiths' question for these restricted curvature classes. (DOI: 10.1017/fms.2023.125, arXiv:2301.03950.)

Fagioli and Mainenti (2025) developed criteria for weak and strong positivity of (2,2)-forms via the associated Hermitian matrix, with a dimensionality reduction to $(2,2)$-forms in $\mathbb{C}^4$, and deduced strong positivity of special families of forms arising in the Griffiths program — progress on the strong-positivity half, where even low-degree cases are delicate. (arXiv:2502.17317.)

Wan (2026) proved positivity of the double mixed discriminant for positive linear maps between spaces of third-order complex matrices, settling the three-dimensional case of Finski's open problem; as applications he obtained the weak positivity of the third Chern form for Griffiths-positive vector bundles and proved that all Schur forms are weakly positive for Griffiths-positive vector bundles of rank three over complex threefolds — a complete affirmative answer to Griffiths' question in the case where both rank and dimension equal three. (arXiv:2601.10424.) No work located settles the general-rank, general-dimension question in either direction. The general weak-positivity question for Schur forms (rank ≥ 4 or dimension ≥ 4, and general Finski double-mixed-discriminant problem), the strong-positivity counterpart, and any complete form-level characterization of the positive cone all remain open. Papers of Demailly, Murakami, and Dong–Hu titled around 'Griffiths conjecture' concern the different conjecture that ampleness is equivalent to existence of a Griffiths-positive metric, and were excluded as adjacent, not this problem.

What remains uncertain: the precise rank generality claimed in Wan's abstract-level statement on $c_3$ (arXiv:2601.10424) is quoted verbatim without access to the full proof; publication status of the 2026 preprint is unknown; and the strong-positivity cone in low degrees is mapped only through examples, so a reviewer should treat strong-positivity claims as the least-charted part of the problem.

## Scientific Significance

Affected-field significance: `high`.

Complex differential geometry and the positivity theory of vector bundles. A resolution would directly convert the cohomological Fulton–Lazarsfeld positivity theory into pointwise Chern–Weil form inequalities, settling a characteristic-function question posed by Griffiths in 1969 and open in general for over five decades. Direct consequences include new pointwise positivity certificates and Chern-form inequalities usable in analytic estimates (e.g. for direct images, degenerate Hermitian metrics, and Chern number inequalities), and resolution of the operator-theoretic double-mixed-discriminant problem for positive linear maps, itself of independent interest in matrix analysis. The 2020–2026 burst of activity (Finski, Diverio–Fagioli, Fagioli, Xiao, Wan, Fagioli–Mainenti) shows the result would directly change the working methods of this active area rather than merely answer an isolated question.
