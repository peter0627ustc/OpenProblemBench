# ORB-MATH-02: Griffiths' 1969 positive-polynomial problem at the level of Chern forms: pointwise (weak) positivity of Schur forms for Griffiths-positive vector bundles

For a Griffiths-positive Hermitian holomorphic vector bundle $(E,h)$, Chern–Weil theory produces closed differential forms $c_k(E,h)$ representing the Chern classes. Since 1969 Griffiths has asked which weighted-homogeneous symmetric polynomials in these Chern forms are pointwise nonnegative as differential forms, and since Fulton–Lazarsfeld (1983) the conjectured form-level analogue of their cohomological description has been that nonnegative combinations of Schur polynomials give pointwise nonnegative forms. Despite substantial 2020–2026 progress — pointwise Gysin and push-forward formulae, positivity for the stronger Nakano and dual Nakano curvature conditions, a reduction to an open statement on double mixed discriminants of positive linear maps, and a complete affirmative answer when the rank and the base dimension are both three (Wan, 2026) — the naive form-level analogue has since been shown to fail in general — for every rank $r \geq 9$ there is a Griffiths-positive bundle whose top Chern form, itself a Schur form, is negative on a nonempty open set (Du, 2026) — so the exact form-level positive cone, and the strong-positivity counterpart, remain to be determined for Griffiths-positive bundles of general rank over bases of general dimension.

## Background

A Hermitian holomorphic vector bundle $(E,h)$ over a complex manifold $X$ carries a unique Chern connection, whose curvature $\Theta_E$ is a $(1,1)$-form with values in the endomorphisms of $E$. The bundle is Griffiths-positive (resp. Griffiths semipositive) if for every point $x \in X$, every tangent vector $v \in T_xX$, and every vector $\xi \in E_x$, the Hermitian form $\langle i\Theta_E(v,\bar v)\xi,\xi\rangle$ is positive (resp. nonnegative); this is the differential-geometric analogue of ampleness. Stronger curvature conditions include Nakano positivity and dual Nakano positivity, where $i\Theta_E$ is positive as a Hermitian form on all of $T_xX \otimes E_x$ or on $T_xX \otimes E_x^*$, respectively. Via Chern–Weil theory the curvature determines closed $(k,k)$-forms $c_k(E,h)$ — the Chern forms — representing the Chern classes $c_k(E)$ in de Rham cohomology, and more generally $P(c_1(E,h),\dots,c_r(E,h))$ for any symmetric weighted-homogeneous polynomial $P$.

Griffiths (1969) introduced two nested notions of pointwise positivity for $(p,p)$-forms: a form is strongly positive if it lies in the cone generated by wedge products $\frac{i}{2}dz_{j_1}\wedge d\bar z_{j_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\frac{i}{2}dz_{j_p}\wedge d\bar z_{j_p}$ in local coordinates, and weakly positive if it pairs nonnegatively against every strongly positive $(n-p,n-p)$-form of complementary degree. Strong positivity implies weak positivity, and on an $n$-dimensional manifold the two cones coincide for $p = 0, 1$ and, by duality of complementary degrees, also for $p = n-1$ and $p = n$; for the remaining degrees the inclusion is strict. On bundles the strong cone is the natural target because wedge products of strongly positive forms are again strongly positive. Griffiths asked which polynomials $P$ give pointwise (weakly or strongly) nonnegative forms $P(c(E,h))$ whenever $(E,h)$ is Griffiths-positive — the characteristic-function analogue of the classical fact that the curvature form of a positive line bundle is a positive $(1,1)$-form.

A complete answer is known cohomologically. The Schur polynomials $s_\lambda$ are the determinantal symmetric polynomials indexed by partitions $\lambda$ (given by the Jacobi–Trudi identities in the complete symmetric functions of the Chern roots); they form a linear basis of the weighted-homogeneous symmetric polynomials. Fulton and Lazarsfeld (1983) proved that on a smooth projective variety, a polynomial is numerically positive on every ample vector bundle (positive degree on all positive-dimensional subvarieties) if and only if it is nonzero and expands with nonnegative coefficients in the Schur basis, $P=\sum_\lambda a_\lambda s_\lambda$ with $a_\lambda \geq 0$ over the partitions $\lambda$ of its weighted degree. This is a statement in cohomology and says nothing about the pointwise behavior of the differential forms $s_\lambda(c(E,h))$, because the techniques it relies on — notably the splitting principle and degeneration to Schubert cycles — have no pointwise analogue: the Chern forms of a bundle cannot be simultaneously diagonalized as differential forms. Diverio and Fagioli (2020) describe Griffiths' question explicitly as the pointwise, hermitianized version of the Fulton–Lazarsfeld theorem.

The pointwise question is nontrivial already in low degree: weak positivity of $c_1(E,h)$ is immediate, weak positivity of $c_2(E,h)$ holds in rank two classically and in any rank by a reduction recorded by Fagioli (2020), and weak positivity of $c_3(E,h)$ for Griffiths-positive bundles was established only in 2026 by Wan, whose proof passes through a purely operator-theoretic statement — positivity of a double mixed discriminant (a multilinear determinant-like invariant) for positive linear maps between spaces of $3\times 3$ complex matrices — that Finski (2020) had identified as the obstruction in general. Wan (2026) also proved that all Schur forms of a Griffiths-positive bundle of rank three over a complex threefold are weakly positive, giving a complete affirmative answer to Griffiths' question in the case where both the rank and the dimension equal three. This affirmative picture does not extend to higher rank: Du (2026) constructs, for every integer $r \geq 9$, a smooth Griffiths-positive Hermitian metric on $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^r}(1)^{\oplus r}$ whose top Chern form — the Schur form $s_{(1^r)} = c_r$ — is pointwise negative on a nonempty open set, giving counterexamples on compact projective manifolds to the form-level positivity expectation. For the stronger Nakano and dual Nakano curvature conditions, Finski (2020) proved that all Schur forms are positive as forms, and Wan (2023) extended this to his intermediate notions of strongly decomposable positivity of types I and II. Complementing these positive results, Finski (2020) proved inverse-problem negatives: already over complex surfaces, neither Griffiths positivity nor ampleness can be characterized by positivity of Schur polynomials, even after passing to all quotients of the bundle — so the exact form-level positive cone is not a priori forced to match the Fulton–Lazarsfeld cone, and the naive form-level conjecture was a one-directional expectation (Griffiths positivity $\Rightarrow$ Schur-form weak positivity), not an equivalence — an expectation that is now known to fail in general.

## Problem Statement

For a Hermitian holomorphic vector bundle $(E,h)$ of rank $r$ that is Griffiths-positive (or Griffiths semipositive) over a complex manifold $X$ of dimension $n$, with Chern forms $c_k(E,h)$ given by Chern–Weil theory from the curvature of $h$: determine, without restriction on $r$ or $n$, the weighted-homogeneous symmetric polynomials $P$ such that the differential form $P(c_1(E,h),\dots,c_r(E,h))$ is pointwise weakly nonnegative — resp. strongly nonnegative — on every such bundle. The conjectured answer, forming the pointwise analogue of the Fulton–Lazarsfeld theorem, was that at least every Schur form $s_\lambda(c_1(E,h),\dots,c_r(E,h))$ is weakly positive for Griffiths semipositive bundles and that the pointwise positive cone should contain the cone of nonnegative combinations of Schur polynomials $s_\lambda$; this naive form-level analogue is now known to fail in general (the top Chern form $c_r = s_{(1^r)}$ is negative for $r \geq 9$; Du, 2026), so the question is to determine the exact form-level positive cone. Known as of 2026: the case $r=1$ is classical, weak positivity of $c_1$, $c_2$ (any rank) and $c_3$ for Griffiths-positive bundles is established, the Nakano and dual Nakano (and strongly decomposable) cases are fully settled, the complete affirmative answer is known when $r = n = 3$, and the naive expectation that all Schur forms are positive fails for ranks $r \geq 9$ (Du, 2026). A resolution consists in determining the exact form-level positive cone at arbitrary rank $r$ and base dimension $n$ — the set of weighted-homogeneous symmetric polynomials $P$ for which $P(c_1(E,h),\dots,c_r(E,h))$ is pointwise weakly nonnegative (and, separately, strongly nonnegative) on every Griffiths-semipositive bundle; neither a partial family of positive polynomials nor the now-known counterexample to the naive Schur-form expectation, taken alone, settles it.
