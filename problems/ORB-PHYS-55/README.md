# ORB-PHYS-55: Classification of planar stochastic electromagnetic sources whose beams have propagation-invariant degree of polarization

The degree of polarization of a random electromagnetic beam generally changes as the beam propagates, even in free space. Zhao and Wolf (2008) constructed the first class of partially polarized sources — planar, quasi-homogeneous, Gaussian-Schell-correlated sources with uncorrelated transverse field components, equal intensity widths, and equal correlation widths — whose radiated beams have a degree of polarization identical at every point in space. Subsequent work produced further sufficient conditions for special families: Gaussian Schell-model beams, completely polarized sources, fields with position-independent stochastic behavior, and Bessel-correlated quasi-homogeneous sources (far zone). No necessary-and-sufficient characterization of the planar sources that generate beams with everywhere propagation-invariant degree of polarization is known. This problem asks for that classification, with physical realizability taken into account, subsuming or delimiting all known families and resolving which of the published sufficient conditions are in fact necessary.

## Background

A stochastic (random) electromagnetic beam is described, at each temporal frequency $\omega$, by the cross-spectral density matrix (CSD matrix): the $2\times2$ matrix of correlations $W_{ij}(\boldsymbol{\rho}_1,\boldsymbol{\rho}_2,\omega)=\langle E_i^*(\boldsymbol{\rho}_1,\omega)E_j(\boldsymbol{\rho}_2,\omega)\rangle_\omega$ between the transverse components $i,j\in\{x,y\}$ of the field at two points of a plane. Only genuine CSD matrices — those that can arise as correlations of a physically realizable random field, and in particular are non-negative definite — correspond to physical sources. From the equal-point matrix one computes the spectral degree of polarization (DoP),

$$
P(\boldsymbol{\rho},z;\omega)=\sqrt{1-\frac{4\det W(\boldsymbol{\rho},\boldsymbol{\rho},z;\omega)}{\left[\operatorname{tr}W(\boldsymbol{\rho},\boldsymbol{\rho},z;\omega)\right]^2}},
$$

whose value in $[0,1]$ gives the fraction of the spectral density carried by the fully polarized part of the field at that point. A Gaussian Schell-model (GSM) source has Gaussian intensity profiles and Gaussian correlation coefficients; an electromagnetic GSM (EGSM) source allows the two components to have different amplitudes, widths, and correlation widths, and possible cross-correlations between them. A source is quasi-homogeneous when its intensity varies on a length scale much larger than its correlation width, so that the correlations depend, to a good approximation, only on the coordinate difference. Propagation from the source plane $z=0$ to any plane $z>0$ is described, within paraxial optics, by the Fresnel integral transform applied to each element of the CSD matrix.

James (1994) first demonstrated that the DoP of a beam generally changes on propagation even in free space, and many examples followed. Wolf (2007) derived sufficient conditions under which the DoP of beams generated by a wide class of uniformly polarized stochastic sources takes the same value in the source plane and throughout the far zone — but said nothing about the intermediate region. Zhao and Wolf (2008) closed this gap for one class: planar, secondary, quasi-homogeneous sources whose $x$ and $y$ components are uncorrelated ($W_{xy}^{(0)}=W_{yx}^{(0)}=0$), whose spectral densities are Gaussian with equal widths $\sigma_x=\sigma_y$, and whose self-correlation coefficients are Gaussian with equal widths $\delta_{xx}=\delta_{yy}$. For these sources the DoP is the same at every point of free space, $P=|A_x^2-A_y^2|/(A_x^2+A_y^2)$, with the polarized part linearly polarized along $x$; the equality of correlation widths was checked numerically to be necessary only within the investigated parameter regime.

Later work broadened the sufficiency landscape but never produced a classification. For EGSM beams, criteria were given for remaining completely unpolarized or completely polarized (Du and Zhao 2008), and a sufficient condition — equal intensity widths and equal $xx$, $yy$, $xy$ correlation widths — for maintaining the full state of polarization on propagation (Zhao et al. 2009), corroborated experimentally by Stokes-parameter modulation measurements (Verma et al. 2013). Completely polarized sources were characterized by a universal correlation-matrix form (Gori 2008), and the fields that remain totally polarized under any deterministic non-polarizing first-order optical system were identified (Martínez-Herrero and Mejías 2007). Martínez-Herrero and Mejías (2010) introduced a general class of random electromagnetic fields with position-independent stochastic behavior whose degree of polarization and related stochastic parameters are preserved at any propagation distance; Korotkova (2011) gave sufficient conditions for DoP and state-of-polarization invariance into the far zone for quasi-homogeneous sources, with an electromagnetic Bessel-correlated example showing that non-Gaussian correlations can qualify. That the off-diagonal source correlations genuinely enter the problem was demonstrated by Xin et al. (2008): two quasi-homogeneous sources with the same spectral degrees of coherence and polarization but different degrees of cross-polarization — a measure of the correlation between the transverse field components — can generate beams with different far-field degrees of polarization. On the experimental side, partially coherent beams with a propagation-invariant transverse polarization pattern were synthesized (Ramírez-Sánchez et al. 2010) and developed into Mueller-matrix polarimetry with invariant-pattern beams (de Sande et al. 2021); the introduction of the former paper states that deriving the conditions a general field must satisfy to keep its polarization characteristics invariant on free propagation is one of the main tasks of the subject. Delimiting results show where invariance fails: twist generally makes the DoP of EGSM array beams evolve (Zhou and Zhao 2019), apertures can polarize partially coherent beams (Wang et al. 2019), the two-component DoP of nonparaxial vector beams decreases on free-space propagation except for special hybrid vector Bessel modal solutions (Petrov 2022), numerical work finds that atmospheric turbulence changes vector-beam DoP only at the $10^{-13}$–$10^{-6}$ level (Tao et al. 2023), and curved spacetime changes the DoP (Chuang and Parihar 2024).

What no inspected work provides is a necessity-and-sufficiency theorem: a characterization, on the genuine CSD matrix of an arbitrary planar source, of exactly those sources whose beams have a DoP identical at every point of free space. All published results are sufficient conditions for subclasses, and even inside the EGSM family the published invariance conditions are stated as sufficient, with necessity examined only numerically in specific regimes. Beyond its theoretical interest for the relation between source coherence and polarization stability, such a classification would ground applications that rely on polarization staying predictable in flight: free-space polarization-shift-keying optical communications, polarimetry, and uniform-polarization imaging and sensing.

## Problem Statement

Derive necessary and sufficient conditions on the genuine cross-spectral density matrix $W^{(0)}(\boldsymbol{\rho}_1,\boldsymbol{\rho}_2,\omega)$ of a planar secondary source such that the beam generated by paraxial free-space propagation has a spectral degree of polarization $P(\boldsymbol{\rho},z;\omega)=\sqrt{1-4\det W/(\operatorname{tr}W)^2}$ that is identical at every point — independent of the transverse position $\boldsymbol{\rho}$ and the propagation distance $z$, for each frequency $\omega$ — and thereby classify the sources with propagation-invariant degree of polarization. The classification must subsume or explicitly delimit every known family: the quasi-homogeneous Gaussian-Schell sources of Zhao and Wolf (2008), the EGSM criteria of Du and Zhao (2008) and Zhao et al. (2009), the completely polarized sources of Martínez-Herrero and Mejías (2007) and Gori (2008), the fields with position-independent stochastic behavior of Martínez-Herrero and Mejías (2010), and the Bessel-correlated quasi-homogeneous sources of Korotkova (2011, far zone). It must resolve which of the published sufficient conditions are necessary — in particular whether everywhere-invariant DoP forces uncorrelated transverse components ($W_{xy}^{(0)}=0$) and matched intensity and correlation widths, or whether cross-correlated and non-quasi-homogeneous sources also qualify — and should clarify how the everywhere-constant notion relates to the weaker requirement that $P$ be independent of $z$ at each fixed $\boldsymbol{\rho}$. Constructions of new realizable invariant classes and delimiting non-invariant families are linked components of this single classification objective, not independent goals. The problem is posed within the paraxial free-space framework of the source paper; nonparaxial propagation and propagation through media (turbulence, curved spacetime) are adjacent extensions outside this completion boundary.

The verification contract below evaluates answers to this statement. It does not narrow or redefine the research question.

Known solving difficulties:

- Necessity requires showing that the Fresnel transform of an arbitrary genuine CSD matrix preserves the scalar ratio defining the DoP at every transverse point and every propagation distance only under restrictive structural conditions; this couples all matrix elements and all spatial scales of the source and does not reduce to known transform identities.
- Physical realizability (non-negative definiteness and genuineness of the CSD matrix) constrains the admissible sources and interacts with the invariance conditions, so the classification cannot be performed on formal matrices alone.
- The known sufficient families are heterogeneous — quasi-homogeneous Gaussian-Schell, Bessel-correlated quasi-homogeneous, position-independent stochastic behavior, completely polarized universal forms — expressed in different parametrizations, and a unified theorem must subsume or delimit all of them consistently.
- Published conditions within model families are sufficient only, and numerical evidence for necessity is regime-specific; separating genuine necessity from artifacts of Gaussian or quasi-homogeneous ansätze requires new arguments.
- Cross-correlated components ($W_{xy}^{(0)}\neq0$) and non-quasi-homogeneous sources are barely explored, so part of the classification space may require extending existing propagation-analysis techniques.

## Current Progress

- Status: `ready`

Source fidelity: the candidate question is not posed by the authors of the source paper; the LKM open-question record itself (node gcn_6cb40fc80a624387 attached to DOI 10.1016/j.optcom.2008.02.006) states that the paper fully characterizes one quasi-homogeneous Gaussian-correlated class and leaves unexplored whether other source classes also yield propagation-invariant degree of polarization. The paper's content was verified through the raw LKM paper graph and multiple independent citing descriptions (the article is Elsevier-paywalled): Zhao and Wolf (2008) prove sufficiency of uncorrelated components with equal Gaussian intensity widths and equal Gaussian correlation widths, with DoP $P=|A_x^2-A_y^2|/(A_x^2+A_y^2)$ at every point and the polarized part linearly polarized; necessity of the equal-correlation-width condition was examined only numerically in one parameter regime. No misreading or conflation of adjacent results by LKM was found, and the audited problem is stated as the classification question, matching the framing the community itself uses (Ramírez-Sánchez et al. 2010 call deriving such conditions for a general field 'one of the main tasks in this subject').

Lineage before the source: James (1994) showed that the DoP generally changes on free propagation; Wolf (2007) obtained sufficiency conditions for DoP equality between the source plane and the far zone only; Zhao and Wolf (2008) settled existence for the quasi-homogeneous Gaussian-Schell class at every point of space.

Within the EGSM family, Du and Zhao (2008) gave criteria for beams staying completely unpolarized or completely polarized on propagation, and Zhao et al. (2009) gave a sufficient condition (equal intensity widths; equal $xx$, $yy$, $xy$ correlation widths) for maintaining the full state of polarization, corroborated experimentally by Verma et al. (2013). All are stated as sufficient conditions; no necessity theorem for this family exists.

Completely polarized sources are understood through the universal correlation-matrix form of Gori (2008) and the propagation-stable totally polarized fields of Martínez-Herrero and Mejías (2007); these give sufficiency for $P=1$ beams but not a general classification.

Martínez-Herrero and Mejías (2010) introduced a general class of random electromagnetic fields with position-independent stochastic behavior whose DoP and related stochastic parameters are preserved for any propagation distance — the strongest known generalization beyond Gaussian and quasi-homogeneous structure. The paper is phrased as exhibiting a certain kind of field (sufficiency), not as a necessity theorem; its precise class definition could not be verified against the paywalled full text (abstract-level evidence only), which is the main residual uncertainty about how broad the known classes already are.

Korotkova (2011) proved sufficient conditions for DoP and state-of-polarization invariance into the far zone for electromagnetic quasi-homogeneous sources, with an electromagnetic Bessel-correlated example — non-Gaussian correlations, but quasi-homogeneous and far-zone only; the title itself ('Sufficient condition...') confirms necessity was open as of 2011.

Experimental and applied line: Ramírez-Sánchez et al. (2010) synthesized partially coherent beams with a propagation-invariant transverse polarization pattern and explicitly identify the general-conditions task as open; de Sande et al. (2021) developed invariant-pattern (totally polarized, Gaussian-mode-combination) beams into Mueller-matrix polarimetry. Vidal et al. (2011) demonstrated experimental control of DoP evolution via source coherence, confirming the practical handle the classification would systematize.

Delimiting and negative results: Xin et al. (2008) show that quasi-homogeneous sources with identical spectral degrees of coherence and polarization but different degrees of cross-polarization generate beams with different far-field degrees of polarization, so the off-diagonal source correlations are an independent knob; Zhou and Zhao (2019) show twist makes the DoP of EGSM array beams evolve; Wang et al. (2019) show apertures can polarize partially coherent beams; Petrov (2022) shows the two-component DoP of nonparaxial vector beams decreases on propagation except for special hybrid vector Bessel modal solutions; Tao et al. (2023) find numerically that atmospheric turbulence changes vector-beam DoP only at the $10^{-13}$–$10^{-6}$ level; Chuang and Parihar (2024) treat DoP change in curved spacetime. None addresses the paraxial free-space classification.

Coverage and status: all 27 forward citations of the source paper (Semantic Scholar) plus forward-citation chains of the key follow-ups and Crossref bibliographic searches through 2026 were inspected; no work states, or claims to state, necessary and sufficient conditions for arbitrary planar sources. The existence half of the candidate question (do non-Gaussian, non-quasi-homogeneous classes exist?) is answered affirmatively by the later literature (Martínez-Herrero and Mejías 2010; Gori 2008; Korotkova 2011 in the far zone; de Sande et al. 2021), so the surviving open core is the complete classification, retained at its natural generality. Coverage limits: Google Scholar and subscriber-only Optica cited-by lists were inaccessible, and the source paper's full text was reconstructed indirectly; a necessity theorem in an obscure or very recent publication cannot be fully excluded, though nothing in the inspected corpus suggests one.

## Scientific Significance

Affected-field significance: `medium`.

A complete classification would directly settle a recognized task of statistical coherence theory — the precise relation between the second-order source structure (the CSD matrix) and the propagation stability of polarization — replacing the current patchwork of sufficient conditions for special families with a single criterion, and it would directly enable principled design of invariant-polarization sources for free-space polarization-shift-keying optical communications, Mueller-matrix polarimetry with invariant-pattern beams, and uniform-polarization imaging and sensing. The impact is direct but confined to the statistical-optics subfield and its applications rather than changing the capabilities of optics as a whole.
