# ORB-PHYS-68: Universality of the critical finite-size free-energy correction with central charge $c=1$ in non-solvable Ashkin–Teller and eight-vertex models

Conformal field theory predicts that the leading finite-size correction to the free energy of a two-dimensional critical lattice model on a cylinder of circumference $\ell$ is $c\pi/(6\ell^2)$, where $c$ is the central charge of the critical theory. Giuliani and Mastropietro proved this universality rigorously for non-solvable perturbed Ising models ($c=1/2$) and explicitly posed its extension to the $c=1$ class — the Ashkin–Teller and eight-vertex families — where the critical exponents are rigorously known to vary continuously with the interaction strength while the finite-size amplitude is predicted by conformal field theory to be pinned at $c=1$, i.e. $\pi/(6\ell^2)$. This audit verified the source formulation against the primary literature and examined the subsequent rigorous literature: forward citation chains of the source paper and of the Ashkin–Teller renormalization-group program, the constructive-renormalization-group work on non-integrable dimers, the authors' own 2020 survey accounts, and the recent rigorous Ashkin–Teller phase diagram. The exponent side (extended scaling relations) is settled, but no proof or refutation of the $c=1$ finite-size free-energy amplitude exists; the problem is recorded as open, with its source-faithful formulation and verification contract.

## Background

A central prediction of two-dimensional conformal field theory (CFT) concerns the finite-size corrections to the free energy of a critical lattice model. If a model at its critical point is placed on a torus $\Lambda_{\ell,L}$ of sides $\ell$ and $L$ with periodic boundary conditions and the limit $L\to\infty$ is taken first (leaving an infinitely long cylinder of circumference $\ell$), then the free energy per site behaves as
$$
\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{1}{\ell L}\log Z_{\beta_c}(\Lambda_{\ell,L}) = f_\infty + \frac{c\pi}{6\ell^2} + o(\ell^{-2}),
$$
where the dimensionless constant $c$ is the central charge — the anomaly coefficient of the stress-energy tensor of the critical theory, the quantity that labels universality classes in the classification of two-dimensional CFTs. This relation, established in the physics literature by Affleck and by Blöte–Cardy–Nightingale, ties a purely thermodynamic observable to the conformal anomaly and makes $c$ one of the most robust fingerprints of a universality class.

For exactly solvable (integrable) models — models whose partition function can be computed in closed form — the relation can be checked directly: for the nearest-neighbor Ising model ($c=1/2$) it follows from the exact solution and the finite-lattice computation of Ferdinand and Fisher. Giuliani and Mastropietro proved the first non-solvable counterpart: for the Ising model with Hamiltonian $H=-J\sum_{x,j}\sigma_x\sigma_{x+\hat e_j}-\lambda\sum_{\{x,y\}}\sigma_x v(x-y)\sigma_y$, where $v$ is a positive, translation- and rotation-invariant, finite-range potential and $\lambda\ge 0$ is small, at the critical temperature $\beta_c(\lambda)$ the same formula holds with $c=1/2$ exactly, independent of the perturbation. The proof combines a Grassmann representation (an exact rewriting of the partition function as an integral over anticommuting variables, which makes the fermionic structure of the critical theory explicit), a partition-function inequality, valid for ferromagnetic $\lambda\ge 0$, that isolates the antiperiodic sector carrying the universal term, and a multiscale fermionic renormalization group (RG) analysis — an iterative, scale-by-scale integration of the degrees of freedom — showing that the interacting partition function equals a renormalized free-fermion determinant times corrections that shift only the bulk free energy and not the leading finite-size term. This was the first rigorous confirmation, in a non-integrable model, of the CFT prediction that links critical exponents to finite-size corrections of thermodynamic functions.

The natural next tier is the class $c=1$, whose paradigmatic lattice representatives are the Ashkin–Teller model — two identical ferromagnetic Ising copies with spins $\sigma,\tau$ coupled by a four-spin term, $H=-J\sum_{\langle x,y\rangle}(\sigma_x\sigma_y+\tau_x\tau_y)-\lambda\sum_{\langle x,y\rangle}\sigma_x\tau_x\sigma_y\tau_y$ — and the eight-vertex model, to which zero-field Ashkin–Teller-type models are related by a standard mapping. Unlike the Ising case, these models possess a critical line rather than an isolated critical point: in RG language, a line of fixed points, along which an exactly marginal operator (a perturbation of scaling dimension exactly 2, whose coupling is neither suppressed nor amplified by the flow) allows the interaction to remain switched on at large scales. Along this line most critical exponents vary continuously with the interaction strength. This non-universality of exponents has itself been proved non-perturbatively in weak coupling: by Mastropietro for stacked Ising models with four-spin interaction, by Giuliani and Mastropietro for the anisotropic Ashkin–Teller model, and by Benfatto–Falco–Mastropietro, who proved the exact extended (Kadanoff) scaling relations expressing all these exponents in terms of a single one. CFT nevertheless predicts that the leading finite-size coefficient of the free energy is pinned along the whole line to the value $c=1$, i.e. $\pi/(6\ell^2)$, independent of the interaction: at the decoupling point this is exactly the sum of two independent Ising contributions (each $\pi/(12\ell^2)$ by Ferdinand–Fisher), and away from it the Gaussian fixed-point line is expected to preserve it. Giuliani and Mastropietro state explicitly that this universality for $c=1$ models “is unproved so far” and that they hoped “to come back to this question in a future publication”; the problem below is that extension.

## Problem Statement

Prove the $c=1$ analogue of the Giuliani–Mastropietro universality theorem for the finite-size free energy at criticality. Consider a two-dimensional lattice model in the $c=1$ universality class with a genuinely non-solvable interaction — for instance the Ashkin–Teller model of two coupled ferromagnetic Ising copies, a stacked pair of Ising models with four-spin interaction, or a weakly perturbed eight-vertex model — with interaction strength $\lambda$ in the weak-coupling regime where the existing rigorous renormalization-group results on the $\lambda$-dependent critical exponents apply. Placed on the torus $\Lambda_{\ell,L}$ with periodic boundary conditions, side $\ell$, and the limit $L\to\infty$ taken first, at its critical line $\beta_c(\lambda)$, show that
$$
\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{1}{\ell L}\log Z_{\beta_c(\lambda)}(\Lambda_{\ell,L}) = f_\infty(\lambda) + \frac{\pi}{6\ell^2} + o(\ell^{-2}) \qquad (\ell\to\infty),
$$
that is, Eq. (1.4) of Giuliani–Mastropietro holds with central charge $c=1$, independent of the interaction strength $\lambda$ — even though other critical exponents and amplitudes of the same model vary continuously with $\lambda$ along the critical line. The result should include explicit, controlled error bounds comparable to those of the $c=1/2$ theorem (for example finite-volume localization errors of order $\ell^{-4}$ or an analogous sharp control), an identification or implicit characterization of $\beta_c(\lambda)$, and a treatment of the marginal direction such that any logarithmic corrections remain subleading to the $\pi/(6\ell^2)$ term. Any rigorous method is admissible; a derivation relying only on the exact solvability of special points of the eight-vertex family does not by itself answer the question, since the target is universality beyond solvability.

The verification contract below evaluates answers to this statement. It does not narrow or redefine the research question.

Known solving difficulties:

- Line of fixed points: universality must be proved along a whole critical line with an exactly marginal direction, where most quantities (exponents and correlation amplitudes) are non-universal; the proof must isolate the single surviving invariant, the central charge, through exact cancellations.
- Renormalization of the Gaussian fixed point: in the $c=1$ fermionic representation (two Grassmann components with a current-current, massless-Thirring-type interaction), the finite-size determinant is evaluated with renormalized Luttinger parameters; one must prove that this renormalization shifts only the bulk free energy and not the leading finite-size term — a statement analogous to anomaly non-renormalization, relying on emergent chiral Ward identities.
- Sign and positivity control: the partition-function inequality that isolates the antiperiodic sector in the $c=1/2$ proof relies on ferromagnetic positivity and has no established analogue for four-spin-coupled models; controlling the boundary-condition sectors of the interacting $c=1$ partition function requires new ideas.
- Critical-line identification: $\beta_c(\lambda)$ is not explicitly known for the interacting model (the rigorous phase diagram of Aoun–Dober–Glazman locates the transition curves but not explicitly), so criticality must be characterized implicitly, for example through the vanishing of the renormalized mass.
- Marginal corrections: subleading logarithmic corrections generated by the marginal operator must be separated from the leading $\pi/(6\ell^2)$ amplitude, and the finite-volume localization error control (of order $\ell^{-4}$ in the Ising case) must be re-established in the presence of the marginal coupling.

## Current Progress

- Status: `ready`

Source fidelity and scope. The candidate derives from an LKM node (gcn_40858a9e2fa14bef) summarizing Giuliani–Mastropietro, Comm. Math. Phys. 324, 179 (2013); the paraphrase was verified against the arXiv full text. The paper states verbatim that for $c=1$ models such as the eight-vertex and Ashkin–Teller models “the finite size correction to the free energy are expected to be independent of $\lambda$ and given by Eq.(1.4) with $c=1$, but this fact is unproved so far”, and the authors hoped to return to the question. The LKM node bundles three further open issues from the same paper: the rigorous computation of the spin-field exponent $1/4$ at $\lambda\neq 0$; removal of the ferromagnetic assumption $\lambda\ge 0$ in the partition-function inequality; and technical extensions of the finite-volume localization scheme. The fourth LKM bullet (longer-range or less symmetric perturbations with $O(\ell^{-4})$ error control) is an extrapolation from technical remarks in the proof rather than an explicitly stated open problem. Since these items are scientifically independent questions with separate completion boundaries, this record focuses on the $c=1$ universality item identified as the coherent core at Discovery; the sibling items are reported here for context.

The exponent side of $c=1$ universality is settled rigorously in weak coupling: Mastropietro, Comm. Math. Phys. 244, 595 (2004), computed the interaction-dependent critical exponents of stacked Ising models with four-spin interaction; Giuliani–Mastropietro, Comm. Math. Phys. 256, 681 (2005), proved anomalous universality in the anisotropic Ashkin–Teller model; Benfatto–Falco–Mastropietro, Comm. Math. Phys. 292, 569 (2009) and Phys. Rev. Lett. 104, 075701 (2010), proved the exact extended (Kadanoff) scaling relations among those exponents. None of these works addresses the finite-size free-energy amplitude.

All forward citations of the source paper (16 on Semantic Scholar, inspected individually) extend the $c=1/2$ Ising story rather than the $c=1$ one: Greenblatt, arXiv:1409.1852, generalized the finite-size result to cylindrical geometry; Antinucci–Giuliani–Greenblatt, Ann. Henri Poincaré (2021), and the companion energy-correlation papers treat non-integrable Ising models on the cylinder; Cava–Giuliani–Greenblatt, J. Math. Phys. 66, 023301 (2025), proved the scaling limit of boundary spin correlations in non-integrable Ising models (progress on the sibling spin-exponent question, where the bulk exponent $1/4$ remains unproved); Gallone–Mastropietro, Comm. Math. Phys. (2024), proved Harris–Luck irrelevance for quasi-periodic Ising models. None proves the $c=1$ amplitude.

The $c=1$ constructive-renormalization-group program on non-integrable dimers proves Gaussian-free-field height fluctuations and the Haldane exponent–amplitude identities (Giuliani–Mastropietro–Toninelli, J. Stat. Mech. 2017, 034002; Giuliani–Toninelli, J. Math. Phys. 60, 103301 (2019)), which is the closest existing technology but concerns correlation amplitudes, not the free energy. Mastropietro's 2020 review of universality on critical lines (arXiv:2011.08924) records the extended scaling relations as the proven core and lists spin exponents, more general boundaries, and optimal coupling-size estimates as open problems; the central-charge/finite-size amplitude is nowhere claimed as solved. Giuliani's 2020 account (arXiv:2012.01084) likewise lists the spin-correlation scaling limit and boundary effects in marginal theories as open.

Adjacent rigorous results locate the phase diagram but not finite-size asymptotics: Aoun–Dober–Glazman, Comm. Math. Phys. 405 (2024), established the Ashkin–Teller transition curves in the ferromagnetic regime, so the critical line on which the conjectured amplitude statement lives is now itself partially rigorous. Physics evidence (the exact solution of the zero-field eight-vertex model, transfer-matrix and numerical studies) uniformly supports $c=1$ along the critical line, but no rigorous derivation of the finite-size amplitude exists for non-solvable models, nor, as far as this audit could establish, for the solvable zero-field eight-vertex model itself.

Coverage and residual uncertainty. The search combined forward-citation chains of the source paper and of Giuliani–Mastropietro (2005), full bibliographies of both authors, arXiv keyword sweeps (the query “finite-size + central charge + non-solvable” returns only the source paper itself; also “Ashkin-Teller + central charge”, “Casimir amplitude”, “interacting dimers + free energy”, “Luttinger liquid + central charge + free energy + rigorous”), and the authors' own 2020 open-problem accounts. No proof, partial proof, or refutation of the $c=1$ finite-size amplitude was found, and no later paper was found that removes the $\lambda\ge 0$ restriction of the original theorem. Possible blind spots: works not indexed on arXiv or Semantic Scholar, or phrased without these keywords; no literal post-2013 “remains open” sentence was located either, so openness rests on the systematic absence across these literature chains rather than on an explicit recent statement.

## Scientific Significance

Affected-field significance: `high`.

A proof would be the first rigorous establishment of the conformal-field-theory central-charge/finite-size prediction for a non-solvable model whose critical exponents are non-universal: it would show that the leading finite-size free-energy amplitude $c=1$ is the invariant labeling the entire line of fixed points of the Ashkin–Teller/eight-vertex family, completing the universality program initiated with the $c=1/2$ Ising theorem of Giuliani–Mastropietro. The impact on rigorous statistical mechanics and on the mathematical verification of conformal field theory is direct: it upgrades the confirmed CFT universality from the minimal-model regime (isolated fixed point, universal exponents) to the marginal $c=1$ regime (line of fixed points, drifting exponents), which is the regime relevant to Luttinger-liquid-type criticality. It would also directly extend constructive renormalization group capability, which currently covers only exponents and scaling relations in this class, to thermodynamic finite-size amplitudes.
